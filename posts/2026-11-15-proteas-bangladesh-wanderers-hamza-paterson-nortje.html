Sport

Hamza and Paterson are back in the Proteas XI. Nortje isn't, and rain might decide the rest.

Sport Desk

The Proteas walk out at the Wanderers this morning carrying a tag they have never had to defend on home soil before: world number one. That ranking was earned the hard way, in a 2-1 series win over Australia that re-set expectations for a team that spent years being judged against its potential rather than its results. Bangladesh arrive as the opponent nobody is supposed to lose to and precisely the kind of fixture where that assumption has burned South African sides before, which is why Temba Bavuma has spent the week talking about ruthlessness rather than margin.

The team sheet tells its own story about where this squad is right now. Zubayr Hamza and Dane Paterson both return to the XI, Hamza getting another chance to cement the middle order and Paterson adding seam options on a Wanderers surface that has historically rewarded exactly that kind of bowling. Simon Harmer keeps his place ahead of Keshav Maharaj, a selection call that leans into conditions rather than reputation. The more consequential absence is Anrich Nortje, ruled out with a groin injury that removes the one bowler in the attack built purely around raw pace, and hands a share of his workload to Kagiso Rabada, who has made no secret of his intentions this week.

"We want to get in their faces," Rabada said ahead of the Test, which is not subtlety so much as a stated gameplan: use the Bullring's pace and bounce to unsettle a Bangladesh top order that Bavuma has called evolving rather than weak, a careful distinction from a captain who watched his own team get complacent against supposedly lesser opposition before. Bangladesh's captain Najmul Hossain Shanto was still weighing up an extra batter when team news broke, a sign that the tourists know exactly what kind of pitch they are walking into and are still deciding how much batting depth it is worth sacrificing bowling options to survive it.

Weather may end up mattering as much as either team sheet. Forecasters have flagged rain for the opening days of the match, which at a ground known for making early inroads count double could compress five days of cricket into three and reward whichever side gets its decisive session in before the covers come on. A rain-shortened Test tends to punish the team that bats second and bowls worse in the conditions that are actually available, which puts extra weight on whatever happens at the toss this morning.

There is a bigger picture sitting behind this specific Test, too. This is the first of two matches, with the series moving to SuperSport Park in Centurion on 23 November, and a world number one side opening its home summer against Bangladesh carries an obligation that goes beyond the scoreline: prove the Australia series was a floor, not a ceiling. Anything less than a dominant result at the Wanderers will reopen exactly the kind of inconsistency questions this squad spent the past two months trying to put away for good.
