581 women killed in three months, and almost 2,000 reported missing: the numbers behind this week’s crime figures

The latest police statistics show 581 women were murdered between July and September. A separate parliamentary reply reveals hundreds of missing-women cases that remain unresolved.

Crime Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • 581 women were killed between July and September 2026, 131 of them in Gauteng
  • Overall murders fell to 5,590 in the quarter, 204 fewer than a year earlier
  • Almost 2,000 women have been reported missing in the past 18 months; 936 cases are unresolved
  • Eight people have been arrested in connection with the killings of 13 women around Ekurhuleni

South Africa’s crime statistics for July to September, released on Friday, showed a small fall in murders overall. But behind the national total sits a figure that is hard to read: 581 women were killed in those three months, an average of more than six a day. Gauteng accounted for 131 of them, according to figures reported by OkayAfrica.

Overall, police recorded 5,590 murders in the quarter, 204 fewer than in the same period last year. It is not clear whether the number of women killed has risen or fallen, because police had recently stopped publishing that breakdown, citing significant anomalies in the data, and comparable figures for last year were not provided alongside it.

A second set of numbers adds to the concern. In a written reply to Parliament, Acting Police Minister Firoz Cachalia disclosed that almost 2,000 women have been reported missing since April 2025: 1,380 cases in the 2025/26 financial year and another 604 between April and September this year. Of those cases, 936 remain unresolved, The Citizen reports.

Arrests in the Ekurhuleni killings

Police say they are making progress in one of the cases that has frightened communities east of Johannesburg most. The bodies of 13 women have been found around Kempton Park, Boksburg and Tembisa since July. Gauteng police commissioner Lieutenant General Tommy Mthombeni said on Thursday that eight people had been arrested in connection with several of those killings.

The first of the 13 women was found in Kempton Park in July, and others were found in Boksburg and at a cemetery in Tembisa in September. Police have not said whether they believe the deaths are linked, and the charges the suspects face, and their court dates, have not yet been made public.

In the Free State, police are searching for three masked men who opened fire on a family at a house in Botshabelo on Wednesday night, killing five women and a young child. The gunmen were reportedly looking for the owner of the house, who was not home. His wife was wounded and taken to hospital. No arrests had been reported by Friday.

What the government says

Cachalia has said there must be greater efforts to prevent violence against women and children. President Cyril Ramaphosa promised in September that no stone would be left unturned in the hunt for those responsible for the Ekurhuleni killings.

The two sets of figures show why consistent data matters. Without a regular breakdown of murders of women, and without clear reporting on what happens to missing-person cases, it is hard for the public to judge whether the police response is improving. The minister’s reply suggests that almost half of the missing-women cases reported since April 2025 are still open.

If you or someone you know is in danger, the Gender-Based Violence Command Centre can be reached at 0800 428 428, and emergencies should be reported to police on 10111.
