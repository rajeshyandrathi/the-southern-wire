Rare spring snow blankets the Drakensberg as a powerful cut-off low sweeps the country

Weather Desk·Sunday, 20 September 2026

Spring has not gone quietly this year. A powerful cut-off low-pressure system tore through the country in mid-September, dragging icy temperatures, heavy rain and disruptive snowfall across a swathe of South Africa and Lesotho that stretched from the Northern and Western Cape all the way through to the Drakensberg and the Free State.

The Southern Drakensberg and the high ground of Lesotho bore the brunt of the whiteout. Reports and footage from the region showed snow accumulating to genuinely striking depths on the higher peaks, transforming the escarpment into a scene more familiar to a July cold snap than a mid-September one, and prompting the usual scramble of snow-chasers and disruption alike across the affected passes.

Down at lower elevations, the story was less picturesque but more disruptive. Southern Namakwa, the Central Karoo, the Little Karoo and the Cape Winelands all found themselves under a very cold, wet and windy warning from the South African Weather Service, with Cape Town enduring an overcast, rain-lashed stretch with temperatures struggling to reach the high teens, and George seeing similarly grim conditions with scattered rain and moderate southwesterly winds keeping the mercury between 11°C and 14°C.

Further east, the same system brought flooding rain to parts of KwaZulu-Natal, the Eastern Cape and the Free State, layering a second hazard — localised flooding — on top of the cold and snow already testing infrastructure and travel across the affected provinces. For a country whose spring is usually associated with warming temperatures and the first hints of summer, this cut-off low was a sharp reminder that Southern Africa's weather rarely reads the calendar.

With the system now clearing, forecasters expect the usual post-front pattern to reassert itself: clearing skies, a rebound in temperatures, and the Drakensberg's rare spring snowpack beginning its slow retreat back to the peaks where it belongs until the next cold snap comes calling.
