Hermanus hits peak whale season as Southern Rights crowd the bay

Lifestyle Desk·Monday, 2 November 2026

Hermanus earned its reputation as one of the best land-based whale watching spots on the planet for a reason that becomes obvious to anyone who stands on its cliff path at the right moment: Southern Right Whales come close enough to shore here that binoculars feel almost unnecessary. Right now, with the 2026 season deep into its peak stretch, that reputation is being put through its paces daily as mothers and calves linger in the bay before the long journey back south.

The whales started arriving as early as late May, when the first calves of the season were spotted alongside their mothers in the sheltered waters off the Overberg coast. Since then the numbers have built steadily toward the August-to-October peak that draws visitors from across the world, and sightings remain strong through November as the final stragglers take their time before departing for sub-Antarctic feeding grounds.

What makes Hermanus unusual, even among the world's best whale-watching destinations, is how little equipment or expense is required to see something genuinely spectacular. The cliff path running along the town's coastline is free, reliably positioned, and — on a good day — close enough to the action that paid boat tours can start to feel optional rather than essential. Licensed boat operators do still run regular trips from the New Harbour throughout the season for visitors who want a closer, more active encounter, weather permitting.

The town leans into the spectacle rather than treating it as incidental tourism infrastructure. Hermanus's whale festival, now a fixture of the local calendar, turns the peak of the season into a genuine civic celebration — part conservation awareness, part tourism showcase, and part excuse for a town built substantially around its marine neighbours to throw a party in their honour.

For visitors weighing when to make the trip, the next few weeks still offer a reasonable window before the whales begin their southward migration in earnest. Beyond that, Hermanus settles back into its quieter off-season rhythm — which is exactly why locals say there's no substitute for catching the bay while it's this full.
