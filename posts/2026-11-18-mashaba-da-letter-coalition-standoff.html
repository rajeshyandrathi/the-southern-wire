Mashaba hands the DA an exit — on one condition: no Zille

The Southern Wire Desk·Johannesburg

Herman Mashaba has found the one lever Johannesburg's coalition math actually responds to, and he is pulling it in two directions at once. In a letter to Democratic Alliance leader Geordin Hill-Lewis, the ActionSA leader proposed that the DA keep running the country's largest city after all — so long as the mayor is not Helen Zille. ActionSA, in his telling, would take the deputy mayorship and let a DA figure other than Zille hold the top job. It is a door left open to the party that topped the polls in Johannesburg. It is also, pointedly, a door built with a single name carved out of it.

That the offer needed making at all says everything about how personal this standoff has become. Zille has long been the DA's most forceful presence in Gauteng politics, and it is precisely that forcefulness Mashaba appears unwilling to govern alongside. His letter does not pretend otherwise: the condition is not about policy, coalition shares or committee seats. It is about who sits in the chair. For a party that spent the campaign insisting it would never again hand power to what Mashaba himself once called an "irresponsible" DA administration, the proposal is a notable softening, provided the DA blinks first on its own candidate.

While that offer sits with Hill-Lewis, a second, more consequential conversation has been happening without the DA in the room. The ANC, ActionSA, the EFF and MK Party have been exploring an alternative arrangement that would instead install Mashaba himself as Johannesburg's mayor, keep Nasiphi Moya running Tshwane, and hand Ekurhuleni to the ANC outright — a three-metro carve-up that would shut the DA out everywhere despite its lead in the vote count. ANC secretary-general Fikile Mbalula has been careful not to commit to either path publicly, saying only that the party's top seven officials will decide once the final results and the ANC's own standing in each metro are formally confirmed.

The numbers explain why ActionSA, not the DA or the ANC, is the one setting terms. Vote counts put the DA comfortably ahead in Johannesburg, with the ANC's own share falling into the high teens and ActionSA landing in the mid-teens — not enough for Mashaba's party to govern alone, but enough to decide who does. That kind of kingmaker arithmetic is exactly why Mashaba can simultaneously dangle cooperation at the DA and negotiate its exclusion with the ANC: whichever side needs his votes more will have to meet his price, and right now neither has been told what that price definitively is.

A clock is running underneath all of it. Councils have a limited window after an inconclusive election to elect a mayor, and that window is already well into its second week since results were finalised, pushing the deadline for Johannesburg to find a leader toward the back end of November. Every day the ANC's top structures take to decide, and every day Hill-Lewis takes to weigh a letter that effectively asks the DA to sideline its own provincial leader, is a day closer to a forced vote nobody can yet predict with confidence.

What is clear is that the DA's plurality in Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni counts for less in this process than the coalition arithmetic surrounding it. Whether that ends with Zille's party still holding the mayoralty under a different face, or with Mashaba himself in the chair backed by the ANC he once swore never to touch, the result will be decided in rooms the voters who delivered the DA its lead were never invited into.
