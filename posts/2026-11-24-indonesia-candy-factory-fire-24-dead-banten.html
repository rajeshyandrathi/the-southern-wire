At least 24 dead and dozens missing after fire tears through a sweet factory near Jakarta

The blaze at a confectionery plant in Banten province started on Saturday evening and was still burning 12 hours later. Families are giving DNA samples because remains cannot be identified by sight.

World Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • The fire broke out at about 5pm on Saturday at a factory in Cikande, Serang regency, Banten
  • A presidential labour adviser said 24 people died; about 30 were still missing on Monday
  • About 300 people were on site, 65 of them in the worst-hit area, the company said
  • Officials are examining whether a short circuit in the air-conditioning system started it

At least 24 people have been killed and about 30 were still missing on Monday after a fire destroyed a confectionery factory in Indonesia, Reuters reported.

The two-storey factory, run by PT Fonusa Agung Mulia in Cikande in Serang regency, Banten province, about an hour’s drive from Jakarta, makes milk sweets and lollipops. The fire broke out at around 5pm on Saturday 21 November. Twelve hours later, 15 fire engines were still fighting it, a Banten search and rescue official told AP.

The victims

Said Iqbal, a labour adviser to President Prabowo Subianto, said 24 people had died. A rescue official said 24 body bags of remains had been recovered, but that the final toll could not yet be confirmed, The Korea Herald reported, citing Reuters.

Chief executive Steven Erwin Wijaya said about 300 people were on site for the shift, 65 of them in the worst-hit area. Twenty-nine people have been confirmed safe, five of them in hospital. Because remains cannot be identified visually, the national police identification unit is collecting DNA from relatives, which could take weeks. The company has set up a hotline for families.

What caused it

The cause is under investigation. After visiting the site on Monday, Iqbal said officials were examining whether a short circuit in the air-conditioning system started the fire, The New York Times reported via People. He said safety procedures were being assessed and that he would summon the company’s chief executive. Rescuers said remaining hot spots and an increasingly unstable building were putting them at risk.

The factory’s parent company, United Family Food, employs about 5,000 people and also makes instant noodles. It did not immediately comment.

Why it matters

With about 30 people still unaccounted for, the toll may rise. The investigation into the cause, and the government’s review of safety procedures at the plant, are the next steps to watch.
