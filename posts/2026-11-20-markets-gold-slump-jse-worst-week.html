Gold has its worst week since March and the JSE slides again: today’s markets in one place

Gold fell to around $3,520 an ounce and platinum lost almost 5% as bets on a US rate hike lifted the dollar. The JSE All Share ended Friday down about 1.5% near 104,700 points, and the rand is trading near R17.46 to the dollar.

Business Desk·Friday, 20 November 2026·4 min read ·Updated 11:30pm SAST

Key points

  • Gold about $3,520/oz late on Friday, down about 2.9% on the day and roughly 8% on the week
  • Silver about $40.9/oz (down about 4.6%); platinum about $1,283/oz (down about 4.8%)
  • JSE All Share about 104,680, down 1.45%; Top 40 about 96,936, down 1.47%
  • Rand about R17.46 to the dollar, R19.19 to the euro and R22.89 to the pound
  • Correction: the rand was near R17.45 this week, not R16.6 as we first reported

It was a rough end to the week for anyone holding precious metals or South African shares. Gold kept sliding through Friday and was trading at about $3,520 an ounce late in the day, down close to 3%, after touching its lowest level since September 2025. Reuters reported that it was on course for a fall of about 8% over the week, its worst week in eight months, as traders priced in near-certain odds of an interest rate hike by the US Federal Reserve in December and the dollar firmed.

Silver and platinum fell harder as the day went on. Silver was at about $40.9 an ounce, down roughly 4.6%, and platinum, which matters a great deal to South Africa’s mining industry, dropped almost 5% to about $1,283 an ounce. Earlier on Friday we reported that platinum was holding up better than gold; that was true in the morning, but it did not last.

On the JSE, the All Share index ended Friday at about 104,680 points, down about 1.45% from Thursday’s close of 106,216, which had already been its lowest close since September 2025. The Top 40 fell by a similar amount to about 96,936. The index is down about 4.5% over the past month. Among the biggest companies, Naspers fell about 4.1%, Richemont about 3.3%, Anglo American Platinum about 3.2% and AngloGold Ashanti about 2.7%, while Vodacom edged up.

Correction: our earlier version of this report said the rand was trading around R16.6 to the US dollar this week. That was wrong. The rand closed Thursday at about R17.45, its weakest close in more than a year, and was trading near R17.46 on Friday. We apologise for the error.

Why gold is falling

Gold pays no interest, so it tends to lose appeal when interest rates rise or are expected to rise. With markets now betting heavily on a US rate hike, the yields on US government bonds have climbed and the dollar has strengthened. Both make gold more expensive for buyers using other currencies and less attractive compared to assets that pay a return.

The fall this year has been steep. Business Day reported this week that gold is down about 30% since late February. Much of the safe-haven premium that pushed it to records earlier in the year has unwound as rate expectations have turned, and Friday’s sell-off also came alongside a sharp fall in US technology shares.

What it means for South Africa

South Africa is one of the world’s biggest producers of platinum and a major gold miner, so metal prices feed straight into mining company profits, tax revenue and the rand. Falling gold, silver and platinum prices, combined with a firmer dollar, help explain why both the JSE and the rand have weakened this month. Local politics has added to the pressure: analysts have linked part of the rand’s slide to uncertainty over who will govern Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni after the local elections.

There is a small upside for consumers. A weaker rand makes imports dearer, but lower global gold prices can ease jewellery prices, and the expected December fuel price cut should offer some relief at the pump.

Diamonds: a market still searching for a floor

Diamonds do not trade on an exchange with a single daily price the way gold and platinum do. Prices are set through private sales and tracked by industry indexes. The latest trade reports show prices for smaller stones, between about 0.3 and 0.7 carats, still falling, while prices for 1 to 2 carat stones are starting to stabilise and larger stones are holding steady.

The biggest story in the industry is the sale of De Beers. Botswana has picked a consortium led by former De Beers chief executive Gareth Penny as its preferred bidder, and wants to double its own stake in the company to 30%. De Beers sold far more rough diamonds in its third quarter after cutting prices in July, but the average price it received fell sharply, a sign of how hard the market has been squeezed by weak demand and competition from lab-grown stones.
