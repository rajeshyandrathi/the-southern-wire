AI giants jostle over chips, copyright and oversight

Tech Desk·Saturday, 19 September 2026

Nvidia is projecting it will sell roughly double the number of AI chips in 2027 compared with 2026, a forecast that underscores just how far the current demand cycle still has to run. For an industry that has spent two years wondering when the chip shortage would ease, the message from Nvidia's leadership is that the curve is still bending upward, not flattening.

That growth is colliding with a messier set of legal and ethical questions. Unredacted filings in a closely watched copyright dispute involving a major AI assistant have revealed internal concern among executives over a steep decline — as much as 93% in some measurements — in the traffic publishers receive when readers get their answers directly from an AI tool instead of clicking through to the original site. It is the clearest evidence yet that the substitution effect long feared by publishers is showing up in the numbers.

The industry's leading figures are also fighting a quieter battle over how they get regulated. Several prominent AI and tech executives have pushed back against proposals for an industry-funded oversight body, arguing it would slow innovation without meaningfully improving safety. That resistance comes as content moderation questions keep surfacing elsewhere in the ecosystem — a major social platform's oversight board recently ordered the removal of two AI-generated deepfake videos, a reminder that enforcement is still happening case by case rather than through any settled framework.

Taken together, the week's developments capture an industry growing faster than the rules meant to govern it — chip demand accelerating, legal exposure mounting, and the loudest voices in the room still arguing over who gets to write the rulebook.
