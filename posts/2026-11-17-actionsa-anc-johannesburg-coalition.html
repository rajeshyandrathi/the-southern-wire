Politics — SA & Africa

ActionSA is about to decide who runs Johannesburg. The DA that won the most votes isn't in the room.

The Southern Wire Desk·Johannesburg

There is a particular kind of absurdity in South African coalition politics that Johannesburg is about to live through again. The Democratic Alliance topped the polls in all three of Gauteng's biggest metros on 4 November, the only party that can make that claim in Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni alike. By the time coalitions are actually stitched together over the next week, it may govern none of them. The party doing the stitching is not the ANC, which finished second almost everywhere, but ActionSA, which finished third and is now behaving like it finished first.

Herman Mashaba's party has edged visibly closer to an agreement with the ANC that would carve up the three metros without the DA in any of them. Under the arrangement now being discussed, Mashaba himself would become Johannesburg's mayor. His ActionSA colleague Nasiphi Moya would keep the Tshwane mayoralty she has already held since October 2024, extending a partnership with the ANC that has quietly outlasted most predictions. Ekurhuleni, the least contested of the three in this round, would go to the ANC outright. It is a tidy division of spoils for two parties that, between them, did not win the most votes in a single one of these cities.

The arithmetic that makes this possible was set in motion on 12 November, when the ANC's national executive committee met in Boksburg and resolved that the DA should be treated as a last resort in forming local coalitions, not a first choice. ActionSA, by multiple accounts, was named as the preferred partner instead. That resolution did not mention Ekurhuleni, Tshwane or Johannesburg by name, but it did not need to. It set the terms for exactly the negotiations now playing out, and it told ActionSA precisely how much leverage it was about to be handed.

ActionSA has not been shy about using that leverage. The party is reportedly insisting that Helen Zille, the DA's own candidate for the Johannesburg mayoralty, be kept out of the post as a condition of any deal — a demand aimed less at policy than at a specific rival. It has also set conditions around service delivery, anti-corruption commitments and mayoral stability that read as much like leverage theatre as governing philosophy, given how unstable Johannesburg's own ActionSA-ANC-adjacent coalitions have been in the past. Whether those conditions survive contact with an actual signed agreement is still an open question.

What is not in question is how badly this outcome would sting the DA, which spent the municipal campaign building exactly the kind of lead it now risks seeing converted into irrelevance. The party remains the largest single bloc on all three councils. It simply cannot find the votes to turn that size into power, because the party with the next-largest bloc in each city would rather share the smaller slice with a weaker partner than hand the DA the premiership of local government. Coalition arithmetic, as Johannesburg has learned repeatedly since 2016, rewards whoever is willing to deal, not whoever the voters actually preferred.

Parties have roughly two weeks from the start of this month to get councils sworn in and mayors elected, which puts a decision point within days, not weeks. The ANC's NEC has already flagged a second sitting early next year to reassess the DA's place in the national Government of National Unity if its local directive is ignored by provincial structures — a reminder that what is unfolding in three city halls is also a dry run for how far the ANC is willing to go in squeezing its biggest national coalition partner everywhere else.
