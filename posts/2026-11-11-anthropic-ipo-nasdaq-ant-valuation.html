One of AI's biggest labs is about to go public, at a price that would stagger any other industry

Tech Desk

Anthropic is pushing ahead with plans to list on the Nasdaq, with a valuation range expected to be set this week and trading reportedly targeted to begin before the end of next week. Reports on the exact number diverge — some put the target as high as $1.5 trillion, others closer to $1.4 trillion — but even the lower figure would make it one of the largest public listings in corporate history, for a company whose core product is a conversational AI model that barely existed in its current form five years ago.

The timing is not incidental. Just days before the IPO process accelerated, Anthropic released its newest model generation at a notably lower price point than rival offerings, pitching it as both cheaper and more capable than OpenAI's latest frontier release. Pairing a product launch with an IPO run-up is a familiar tactic for companies trying to walk investors through a growth story in real time, rather than asking them to take projections on faith.

Anthropic has also been pushing beyond its original chatbot product into workplace tools, with a feature that lets its AI assistant join video calls as what the company describes as a visible participant — an attempt to move the product from something people open in a browser tab to something that sits inside the actual meetings and workflows of corporate life. That shift matters enormously to the IPO story: investors pricing a $1.4 trillion valuation are not betting on chatbot subscriptions alone, but on AI embedding itself into enterprise software budgets at scale.

Not every signal from inside the AI research world has been reassuring. Independent evaluators at METR reportedly found that Anthropic's own models are improving at nearly twice the pace they were previously, a finding that feeds directly into longstanding concerns about AI systems accelerating their own development in ways that outpace human oversight. That it comes from an outside evaluator, not a competitor with an obvious incentive to raise alarms, is part of why the finding has drawn attention even amid IPO euphoria.

The broader industry backdrop makes Anthropic's valuation look less like an outlier and more like a marker of where the whole sector has moved. OpenAI was reportedly valued at $1.25 trillion after a fresh $11 billion capital raise, Google has pushed out new multimodal models with expanding enterprise availability, and chipmaker TSMC has committed up to $1 billion in investment spread across both Anthropic and OpenAI. A voluntary AI accord signed at the White House by the leaders of every major American AI lab suggests policymakers are trying to keep pace with an industry whose market capitalisation now rivals the GDP of mid-sized nations.

Whether Anthropic's public listing performs the way its bankers expect will say as much about investor appetite for AI as a category as it will about the company itself. A trillion-dollar-plus valuation leaves almost no room for disappointment, and the gap between the figures different outlets are reporting — a full $100 billion of uncertainty — is itself a reminder of how much of this valuation still rests on projection rather than audited history.
