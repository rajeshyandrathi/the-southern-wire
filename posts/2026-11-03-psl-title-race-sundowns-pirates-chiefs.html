Sundowns and Pirates trade blows at the Betway Premiership summit as Chiefs refuse to fade

Sport Desk·Tuesday, 3 November 2026

South Africa's two most decorated football rivals, Mamelodi Sundowns and Orlando Pirates, are locked into one of the tightest Betway Premiership title races in recent memory, separated by the narrowest of margins at the top of the table after a run of weekend fixtures that did little to settle the argument over which club holds the early psychological edge. Sundowns' recent dominance of the league has made them the default favourites every season, but Pirates have refused to let the gap open up.

What has changed this campaign is the third name forcing its way back into genuine title contention: Kaizer Chiefs. After years in which Amakhosi's league form barely registered as a factor in the destination of the trophy, a run of consistent results has put them back in touching distance of the top two, reviving a three-way Soweto-and-Pretoria rivalry that the league's marketing department has been waiting years to sell again.

The compressed table reflects a broader shift in the Premiership's competitive balance. Clubs like Sekhukhune United and AmaZulu have shown they can hang near the summit for stretches of a season rather than fading by October, forcing the traditional big three to drop fewer points than they have grown accustomed to. Every midweek fixture now carries table-shifting weight in a way that simply wasn't true when Sundowns were winning the league by double-digit margins.

For Pirates and Chiefs, the calculation is straightforward: keep pace with Sundowns' relentless squad depth for long enough, and the brutal accumulation of fixtures across cup competitions and African continental football could eventually force errors from a Sundowns side also chasing silverware on multiple fronts. It is a wager both clubs have lost before, but rarely have they entered November this well-placed to test it again.

With the business end of the season still months away, nothing about this title race is close to decided — but for the first time in a while, Premiership followers have a genuine reason to watch the table every single week rather than simply assuming they already know how it ends.
