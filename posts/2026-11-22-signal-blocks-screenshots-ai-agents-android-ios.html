Signal starts blocking screenshots on phones to keep AI assistants out of your chats

The private messaging app says AI agents built into phone operating systems are an existential threat, because they read what is on screen. Its new protections are on by default.

Tech Desk·Sunday, 22 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Signal now restricts screenshots inside the app on Android and iOS to stop operating-system AI agents reading chats
  • Signal’s president called OS-level AI agents an existential threat to the app
  • Users can switch the protection off, but Signal warns this may expose messages to AI agents
  • Signal is asking Apple and Google for a proper way for apps to opt out of AI data access

Signal, the encrypted messaging app, has started restricting screenshots inside the app on Android and iOS, to stop the AI assistants built into phone operating systems from reading private conversations, The Hacker News and Digital Trends reported.

Signal Foundation president Meredith Whittaker described operating-system AI agents on Android, iOS and Windows as an existential threat to Signal. Her argument is that these agents work by looking at what is on the screen, which means they can see messages that Signal’s encryption is meant to keep private.

How it works

On iOS 27, Signal now blocks screenshots in the app and adds a setting to block Siri’s AI. On Android, its Screen Security feature switches on when Signal detects an AI assistant trying to read the screen. The Hacker News reported that the restriction is on by default. Users can turn it off under the privacy settings, but Signal warns that doing so may expose their own and their contacts’ messages to AI agents.

On a Mac, Signal says it cannot reliably block screenshots, and advises users to add Signal to the excluded apps list in the Siri settings.

The limits

Signal calls the fix incomplete. An AI agent could still read content already stored on the device in other ways. The company is asking Apple and Google to give apps a documented way to fully opt out of AI data access, and to explain more clearly how their agents handle data.

The move builds on a block Signal introduced on Windows 11 in May 2025, after Microsoft’s Recall feature, which takes regular snapshots of the screen, drew criticism. The Hacker News also cited a Reuters report that Meta’s AI agent read Signal messages through accessibility and screenshot tools on macOS and through notifications on Android.

What it means for you

If you use Signal, you may find you can no longer screenshot your chats. That is deliberate. More broadly, the change shows a growing clash between private messaging and AI assistants that want to see everything on your phone. The next move is up to Apple and Google.
