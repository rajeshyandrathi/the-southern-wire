Missiles shot down over Riyadh after Houthi strike on PIF tower injures 11

Saudi air defences intercepted two ballistic missiles fired at the capital overnight, two days after a drone hit the skyscraper that houses the kingdom’s sovereign wealth fund. The Houthis also claim to have set the Ras Tanura refinery on fire.

World Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • The Saudi-led coalition says it destroyed two Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh early on Thursday, 26 November, and two drones over the Eastern Province
  • On Tuesday a drone hit a tower in Riyadh’s King Abdullah Financial District, which witnesses said was the PIF skyscraper, injuring 11 people
  • The Houthis say they struck the Ras Tanura refinery and caused fires; a satellite image from 25 November shows a large smoke cloud over the site
  • The coalition also reports a drone downed near Jeddah Islamic Port, a cruise missile destroyed and minor damage at Jazan port

Saudi air defences destroyed two ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh early on Thursday, 26 November, the Saudi-led coalition fighting in Yemen said. Spokesman Turki al-Maliki blamed the Houthis and later said two drones had also been shot down over the Eastern Province, Anadolu Agency reported. He gave no details of casualties or damage.

Gulf News reported that an explosion was heard around midnight after an alert for a “hostile aerial threat” was issued and then lifted. Residents in the north and east of the capital reported the blast, and one said their windows shook violently. China’s Xinhua news agency reported three interceptions in all, counting a cruise missile aimed at the west of the kingdom.

On Wednesday the coalition also said it shot down a drone near Jeddah Islamic Port and that a Houthi projectile caused minor damage, but no civilian injuries, at Jazan port. The Houthis said those strikes on Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea ports, reported here on Wednesday, were revenge for coalition attacks on Hodeidah port in Yemen.

Eleven hurt in the financial district

The overnight interceptions followed a drone strike on Tuesday, 24 November, on a tower in Riyadh’s King Abdullah Financial District. Saudi civil defence said 11 people were injured: two Saudis, two Indians, three Nepalis and four Egyptians, Al Jazeera reported. The coalition did not name the building, but three witnesses told AFP it was the skyscraper that houses the Public Investment Fund, the country’s sovereign wealth fund and its tallest tower. A civil defence photo showed damage about halfway up the facade.

It was the second strike on the district in less than a week, after the 19 November attack that damaged the same tower and was reported here on 21 November. Unlike some recent attacks, no emergency alert was sent to phones beforehand; one witness said a vehicle with a loudspeaker told pedestrians to take cover. Al-Maliki called the attack “cowardly” and promised a “firm response”.

The Gulf Cooperation Council, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Pakistan, Lebanon and the United States all condemned the strike. According to Al-Monitor, citing Semafor, Saudi officials have asked partners including the US, Britain and France for more air defence equipment, including systems for intercepting drones.

Ras Tanura and the ground war

The Houthis also claimed an attack on the Ras Tanura refinery on the Gulf coast. Spokesman Yahya Saree called it “accurate and direct” and said it started fires, CBS News reported. Our report on Wednesday carried the coalition’s statement that it had destroyed four drones over the refinery and that falling fragments caused no injuries; since then, a Planet Labs satellite image dated 25 November, published by Al Jazeera, has shown a large smoke cloud over the site. Saudi authorities had not described any damage by the time of publishing.

The war is also turning on the ground. Gulf News reported that Houthi fighters are about 60 kilometres from Aden, the government’s main southern stronghold, and a Yemeni military spokesman said the city’s defences were being prepared. Saree has said Saudi forces carried out 72 air strikes on Sanaa and six other provinces, killing and wounding civilians, though he gave no figures. Al Jazeera reported an explosion in Sanaa on Thursday after recent strikes near its airport.

The attacks have already forced the cancellation of most international flights to Riyadh and the postponement or cancellation of major events, including the AFC Asian Cup, Al Jazeera reported. With the Houthis promising to keep hitting Saudi cities and the coalition vowing to respond, there is no sign of the 2022 truce returning soon.
