Top of the group, one night from a derby: Bafana's eve in Soweto

Sport Desk

Bafana Bafana train under the Soweto lights tonight with a position most African qualifying campaigns would envy: top of Group D, unbeaten, and one win away from putting real daylight between themselves and the rest of the pack before Friday's visit from Kenya. The platform was built in September, when a 2-1 win over Guinea marked Hugo Broos's successor era under the returning Steve Mosimane with exactly the kind of statement victory a new coaching setup needs to settle nerves inside a dressing room still absorbing a change at the top.

Kenya travel to Orlando Stadium with a very different set of incentives. Benni McCarthy's side has already secured its place at the 2027 tournament as a co-host alongside Uganda and Tanzania, which removes the desperation that usually accompanies a qualifier but replaces it with something almost as motivating: a former Bafana striker given the chance to beat the national team that made him, on South African soil, in front of a crowd that will not let him forget where he came from.

Kenya's results so far tell a story of a team still finding its identity under McCarthy. A 1-1 draw with Eritrea exposed gaps that will concern him against a South African side playing with far more attacking fluency, but Kenya have shown the capacity to frustrate stronger opponents when their back line sets up compactly, and McCarthy has made no secret of wanting this fixture to be the moment his rebuilding project announces itself against top-tier continental opposition.

For South Africa, the calculus is brutally simple: win, and the gap at the top of Group D becomes close to unassailable with qualification all but secured well ahead of schedule; stumble, and a tight five-day turnaround to the return leg in Nairobi suddenly carries far more pressure than anyone wanted heading into it. Samukelo Kabini's late winner against Guinea has already made him a player opposition defences are having to plan specifically around, and expect Orlando Stadium's famously vocal crowd to lean on every mistake Kenya's back four makes under pressure.

Friday's kickoff will be the first real test of whether September's win over Guinea was the start of something durable or a one-off high against a side having a bad night. Either way, the stakes are set: a home win puts Bafana in command of the group with games to spare, while anything less turns next week's trip to Nairobi from a formality into a genuine six-pointer.
