Russia hits Kyiv’s bridges over the Dnipro, killing at least eight people across Ukraine

Ballistic missiles damaged three of the capital’s river crossings and cut power and water in several districts on Friday, a day after Vladimir Putin promised a painful response to Ukraine’s strikes on the Crimean Bridge.

World Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Three Kyiv bridges over the Dnipro damaged: Podilskyi, Darnytskyi and Pivdennyi
  • Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi says eight people were killed nationwide, six in Kyiv, and 28 wounded
  • Power cuts in Kyiv and 10 regions after nuclear plants reduced output
  • Russia says it targeted bridges used to move weapons; Putin had vowed a “much more painful” response

Russia launched a heavy missile and drone attack on Ukraine overnight into Friday, striking three of the bridges that link the two halves of Kyiv across the Dnipro River. Ukraine’s Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said eight people were killed across the country, six of them in Kyiv, and 28 were wounded, according to Al Jazeera and Euromaidan Press. Early reports from Kyiv’s mayor had put the city’s toll at three.

Ballistic missiles damaged the Podilskyi crossing and the Darnytskyi bridge, and struck the Pivdennyi, or Southern, bridge again. RTE described it as the biggest attack on the routes between the river’s banks since the war began. Kyiv, a city of about three million people, relies on six main bridges for commuters. Traffic on the Southern bridge was restricted to lighter vehicles and public transport.

“Missiles struck the capital’s critical infrastructure, including bridges,” Koretskyi said, adding that a water facility and office buildings were also damaged. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the dead in Kyiv were preliminarily employees of the city’s water utility, and he urged residents to avoid driving into the city and asked employers to let staff work from home.

Power and water cut

The energy company DTEK said parts of five Kyiv districts lost electricity, and Kyiv’s water utility reported low water pressure across several districts. First Deputy Energy Minister Artem Nekrasov said nuclear power plants had reduced their output, causing outages in Kyiv and 10 regions, Al Jazeera reports.

Other cities were also hit. In Lviv, near the Polish border, a strike on a railway station killed two people, according to regional officials quoted by Euromaidan Press. Drones hit a hospital in Kharkiv and a tram in Zaporizhzhia. In Poland, authorities said they were investigating an object that fell in a field in the southeast of the country, and flights at Rzeszow airport were briefly suspended.

Russia’s defence ministry said it had targeted road and rail bridges in Kyiv that it claimed were used to move weapons and military equipment, as well as defence, energy and transport sites. It also said it had shot down 310 Ukrainian drones.

Retaliation for the Crimean Bridge

The attack came a day after President Vladimir Putin, speaking in China at his summit with Donald Trump and Xi Jinping, said Russia would respond to Ukraine’s strikes on the Kerch Bridge, the road and rail link between Russia and occupied Crimea. He said the response would be “much more painful”. Ukraine has hit the bridge three times in a week, and Putin said its road section was seriously damaged.

The strikes complicate a diplomatic picture that had looked slightly more hopeful. President Volodymyr Zelensky said this week that cooperation between Ukraine, the US and Europe on negotiations was becoming closer, and he hoped the US would host a first multilateral meeting with Europeans soon. US envoy Jared Kushner said Russia was comfortable with Europe joining talks.

Moscow’s position on the core issues has not moved. Deputy Foreign Minister Sergey Ryabkov said any form of Ukrainian NATO membership was unacceptable, though security guarantees could be discussed once Kyiv accepted that. For the people of Kyiv, the immediate concern is getting across a damaged city, and keeping the lights and taps on, as winter sets in.
