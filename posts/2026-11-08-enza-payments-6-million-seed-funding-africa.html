A Johannesburg-to-Cairo payments startup just raised $6 million betting Africa's merchants need fewer middlemen, not more

Tech Desk

Enza, a payments infrastructure company operating across Egypt, South Africa, Ghana and Nigeria, has closed a $6 million Seed+ round led by 4DX Ventures, with British International Investment, Quona Capital and Algebra Ventures also participating. The raise adds to a year in which capital for African fintech has kept flowing toward infrastructure plays, the unglamorous plumbing that lets merchants accept and move money, even as funding for more consumer-facing products has grown noticeably more selective.

The company's four-market footprint is itself the pitch. Rather than building a single-country payments product and expanding opportunistically, Enza has positioned itself from early on as cross-border infrastructure, aiming to let merchants and partner platforms plug into one system across markets with wildly different regulatory regimes, currencies and banking infrastructure. That ambition is harder to execute than a single-market product, but it is also the kind of structural bet that investors focused on African fintech have increasingly favoured over the past two years, after watching several high-growth, single-market consumer apps struggle to translate early traction into durable margins.

The round lands against a broader funding backdrop that is recovering only unevenly. Startup funding across Africa rose just 1.4% year on year to $1.44 billion in the first half of 2026, even as the number of completed deals fell sharply, a pattern industry trackers describe as capital concentrating into fewer, larger rounds for companies perceived as lower-risk rather than spreading more thinly across a wider base of early-stage bets. South African fintechs in particular have continued attracting a disproportionate share of that capital, building on a 2025 in which the sector took more than a third of all funding raised by South African startups.

Enza's backers include names that have become familiar presences in African fintech rounds over the past eighteen months, and 4DX Ventures' leadership of this raise continues a pattern of US-based funds anchoring deals for companies with genuinely pan-African ambitions rather than single-market plays. British International Investment's participation adds a development-finance dimension to the round, reflecting the kind of blended public-private capital that has increasingly underwritten African payments infrastructure as purely commercial venture capital has grown more cautious.

For merchants and smaller platforms across the four markets Enza serves, the practical test of this funding will be whether it translates into meaningfully easier cross-border payment acceptance, the problem the company was built to solve, rather than simply extending its runway in a funding environment where staying capitalised has itself become a competitive advantage.
