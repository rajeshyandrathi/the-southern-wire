Indonesia has no vice president, and the race to choose one is reshaping its politics

Gibran Rakabuming Raka, son of former president Joko Widodo, resigned last week after courts found his school documents did not meet election rules. Parliament is expected to pick a successor next month.

World Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Gibran resigned on 12 November after two courts ruled against his education documents
  • President Prabowo Subianto must propose two candidates; the People’s Consultative Assembly then votes
  • The vote was set for 2 December; one report says it has moved to 9 December at Prabowo’s request
  • The resignation has strained the alliance between Prabowo and Joko Widodo

Indonesia, the world’s fourth most populous country, has been without a vice president for more than a week. Gibran Rakabuming Raka, the eldest son of former president Joko Widodo, handed a handwritten resignation letter to leaders of the People’s Consultative Assembly (MPR) on 12 November, according to the Jakarta Post, Al Jazeera and Arab News.

His resignation followed rulings in October by both the Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court that the overseas education documents he submitted did not meet the requirements for the 2024 election. Critics had accused him of never obtaining a high school diploma. Gibran insisted the decision was his own. “This decision is my personal decision and has nothing to do with diplomas,” he said in a video statement.

President Prabowo Subianto said he accepted the resignation “with a heavy heart” and praised Gibran for putting the interests of the nation first. Gibran urged his supporters to keep backing the president, inside or outside government.

How a new vice president is chosen

Indonesia’s constitution does not call a new election when the vice presidency falls vacant. Instead, the president proposes two candidates and the MPR, which brings together both houses of parliament, elects one of them within 60 days. The winner serves out the rest of the term to 2029.

MPR Speaker Ahmad Muzani said the assembly would vote on 2 December and formed a 36-member team to check candidates’ credentials. On Friday, the Rio Times reported that the vote had been moved to 9 December at Prabowo’s request and that he had until 25 November to submit two names. We have not been able to confirm the new date from an official source.

No candidates have been officially named. Political reports cited by the Rio Times say Gerindra chairman Sufmi Dasco Ahmad is the name mentioned most often.

Cracks in the ruling alliance

Gibran’s place on the 2024 ticket was the product of an alliance between Prabowo and Widodo, who endorsed his former rival. His candidacy was only possible after the Constitutional Court, then headed by Widodo’s brother-in-law, lowered the minimum age for the role. Analysts say the resignation could widen the gulf between the two men. “The political alliance has become strained, effectively broken,” political scientist Ujang Komarudin told Arab News.

Widodo has played down the tension, saying political dynamics are normal and that he will keep supporting Prabowo’s government. But the resignation comes during a turbulent month. Several senior officials, including the president’s chief of staff, were arrested earlier in November over alleged wiretapping of the president and other public figures, although Al Jazeera reports the investigation into Gibran’s credentials was apparently unrelated.

The pressure is also economic. A survey in October put Prabowo’s approval rating at 44.5%, down from 81.2% a year earlier, and the rupiah hit a record low of 18,253 to the dollar on the day Gibran resigned, according to Arab News.
