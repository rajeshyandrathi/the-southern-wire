Black Doves, a singing plumber and a wrestling spectacle headline Netflix's November slate

Culture Desk·Monday, 2 November 2026

Netflix has quietly built a habit of saving some of its biggest swings for November, when the run-up to the festive season turns couch time into a competitive sport, and this year's slate follows the pattern faithfully. Black Doves returns for a second season on 5 November, picking up the glossy spy-thriller formula that made the first run one of the platform's bigger surprise hits and giving subscribers an early reason to clear a weekend.

The month's animated centrepiece arrives later, when The Super Mario Galaxy Movie lands on 30 November. After the first Mario film became one of the defining box-office stories of its release year, a sequel landing straight on the platform rather than in cinemas says something about how studios are increasingly willing to route even their most bankable franchises directly to streaming once the theatrical window has done its work.

Prestige television gets its own entry too, with The Altruists — an eight-episode series led by Julia Garner — premiering on 19 November. Garner's track record of elevating morally complicated characters into some of the most talked-about performances in recent television has made her name alone enough to generate anticipation, and an eight-episode order suggests Netflix is backing the show as a genuine awards-season play rather than a quick, disposable watch.

For subscribers who want their entertainment a little less scripted, the month also brings a dose of live programming: WWE Crown Jewel lands on 7 November as part of Netflix's ongoing push into live sports and sports-adjacent spectacle, following the same playbook that has already brought NFL games and other live events onto the platform over the past year.

Taken together, the lineup reads like a studio hedging every bet at once — a returning genre favourite, a four-quadrant animated tentpole, a prestige drama built around a single star, and a live event for the crowd that wants neither scripted drama nor comedy. For a platform that has spent years being accused of chasing volume over curation, November's slate suggests Netflix has settled on volume and curation as complementary strategies rather than competing ones.
