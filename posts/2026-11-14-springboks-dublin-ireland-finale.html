One Test left in the Springboks' November gauntlet, and Rassie Erasmus is still rotating for 2027

Sport Desk

The Springboks' November has been built as a stress test rather than a victory lap. Three matches across three cities in three weeks — Italy in Turin, France in Paris, Ireland in Dublin — make up the bulk of South Africa's Nations Championship campaign, and with the Aviva Stadium now the only fixture left on the itinerary, Rassie Erasmus is still treating the squad less like a finished product defending a World Cup title and more like a 2027 project still being assembled in public.

That approach has been forced on him as much as chosen. Cheslin Kolbe, Eben Etzebeth, Andre Esterhuizen and Handre Pollard have all been ruled out of the window through injury, stripping out four players who would ordinarily be automatic selections in a squad built around continuity. Rather than simply plugging the gaps with the next names on a depth chart, Erasmus has used the enforced changes to blood six uncapped players across the Turin and Paris legs, a decision that reads as calculated rather than reluctant from a coach who has never been shy about using matches South Africa are favoured to win as auditions for the ones they are not.

Dublin is a different proposition entirely. Ireland at home remain one of the most difficult assignments in world rugby regardless of form, and a Springbok squad that has spent two matches absorbing new combinations arrives at the Aviva Stadium having to find out, in real time, which of those combinations can actually hold up against a side with genuine designs on topping its own Nations Championship pool. There is no easing into this fixture the way a team might against a struggling Italy; Ireland punish hesitation and structural gaps with a precision few other sides in the competition can match.

The broader context is the one Erasmus keeps returning to in public: the 2027 World Cup is close enough now that every November Test doubles as evaluation, not just competition. A squad defending back-to-back world titles has the luxury of experimenting that a squad still searching for form would not, and Erasmus has used that luxury deliberately, accepting short-term inconsistency in exchange for knowing, well before the tournament proper, which of his uncapped prospects belong in a World Cup squad and which do not.

Whatever happens in Dublin, the Springboks' Nations Championship campaign concludes with a Finals Weekend on 29 November, closing out a month that has told Erasmus more about his squad's depth than any dominant run of wins would have. For a coaching staff planning two years ahead rather than one match at a time, that trade has always looked like the point.
