Rand holds its breath as SARB weighs a rate call with inflation finally cooling

Business Desk·Sunday, 20 September 2026

The rand has spent September in a holding pattern, buffeted more by decisions made in Washington and oil markets than anything happening at home. The currency touched 16.0250 to the dollar on firmer days this month before slipping back toward 16.2450 as a stronger greenback and rising oil prices reasserted themselves — a reminder that, for all the attention paid to domestic policy, the rand often takes its cues from forces well beyond Pretoria's control.

Underneath that currency noise sits a genuinely interesting domestic story. Headline inflation cooled to 4.3% year-on-year in July, its first decline in five months, arriving just as the South African Reserve Bank heads into a meeting where a rate hike has widely been expected. Cooling inflation and an anticipated hike aren't necessarily contradictory — the SARB has previously held its main lending rate steady even when hawkish, arguing its policy stance was already restrictive enough to bring inflation back to target within two years — but the combination leaves plenty of room for surprise.

The mixed economic backdrop hasn't made the central bank's job any easier. Manufacturing business activity slumped further in August to its lowest level this year, exactly the kind of soft data point that complicates a hiking case even when inflation dynamics might otherwise support one. It's the classic central-bank bind: tighten too much and risk choking off already-fragile output; hold too long and risk losing the inflation fight just as it looked winnable.

For now, markets are doing what markets do best in moments of genuine uncertainty — waiting. The rand's next real move looks likely to come not from anything happening today, but from whatever the Reserve Bank decides once it's finished weighing a cooling inflation print against a weakening manufacturing sector and a currency that's taking its orders from Washington and the oil markets rather than Pretoria.
