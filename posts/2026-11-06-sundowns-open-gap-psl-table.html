Sundowns pull clear at the top of the Premiership as Pirates chase the crown they just lost

Sport Desk

Six rounds into the new Betway Premiership season, Mamelodi Sundowns have already opened up a three-point cushion at the top of the table, a result built on an unbeaten start of five wins and a single draw. It is precisely the kind of early-season dominance Sundowns fans have come to expect and precisely the scenario Orlando Pirates, sitting second with four wins from five games, were hoping to avoid after finally ending the Brazilians' grip on the league title last season.

The context sharpens the stakes. Pirates' championship last term was their first in fourteen years, a drought long enough that an entire generation of the club's younger supporters had never experienced the feeling before it finally arrived. Sundowns, for whom league titles had become close to an annual entitlement over the preceding decade, treated that loss as an aberration to be corrected rather than a genuine changing of the guard, and their form so far this season suggests the players share that read of what went wrong.

The season's first Soweto Derby, played at Pirates' adopted home ground on the final weekend of October, offered both clubs a measuring stick against each other rather than the rest of the division, with the return fixture not due until Chiefs host Pirates again in March. In the rounds since, both Pirates and Sundowns have gone about their business against the division's less glamorous sides, Sundowns extending their lead with routine results while Pirates have had to work harder for points, including away trips that have tested a squad still integrating new signings brought in to defend their title.

Kaizer Chiefs, for their part, remain the league's most-watched underachievers, a tag that has followed the club through season after season of coaching changes and rebuilding projects that have yet to translate into genuine title contention. Their home match against AmaZulu this past weekend drew the usual outsized attention relative to the club's current league position, a reminder that Chiefs' commercial and emotional weight in South African football still outstrips their results on the pitch.

Six matches is too early in a 30-game season to call anything decided, and Sundowns' coaching staff have been careful in public comments not to talk about the title as already secured. But a three-point gap built on an unbeaten run, against a Pirates side still finding its best form as defending champions, is exactly the kind of early separation that has historically been very hard for the rest of the Premiership to claw back once Sundowns' squad depth and experience start to tell over the length of a long season.
