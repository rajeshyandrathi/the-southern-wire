A R1.1bn bank deal has cleared competition law. Now it's waiting on the Reserve Bank

Business Desk

Lesaka Technologies' acquisition of Bank Zero has cleared one of its two hardest regulatory hurdles, with the Competition Tribunal having approved the deal and handed Lesaka the green light to take direct control of Zero Research, Bank Zero's parent company. What has not yet arrived is sign-off from the Prudential Authority of the South African Reserve Bank — the approval that actually governs who is allowed to own a licensed bank in this country, and the one still sitting, unresolved, in Lesaka's most recent quarterly disclosures.

The deal itself, agreed in mid-2025, values Bank Zero at roughly R1.1 billion, structured as up to R91 million in cash with the remainder financed through newly issued Lesaka shares — a structure that leaves Bank Zero's former owners holding a direct stake in the combined group's fortunes rather than simply cashing out. For Lesaka, a payments and fintech group already serving millions of South African consumers and merchants, the acquisition is a deliberate move into owning a fully licensed digital bank outright, rather than merely partnering with one.

The stakes of finally clearing that last approval extend well beyond the two companies' balance sheets. Bank Zero built its reputation as one of South Africa's genuine zero-fee digital challengers, competing directly against TymeBank for customers who had grown tired of the major banks' fee structures. Folding that challenger brand into a larger, already-diversified payments group changes its competitive position — for better or worse depends on whether Lesaka's scale accelerates Bank Zero's growth or dilutes the stripped-down simplicity that won it customers in the first place.

Leadership continuity has been built into the deal's terms as a signal of intent: once the transaction completes, Bank Zero chair Michael Jordaan will join Lesaka's board, while Yatin Narsai continues running Bank Zero day to day as chief executive. Jordaan's own public profile as a fintech evangelist and former FNB chief executive makes his board seat something of a reputational guarantee that Lesaka intends to keep investing in the brand rather than quietly absorbing it.

Other approvals — from the Competition Commission and South Africa's Exchange Control authorities — were flagged as necessary alongside the Prudential Authority's sign-off, but it is the Reserve Bank's own review that appears to be the genuine bottleneck now. Banking ownership changes attract exactly the kind of deliberate, unhurried regulatory scrutiny that competition clearances do not, and until that review concludes, the deal that has already been announced, priced and structured remains, technically, still just a plan.
