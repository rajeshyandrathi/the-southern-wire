ActionSA calls the DA’s Gauteng offer “an insult” and gives it until 8pm to try again

A Thursday-night meeting between the DA and ActionSA ended with both sides rejecting each other’s power-sharing plans for Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni. Herman Mashaba says the ANC’s rival offer looks better, and mayors are due to be elected next week.

Politics Desk·Friday, 20 November 2026

The last real chance for a DA and ActionSA deal in Gauteng’s three big metros ran into a wall on Thursday night. The two parties met behind closed doors to talk about sharing power in Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni, where no party won a majority in this month’s local elections. They walked out with nothing agreed.

According to ActionSA, the DA asked it to put a proposal on the table first. ActionSA did. The DA turned it down and came back with a counteroffer of its own. ActionSA leader Herman Mashaba then rejected that too, saying the DA’s offer was “not a proposal but an insult” and telling the party to forget about it.

That is not quite the end of the road. On Friday morning ActionSA wrote back to the DA and gave it until 8pm the same evening to come back with a revised offer. It has not said what happens if the deadline passes without one, but Mashaba has made it plain which offer he prefers. ActionSA says it is more satisfied with what the ANC has put forward.

The ANC’s proposal, reported earlier this week, would hand ActionSA the mayor’s office in both Johannesburg and Tshwane, with Mashaba in Joburg and Nasiphi Moya staying on in Tshwane, while the ANC takes Ekurhuleni. That arrangement would lock the DA out of all three metros even though it won the most votes in each of them: about 35% in Johannesburg, 34% in Tshwane and 31% in Ekurhuleni.

Behind the numbers sits an old personal fight. Mashaba left the DA in 2019 after falling out with Helen Zille, and he has said repeatedly that he will not back her as Johannesburg mayor. The DA has tried other combinations since, but DA leader Geordin Hill-Lewis conceded this week that his party was unlikely to match what the ANC is offering.

Time is short. Councils are expected to sit next week to elect mayors, which means the deals have to be signed in the next few days. If the DA does not move by tonight, the most likely outcome is an ANC and ActionSA bloc, possibly with other parties, running Gauteng’s economic heartland for the next five years.
