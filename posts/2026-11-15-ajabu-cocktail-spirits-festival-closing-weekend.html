Cape Town's biggest cocktail festival closes out its sixth year today

Lifestyle Desk

The AJABU Cocktail & Spirits Festival wraps its sixth edition in Cape Town today, closing a weekend that has turned the city's bar scene into its own kind of tourist attraction. What started as a niche gathering for industry insiders has grown, edition by edition, into one of the continent's biggest showcases for cocktail culture, pulling in bartenders, distillers and spirits brands from across South Africa and beyond to mix, pour and compete in front of a public that has shown up in bigger numbers every year since the first one.

The timing of this year's festival has worked in its favour in a way organisers couldn't have planned for precisely but clearly hoped for in spirit: a run of warm, clear evenings that turned every outdoor pour station into exactly the kind of setting the festival's marketing promises. Saturday's programme leaned into that with a sunset hike at Chapman's Peak running from five until half past eight in the evening, pairing the city's single most reliable view with a festival built around savouring things slowly, a combination that sold out the kind of tickets that don't usually sell out on their own merits.

Six editions in, AJABU has become something more than a drinks festival for the city's hospitality economy. Bars and restaurants that host satellite events during the festival weekend report some of their busiest trading of the entire spring calendar, and the exposure for smaller, independent South African spirits brands competing for shelf space against international names has turned into a genuine business opportunity rather than just a marketing moment. A festival that started as a celebration of mixology has become, almost incidentally, a distribution channel.

For Cape Town's broader tourism calendar, AJABU now functions as the opening act for a run of major events that will carry the city through to the holidays: the Cape Town Coffee Show lands at Newlands Cricket Ground in the coming week, followed by Red Bull King of the Air at Bloubergstrand and, at the very end of the month, the debut of Taste of Cape Town at Green Point. Each event draws a slightly different crowd, but together they are turning what used to be a single big festival season into a near-continuous run of reasons to visit.

Today's closing sessions will do what every closing day does: give the stragglers one last chance to taste what they missed and give organisers their first real data point on whether this was the festival's biggest year yet. Early signs, and a weekend of good weather, suggest the answer is yes.
