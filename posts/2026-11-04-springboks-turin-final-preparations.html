Springboks fine-tune their attack in Turin as a title defence of sorts begins against Italy

Sport Desk·Wednesday, 4 November 2026

The Springboks have spent this week putting the final touches on a campaign that carries more weight than a simple November tour. South Africa arrive at Allianz Stadium in Turin for Saturday's Nations Championship opener against Italy sitting at number one in the World Rugby rankings, a position they have held through a season of attrition, and the four-match European swing that follows — Italy, then France, then Ireland, before finals weekend in London — will decide whether that ranking survives the year intact.

Training sessions in Turin this week have focused squarely on sharpening the squad's attacking shape, after a matchday 23 that blends six uncapped newcomers with the spine of a side that has won back-to-back World Cups. Coach Rassie Erasmus opted to introduce his new faces from the bench rather than the starting line-up, a decision that keeps the senior players carrying the heaviest attacking responsibility while the uncapped group absorbs the pace of Test rugby in short, high-intensity bursts. That selection logic has shaped training all week, with the squad's leadership group working closely with the newcomers on combinations they will need to execute under pressure rather than in the comfort of a training paddock.

Italy, ranked tenth in the world, are not the test of a World Cup final, but Erasmus has been careful all week not to treat them as a formality. Azzurri sides have previously used home advantage in Turin to disrupt more fancied opponents with physicality up front, and South Africa's management has repeatedly stressed that a side missing some of its biggest names from the run-on team cannot afford to take a slow start to the match lightly.

The broader context of this tour matters as much as any single result. With France and Ireland waiting in the following two weeks, and both sides capable of testing the Boks' depth in ways Italy likely cannot, this opening match is as much about building cohesion among players who have not yet started together as it is about collecting the points. A comfortable win would let Erasmus rotate further for the French leg; a scare would force an earlier recall of experience he had hoped to rest.

By kickoff on Saturday at 13:40 SAST, the Springboks' build-up will be complete, and attention will shift from preparation to execution. For a squad that has made winning look close to routine over the past two World Cup cycles, Turin offers a different kind of test: proving that the next generation waiting in the wings can be trusted with the jersey when it matters.
