Mamelodi Sundowns reclaim the Betway Premiership summit with a four-goal rout

Sport Desk·Monday, 2 November 2026

Mamelodi Sundowns are back where they are most accustomed to being: top of the Betway Premiership. A clinical 4-1 demolition of Durban City at Loftus Versfeld over the weekend put Masandawana back in first place, a reminder to the rest of the league that no amount of early-season upheaval seems to shift the balance of power in South African football for very long.

The scoreline flattered Durban City not at all. Sundowns were ruthless in the spaces that mattered — quick in transition, clinical in the final third, and comfortable enough defensively that the visitors never looked capable of mounting the kind of comeback that has occasionally rattled the champions in recent seasons. It was the sort of performance that doesn't just win three points; it sends a message to a title race that had briefly looked more open than usual.

That openness hasn't disappeared entirely. Elsewhere over the weekend, Kruger United ground out a hard-fought 3-1 win over Richards Bay at Mbombela Stadium, the kind of result that keeps a smaller club's European-style ambitions alive and adds another layer of unpredictability to a league table that has refused to settle into its usual hierarchy this season. Sides like Orlando Pirates, Sekhukhune United and Milford FC have all taken turns occupying top spot at various points, and none of them have looked willing to cede ground quietly.

For Sundowns, the challenge now is less about talent than consistency. The club's dominance over the past decade has rested on an ability to grind through congested fixture lists without the kind of mid-season slump that derails less disciplined squads, and early signs suggest this campaign is no different. Durban City will not be the last side to leave Loftus wondering how the gap opened up so quickly.

With the season still finding its rhythm and several clubs within touching distance of the top, the real test for Sundowns will come over the next run of fixtures, when the sides chasing them get the chance to close the gap again. For now, though, the Brazilians have reasserted the kind of authority that has defined the Premiership for the better part of a decade.
