Top of Group D and unbeaten under Mosimane, Bafana face Kenya for the result that puts qualification almost out of reach

Sport Desk

Soweto's Orlando Stadium fills up tonight for a match that carries more weight than a typical November international, because Bafana Bafana have spent the last two months quietly building something under Pitso Mosimane that looks less like a rebuild and more like a run. Three wins from three since his return, the most recent a 2-1 defeat of Guinea, have put South Africa top of their 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifying group, and a win over Kenya tonight would push that lead to a point most rivals cannot realistically chase down with two fixtures left.

The coaching match-up gives the night its own storyline. Benni McCarthy, who built much of his reputation as a player and then as a club coach inside South African football, now sits in the opposing dugout leading the Kenyan national team, and Mosimane has spent the week publicly refusing to treat that familiarity as an advantage for either side. His message to his own players has been blunt rather than diplomatic: Kenya field professionals from leagues across Europe, their recent draw with Eritrea notwithstanding, and any South African side that walks into Orlando assuming an easy night deserves what McCarthy's team does to it.

Mosimane's own assessment of his team has been more self-critical than the results suggest it needs to be. He has flagged a reluctance to convert dominance into goals as the one habit this group needs to break before the qualifying campaign tightens, aware that three narrow wins can just as easily become three missed chances if the finishing does not sharpen. Kenya arrived in South Africa earlier in the week with their own motivation intact, aware that a result in Soweto would do more for McCarthy's early tenure than almost anything else on his calendar this year.

What tonight settles, beyond three points, is how much daylight South Africa can put between themselves and the rest of Group D before the return trip to Kenya next week turns from formality into a fixture that actually matters. AFCON 2027's co-hosts include Kenya itself, alongside Tanzania and Uganda, which means the stakes for the home side run deeper than qualification math; a shot at playing a continental tournament in front of their own public sits behind every Kenyan performance this campaign. For Bafana, the calculation is simpler and colder: win tonight, and the second leg in Nairobi becomes a formality rather than a must-win.

Orlando Stadium's record against Kenya gives the home side one more reason for confidence walking in, but Mosimane has made clear all week that history is the one thing he does not want his players leaning on tonight.
