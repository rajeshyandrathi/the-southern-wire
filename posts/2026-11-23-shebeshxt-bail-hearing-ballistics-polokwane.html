Shebeshxt bail hearing: investigating officer defends the ballistics evidence against the lekompo star

Capt Phaladi Makola told the Polokwane Magistrate’s Court on Monday that forensic reports link a pistol to the October 2025 shooting. The hearing runs until Friday.

Crime Desk·Monday, 23 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Lehlogonolo “Shebeshxt” Chauke faces charges including attempted murder over a shooting in Polokwane in October 2025
  • He has been in custody since November 2025 after several failed bail bids
  • The investigating officer said a projectile from the victim’s jaw came from a semi-automatic pistol
  • The bail hearing continues until Friday 27 November

The latest bail application by lekompo musician Lehlogonolo “Shebeshxt” Chauke continued in the Polokwane Magistrate’s Court on Monday 23 November, with the investigating officer defending the state’s evidence, The Citizen reported.

Chauke faces charges including attempted murder, linked to the shooting of Petros Ralefadana in Verkleps Street, Polokwane, on 19 October 2025. He has been in custody since his arrest in November 2025, and both the magistrate’s court and the high court have turned down earlier bail bids. He has not been convicted of any offence.

Monday’s evidence

Questioned by the prosecutor, Captain Phaladi Makola said two forensic reports supported the state’s case: one found residue indicating a firearm had been fired in a blue Golf 8 GTI, and another found that a projectile removed from the victim’s jaw came from a semi-automatic pistol.

Makola disputed the evidence of a defence expert, Jan Christophell de Klerk, who had said he received only part of the docket. Makola suggested the expert might have reached a different view had he seen the whole file. He said he held hospital statements from the driver and the passenger, and that the lack of CCTV footage did not weaken the case.

He also told the court that Chauke was allegedly found with a smartphone in the Polokwane holding cells earlier this year, and that a sworn statement by Chauke’s friend Lehlohonolo Molapo, who was allegedly in the car, was untrue. Makola said Molapo would be charged with making a false statement under oath.

What happens next

The hearing, which SABC streamed live, is set to continue until Friday 27 November. The court has not said when it will rule on whether Chauke can be released.

All the evidence presented so far is the state’s version, and it will be tested by the defence before the court decides.
