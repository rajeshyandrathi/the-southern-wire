Seven changes, Libbok at 10 and Etzebeth on the bench: the Boks pick their team for the France final

South Africa meet France in the first Nations Championship final at Twickenham on Sunday, a rematch of the 21-19 defeat in Paris. Rassie Erasmus has shaken up his side after two losses in a row.

Sport Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • The final is on Sunday 29 November at Twickenham, kicking off at 18:40 SA time
  • Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu moves to fullback for the injured Damian Willemse, and Manie Libbok starts at flyhalf
  • Eben Etzebeth drops to the bench; Emmanuel Tshituka could make his Test debut
  • Marco van Staden’s yellow card in Dublin was upgraded to red and goes to a hearing

The Springboks will try to end a two-match losing run in the biggest game of their season. Rassie Erasmus named a side with seven changes on Monday for Sunday’s inaugural Nations Championship final against France at Twickenham, Planet Rugby reported. Kick-off is at 16:40 UK time, 18:40 in South Africa.

South Africa reached the final despite losing 20-17 to Ireland in Dublin on Saturday, because their losing bonus point was enough to top the southern hemisphere table ahead of New Zealand. France topped the northern table after beating Argentina 45-17. They also beat the Boks 21-19 in Paris in round five, so Sunday is a rematch.

The team

Starting XV: Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu, Kurt-Lee Arendse, Jesse Kriel, Damian de Allende, Ethan Hooker, Manie Libbok, Cobus Reinach, Cameron Hanekom, Pieter-Steph du Toit, Siya Kolisi (captain), Lood de Jager, Salmaan Moerat, Thomas du Toit, Jan-Hendrik Wessels, Gerhard Steenekamp.

Bench: Malcolm Marx, Ox Nche, Wilco Louw, Eben Etzebeth, Paul de Villiers, Emmanuel Tshituka, Morne van den Berg, Canan Moodie. It is a six-two split between forwards and backs.

Feinberg-Mngomezulu moves from flyhalf to fullback in place of the injured Damian Willemse, with Libbok coming in at 10. Etzebeth, who won his 148th cap in Dublin, drops to the bench, and Tshituka could make his Test debut, Flashscore reported. Erasmus said the team knew it had let itself and the country down in the last two weeks, and that there would be no room for error.

Discipline off the field

Marco van Staden’s yellow card in Dublin, for re-entering a ruck, was upgraded to red after the match, as was Ireland captain Dan Sheehan’s yellow. Both players must appear before a disciplinary committee, XV Ovalie reported, and no hearing date had been published by Monday morning.

Erasmus himself is still serving the six-match ban World Rugby imposed on 17 November over his comments about the television match official after the France defeat. It bars him from the stadium, contact with the team on match days and media duties. SA Rugby Magazine reports that no formal appeal has been lodged.

What else is on at Twickenham

The final caps a finals weekend at the same ground. England, who beat New Zealand 37-12 on Saturday, meet the All Blacks again in the third-place match on Saturday. A Boks win would be a strong answer to a difficult fortnight; a third defeat in a row would be a first in the Erasmus era, The National noted.
