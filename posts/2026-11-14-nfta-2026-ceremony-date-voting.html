South Africa's newest screen awards set their date, and 'The Polygamist' just needs the votes now

Culture Desk

The fourth National Film & Television Awards South Africa now has a fixed date and a fixed room: 19 November, at the Theatre of Marcellus inside Emperors Palace in Johannesburg, where the ceremony will reveal its winners live. For an awards show still establishing itself against the much older South African Film and Television Awards, locking down a glamorous, specific venue rather than a vague "later this month" date is itself a small statement of intent about how seriously the NFTA wants to be taken.

'The Polygamist', Stained Glass Productions' drama, continues to lead the nominations field, with multiple nods including Best Scripted TV Series, Best TV Drama Series and several acting categories. That dominance was already clear when the nominations were announced; what has changed since is that the contest has now moved from a jury's longlist into a format the NFTA has deliberately built around audience participation rather than closed-door deliberation.

Public voting for the awards is live now via the NFTA's official website, a structural choice that distinguishes the ceremony from the SAFTAs' more traditional industry-panel judging. Letting viewers vote directly injects a populist element into an industry that has often felt, fairly or not, like it rewards the same small circle of production houses and performers year after year — and gives a drama like 'The Polygamist' a second, more public test of whether its nominations reflect genuine audience enthusiasm rather than just a strong submission strategy.

The timing lands at a moment when South Africa's screen industry badly needs a visible, celebratory moment. Productions have stalled and jobs have disappeared across the sector over the past year, with workers blaming a broken incentive scheme and staging public protests demanding intervention from Pretoria. An industry under that kind of strain does not stop needing awards shows; if anything, a ceremony that photographs well and generates genuine public buzz matters more, not less, when the underlying economics of the industry it celebrates are this fragile.

Whether 'The Polygamist' converts its nominations lead into actual trophies will only be known once the ceremony airs. What is already clear is that the NFTA, in its fourth year, has found a format — public voting, a marquee venue, a concentrated announcement night — that gives it a distinct identity from the SAFTAs rather than simply competing as a smaller, newer version of the same thing.
