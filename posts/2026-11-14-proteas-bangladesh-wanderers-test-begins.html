Bavuma's world number ones open at the Wanderers with two senior recalls and one big omission

Sport Desk

South Africa's cricketers take the field at the DP World Wanderers Stadium tomorrow carrying a tag few of this Proteas generation have had to defend for long: world number one in Test cricket. The ranking survives a demanding run that included a come-from-behind 2-1 series win over Australia, and now faces its next challenge against a Bangladesh side with far less to lose and, on recent history, a habit of making South Africa work harder than the ranking gap would suggest.

Temba Bavuma continues as captain of a squad reshaped by two significant recalls. Tony de Zorzi and Zubayr Hamza both return to the side, Hamza in particular coming back into contention after time out of the national picture, while Anrich Nortje's return gives the attack a pace option that has been missing through injury for long stretches of the past year. All three slot into a side that, on paper, looks considerably stronger than the one that finished off Australia.

The squad's one conspicuous omission is Lungi Ngidi, left out of the fifteen while his rehabilitation continues rather than being rushed back into Test cricket's heaviest workload. For a bowling attack that has leaned on him in previous series, his absence puts more responsibility on Nortje and the squad's other seam options from the first ball, a decision selectors have clearly judged worth the short-term cost against the risk of a setback.

The series itself is a two-match affair that will keep the Proteas in South Africa through most of the rest of November: this first Test runs at the Wanderers from tomorrow through 19 November, before the squad moves to SuperSport Park in Centurion for the second Test from 23 to 27 November. Both venues have hosted some of South Africa's most convincing home performances in recent seasons, conditions that typically favour the kind of seam-heavy attack this squad has been built around.

Bangladesh arrive as clear underdogs by ranking, but Test cricket's shorter formats have punished South African sides for complacency against supposedly weaker opposition before. For Bavuma's group, the series carries less romance than a showdown against Australia or England, but exactly the kind of must-not-slip-up stakes that come with defending a number one ranking rather than chasing one.
