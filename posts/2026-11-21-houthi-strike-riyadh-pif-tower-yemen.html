A Houthi missile attack damages the tower that houses Saudi Arabia’s wealth fund

Several floors of the Public Investment Fund tower in Riyadh’s financial district were damaged on Thursday, with no injuries reported. The war between Saudi Arabia and Yemen’s Houthis is escalating again.

World Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • The PIF tower, Riyadh’s tallest building at 385m, was damaged in an attack before dawn on Thursday
  • Saudi authorities blamed debris from a missile threat; no injuries were reported
  • The coalition says it intercepted four ballistic missiles fired at southern Saudi Arabia
  • The UN children’s agency says more than 330,000 people have been displaced in Yemen since fighting resumed

A Houthi attack on Riyadh before dawn on Thursday damaged several floors of the tower that houses Saudi Arabia’s sovereign wealth fund. The Public Investment Fund tower in the King Abdullah Financial District is the city’s tallest building, at about 385 metres, and nearby buildings were also damaged, according to Saudi Civil Defence, The National and The New Arab.

Residents described a loud blast across the city at about 4.45am, after mobile phone alerts warned of an incoming threat. The Saudi-led coalition said its forces responded to a missile fired towards Riyadh and that falling debris caused material damage. Saudi authorities reported no injuries.

Later the coalition said its air defences intercepted four ballistic missiles fired at the southern region bordering Yemen, and that it destroyed a launch platform in Saada province. The Houthis’ military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed that cruise missiles had directly hit Riyadh’s financial centre and King Khalid International Airport. Those claims could not be independently verified.

A war that has restarted

The attack is the latest in an escalating campaign. A 2022 UN-brokered truce had largely halted fighting between the Houthis and the Saudi-led coalition, which backs Yemen’s internationally recognised government, but fighting resumed in the second half of this year. In October the Houthis seized strategic areas along the Red Sea coast, and attacks on Riyadh’s airport that month killed more than a dozen people.

The Saudi foreign ministry called Thursday’s attacks terrorist and said the kingdom has the full right to take all necessary measures to defend itself. Saudi aircraft struck the Yemeni capital Sanaa the same day, according to the Houthi-run Saba news agency. On the same day, Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman met the head of US Central Command, Admiral Brad Cooper, in Riyadh.

The Houthis are aligned with Iran, and the renewed war in Yemen is unfolding alongside the wider conflict between the US, Israel and Iran, with Iran continuing to disrupt shipping in the Strait of Hormuz.

The human cost

Inside Yemen, the toll is growing. The World Health Organization says 2,260 people have been killed and more than 10,500 wounded since the latest escalation began, and UNICEF says more than 330,000 people have been displaced, including about 174,000 children. UNICEF warned that casualties are likely to rise, especially in Taiz and Lahij.

Analysts quoted by The New Arab say Saudi Arabia is entering a new phase of escalation, but that the conflict will ultimately only end through negotiation. For now, neither side has signalled any move towards talks.
