Mi Casa opens Kirstenbosch's 19-concert summer season, with Freshlyground, Mandisi Dyantyis and an orchestra still to come

Lifestyle Desk

Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden's Summer Sunset Concerts return for their 2026/27 season on 22 November, opening with Mi Casa and running across nineteen concerts through to 28 March. For Cape Town, the season's arrival is less a single event than the resumption of one of the city's most dependable live-music fixtures, a weekly ritual of picnic blankets, wine and live sets against the backdrop of Table Mountain that has anchored the garden's summer calendar for more than three decades.

The early line-up balances crowd-pleasing local staples with acts drawing specifically on the garden's reputation for eclectic, genre-crossing programming. Booshle G and Freshlyground share the bill on 29 November, a week after the opening night, while Mandisi Dyantyis headlines on 6 December, bringing a jazz-inflected sensibility to a season that has traditionally leaned toward pop and Afro-fusion acts in its opening weeks. The Cape Town Philharmonic Orchestra is booked for three special performances across the run, giving classical and orchestral music a regular, if occasional, place in a season otherwise dominated by contemporary South African acts.

International names close out the run, with Neon Dreams and Chef'Special both scheduled as season-ending acts in late March, a pattern the Summer Sunset Concerts have followed in recent years of bookending a largely homegrown programme with a handful of international draws at the season's start or finish. For organisers, that mix has proven commercially durable: a format built around sunset timing, a non-smoking outdoor venue and relatively accessible ticket pricing has kept the series full even as competing festival and concert options have multiplied across the city.

The season's length, running nearly to the end of March, means it will overlap with much of Cape Town's broader summer events calendar, including the city's food festival season and its peak tourist months, giving residents and visitors alike a recurring, lower-commitment alternative to the larger, ticketed festivals that cluster around November and December specifically. For many Capetonians, the Sunday-evening rhythm of the Summer Sunset Concerts has become as much a marker of the change of season as the first hot weekend or the whales departing Walker Bay.

Tickets and full pricing for the new season had not yet been confirmed publicly at the time of writing, continuing a pattern from recent years where lineup announcements have preceded on-sale dates by several weeks. For now, the opening night on 22 November gives Cape Town a firm date to mark the start of a season that will carry the city's live-music calendar through to the following autumn.
