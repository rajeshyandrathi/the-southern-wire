Halfway through the count, South Africa's municipal map keeps tilting away from the ANC

The Southern Wire Desk·Johannesburg

By Friday, with more than half of the country's roughly 66,000 results slips captured and verified, the shape of South Africa's municipal election had sharpened considerably from the fragmentary picture election night offered. The headline trend is unmistakable: the ANC, which opened the count clinging to a narrow national lead, has seen its position erode steadily as more urban and township districts report, while the DA has pulled further ahead in the one metro that matters most to both parties' national standing. The Independent Electoral Commission says it remains on track to make its official declaration on Sunday, but the broad outcome in several of the country's biggest municipalities is no longer much in doubt.

Johannesburg has produced the clearest verdict of all. With results past the halfway mark, the DA was sitting on just over 40 percent of the vote in the city, more than double the ANC's share, with ActionSA a close third and the EFF a distant fourth. That margin is wide enough that the DA's path to leading a new governing coalition in South Africa's largest city looks far more secure than it did on election night, even if a majority on its own remains out of reach and a multi-party deal will still be required to install a mayor.

The national trend lines matter beyond any single city. Early results had shown the ANC and DA separated by only a few points on the proportional vote, but as counting has moved from the smaller, faster-reporting municipalities into the country's bigger urban centres, the ANC's share has kept sliding, prompting the party's own structures in several provinces to begin quietly bracing for a worse showing than 2021. Officials close to the count caution that proportional and ward results can still shift as rural and traditionally ANC-aligned districts report, but the trajectory over the past two days has run in one direction only.

The commission's own assessment of the process has been more reassuring than the politics it is producing. The IEC's chairperson has repeatedly described the vote as largely trouble-free, a notable statement given the run-up to polling day was marked by a string of politically linked killings and at least one storming of a ballot storage site. That the count itself has proceeded without major disruption will matter for the legitimacy of whatever coalitions now have to be negotiated in Johannesburg, Tshwane and the other hung councils still taking shape.

What happens between now and Sunday's declaration is mostly arithmetic rather than suspense in Johannesburg, but several other councils remain genuinely open, most pointedly the capital, where a three-way finish is tightening by the hour. For the Government of National Unity in Pretoria, a result in which the ANC loses ground in the metros it can least afford to lose would sharpen an argument already underway inside the party about whether its coalition partners have won the trust of voters that the ANC itself is visibly losing. For now, the IEC's count keeps moving, slip by slip, toward a Sunday declaration that looks set to confirm what the past two days have already made clear.
