Shoprite buys its way into mobile with a R3.6bn DNI stake, as Absa picks Vodacom over its own network

South Africa’s biggest retailer is taking 40% of telecoms and fintech group DNI, while Investec buys into Shoprite’s new money business. On the same day Absa shelved plans for a bank-branded mobile network and teamed up with Vodacom instead.

Tech Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • Shoprite is buying 40% of DNI for about R3.6 billion, with up to R213 million more depending on DNI’s pending deals
  • Investec will pay about R1.9 billion for 19.99% of Shoprite Financial Services, valuing it at about R9.5 billion
  • DNI has more than 4.2 million active SIM subscribers and fibre that passes over 700,000 homes
  • Absa will not launch its own mobile network and will build joint offers with Vodacom instead

Shoprite has made its biggest move yet beyond the grocery aisle. On Monday, 23 November, the retailer announced it would buy 40% of DNI, a privately owned investment group with businesses in SIM and handset distribution, fibre and fintech, for about R3.6 billion, TechCentral reported. A further payment of up to R213 million depends on DNI completing acquisitions it already has under way, and the deal is expected to close on or about 1 December.

At the same time Investec agreed to take 19.99% of Shoprite Financial Services, the new holding company for the group’s money services, for about R1.9 billion. That price implies a value of about R9.5 billion for the business. Shoprite’s employee share trust will take another 5% for about R473 million, leaving Shoprite Checkers with 75.01% and control.

The Investec stake builds on the alliance banking agreement the two companies announced on 9 November, under which Investec will be the licensed bank behind a Shoprite-branded debit card and transactional account. Under the structure described by ITWeb, Shoprite owns the brand and the customer offer, while Investec supplies the banking infrastructure, balance sheet, payments and compliance. The agreement is not exclusive.

Shoprite and DNI have also agreed put and call options that could take Shoprite to full ownership of DNI, exercisable from the fourth year and subject to regulatory approval. Investec, for its part, can raise its stake in the financial services business to as much as 40% over time.

What Shoprite is buying

DNI was founded by Andrew Dunn, who began by bundling SIM cards with bread deliveries to reach informal traders, according to chief executive Ryan Noach. The group reported consolidated EBITDA of R1.2 billion in the year to June 2026, has more than 4.2 million active SIM subscribers, and advanced more than R11 billion in handsets, data and airtime on credit over the past year, TechCentral reported.

Through stakes in fibre operator Frogfoot and internet provider Vox, DNI’s networks pass more than 700,000 homes, and Frogfoot plans to add about 360,000 a year for the next three to four years, focusing on lower-income areas. As part of the deal Shoprite Financial Services also takes a direct 25% stake in 3G Mobile, a DNI device distributor, which should give the retailer access to a wider range of phones.

Chief executive Pieter Engelbrecht was frank on an investor call. Shoprite has “not done a particularly good job in the cellular space,” he said, and building a mobile business from scratch “will just take way too long.” He stressed that Shoprite does not intend to become a bank, and warned investors against over-expectation. Its existing K’nect virtual mobile network, which runs on Cell C, will stay.

Absa changes course

Also on Monday, Absa and Vodacom announced an alliance to develop joint mobile offers, digital services, loyalty benefits and device financing. The companies described it as “a shift beyond the traditional standalone” virtual mobile network model, TechCentral reported. Absa had said in October 2025 that it was preparing its own network, but by June this year said only that it was assessing options.

Under the arrangement Vodacom will look after the connectivity side and Absa the banking, with each keeping its own customers. No products, prices or launch dates were announced, and offers will be introduced as commercial, technical and regulatory arrangements are finalised. Absa’s Sitoyo Lopokoiyit said the aim is to design around customer needs rather than “around the boundaries of our businesses.”

For consumers, the two deals point the same way: banks and retailers want to sell you data, airtime, phones and credit alongside your account, in the way Capitec has done with Capitec Connect. Capitec, FNB, Standard Bank and Nedbank already run their own mobile networks. Shoprite expects the DNI deal to add to earnings in its 2027 financial year, while Investec’s stake needs regulatory approval, expected around mid-2027, so most of the new offers are likely to arrive next year.
