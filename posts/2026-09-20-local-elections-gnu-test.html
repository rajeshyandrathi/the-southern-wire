2026 local elections shape up as a make-or-break test for the GNU

Politics Desk·Sunday, 20 September 2026

South Africa's looming local government elections are shaping up to be far more than a routine municipal contest. With the Government of National Unity now more than two years into its first term, the 2026 polls are widely being read as a referendum on how well that unprecedented ten-party coalition has actually governed — and early indicators suggest the verdict could be uncomfortable for its largest partner.

The numbers driving that anxiety are stark. Having secured just 40% nationally in the 2024 election that forced the GNU into existence, the ANC now faces projections of further erosion in its traditional strongholds, with some forecasts putting support as low as 30% in both Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal — provinces that together account for a huge share of the party's historical base.

Compounding the pressure is the continued rise of the uMkhonto we Sizwe Party, Jacob Zuma's breakaway movement that captured 14% of the national vote in its first election in 2024. A party that disruptive in its debut outing is exactly the kind of variable that makes local election forecasting in KwaZulu-Natal in particular genuinely unpredictable this cycle.

The ANC itself is clearly alive to the stakes, actively campaigning this month to encourage all citizens aged 18 and over to register ahead of the vote — a straightforward turnout push, but one that also implicitly acknowledges how much ground the party may need to defend simply to hold its current position, let alone recover lost support.

For the coalition as a whole, the stakes go beyond any single party's fortunes. Local elections that fracture support further across the GNU's ten constituent parties would complicate municipal governance at exactly the level — service delivery, infrastructure, basic administration — where ordinary South Africans feel the consequences of political dysfunction most directly and immediately.
