Morocco heads to the polls in its first parliamentary election since last year's youth protests

Politics Desk·Sunday, 20 September 2026

Morocco will vote on 23 September in an election to the lower house of parliament that carries far more weight than a routine legislative contest. It is the first national vote since the youth-led protests that swept the country in autumn 2025, and both government and opposition know it will be read, at home and abroad, as a verdict on how the political system responded to that unrest.

The protests themselves were driven by frustrations that will feel familiar across much of the continent: youth unemployment, the cost of living, and a sense among younger Moroccans that political institutions were slow to register their concerns at all, let alone act on them. How directly this election addresses those grievances, through candidate lists, campaign platforms, and eventual governing priorities, will say a great deal about whether the protests produced structural political change or simply a temporary crisis response.

For a region where youth-driven political movements have repeatedly outpaced the institutions meant to represent them, Morocco's vote functions as something of a test case. A result that channels that energy into the formal political system would offer a rare example of protest translating cleanly into electoral participation rather than disillusionment; a low-turnout result among younger voters would suggest the opposite lesson, and one other governments across Africa will be watching closely either way.
