Drones strike Khartoum, killing four and damaging a UN building, as Sudan’s army points at Ethiopia

The overnight attack on Sudan’s capital on Sunday was the first reported strike on a UN compound there since the army retook the city. The UN says the drones were reportedly launched by the RSF.

World Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Drone strikes on Khartoum early on Sunday killed at least four people, including a child
  • A UN building in a residential area was damaged while staff were inside
  • A Sudanese army source claimed the drones took off from Ethiopia; Ethiopia rejects such allegations
  • UN figures say drones caused almost 75% of confirmed civilian deaths from military operations in Sudan this year

Drones struck Sudan’s capital in the early hours of Sunday 22 November, killing at least four people and damaging a United Nations building with staff inside.

Witnesses told AFP that explosions were heard across Khartoum for more than two hours as the army tried to shoot the drones down. They appeared to target government buildings and the airport. In northern Omdurman a drone hit a home, killing three people and wounding nine, Al Jazeera reported. A child was killed in Jebel Aulia, about 45 kilometres south of the capital.

The UN under fire

UN humanitarian coordinator Denise Brown said a UN building in a residential area was damaged while staff were inside, and the UN’s humanitarian office called the risk to its staff unacceptable. It was the first reported attack on a UN compound in Khartoum since the army retook the capital in 2025, allAfrica reported.

The UN said the drones were reportedly launched by the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, which have been at war with the army since April 2023. Brown said strikes with foreign-supplied drones were killing civilians across Sudan almost every day, and called for an end to the flow of weapons and a humanitarian truce. US senior adviser Massad Boulos said the attacks had caused major damage.

The Ethiopia claim

A Sudanese army source told AFP that the drones took off from Bahir Dar airport in Ethiopia, calling it a continuation of Ethiopian aggression. This has not been independently verified, and Ethiopia has rejected similar allegations. Earlier this month Sudan’s army-aligned sovereignty council said 70 attacks using 187 drones had been launched from Ethiopia between February and October, and took its complaint to the UN Security Council and the African Union. Saudi Arabia condemned Sunday’s attacks.

A war increasingly fought from the air

UN figures released on 17 November show that drone strikes caused almost 75% of confirmed civilian deaths from military operations in Sudan in 2026, more than 1,630 people, AFP reported. The war has displaced about 13 million people, and aid workers estimate that more than 200,000 have died.

If the claim of drones being flown from a neighbouring country were proved, it would turn Sudan’s war into an open regional confrontation in the Horn of Africa. For now it remains an allegation that Ethiopia denies.
