Nearly a million matrics have one hard stretch left, and the Department says it's still on track

Lifestyle Desk

The biggest matric class South Africa has ever produced is heading into the final, hardest fortnight of its exams with its early momentum largely intact. Just over 1.08 million candidates sat down at more than 9,000 centres across the country when the National Senior Certificate exams opened on 13 October, and the Department of Basic Education has kept up a steady drumbeat of updates insisting the process remains on track despite the scale of what it is managing.

The clearest checkpoint so far came on 30 October, when the Department reported that candidates had passed the halfway mark of the exam cycle, with 104 of the year's 181 question papers already written nationwide. Officials described the progress as steady across all nine provinces, a notably calmer tone than some previous years have produced at the same stage, when isolated leaks or logistical failures have occasionally dominated the halfway-point headlines instead.

That is not to say the 2026 cycle has run entirely without friction. A taxi strike in the Jan Kemdorp area disrupted transport for five schools, a localised problem that could easily have cost affected candidates a missed paper. The Department's insistence that every one of those candidates still managed to write their exams, despite the disruption, is exactly the kind of detail that tends to separate a cycle remembered as smoothly run from one remembered for its failures.

The exams arrived this year against an unusually charged backdrop: the run-up to and through the 4 November local government elections raised concerns in advance about possible disruption from protest action during the same weeks candidates would be sitting their papers. That those concerns have not, so far, translated into widespread disruption to the exam schedule is itself a quieter success story running alongside the more visible electoral drama consuming the country's attention.

What remains is the final stretch: the last paper of the 2026 cycle is scheduled for 26 November, less than two weeks from today, after which this record cohort of candidates faces the long wait for results that has become its own annual ritual of national anxiety. For now, the Department's steady-progress message holds, and a class of candidates who have already weathered a disrupted school calendar and the backdrop of an election season are closer than not to being able to put the hardest exams of their lives behind them.
