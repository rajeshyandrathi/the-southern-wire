Ten years after Brexit, Britain and the EU agree their biggest reset yet

Prime Minister Andy Burnham and EU leaders agreed in Brussels on Friday to align food standards, link carbon markets and open a youth work and study scheme. Nigel Farage calls it a betrayal.

World Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Deals on food and animal standards, linked emissions trading and a youth mobility scheme agreed in Brussels
  • Up to 100,000 young people a year from each side could live, work or study in the other for up to three years
  • UK government says the deals could add up to 0.5% to UK GDP
  • Tuition fees for EU students remain unresolved; talks to finish within six months

Britain and the European Union took their biggest step towards each other since the 2016 Brexit vote on Friday, agreeing a package of deals at a summit in Brussels. UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham met European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President Antonio Costa, with British cabinet ministers and EU commissioners also at the table.

Burnham, who replaced Keir Starmer as Labour leader and prime minister after Starmer resigned, said the agreement would “rebuild the bridges that were broken all those years ago”, according to Al Jazeera. “This is a good day for Britain,” he said as talks opened.

The package has three main parts. The first aligns sanitary and phytosanitary standards, the rules on food, plants and animals, so that most movements of these goods between Britain and the EU no longer need current certificates and checks. The deal values this at about £5.1 billion to the British economy, and Bloomberg reports the changes are due to take effect from March 2028.

Carbon markets and young people

The second part links the British and European emissions trading systems, the markets in which companies buy permits to emit carbon dioxide. Linking them means goods such as steel would be exempt from each side’s border carbon levies, a significant issue for British exporters. That link is due to apply from 2028.

The third part is a youth scheme. Up to 100,000 young people a year from each side will be able to live, work or study in the other’s territory for up to three years, according to Bloomberg, which says the cap was finalised only late on Thursday. The scheme should be available no later than January 2030.

Burnham says the deals together could add as much as 0.5% to UK GDP. He said his government would start legislating immediately, and Bloomberg reports a bill will be introduced next week. Von der Leyen stressed that the relationship works both ways. “It is a two-way street,” she said.

What is still unresolved, and the backlash

Not everything was settled. The two sides did not agree on university tuition fees, which EU students currently pay at international rates; they committed to finishing those talks within six months. Further talks are planned on the EU’s new “made in Europe” rules for industries such as carmaking, and on returning migrants without legal status.

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage, who was in Brussels on Friday, attacked the deal. “That’s the betrayal that Burnham is committing today,” he said, according to Bloomberg, and he warned that the Brexit debate had started all over again. The Conservatives also criticised the government’s direction.

Burnham has said that after the summit he will begin a wider discussion with EU partners about the long-term relationship, with options ranging from keeping current arrangements to joining a customs union or one day rejoining. Rejoining would need British voters’ support and the unanimous approval of EU members. A YouGov poll this week found 54% of Britons would find a rejoin referendum within five years acceptable.
