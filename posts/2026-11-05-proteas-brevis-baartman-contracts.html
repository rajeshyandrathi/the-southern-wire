Cricket South Africa hands out its new contracts, and two uncapped names make the cut

Sport Desk

Cricket South Africa's newly announced central contracts for the 2026/27 season carry two names that will mean little to casual followers of the game but a great deal to those who have watched the domestic circuit closely: Dewald Brevis and Ottneil Baartman, both handed their maiden national contracts after breakthrough seasons that finally translated into selection. Wicketkeeper Karabo Meso also receives a first national deal, rounding out a group CSA has explicitly framed as an investment in the squad's next generation rather than a reward for the current one.

Brevis, long tipped as a generational batting talent since his teenage years in age-group cricket, has taken the unusually long route to a senior contract that South African cricket has become known for, years of domestic form and franchise cricket performances before the national selectors finally committed. Baartman's path has been built more on consistency with the ball than headline-grabbing moments, the kind of attritional seam-bowling case that rarely makes highlight reels but increasingly convinces selectors building a squad for Test cricket's longer rhythms.

The contracts land just ahead of a home summer built around two Tests against Bangladesh on the Highveld, the first at the Wanderers from 15 to 19 November, the second at SuperSport Park in Centurion later in the month. It is not, on paper, the most glamorous fixture list CSA could have scheduled, but it gives a squad in transition a lower-pressure opportunity to integrate its newly contracted players into the Test set-up before sterner series against stronger opposition later in the season.

For Baartman and Meso in particular, a maiden contract is as much a statement of selectorial intent as a reward for past form: both now carry an expectation of involvement across the summer's matches rather than a single opportunistic call-up. CSA's selection panel has talked for several seasons about needing to widen a national pool that leaned heavily on a small core of established players, and this round of contracts is the clearest evidence yet that the conversation has moved from policy into actual squad selection.

Whether any of the newly contracted trio feature in the starting eleven when the first Test begins at the Wanderers remains to be seen, with national coach and captain still to confirm a squad for the series. But for a domestic system that has produced plenty of promising talent without always finding a route to convert it into Test careers, the contracts themselves mark a small but concrete step for three players who have spent years waiting for exactly this kind of recognition.
