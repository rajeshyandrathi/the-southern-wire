Gold drops below $3,500 as hot US inflation seals rate-hike bets, and the JSE falls 1%

US core PCE inflation came in hotter than expected on Wednesday, pushing spot gold down more than 3% to about $3,446, its lowest in more than a year, and silver more than 5% lower. Mining shares dragged the JSE All Share down about 1% by mid-afternoon, and the rand traded around R17.64 to the dollar.

Business Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·6 min read ·Updated 11:45pm SAST

Key points

  • US core PCE inflation rose to 3.3% a year in October, above the 3.2% expected; headline PCE was 3.8%
  • Spot gold fell about 2% to around $3,482/oz and silver about 3.7% to $39.29 after the data (Kitco, 16:01 SAST)
  • JSE All Share down 1.05% at 105,106 and Top 40 down 1.15% at 97,242 at 14:16, with resources down 4%
  • Rand around R17.64 to the dollar late morning, about 0.5% weaker on the day; markets price a December Fed hike as near certain

The rebound that followed Nvidia’s reinstatement of Jensen Huang lasted barely a day. On Tuesday precious metals fell sharply, the rand gave back most of Monday’s gain and the JSE drifted lower, as traders grew more certain that the US Federal Reserve will raise interest rates next month. The next test came at 15:30 SAST on Wednesday with the US PCE inflation figures, the Fed’s preferred measure of price pressure, and the core reading came in hotter than expected.

On Wall Street, Nvidia fell 5.9% on Tuesday after Monday’s jump of 31.5%, its best day in more than 23 years. Reuters reported that the Nasdaq Composite closed 1.45% lower at 23,740.52 and the S&P 500 0.48% lower at 7,202.45 in preliminary figures, while the Dow rose 0.87% to 50,028.79. Investors weighed governance and political-risk questions around Huang’s return, Reuters said, and new US import duties are expected after a probe into excess industrial capacity in 16 trading partners.

Precious metals took the brunt. Reuters reported spot gold down 1.8% at $3,566.45 an ounce late on Tuesday, with silver down 5% and platinum down 3.9%, as losses in AI-linked shares again pushed some investors to sell metals to raise cash for margin calls. Early on Wednesday gold dipped a further 0.3% to $3,544.54 at 02:24 SAST, then steadied: Reuters put spot gold at $3,548.03 an ounce at 07:20 SAST, with US gold futures down 0.4% at $3,546.90. CNBC data put silver at $40.89 and platinum about 1% lower at $1,263.62 at 05:12 SAST. Weaker platinum is unwelcome news for South Africa’s platinum miners.

Wednesday afternoon: hotter inflation hits metals and miners

The US figures released at 15:30 SAST did little to calm the market. Personal consumption expenditures prices rose 0.3% in October and 3.8% from a year earlier, up from 3.6% in September, Kitco reported. Core PCE, which strips out food and energy and is the Federal Reserve’s preferred gauge, also rose 0.3% for a third straight month and reached 3.3% a year, above the 3.2% economists had expected. A separate revision put US third-quarter growth at an annualised 4.3%.

Gold, which pays no interest, fell further as the data reinforced expectations of a quarter-point Fed rate hike at the 8 to 9 December meeting, which futures had already priced at about 96% to 98%. Kitco put spot gold at about $3,482 an ounce in early US trade, down $72.80 or 2.05%, after a session low of $3,469, with silver down 3.71% at $39.29. COMEX gold futures were 2.38% lower at $3,477.20 at about 16:32 SAST, Upstox reported, and it said gold had lost about 13% over the past month and silver about 26%. Platinum was near $1,223 at 16:10 SAST, according to spot data cited by The Library Estate.

On the JSE, Sharenet’s delayed data at 14:16 had the All Share down 1.05% at 105,105.77 and the Top 40 down 1.15% at 97,241.55, with the Resource 20 index down 4.04% and the precious metals and mining index down 4.85%. Financial shares held up better, with the Financial 15 index up 0.70%. Final closing levels were not yet available from a reliable free source when we updated.

The selling in metals deepened into the evening. By 11:13 a.m. New York time (18:13 SAST), Reuters reported spot gold at $3,445.89 an ounce, down more than 3% and at its lowest since August 2025, the first time it has traded below $3,500 since September last year. Silver was down 5.4% at $38.65, platinum 4.1% lower at $1,224.13, its weakest since June 2025, and palladium down 3.2% at $826. At the rand’s last timestamped rate of R17.64, gold works out at roughly R60,800 an ounce. A JSE closing level and a later rand rate were still not available from a reliable free source late on Wednesday.

Rate-hike fears weigh on the rand

The main pressure is coming from the US. The CME FedWatch tool shows a 96.4% chance of a quarter-point Fed hike in December and a 3.6% chance of a half-point move, Reuters reported. New York Fed President John Williams said on Monday that persistently high inflation points towards tighter policy. US consumer confidence also rose more than expected in November. Higher US rates make non-yielding gold less attractive and tend to draw money away from emerging markets such as South Africa.

The rand was at R17.54 to the dollar at 16:03 SAST on Tuesday, about 0.8% weaker on the day, even though the US dollar slipped about 0.5% against a basket of currencies, Reuters reported. At home, the composite leading business cycle indicator fell 0.3% month on month in September, another sign of a soft economy, and the 2035 government bond yield edged up to 8.665%. Sharenet’s late Tuesday rates put the rand at R19.24 to the euro and R23.01 to the pound, both weaker on the day.

The slide continued on Wednesday morning. By 08:01 SAST the rand was at R17.6275 to the dollar, about 0.5% weaker than its previous close, Reuters reported, while the 2035 bond was steady with a yield of 8.66%. BusinessTech’s Wednesday morning rates had the rand at R19.26 to the euro and R23.03 to the pound. Analysts at ETM Analytics said markets were pricing a 97% chance of a December Fed hike and a 66% chance of another in January, which leaves this afternoon’s PCE figures, the last big inflation release before the Fed’s final meeting of 2026, as the day’s main event.

On the JSE, the All Share index closed Tuesday at 106,216.57 points and the Top 40 at 98,372.69, according to Sharenet data, down about 0.2% and 0.3% from Monday’s close. Miners led the fallers: Sharenet’s list of the biggest moves among the top 100 shares showed Implats down 4.18%, Pan African Resources down 3.68%, African Rainbow Minerals down 3.57% and Anglo American down 3.26%, with Optasia also down 3.74%. On the other side, Pepkor rose 7.55%, Canal+ 6.98%, Mondi 4.42% and Netcare 3.51%.

Oil moved the other way. Brent crude settled 2.27% higher at $91.40 a barrel on Tuesday after earlier touching $88.10, its lowest since 27 August, The Nation reported. Prices turned up after more reports of attacks on shipping in and around the Strait of Hormuz, including an Iranian state media report that a container ship hit a mine. Traders are also watching signs of oversupply, which have kept a lid on prices in recent weeks.

Diamonds: Angola puts a price on De Beers

Diamonds do not trade on an exchange with a single daily price, but the sale of De Beers moved forward. Angola expects to pay about $180 million for a 10% stake, Endiama chief executive José Manuel Augusto Ganga Júnior said on 20 November, according to Reuters as reported by JCK. He called the stake “worthwhile” despite depressed diamond prices. JCK noted that this implies a value of about $1.8 billion for De Beers, well above the roughly $1.18 billion implied by the Global Diamond Consortium’s reported offer of about $1 billion for Anglo American’s 85%.

Botswana, which owns 15% of De Beers, wants to double its holding to 30%, while Angola and Namibia are expected to seek 10% each and Oman is involved in talks, JCK reported; the final split has not been announced, and Anglo American is still working towards binding agreements. On prices, Rapaport’s latest weekly comment, of 19 November, said smaller stones of 0.30 to 0.59 carats were still falling while larger diamonds were stable.
