Six more tankers hit around Hormuz as Iran vows to break the US blockade and talks stall

Iran says three tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, after three others were hit on Saturday. An Iranian attack damaged a facility in Bahrain, and Qatar-led mediation appears stuck.

World Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Iran’s navy said three tankers hit naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, causing large fires
  • Seven tankers have been hit since 16 November, according to the Joint Maritime Information Centre
  • Bahrain says an Iranian attack hit a civilian industrial facility on Saturday
  • Iran’s foreign minister says talks on reopening Hormuz are pointless until its demands are met

The confrontation in the Gulf escalated again over the weekend. On Monday 23 November, Iran’s navy said three oil tankers had struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz, causing explosions and large fires, CBS News reported. No casualties were immediately reported. Three other tankers were hit on Saturday in what appeared to be Iranian strikes.

The Joint Maritime Information Centre counts seven tankers hit since 16 November, including one operated by the UAE’s national oil company ADNOC, Gulf News reported. The UAE and Oman are searching for a missing Jordanian sailor. Shipping through Hormuz, is reported to be 65% below pre-war levels, and the US naval blockade of Iran has now run for about 130 days.

An attack on Bahrain

On Saturday, Bahrain’s interior ministry said an Iranian attack had hit a civilian industrial facility, AFP reported via Gulf News. Residents reported smoke over Sitra island, home to the Bapco refinery. One Bahraini was in a stable condition and a Pakistani resident in a critical condition. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said they had targeted US positions in Bahrain, and Bahrain’s defence force said it intercepted several attacks on Sunday. The Arab League, the UAE, Jordan and Saudi Arabia condemned the attack.

Hard words, stalled talks

Iran’s foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei said on Monday that Iran would use all available means to counter what it calls US aggression, and a deputy foreign minister called the blockade an act of war. Media linked to Tehran reported plans to send 14 tankers through the blockade. President Trump told Fox News that any Iranian vessels that tried would end up at the bottom of the sea, and said the US would resume military action after New Year if there is no deal, Gulf News reported.

A Qatari delegation met Iranian officials in Tehran on Saturday, but Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said talks on reopening Hormuz were pointless until Iran’s demands were met. Those include ending the war on all fronts, unfreezing assets and lifting oil sanctions. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio met Qatar’s prime minister in Washington on Monday, and CBS reported that indirect US-Iran talks have stalled. The US Treasury announced new sanctions on Iranian front companies the same day.

Why it matters for South Africa

Every attack in the Gulf ripples through to fuel prices here. Oil fell on Monday, with Brent near a three-month low of about $89 a barrel, but further damage to tankers or refineries could quickly reverse that. In Washington, incoming House Speaker Hakeem Jeffries said Democrats would try to end the Iran war when they take control of the House in January.
