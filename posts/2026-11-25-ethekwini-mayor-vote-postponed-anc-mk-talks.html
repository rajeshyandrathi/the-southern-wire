eThekwini delays its mayoral vote as the ANC claims a deal and MK waits for orders

Durban’s 223 new councillors were sworn in on Tuesday, then voted overwhelmingly to adjourn before electing a mayor, Speaker or deputy mayor. No party has a majority, and Friday is the deadline.

Politics Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • The adjournment motion passed by 213 votes to 4, with one abstention
  • MK has 86 seats, the DA 57, the ANC 37 and the IFP 15, with 112 needed for a majority
  • The ANC says it has an agreement and will be in government
  • MK says it is still waiting for direction from its national leaders
  • No new date has been set, but councils must elect leaders by Friday, 27 November

Durban still has no mayor. The 223 councillors elected in this month’s local government elections were sworn in at the Durban Exhibition Centre on Tuesday, 24 November, but the council then voted to postpone the election of a mayor, Speaker and other office-bearers so that coalition talks could finish, Daily Maverick and The Witness reported.

The Mail & Guardian put the vote at 213 to 4, with one abstention. The motion was moved by ANC councillor Thabani Nyawose, a former Speaker, who said parties needed enough time to conclude their talks. No date has been set for the reconvened sitting.

The council arithmetic makes a deal essential. According to Daily Maverick, the MK Party has 86 seats, the DA 57, the ANC 37, the IFP 15, ActionSA 7 and the EFF 4, with a long tail of smaller parties. A majority is 112, so no party can govern alone.

When we reported on Tuesday, unconfirmed reports said the ANC and MK had agreed to co-govern eThekwini. The parties have still not announced a final agreement.

Who wants what

The ANC says it has an agreement. Its eThekwini task team coordinator, Thanduxolo Sabelo, told the Mail & Guardian that “the agreement has been reached” and that the ANC would be part of the metro’s government, pending an announcement from the party’s leadership. He told The Witness the ANC would get at least two of the four top posts. The M&G understands that MK, the ANC, ActionSA and the EFF are negotiating, with MK expected to take the mayoralty.

MK is less definite. Its KwaZulu-Natal convenor, Willies Mchunu, said the party was still waiting for direction from its national leaders, though it wants the mayor’s office. The Witness reported that MK’s mayoral candidate is Apostle Mxolisi Phakathi and that the party has set itself a Friday deadline to close negotiations.

The DA is still refusing to work with MK. Daily Maverick reported that the DA is pushing Haniff Hoosen for mayor under a proposed ANC, IFP and DA arrangement, with the ANC choosing the Speaker and the IFP taking the deputy mayor post. Those three parties hold 109 seats, so they would also need smaller parties such as the FF Plus and ACDP. The DA’s Dean Macpherson said talks would resume on Wednesday and described the race as close.

A sitting held against provincial advice

The meeting went ahead despite a directive from KwaZulu-Natal Cogta MEC Thulasizwe Buthelezi to postpone inaugural sittings in eThekwini and 12 other municipalities, The Witness reported. Municipal manager Musa Mbhele questioned the department’s authority and said the city had already spent money it could not recover. He warned that rushing talks could produce leaders who are quickly removed.

Durban is the biggest test of the ANC national executive’s decision to favour MK in hung councils rather than its national unity government partner, the DA. A deal must be done by Friday, 27 November, the deadline for councils to elect their office-bearers.
