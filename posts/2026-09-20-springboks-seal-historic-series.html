Springboks end 50-year wait, seal series win over All Blacks in Baltimore

Sport Desk·Sunday, 20 September 2026

Fifty years is a long time to wait for anything, and rugby-mad South Africa has spent almost all of them waiting for exactly this. A commanding 43-28 victory over New Zealand in Baltimore has sealed a 3-1 series triumph for the Springboks — the first time South Africa has beaten the All Blacks in a bilateral series since 1976, an era so distant that most of the current squad's parents weren't yet born.

The series didn't start like history in the making. New Zealand took the opener 33-16, a scoreline that had the All Blacks' travelling support daring to believe old ghosts might finally be laid to rest in the other direction. What followed instead was one of the great Springbok turnarounds: a gritty 33-26 win in Cape Town, a tighter 29-24 follow-up, and then the statement performance in Baltimore that put the series beyond doubt.

That final scoreline tells its own story about where this Springbok side now sits in the world order. Forty-three points against a New Zealand side missing very little in terms of full-strength selection is not a scrappy, backs-to-the-wall win — it's a team imposing itself with genuine authority, exactly the kind of dominant performance Rassie Erasmus has spent years building toward.

For captain Siya Kolisi, the series carries weight beyond the scoreboard. Leading South Africa to a first series win over New Zealand in half a century places this group alongside the sport's most storied Springbok teams, and does so at a moment when the squad is simultaneously being rebuilt with an eye on the next World Cup cycle — proof, if any were needed, that transition and dominance aren't mutually exclusive under this coaching group.

What happens next matters almost as much as what just happened. A series win of this magnitude resets expectations heading into the rest of the international calendar, and rival nations now have fresh film of a Springbok side that looks faster, deeper and more clinical than the one that started this series a month ago. Fifty years of waiting has ended. The bigger question is how long South Africa now stays on top.
