Senegal are African champions again after sport’s top court overturns Morocco’s AFCON title

The Court of Arbitration for Sport has unanimously reinstated Senegal’s 1-0 extra-time win over Morocco in January’s Africa Cup of Nations final. Morocco says it will keep fighting.

World Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • CAS unanimously upheld Senegal’s appeal on Tuesday, 24 November
  • The ruling restores Senegal’s 1-0 extra-time win in the 18 January final in Rabat
  • The court found the referee alone decides whether a match resumes, and he restarted it
  • Morocco’s federation vows further legal action and questions contact involving an arbitrator, which both sides deny
  • It is Senegal’s second continental title, after 2022

Senegal have been restored as champions of Africa. On Tuesday, 24 November, the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) in Switzerland upheld the Senegalese Football Federation’s appeal against a decision by the Confederation of African Football (CAF) that had stripped them of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations title and given it to Morocco, Al Jazeera and AFP reported.

The three-member panel, chaired by Gerald Simon with Luigi Fumagalli and Ulrich Haas, ruled unanimously. Its decision reinstates Senegal’s 1-0 extra-time win over the hosts in the final in Rabat on 18 January, giving Senegal their second continental crown after 2022.

CAS director general Matthieu Reeb called it “a huge decision”, noting that it was the first time the court had ruled on the outcome of a continental final, according to AFP.

How the final unravelled

With the final goalless in second-half stoppage time, referee Jean-Jacques Ndala awarded Morocco a penalty after a VAR review of a challenge on Brahim Díaz. Senegal’s players and staff, led by coach Pape Thiaw, walked off in protest for around 14 to 15 minutes before returning, Al Jazeera reported. Díaz failed to score from the spot and Pape Gueye won the match in extra time.

In March, CAF’s appeals board ruled that Senegal had forfeited the match by leaving the field and recorded a 3-0 win for Morocco. It relied on two articles of the tournament rules that treat a team leaving the ground without the referee’s permission as the loser, AFP reported.

CAS disagreed. According to Gulf News and AFP, the panel found that the referee had sole authority to decide whether play would resume, and that because he restarted the match and it was completed, the rules on abandoned matches did not apply. Al Jazeera quoted Reeb as saying the panel found “the referee did not commit any error of law”.

Morocco is not giving up

For Senegal, the ruling ends months of fury. Their federation had called CAF’s decision the “most blatant administrative robbery in the history of our sport”, AFP reported, and its lawyer, Juan de Dios Crespo, described it as “so crude, so absurd, so irrational”, according to Al Jazeera. Senegal had also protested a stoppage-time goal by Ibrahim Sarr that was ruled out for a foul.

Morocco’s federation said it would “pursue all necessary legal avenues”. It pointed to alleged contact between arbitrator Ulrich Haas and Senegal’s federation president 48 hours before the ruling, AFP reported. Both the arbitrator and the Senegalese federation denied the allegation, according to Gulf News.

Morocco can still go to Switzerland’s Federal Supreme Court, but appeals against CAS decisions are allowed only on narrow legal and procedural grounds. Senegal has already paraded the trophy in Paris before a friendly at the Stade de France. Both teams have qualified for the next Cup of Nations, to be co-hosted by Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda in June and July next year, where Bafana Bafana fans will be watching a rivalry that now has extra edge.
