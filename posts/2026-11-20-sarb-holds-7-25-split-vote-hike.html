Business & Economy

The Reserve Bank held at 7.25%. Three of its seven members wanted to hike.

The rate stayed where it was on Thursday, but the vote was 4 to 3, with the minority pushing for another increase after inflation jumped to 5.2%. Unions are angry, the ANC points to fuel relief, and the Bank still sees cuts next year.

Business Desk·Friday, 20 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Repo rate unchanged at 7.25%, prime at 10.75%
  • Vote was 4 to 3, with three members wanting a hike
  • October inflation was 5.2%, highest in almost two and a half years
  • Next decision: 28 January 2027

Borrowers got a pause, not relief. The South African Reserve Bank’s Monetary Policy Committee left the repo rate at 7.25% on Thursday, which keeps the prime lending rate at 10.75%. What made the decision stand out was how close it came to going the other way. Four members voted to hold. Three wanted a 25 basis point hike.

That split matters more than the headline. A correction is due here: our preview yesterday described the market as expecting a cut, but the real debate going into the meeting was between a hold and a hike, and the vote shows that. Consumer inflation rose to 5.2% in October, its highest level in almost two and a half years and well above the Bank’s 3% target. Governor Lesetja Kganyago said the committee was concerned about the upward trend in core inflation, not only fuel.

The rate has been at 7.25% since September. The Bank started hiking again in May, after the US-Iran war pushed up oil and import prices, having spent most of 2024 and 2025 cutting. The Bank’s own model still points to easing ahead, with the repo rate drifting from about 7.15% in the last quarter of this year to roughly 6.6% by the third quarter of 2027. Kganyago stressed that this path is only a broad guide and decisions are made meeting by meeting.

Reaction split along familiar lines. The General Industries Workers Union of South Africa said workers and the middle class are squeezed by flat salaries and rising debt, calling the Bank’s approach a slow, deliberate squeeze. The ANC instead pointed to government support for consumers, including a R10 billion fuel subsidy that is set to continue into 2027.

For households, the practical effect is simple. Bond, car and credit card repayments stay where they are through the festive season. The next decision is due on 28 January 2027, and with three members already leaning towards a hike, the January call will depend heavily on whether December fuel price relief shows up in the inflation numbers.

What it means for your money

Because the rate did not move, nothing changes on your monthly debt repayments for now. Earlier estimates show what is at stake each time the rate moves by a quarter point: about R170 a month on a R1 million home loan, and roughly R15 a month on a R100,000 personal loan.

Savers, on the other hand, keep earning relatively high interest on deposits and fixed-term accounts, which is one reason the Bank uses higher rates to slow spending and cool prices.

Why the Bank is worried

The main concern is that price pressures are spreading beyond fuel. Core inflation, which strips out food and fuel, has been rising, and that is the measure central banks watch most closely because it reflects broader price setting in the economy. The Bank has also said it expects inflation to stay above 5% into early next year.

That explains why three members were ready to hike even with households already under strain. Their view is that letting inflation settle at a higher level now would mean tougher action later.

The road to January

Two things will matter most before the next meeting. The first is the December fuel price adjustment, which is expected to bring relief at the pump. The second is the rand, which affects the cost of imports from fuel to food and medicine. If both move the right way, the Bank may get room to start cutting next year, as its own model suggests. If not, the three members who wanted a hike this week could become a majority.
