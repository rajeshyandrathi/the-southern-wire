Court overturns MK's expulsion of Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla and Nhlamulo Ndhlela

The Johannesburg High Court found the party could not prove the 'presidential decrees' it relied on and had skipped its own disciplinary rules. Ndhlela must be restored to his seat in Parliament within 48 hours.

Crime Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·5 min read

Key points

  • Judge Lebogang Modiba of the Johannesburg High Court on Wednesday 25 November set aside MK's June expulsions of Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla and Nhlamulo Ndhlela as unlawful and invalid
  • The court found MK had not proved that the 'presidential decrees' it said Jacob Zuma issued actually existed
  • The Speaker of the National Assembly and the IEC must restore Ndhlela to his seat in Parliament within 48 hours of the order being served
  • MK said it had not yet studied the judgment; it is already appealing a similar ruling from the Western Cape

Jacob Zuma's daughter Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla and former MK spokesperson Nhlamulo Ndhlela are members of the uMkhonto weSizwe Party again, after the Johannesburg High Court ruled that the party expelled them unlawfully. Judge Lebogang Modiba declared the June expulsions unlawful, unconstitutional and invalid on Wednesday afternoon, 25 November, TimesLIVE and the Mail & Guardian reported, and ordered that both keep full membership rights.

The ruling goes further for Ndhlela. The court also set aside the letter that MK's then secretary-general, Sibonelo Nomvalo, sent to National Assembly Speaker Thoko Didiza on 6 July saying Ndhlela was no longer a member, which led to him losing his seat as an MP. The Speaker and the Electoral Commission must now restore him to the National Assembly within 48 hours of the order being served, TimesLIVE reported.

MK announced the expulsions on 18 June, accusing the pair of undermining the leadership, promoting factions and acting against party discipline. Zuma-Sambudla was accused of public and social media statements that damaged unity, and Ndhlela of acting without proper authority, including holding media briefings the party had not approved. Both went to court within days.

Why the court ruled against MK

MK's case rested on what it called presidential decrees, which it said Zuma issued under a section of the party constitution that gives the president general administrative powers. The court found the party had not proved those decrees existed. "The MK Party have failed to prove the existence of valid presidential decrees," the judgment said, according to TimesLIVE. The documents MK put before the court were appointment letters or extracts from the constitution, and Sunday World quoted the judge as saying the party could easily have produced the decrees had they existed.

The judgment also found that the general administrative power could not be used for discipline, because the MK constitution sets out detailed disciplinary procedures that must be followed. "Compliance with the MK Party Constitution is not optional," the court said, according to Sunday World. MK had given different accounts of who made the decision: its public statement said the national officials did, its court papers said Zuma acted alone, and elsewhere it said they acted together. No record of a decision was produced.

Neither of the two was given a fair process, the court found. Zuma-Sambudla was never charged or heard. Ndhlela was charged and sent written representations on 3 June, but no hearing was held and no sanction was ever imposed. Because the process was fatally flawed, the court said it did not need to decide whether the allegations against them were true. Sunday World reported that the party was ordered to pay punitive costs, though other reports did not mention costs.

What it means for MK

This is the second time in two months that a court has thrown out MK expulsions. On 25 September Judge Daniel Thulare of the Western Cape High Court set aside the expulsions of Pumlani Kubukeli and Khanyisile Litchfield-Tshabalala, which had also been based on decrees. MK is appealing that ruling to the Supreme Court of Appeal, but a further order keeps it in force while the appeal is heard.

An MK spokesperson said the party had "not yet studied the judgment", the Mail & Guardian and The Witness reported, and none of the reports we read said whether MK will appeal this ruling. Zuma-Sambudla and Ndhlela did not respond to the Mail & Guardian. Nomvalo, who signed the expulsions, is described by Sunday World as the party's former secretary-general.

The practical next step is in Parliament, where the Speaker's office must act on the order to return Ndhlela to his seat once it is formally served. For a party that has relied heavily on its leader's personal authority, two judgments in a row saying that authority does not override its own written rules are a significant setback.
