Etzebeth is back, and the Springboks want a fifth straight win at the Stade de France

Sport Desk

A 66-7 demolition of Italy in Turin, nine tries scored and barely a flicker of resistance offered, was always going to be a hard act to follow — and Rassie Erasmus has responded by making almost no changes at all. The Springboks travel to Paris unbeaten in four Nations Championship matches this campaign, and Erasmus's team sheet for Friday's meeting with France tells its own story of confidence: only two alterations from the side that put Italy to the sword, with Wilco Louw promoted into the starting front row and Cobus Reinach added to the bench for cover.

The bigger story in the squad is Eben Etzebeth's recall. The 147-cap lock, a figure as central to the Springboks' modern identity as almost anyone in the squad, had been missing through injury, and his return lands at exactly the moment South Africa needs physical certainty up front against a France pack that has troubled them before on their own ground. Etzebeth is due to arrive in France on Monday, replacing young lock Yaqeen Ahmed, who has returned home after picking up a shoulder problem — a reminder of how thin the margin is between a squad humming and a squad scrambling, even for the world champions.

The history Erasmus's team is chasing is specific and meaningful: a fifth consecutive Springbok win at the Stade de France, and a fifth win in this season's Nations Championship, a record that would put South Africa in rare company against a French side that treats home advantage in Paris as close to sacred. France have not made Erasmus's task easy in recent meetings, and nothing about this fixture suggests Friday will be a repeat of the Italy scoreline.

What the minimal changes really signal is a coaching staff unwilling to tinker with a formula that is working. After a season defined by squad rotation and selection experiments in earlier years, settling on close to the same starting fifteen two matches running suggests Erasmus believes he has found a combination worth protecting, rather than one that needs fixing after a one-sided win that, by its nature, tested very little.

For South African rugby followers, the appeal of Friday's fixture is less about the Nations Championship table — South Africa's position there is already commanding — and more about whether a Springbok side built around continuity can do in Paris what France sides have made notoriously difficult for visiting teams for years. A fifth straight win at the venue would say as much about this group's temperament as it would about its talent.
