Motorists face a coin-toss at the pumps as conflicting fuel price forecasts collide

Business Desk·Tuesday, 3 November 2026

South African motorists will find out on Wednesday whether November brings relief or further pain at the pumps, and even the analysts who track the Central Energy Fund's daily data in granular detail have struggled to agree on which way the official announcement will go. Early-month forecasts pointed to a steep increase, with some projections putting inland 95 petrol as much as R2.28 a litre higher than October's record R30.25 price. More recent recalculations have swung the other way, suggesting the first petrol price drop since July.

The source of the confusion is a temporary government intervention rather than any sudden shift in global oil markets: Treasury's decision to cut the fuel levy by R3 a litre on diesel and R1.50 a litre on petrol, in effect until the start of December, is working its way through the price-setting formula alongside the usual Rand-dollar and international product price inputs that the Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources uses each month. Depending on how far international prices move between now and Wednesday's cut-off, that levy relief could either offset an underlying increase entirely or compound into a genuine decrease.

For a country where fuel costs ripple directly into the price of nearly everything else, from the cost of a taxi ride to the price of groceries trucked between provinces, the stakes of the announcement go well beyond what any individual motorist pays to fill a tank. October's record petrol price already squeezed household budgets already strained by years of above-target inflation in food and transport costs specifically, even as headline inflation has cooled.

Industry watchers note that the uncertainty itself is unusual. Most months, the broad direction of the adjustment is reasonably clear well before the official announcement; this time, legitimate, well-sourced forecasts have pointed in opposite directions within the same week, reflecting just how finely balanced the rand-dollar exchange rate and international refined product prices currently are.

Whichever way Wednesday's announcement falls, the levy relief is only guaranteed until the end of November, meaning December's fuel price — arriving just as the festive season travel rush begins — could look very different again, regardless of how this month's numbers land.
