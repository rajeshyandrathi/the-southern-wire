Tanker carrying diesel capsizes off Ibiza, leaving one sailor dead and Spain on pollution alert

The Malta-flagged Med Emre overturned overnight off the north-west of the Spanish island with as much as 7,000 tonnes of diesel on board. All 17 crew were pulled from the sea, but one later died in hospital.

World Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • The Malta-flagged tanker Med Emre capsized off north-west Ibiza early on Thursday 26 November while sailing from Algeciras to Barcelona
  • All 17 crew, Turkish and Indian nationals, were recovered from the sea, but one died after being taken to hospital in Ibiza
  • The ship may be carrying up to 7,000 tonnes of diesel; Spain has activated its national maritime pollution plan at its second-highest level
  • Officials said the hull is floating upside down, not on the seabed, and only small sheens had been seen, with no significant spill so far

A tanker that may be carrying as much as 7,000 tonnes of diesel capsized in the Mediterranean off the Spanish island of Ibiza early on Thursday, 26 November, and one of its crew died after being rescued, Reuters reported. The Malta-flagged Med Emre was sailing from Algeciras, in southern Spain, to Barcelona when it overturned off the north-west of the island.

All 17 people on board, Turkish and Indian nationals, were pulled from the sea, according to Reuters. One man died after being taken to Can Misses hospital in Ibiza; his nationality had not been confirmed. Six others were taken to hospital or to Ibiza airport with light injuries, mostly hypothermia, while the rest were taken to Valencia or kept on board a rescue vessel, local reports said.

Reports put the wreck at between about 25 and 30 nautical miles (roughly 46 to 56 km) from Ibiza. Pablo Gárriz, the head of emergencies for the Balearic government, said the vessel "is not on the seabed but remains semi-submerged at the surface", floating keel up, Reuters reported. Spanish newspaper Última Hora described the Med Emre as a 120-metre chemical and oil products tanker built in 2019.

The rescue and the pollution risk

Spain's maritime rescue service, Salvamento Marítimo, sent three helicopters from Palma, Valencia and Tarragona along with several of its boats, and merchant ships in the area also helped, Mallorca.com and Última Hora reported. It also sent the pollution response vessel Clara Campoamor and a surveillance aircraft to the scene.

The amount of diesel on board is the main concern. The Balearic interior department said the ship could be carrying up to 7,000 tonnes. Spain's merchant navy directorate activated the national maritime pollution plan at level 2, its second-highest, and the Balearic government put its own civil protection plan on early alert. By Thursday only small sheens had been seen on the water, Gárriz said, with "no evidence of a significant spill" and no impact on the islands' coasts so far.

The waters around Ibiza are home to sea turtles, dolphins and the Balearic shearwater, a seabird found only in the region, as well as Posidonia seagrass meadows that protect the islands' beaches, local reports noted. Reuters recalled that Spain's worst maritime pollution disaster, the sinking of the tanker Prestige off Galicia in 2002, spilled heavy fuel oil along hundreds of kilometres of coast.

What happens next

Experts are assessing whether the overturned hull is stable before deciding how to salvage it and recover the diesel, Reuters reported. Diesel is lighter than the heavy fuel oil the Prestige carried and tends to spread and evaporate faster, but a large release would still harm marine life. The cause of the capsize has not been established, and the authorities have not said when salvage work will begin.
