Gold miners power JSE to record highs as earnings season splits winners from losers

Business Desk·Sunday, 20 September 2026

South Africa's earnings season has delivered a study in contrasts this month, and nowhere was that clearer than on the JSE in the first week of September, when the benchmark All Share Index pushed to a fresh high of 116,725.80 points on the back of blowout numbers from the country's precious metals miners.

African Rainbow Minerals set the tone, posting a 19% rise in headline earnings to R3.2 billion and rewarding shareholders with a generous final dividend of R7.00 per share — the kind of result that instantly reframes a stock's story from steady to standout. Harmony Gold went even further, reporting record full-year net profit of R29.5 billion on the back of elevated gold prices, a number that would have seemed implausible for the miner just a few years ago.

Not every sector shared in the rally. Profit-taking and sector rebalancing weighed heavily on telecoms and property counters even as miners surged, with Telkom sliding 4.41%, MTN Group down 3.01%, MAS Real Estate off 2.93%, and pharmaceutical group Aspen shedding 2.60% over the same week. None of those declines point to distress so much as rotation — investors moving profits out of last year's winners and into this year's gold and platinum story.

The split captures something real about where value is currently being created in the South African economy: a resources sector riding a genuinely favourable global pricing environment, alongside domestically-exposed telecoms and consumer names still working through a tougher local trading backdrop. Earnings season, in other words, is telling two very different stories depending on which part of the JSE you're reading.
