Nvidia’s U-turn on Jensen Huang sparks a relief rally: gold, the rand and the JSE all bounce back

The JSE All Share rose 1.7% on Monday and gold climbed back above $3,650 an ounce after Nvidia reinstated its chief executive. But traders now see a US rate hike in December as certain, which keeps the pressure on the rand.

Business Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·5 min read·Updated 5:30pm SAST

Key points

  • JSE All Share closed Monday at 106,428, up 1.71%, and the Top 40 at 98,669, up 1.90%, after Friday’s fall to a 52-week low
  • Gold about $3,625/oz by midday on Tuesday, down 0.2%, after a rise of about 3% on Monday; silver fell 2.5% and platinum nearly 3%
  • Rand about R17.42 to the dollar on Monday, roughly 1% firmer; still near one-year lows
  • Markets now price a December US Fed rate hike as certain; US inflation data is due on Wednesday

After the worst week for many markets in months, Monday brought a sharp rebound. The trigger was in California: Nvidia’s board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, 11 days after he resigned, following direct intervention by President Donald Trump, Reuters reported. Investigations into Huang and the company are continuing.

Nvidia shares jumped about 31.5% on Monday, their best day in more than 23 years, restoring nearly $1 trillion in market value, according to Reuters. The S&P 500 closed 4.7% higher, the Nasdaq Composite 6.5% higher and the Dow 3% higher. That followed Friday’s technology sell-off, the worst since 2020, when the Nasdaq fell 5.28%.

By Tuesday the mood had cooled. Precious metals gave back part of Monday’s gains during the day, with silver and platinum falling more sharply than gold, as investors waited for Wednesday’s US inflation figures.

Gold, the rand and the JSE

Gold recovered strongly on Monday, rising about 3% to roughly $3,640 an ounce, Reuters reported. Trading Economics said Huang’s return eased the margin-call selling that had forced investors to sell gold to cover losses elsewhere. Gold had fallen about 9% last week.

Updated on Tuesday afternoon: the recovery stalled. Spot gold was down 0.2% at about $3,625 an ounce at 12:46 SAST, according to Reuters. Silver fell 2.5% to about $41.97 and platinum dropped nearly 3% to about $1,286, which matters for South Africa’s platinum miners. Traders are holding back before Wednesday’s US PCE inflation data, which could firm up the case for a December Fed rate hike.

The rand firmed. Reuters put it at R17.43 to the dollar on Monday morning, about 0.9% stronger, and Trading Economics showed it at R17.42 later in the day, with the euro at R18.99 and the pound at R22.90. Trading Economics noted that the rand is still hovering around one-year lows, weighed down by coalition uncertainty in the Gauteng metros and the Reserve Bank’s narrow 4-3 vote to hold rates last week.

On the JSE, the All Share index closed Monday at 106,428 points, up 1.71%, according to Rateweb closing data published in The Daily Rand. A correction to our weekend report: we could not then confirm Friday’s close. The official figures show the All Share fell 1.49% on Friday to 104,639, below Thursday’s level, and the Top 40 closed Friday at 96,833, down 1.58%. Updated on Tuesday morning: the Top 40 closed Monday at 98,669 points, up 1.90%, from the same source. By Tuesday evening we had not found a reliable source for Tuesday’s JSE close, so Monday’s levels are shown. The day’s big listing news was Coca-Cola HBC’s debut, covered in a separate story.

The catch: a US rate hike now looks certain

The rally did not remove the main worry. The US dollar rose about 0.5% to its strongest level since March 2025, and the CME FedWatch tool now prices a December rate hike by the Federal Reserve as certain, up from 89% on Friday, Reuters reported. The US 10-year Treasury yield was 4.94%. Higher US rates tend to pull money out of emerging markets such as South Africa, which is bad for the rand.

The next big test is US inflation data, the PCE index, due on Wednesday. Oil, by contrast, is helping: Brent crude settled 3.5% lower on Monday at $89.37 a barrel, near a three-month low, which could ease fuel price pressure if it lasts.

Diamonds: no daily price, little new news

Diamonds do not trade on an exchange with a single daily price. Rapaport’s market comment of 19 November said prices for smaller stones of 0.30 to 0.59 carats were still falling while larger stones were stable, and the US market was firm ahead of Thanksgiving. On the De Beers sale, Botswana has confirmed a consortium led by former De Beers chief Gareth Penny as preferred bidder and wants a 30% stake, Rapaport and Business Day reported; Anglo American says no binding agreement exists yet.
