Cape Town gets its first dedicated coffee show, and Newlands Cricket Ground is the venue

Lifestyle Desk

The inaugural Cape Town Coffee Show opens on 21 and 22 November at Newlands Cricket Ground, filling a gap that has looked increasingly obvious given how seriously the city has come to take its coffee culture over the past decade. Cape Town's specialty coffee scene has grown from a handful of dedicated roasteries into a genuine industry, with local roasters now competing on an international stage, yet the city has never had an event built specifically around that growth until now.

Choosing Newlands Cricket Ground as the venue is a deliberate signal about the scale organisers are aiming for. The ground's existing infrastructure for hosting large public events, combined with its accessibility from both the southern suburbs and the city centre, gives a first-year festival room to establish itself without the constraints of a smaller, more niche setting. First editions of any new event carry real risk, and venue choice is one of the clearest ways organisers can show they are planning for genuine crowds rather than a modest trial run.

The timing, in the third week of November, places the show just ahead of Cape Town's busiest tourist season, a scheduling decision that makes sense for an event hoping to draw both local coffee enthusiasts and the international visitors who increasingly treat specialty coffee culture as part of what they expect from a destination city. Melbourne, London and Seattle have each built significant tourism and cultural cachet around their coffee scenes; a dedicated Cape Town show is a clear step toward claiming similar territory for South Africa's coffee capital.

What exactly the show's programme includes beyond the obvious tastings and roaster showcases remains to be seen in a first edition still finding its footing, but local industry events of this kind typically lean into competitions, barista showcases and educational sessions aimed at both professionals and enthusiastic amateurs. A debut event has the freedom to experiment with format in ways a long-running festival often cannot, since there is no established tradition yet to protect or disappoint.

For a city whose festival calendar already runs deep with food, wine and music events, a coffee-specific show slots into familiar territory while carving out its own identity. If the first edition lands well at Newlands, it joins a growing cluster of November events giving Cape Town residents and visitors reasons to fill out the weeks before the December rush properly begins.
