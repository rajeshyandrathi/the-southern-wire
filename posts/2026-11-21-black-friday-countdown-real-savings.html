Black Friday is six days away. Here is how to tell a real saving from a clever sales pitch.

The big day is Friday 27 November, but many deals have been running for weeks. Online shopping is booming, and money experts say the best protection is knowing what you would normally pay.

Lifestyle Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Black Friday is on 27 November; Takealot’s weekly deals run until 23 November, with its main sale from midnight on the 27th
  • Online retail in South Africa is expected to reach R159 billion this year
  • About a third of adults now shop online, according to a major industry survey
  • Experts say compare prices across stores and decide your budget before the sales start

Black Friday falls on 27 November this year, but for many shoppers the sales have already been running for a month. Takealot has been offering rotating weekly Black Friday-priced deals since 26 October, with the last of these running until 23 November, and its main sale starts at midnight on the 27th, BusinessTech reports. Brands including L’Oréal, Xiaomi and Canon have been running flash sales on the site through November.

Takealot’s chief marketing officer, Karla Levick, said the longer format was a response to how shoppers have been behaving on the platform. The company says Gauteng shoppers are the most likely to buy at midnight on Black Friday, followed by the Western Cape and KwaZulu-Natal.

The wider online market is growing fast. The Online Retail in South Africa 2026 report by World Wide Worx, cited by IOL, expects online retail to reach R159 billion this year, about 10% of all retail sales, with annual growth of about 22.5%. The same research found that 79.1% of adults use the internet and 34.2% shop online.

A discount is not the same as a saving

The most useful advice is also the simplest. Sarah Nicholson of JustMoney has warned that spending R1,000 on something you would not otherwise have bought is not saving R1,000. “You’ve actually spent R1,000,” she said in an interview quoted by EWN.

Before the sales start, make a list of what you need and set a budget. Check what an item normally costs, ideally over several weeks, so you can tell whether a so-called discount is real. Compare the same product at several stores, including delivery fees, and be wary of prices that were raised just before being cut.

Saving 40% on something you did not need is still money out of your pocket, EWN points out. If you are buying on credit, add up the interest you will pay, because it can quickly wipe out any discount.

Shopping safely online

With so much shopping now done on phones, it pays to be careful. Buy from retailers you know, type their web address yourself rather than clicking links in messages, and be suspicious of deals that look far too good to be true.

Keep records of what you buy, check the returns policy before you pay, and remember that the Consumer Protection Act gives you rights if goods are faulty. If a deal disappears or the price changes at checkout, take a screenshot.

Retailers know many households are stretched this year, with inflation at 5.2% and interest rates unchanged. That makes it more important than ever to treat Black Friday as a chance to buy what you planned to buy anyway, for less.
