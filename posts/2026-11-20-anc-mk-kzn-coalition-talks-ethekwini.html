The ANC needs the MK Party in KwaZulu-Natal, and the two are set to talk this weekend

With 34 hung councils in the province and mayors due to be elected next week, the ANC and Jacob Zuma’s MK Party are expected to meet about a power-sharing deal. In eThekwini, nobody can govern without MK.

Politics Desk·Friday, 20 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • ANC and MK expected to meet this weekend about KZN councils
  • 34 KZN municipalities have no outright winner
  • In eThekwini: MK 38%, DA 25%, ANC 16%
  • Mayors due to be elected next week

The coalition scramble is not only a Gauteng story. In KwaZulu-Natal, the ANC and the MK Party are expected to meet this weekend to talk about working together in the province’s municipalities, according to Eyewitness News.

The numbers explain why. Thirty-four KwaZulu-Natal municipalities produced no outright winner in the 4 November local elections. In the biggest of them, the eThekwini metro around Durban, MK won about 38% of the vote, the DA about 25% and the ANC just 16%. In practical terms, no party can form a government in eThekwini without MK.

That is a sharp reversal for the ANC, which ran Durban for most of the democratic era. MK, formed by former president Jacob Zuma, has eaten heavily into the ANC’s support base in the province since the 2024 national election, and the local results confirmed that the shift has held.

The relationship between the two parties is tense. On Friday MK accused Cabinet minister Khumbudzo Ntshavheni of abusing her position by threatening to review Zuma’s post-presidential benefits. Any deal would have to be built on top of that kind of friction.

MK is also learning what it means to govern. In Impendle, a small municipality it won outright, the party said this week that it faces a tough job turning around a council it described as deeply troubled.

Councils across the country are expected to elect mayors next week, so whatever the ANC and MK agree, or fail to agree, this weekend will shape who runs Durban and much of KwaZulu-Natal for the next five years.

A difficult partnership

Any ANC and MK deal would bring together two parties that have spent the last two years fighting for the same voters. MK was launched with Zuma as its face after his split with the ANC, and its rapid rise in KwaZulu-Natal came almost entirely at the ANC’s expense. Analysts have identified MK as one of the main reasons for the ANC’s falling support in the province.

For the ANC, the logic is simple arithmetic. With only 16% in eThekwini, it cannot hope to govern Durban without a much bigger partner. For MK, a deal could offer a route into government in councils where it is the largest party but short of a majority.

Other options on the table

The DA, with about a quarter of the eThekwini vote, is the other major player in Durban. An MK and DA arrangement looks unlikely given how far apart the two parties are on most issues, but in hung councils unusual pairings are not impossible. Smaller parties, including the IFP in many rural councils, will also have a say in the smaller municipalities.

The coming week will show whether KwaZulu-Natal follows Gauteng into big multi-party deals or ends up with a patchwork of minority governments that have to win votes issue by issue.
