GTA VI may have sold 44.9 million copies in four days, analysts estimate

A research firm reckons Grand Theft Auto VI drew more than $4 billion in consumer spending in its first four days, pulling players away from Call of Duty and Fortnite. Rockstar and Take-Two have not confirmed the figures.

Tech Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Alinea Analytics estimates 44.9 million copies sold from 19 to 22 November and more than $4 billion in spending
  • The figures are unaudited estimates and have not been confirmed by Rockstar or Take-Two
  • About three-quarters of the estimated copies were on PlayStation 5
  • In South Africa the game sells for about R1,500, or R1,900 for the Ultimate Edition

Grand Theft Auto VI appears to have made a record-breaking start, though the numbers are still estimates. Research firm Alinea Analytics believes the game sold about 44.9 million copies between its release on Thursday, 19 November and Sunday, 22 November, and generated more than $4 billion in consumer spending, Gadget Review and WinBuzzer reported.

Those figures have not been independently audited. “Neither number has been confirmed by Rockstar Games or parent company Take-Two Interactive,” Gadget Review noted. Alinea has not disclosed how it estimates physical sales or its margin of error, and its count excludes unsold stock still on shop shelves.

Copies sold also do not translate neatly into players, because account sharing and people buying more than one copy can push the count either way. Take-Two has not yet reported official sales, and could do so in an earnings report or investor update.

PlayStation dominates

According to Alinea, about 33.7 million copies, or 75%, were sold on PlayStation 5 and about 11.2 million on Xbox Series X and S. Spending on PS5 alone was estimated at more than $3 billion, WinBuzzer reported. Alinea also estimated that about 40.8 million people logged in on Sunday, 22 November.

Other early figures point in the same direction. Rockstar itself estimated worldwide retail sales of $3.79 billion by the end of launch day, while Sensor Tower estimated about 24.6 million copies, including pre-orders, by the evening of release. For comparison, GTA V took more than $1 billion at retail in its first three days in 2013.

The launch appears to have pulled players away from other big games. Alinea estimated that Call of Duty’s average daily players fell from 7.5 million to 4.4 million, Fortnite’s from 17.8 million to 12.8 million and EA Sports FC 27’s from 5.9 million to 4.3 million.

What it costs in South Africa

Local players have paid a premium. Stuff South Africa reported that the standard edition costs about R1,500 and the Ultimate Edition about R1,900, R400 more. The game is available only on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X and S, and boxed copies contain a download code rather than a disc.

No PC version has been announced. Before launch, Newzoo analyst Emmanuel Rosier said a PC release could widen the game’s reach in markets such as China, where PC gaming is bigger than console gaming, WinBuzzer reported.

The first official numbers from Take-Two will show how close the analysts came. Even at the low end of the early estimates, GTA VI has already taken more in a day than its predecessor did in its first three.
