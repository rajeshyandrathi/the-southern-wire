The Houthis take their war to Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea ports

Yemen’s Houthis say they hit the ports of Jeddah and Jazan in revenge for coalition strikes on Hodeidah. The coalition reports only minor damage at Jazan and no casualties.

World Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • Houthis say they attacked Jeddah and Jazan ports on Wednesday, 25 November
  • They call it retaliation for Saudi-led coalition strikes on Hodeidah port
  • Coalition says a projectile caused minor damage at Jazan, with no civilian casualties
  • Coalition also reports downing a drone near Jeddah and, after midnight, two missiles aimed at Riyadh
  • Red Sea shipping risk rises, which can push more ships round the Cape

The war between Yemen’s Houthis and the Saudi-led coalition moved to Saudi Arabia’s main sea gateway on Wednesday. The Houthis said they attacked the Red Sea ports of Jeddah and Jazan, calling it retaliation for coalition air strikes on Hodeidah, the Yemeni port they control, This is Beirut reported.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said both ports were hit accurately and damaged. He promised more operations “deep inside Saudi territory” under what the group calls a policy of “blockade for blockade and escalation for escalation”.

The coalition gave a narrower account. It said a Houthi projectile struck Jazan port, causing minor damage and no civilian casualties, and that it was still identifying what kind of weapon had been used, according to an agency report carried by KhanList. Jordan’s Roya News reported that the coalition also said it destroyed a drone near Jeddah Islamic Port. Neither side’s claims could be independently verified.

How it escalated

The Houthis had warned this was coming. On Tuesday Saree threatened Jeddah and every Saudi port on the Red Sea if Hodeidah was bombed, and told port workers and shipping companies to leave, the Jerusalem Post reported, citing Reuters. Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki then told civilians to stay away from Hodeidah port, and the coalition said it struck Houthi military targets there and in Saada and Amran provinces.

The coalition also said it destroyed four drones over the Ras Tanura refinery on the Gulf coast, where falling fragments caused no injuries. Late on Wednesday, Roya News reported that the coalition said it had intercepted two ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh after midnight, and that AFP reported an explosion in the capital shortly after a Saudi warning of a “hostile aerial threat”. That report had not been confirmed by a second source by the time of publishing.

The attacks follow the Houthi strike that damaged the Public Investment Fund tower in Riyadh, reported here on 21 November. A UN-brokered truce in 2022 had largely halted fighting between the Houthis and the coalition, which backs Yemen’s internationally recognised government, but the war flared again this year.

Why it matters beyond the Gulf

Jeddah is Saudi Arabia’s busiest commercial port and the main sea entry point for pilgrims heading to Mecca, so any threat to it raises shipping and insurance costs on one of the world’s key trade routes. It comes while Gulf shipping is already disrupted, with traffic through the Strait of Hormuz sharply reduced since Iran closed it, as this site reported on Wednesday.

For South Africa the knock-on effect is familiar: when Red Sea routes look unsafe, more ships sail round the Cape, which adds days and costs to voyages between Asia and Europe and puts more pressure on South African ports. Roya News reported that the US State Department has warned Americans across the Middle East that attacks could escalate quickly.

No casualties had been reported from the strikes on Jeddah, Jazan or Hodeidah by late Wednesday. The Houthis say they will keep answering coalition strikes, which leaves Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea coast, until now largely spared, firmly inside the conflict.
