Spider-Man just swung past every local record in South African cinema history

Culture Desk

South African cinemas have not seen an opening weekend like this one. Spider-Man: Brand New Day pulled in sold-out screenings across the country's major circuits, setting a new record for the biggest local cinema debut on record and crossing well past the R44-million mark in opening-weekend takings. For an industry that spends most of its time worrying about whether anyone still shows up to a physical cinema at all, this was the kind of weekend that answers the question decisively, at least for the right film.

The scale of it eclipses the previous benchmark set by Deadpool & Wolverine in 2024, itself considered a watershed moment for how aggressively Marvel properties could perform in a market that competes for entertainment spend against load-shedding-era streaming habits, rand-denominated ticket prices and an increasingly crowded festive-season release calendar. That this new record arrived from a solo Spider-Man film rather than an ensemble crossover says something about how durable the character's local following has become across two decades of consistent box office performance.

What makes the number more striking is the context it arrives in. South African exhibitors have spent the past two years anxiously watching streaming windows shrink and major studios experiment with day-and-date releases that bypass theatrical runs altogether. A sold-out opening weekend of this scale is a direct rebuttal to the theory that local audiences have simply stopped valuing the cinema experience — it suggests, instead, that they stopped valuing it for films that don't feel essential to see on the biggest screen available, and that the right blockbuster can still fill every seat in the house.

It also lands at a pointed moment for South African screens more broadly, with Canal+'s completed takeover of MultiChoice still reshaping conversations about who actually controls what South Africans watch and on what platform. A theatrical record of this size is a reminder that, whatever happens to the streaming and pay-TV landscape upstream, the cinema box office remains one part of the local screen economy that foreign ownership structures cannot touch directly — ticket sales still happen one person, one seat, one weekend at a time.

Local exhibitors will be watching closely to see whether this opening translates into a genuinely strong multi-week run or simply the usual superhero-franchise front-loading, where a handful of enormous early weekends mask a steep fall-off once the most committed fans have already been. Either way, for one weekend at least, South African cinema chains got to post a number nobody in the industry expected to see this high, in a year when expectations for theatrical business had been trending quietly downward.
