A fresh wave of Afrikaans-language films is filling South African cinemas

Culture Desk·Sunday, 20 September 2026

While global streaming platforms chase billion-dollar franchises, a quieter but genuinely significant story has been unfolding in South African cinemas this month: a run of new Afrikaans-language releases finding real audiences at the local box office. Landmyn, a drama directed and written by Nico Scheepers, opened on 4 September, followed two weeks later by Silas en die Ysbeer op Tafelberg, a family fantasy-adventure directed by Zandré Coetzer.

The two films could hardly sit further apart tonally — one a serious drama built around its title's literal meaning, the other a family-friendly fantasy adventure set against the unmistakable backdrop of Table Mountain — and that range is itself part of the story. A local film industry capable of supporting both a weighty drama and a whimsical family adventure in the same month, in the same language, is a healthier sign for Afrikaans-language cinema than either film succeeding in isolation would be.

This local wave is also landing inside a broader Heritage Month moment for South African screen culture, with cinema showcases this September deliberately pairing local action, drama and family releases alongside the sounds and stories that have shaped South African identity more broadly. For an industry that has spent years fighting for shelf space against dominant international releases, a run of homegrown films finding their own audience niche is exactly the kind of quiet momentum worth watching.
