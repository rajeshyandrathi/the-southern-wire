The biggest IPO ever opened 34% higher, then gave half of it back

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI models, raised $81.3 billion at $83 a share, beating SpaceX’s June listing as the largest IPO in history. The shares jumped at the open on Nasdaq and closed at $97.17.

Tech Desk·Friday, 20 November 2026

Anthropic’s stock market debut on Thursday came with a record attached. The AI company sold 980 million shares at $83 each, above the $70 to $78 range it had set, raising about $81.3 billion. That makes it the largest initial public offering ever, ahead of SpaceX’s $75 billion listing in June.

Trading under the ticker ANT on Nasdaq, the shares opened about 34% above the offer price before cooling through the day. They closed at $97.17, still around 17% higher than the IPO price, but well off the early peak.

That pattern, a big pop followed by a fade, is common for hot listings, and it reflects the central question hanging over the whole AI sector: how much revenue these companies can earn against the huge sums they are spending on computing power. Critics have pointed to Anthropic’s very large commitments to data centres and chips, and to how fast revenue would need to grow to justify them.

For South African readers, the listing is mostly a signal of where global capital is flowing. Local investors can only get exposure through offshore accounts or funds that hold US tech shares, but the scale of the raise shows how central AI has become to the world’s biggest markets.
