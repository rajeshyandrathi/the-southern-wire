Mexico captures ‘El Chapo Isidro’, one of the FBI’s most wanted, in a raid that left 11 dead

Special forces seized Fausto Isidro Meza Flores in Guasave, Sinaloa, after weeks of planning with US intelligence. Gunfights and roadblocks followed across the state.

World Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, 44, was captured on Friday in Guasave, Sinaloa, and flown to Mexico City
  • Four members of the security forces and seven suspected gunmen were killed
  • The US had offered up to $5 million for information leading to his capture
  • Eight more suspects, one of them a minor, were arrested on Sunday

Mexican special forces have captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, 44, known as “El Chapo Isidro”, one of the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted fugitives. The raid took place on Friday 21 November at a house in Guasave, an agricultural area of Sinaloa state, and was announced on Saturday, Al Jazeera reported.

Meza Flores is believed to lead Los Mazatlecos, a group that split from the Beltrán Leyva cartel and has operated on its own since 2013. CBS News described his organisation as allied with one of the two warring factions of the Sinaloa cartel.

How the raid unfolded

The capture followed two earlier failed attempts and weeks of planning, using drone surveillance and intercepted communications. Diversionary raids in nearby Los Mochis drew cartel fire, before more than two dozen undercover soldiers stormed the house while two Black Hawk helicopters fought gunmen in armoured pickups, Al Jazeera reported. He was flown to Mexico City early on Saturday.

Eleven people died: four members of the security forces and seven suspected gunmen. Gunfire caused panic in Guasave, a baseball game was suspended, and gang members put up roadblocks elsewhere in Sinaloa. On Sunday the defence ministry said eight suspected members of a group linked to the Beltrán Leyva organisation had been arrested, one of them a minor, and that troops had seized heavy weapons and six vehicles, CBS reported.

The US connection

The raid was coordinated with US intelligence. The US Justice Department described Meza Flores as a senior leader of the Sinaloa cartel. Washington had offered up to $5 million for information leading to his capture, and he faces US charges that carry a possible life sentence, as well as Mexican warrants for organised crime. None of the reports said whether Mexico would extradite him.

The arrest came days after President Claudia Sheinbaum met President Trump at a summit in Asia. Mexico killed the Jalisco cartel leader known as “El Mencho” in February, CBS noted.

What happens next

Security Secretary Omar García Harfuch said troops would remain in Sinaloa to protect civilians. Past arrests of cartel leaders have often been followed by violent fights over succession, and Sinaloa residents will be watching to see whether this one is different.
