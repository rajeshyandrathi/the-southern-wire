Three retired Security Branch officers granted bail over 1991 killing of a police informer

The NPA calls it one of the outstanding apartheid-era cases linked to the Truth and Reconciliation Commission. Three men now in their 60s and 80s are charged with the planned murder of Mozambican informer Adriano Bambo near Matsulu.

Crime Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Johan Koekemoer (68), Ivor Human (82) and Isaak Engelbrecht were arrested on Tuesday, 24 November
  • They are charged with the premeditated murder of Adriano Bambo near Matsulu in March 1991
  • Bail was set at R5,000 each, and the state did not oppose it
  • The case returns to court on 23 February 2027 ahead of a move to the High Court

Three former Security Branch officers have appeared in court over a killing that took place 35 years ago. Johan Petrus Koekemoer, 68, Ivor Human, 82, and Isaak Engelbrecht were granted bail of R5,000 each in the Nelspruit Magistrate’s Court on Tuesday, 24 November, Lowvelder reported for Caxton’s Network News.

They are charged with the murder of Adriano Louis Bambo, a Mozambican national who worked as a police informer. The state alleges he was killed on or about 5 March 1991 at or near Matsulu, outside Mbombela, in a planned killing carried out by a group acting with a common purpose.

Nuusflits reported that the three held the ranks of lieutenant colonel, brigadier and general at the time, and that they were arrested on warrants on Tuesday morning.

The NPA described the matter as one of the outstanding apartheid-era cases related to the work of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission. The accused have not pleaded, and they are presumed innocent until the allegations are proved in court.

Bail and the road to trial

Magistrate Patrick Morris granted bail, which the state did not oppose, partly because it did not consider the men a flight risk. As a condition, they must surrender their passports to the investigating officer and inform him before leaving the country. Two handed over their passports in court, while the third said through his lawyer that he does not have one.

The charge is brought under the section of the Criminal Law Amendment Act that covers premeditated murder. A conviction would carry a prescribed minimum sentence of life imprisonment unless the court finds substantial and compelling reasons to impose less. The case was postponed to 23 February 2027, when a date is expected to be set for its transfer to the High Court.

NPA regional spokesperson Monica Nyuswa confirmed the arrests and the court appearance. According to Nuusflits, the NPA said the case had also been considered in the context of the Khampepe Commission’s work.

A case first heard at the TRC

The killing came up before the Truth and Reconciliation Commission more than two decades ago. Lowvelder noted that former Vlakplaas commander Eugene de Kock told the TRC’s amnesty hearings in 2000 that a staged weapons cache was planned to make the killing look like an escape attempt or self-defence. That evidence was not raised in court on Tuesday.

The two reports differ on some details. Nuusflits gives Engelbrecht’s age as 87 and spells some names differently, while Lowvelder says he is 86. The case is the latest of the apartheid-era matters linked to the TRC to reach a courtroom, more than three decades after the killing.
