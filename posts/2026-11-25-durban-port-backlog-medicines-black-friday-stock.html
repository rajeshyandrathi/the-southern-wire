Durban port backlog puts medicines and festive stock at risk days before Black Friday

Equipment breakdowns and a new truck-booking system have slowed Durban’s main container pier. The health department is trying to get urgent medicine containers out, and retailers are waiting on summer stock.

Business Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Delays are at Durban Container Terminal Pier 2, run by ICTSI since 1 January
  • The health department flagged 41 urgent medicine containers, of which 18 have been cleared
  • Transnet says it spent about R1.5bn on equipment at the pier before handing it over
  • A KZN business group warns losses could reach hundreds of millions of rand
  • Pepkor says its summer stock deliveries were disrupted ahead of Black Friday

The country’s busiest container terminal is struggling just as retailers need it most. Delays at Pier 2 of the Durban Container Terminal are putting medicines and festive-season goods at risk, The Mercury reported on Tuesday, 24 November, three days before Black Friday.

Since 1 January 2026 the pier has been run by International Container Terminal Services Inc. (ICTSI), a Philippines-based company, as Durban Gateway Terminal. Transnet says the pier handles about 60% of South Africa’s container volumes. The freight forwarders’ association, quoted by The Rio Times, puts the share at almost half of containerised freight.

Reports blame repeated breakdowns of container-handling equipment and capacity problems linked to a new truck-booking system introduced before the festive peak. Transport Minister Barbara Creecy said on 18 November that the new terminal operating system was one cause and that she had been working on the problem “for several months”, The Rio Times reported.

Medicines first

According to The Mercury, the Department of Health identified 41 pharmaceutical containers that needed to be moved out of the port urgently. Eighteen had been cleared and delivered, and seven more were due to arrive between 23 November and 2 December.

Creecy has said that vessels carrying medicine are the top priority, along with car parts. Transnet has set aside Berth 102 for ships with their own cranes, diverted some ships to the Ngqura terminal near Gqeberha and provided trains to move import containers to Gauteng. The last of six ship-to-shore cranes was due to be commissioned and handed over on 20 and 21 November.

Transnet has not given a date for clearing the backlog. Estimates of how long it will take differ. The Mercury’s report mentions delays of nearly three months, while Andrzej Kiepiela of the KZN Growth Coalition said he had been told up to 20 days.

Who is to blame

Kiepiela told The Mercury that the new system was introduced at the wrong time and that losses “could run into hundreds of millions.” He also repeated claims that there had been no investment in the pier.

Transnet rejects that claim. In a statement it said ICTSI took over a working operation after a Transnet-funded equipment programme that included four new ship-to-shore cranes costing R967m and 20 straddle carriers. It put its spending on Pier 2 equipment in the 18 months before the handover at about R1.5bn, and said it is working with the operator to prioritise essential cargo.

The timing is awkward for a port that had been recovering. The World Bank and S&P Global’s 2025 Container Port Performance Index named Durban the most improved port in its rankings. For shoppers, the question this week is whether shelves will be full. Pepkor, the owner of PEP and Ackermans, has already said that summer deliveries were disrupted by weather and port congestion.
