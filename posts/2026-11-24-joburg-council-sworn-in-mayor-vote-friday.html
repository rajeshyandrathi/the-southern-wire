Johannesburg’s 270 councillors are sworn in, but the vote for mayor is pushed to Friday

The new council met for the first time on Monday and adjourned without electing a speaker or mayor. An ANC, ActionSA, EFF and MK bloc has the numbers to make Herman Mashaba mayor when it reconvenes.

Politics Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Johannesburg’s new 270-seat council was sworn in on Monday but elected no speaker, mayor or deputy mayor
  • The council reconvenes on Friday 27 November at 10:00, according to The National
  • ANC, ActionSA, EFF and MK together hold 137 seats, one more than the 136 needed for a majority
  • The DA, the biggest single party with 91 seats, had demanded the delay and threatened legal action

Johannesburg has a new council, but still no mayor. The 270 councillors elected in this month’s local government elections were sworn in on Monday 23 November at the council’s first sitting, but the meeting adjourned without electing a speaker, an executive mayor or a deputy mayor, EWN and The National reported.

According to The National, the EFF tabled a motion to postpone the rest of the business, and every party in the chamber seconded it. The council is due to reconvene on Friday 27 November at 10:00. City manager Floyd Brink, who chaired the opening of the meeting, said notice of a new sitting must be served at least three calendar days in advance. Some earlier reports had pointed to Thursday; The National’s later report gives Friday.

Both of the main mayoral contenders, the DA’s Helen Zille and ActionSA leader Herman Mashaba, were sworn in as councillors on Monday, the Mail & Guardian reported.

The numbers that matter

The DA is the biggest party in the new council with 91 seats, followed by the ANC with 63, ActionSA with 42, the EFF with 17 and the MK Party with 15, according to The National. A majority is 136 votes. Together, the ANC, ActionSA, the EFF and MK hold 137 seats, enough to elect a mayor without the DA.

The deal on the table would return Mashaba to the mayor’s office six years after he resigned from it. MK is expected to take the deputy mayor post, and Daily Maverick reported that the four parties had sealed a metro pact. The National reported that the agreement was expected to be signed on Tuesday. Daily Maverick also reported that the ANC wants the finance and infrastructure portfolios.

There was a side row over the ANC’s own representation. The ANC won no proportional representation seats in Johannesburg, and the Mail & Guardian reported that it turned down an offer from ActionSA to seat ANC regional chair Loyiso Masuku as an ActionSA PR councillor.

Why the DA wanted a delay

The DA had pushed hard for Monday’s sitting to be postponed. Zille wrote to Brink on Saturday and again on Sunday, and the party’s attorneys sent a third letter on Sunday evening, EWN reported. Daily Maverick said the DA had threatened legal action if the council went ahead with elections while coalition talks were unfinished.

In the chamber, DA councillor Alex Christians argued that what had been tabled was a proposal rather than a formal motion, and that Friday’s meeting should count as a continuation of Monday’s. ANC councillor Vhonani Marema disagreed, The National reported. How that procedural point is settled could matter if the DA goes to court.

What happens next

Councils across the country are under pressure to finish electing office-bearers. ANC spokesperson Mahlengi Bhengu-Motsiri said that by 27 November “everything should be set”. Business Day reported that Tshwane, Ekurhuleni and Nelson Mandela Bay were also expected to sit this week. Business leaders Mxolisi Mgojo, Adrian Gore and Martin Kingston have called the Johannesburg stalemate a national economic emergency, Business Day reported.

For residents, the practical question is whether a new administration can be in place before the festive season, when water, power and refuse problems tend to peak. Friday’s vote is the next real test.
