Another Level 4 storm warning for Gauteng today as severe weather spreads into Limpopo

The Weather Service has issued a fresh Yellow Level 4 warning for Gauteng, the Western Bushveld, central Limpopo, western Mpumalanga and far eastern North West. Extreme heat is expected in parts of the Northern and Western Cape on Thursday and Friday.

Weather Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Yellow Level 4 warning for severe thunderstorms over Gauteng, the Western Bushveld, central Limpopo, western Mpumalanga and far eastern North West
  • Yellow Level 2 warning for eastern Limpopo, southern and eastern Mpumalanga, eastern North West and the Free State
  • Extreme heat advisories for parts of the Northern Cape and Western Cape on Thursday and Friday
  • No damage from Tuesday’s storms had been reported by Wednesday morning
  • Gauteng’s dams are at 99.2%

Gauteng is under a severe storm warning for a second day in a row. The South African Weather Service has issued a Yellow Level 4 warning for severe thunderstorms on Wednesday, 25 November, covering Gauteng, the Western Bushveld, central Limpopo, western Mpumalanga and the extreme eastern parts of North West, The Citizen and Snow Report reported.

The warning covers heavy downpours that could flood roads and damage infrastructure, as well as damaging winds, hail and excessive lightning. The danger zone has moved north and west since Tuesday. Northern KwaZulu-Natal and the eastern Free State are no longer in the Level 4 area, while the Bushveld, central Limpopo and western Mpumalanga have been added.

A wider Yellow Level 2 warning covers eastern Limpopo, southern and eastern Mpumalanga, the eastern North West and the Free State. There, the Weather Service expects localised flooding of vulnerable roads, low-lying areas and bridges, with strong winds and hail that could cause some damage to homes and infrastructure.

What to expect today

Gauteng should be partly cloudy with scattered showers and thundershowers. Storms usually build in the afternoon, and the UV index is high. In Limpopo and Mpumalanga, morning fog over the escarpment will give way to showers and thundershowers, widespread in the east and north-east.

The west is dry and warm. The Western Cape should be fine and warm, hot along the west coast, with strong afternoon gusts along the west and south-west coast and an extreme UV index. The eastern half of the Eastern Cape can expect drizzle and light rain south of the escarpment, while KwaZulu-Natal will be cloudy and cool with scattered showers.

Johannesburg Emergency Management Services has asked motorists not to drive through flooded roads or over low-lying bridges, and parents to keep children away from rivers and streams. Spokesperson Robert Mulaudzi also asked faith groups not to hold river ceremonies, saying “do not conduct baptism/cleansing rituals during these severe thunderstorms”, EWN reported.

Heat later in the week, and full dams

The Weather Service expects extremely hot and uncomfortable conditions on Thursday in the Nama Khoi, Kamiesberg, Khâi-Ma and Hantam municipalities of the Northern Cape, and in Matzikama and Cederberg in the Western Cape. On Friday the heat moves to Beaufort West, Prince Albert and Oudtshoorn.

By early Wednesday no damage, injuries or deaths from Tuesday’s Level 4 storms over Gauteng had been reported in the sources we checked. Earlier storms did cause damage. On Monday the Gauteng health department said three facilities in Soweto had been damaged by last week’s heavy rain.

The rain has filled the dams. In the weekly update of 24 November, Snow Report put provincial dam levels at 99.3% in North West and 99.2% in Gauteng, with the Free State and Mpumalanga at 97.6%. The Western Cape is lowest at 74.2%, and KwaZulu-Natal is at 84.5%.
