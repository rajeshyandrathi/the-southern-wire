Fire at Bangkok’s Bangchak refinery leaves seven hurt, four critically

A blaze broke out in processing equipment at the urban refinery in Phra Khanong on Thursday afternoon. It was out within half an hour, but all production has been shut down while investigators look for the cause.

World Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • A fire, reported by several outlets as an explosion, broke out at Bangchak Corporation’s Phra Khanong refinery in eastern Bangkok at about 2:45pm local time (09:45 SAST) on Thursday
  • Bangkok’s Erawan Emergency Medical Centre said seven people were hurt, four of them critically
  • Bangchak said the fire was out by 3:15pm and that it had shut down all production units at the plant
  • Officials said the fire started in equipment attached to a distillation column, not a fuel storage tank; the cause is under investigation
  • The 120,000-barrel-a-day refinery is Thailand’s only one inside a city, and it had a fire in 2012

Seven people were injured, four of them critically, when fire broke out at an oil refinery in the eastern part of Bangkok on Thursday afternoon, 26 November. The blaze started at about 2:45pm local time (09:45 SAST) at the Phra Khanong refinery run by Bangchak Corporation, a Thai energy group, and sent a thick column of black smoke over the Thai capital.

Bangkok’s Erawan Emergency Medical Centre, which is run by the city, said it was treating four people in critical condition and three with less serious injuries, according to the BBC and Thai PBS World. The authorities have not said whether the injured were refinery staff or people from the surrounding area, and the figures were early ones that could change.

Bangchak said in a statement on Facebook that it had moved quickly to contain the fire, shut down all of the refinery’s production units and put the flames out by 3:15pm, about half an hour after they started. Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul told reporters he was following the situation and that everything appeared to be under control, AFP reported.

Smoke over a crowded district

The refinery stands off Sukhumvit Soi 62 in Phra Khanong district, surrounded by homes, schools and offices, and not far from Bangkok’s main port. The Bang Na district office first posted that an explosion had happened at the site and urged people nearby to leave, and AFP reported that police said students at nearby schools had been taken out of their classrooms. A city-wide phone alert later said the fire was under control but asked people to stay away from the area. Traffic around the site came to a standstill as fire engines and ambulances arrived.

Later in the afternoon, city officials played down the risk to residents. Thai PBS World reported that the director of the city’s disaster prevention department said the smoke had died down and that no formal evacuation order had been issued, though rescue teams stayed on standby. The Phra Khanong district director said the two or three communities around the refinery were safe, and the district set up a shelter near its office, upwind of the plant, in case people needed to move.

What started the fire

A secretary to Thailand’s energy minister said the fire had started in auxiliary equipment attached to a distillation column, part of the machinery used to process crude oil, rather than in a fuel storage tank, Thai PBS World reported. The prime minister said the fire had not been in the main production area, but that it was too early to say who was to blame. Bangchak said the cause was under investigation.

According to Bangchak’s website, quoted by AFP, the Phra Khanong plant can process up to 120,000 barrels of oil a day. It is the fifth-largest refinery in Thailand and the only one located in an urban area. The company also runs a larger refinery of about 180,000 barrels a day outside the capital, the BBC reported. Bangchak has not said when the Phra Khanong units will restart or whether fuel deliveries will be affected.

Questions about a refinery in the city

It is not the first fire at the site. In July 2012 an explosion at the same refinery, then owned by Bangchak Petroleum, set off a large blaze, although no injuries were reported at the time, AFP noted. That incident raised questions about keeping a large refinery in what has become a busy residential part of Bangkok, and Thursday’s fire is likely to bring those questions back.

For now, attention is on the four people in critical condition and on what investigators find inside the damaged unit. Thai energy officials and Bangchak are expected to give more detail once the site has been inspected.
