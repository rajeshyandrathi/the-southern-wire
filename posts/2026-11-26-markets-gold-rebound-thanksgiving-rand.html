Gold slips back to about $3,566 as the JSE rebounds 1.8% on Thursday

Gold drifted lower through a quiet Thanksgiving session and was at about $3,566 an ounce on Thursday evening, while the JSE won back more than Wednesday's losses: the All Share closed 1.8% higher at 106,632, led by mining shares. The rand held near R17.54 to the dollar.

Business Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·6 min read

Key points

  • Update: the JSE All Share closed Thursday at 106,631.52, up 1.80%, and the Top 40 at 98,596.15, also up 1.80% (Sharenet, as at 17:59)
  • Gold was at a bid of $3,565.50 at 21:49 SAST on Thursday, down 0.63% on the day; silver $41.42 and platinum $1,294 were both higher (Kitco)
  • Gold hit a session low of $3,426.40 on Wednesday, then closed the US session about 0.5% higher near $3,575 (Kitco)
  • On Thursday morning spot gold was at $3,565.39, down 0.4% on the day, at 08:43 SAST; silver was about $41.36 (FinanceFeeds, CNBC data)
  • The JSE All Share closed Wednesday at 104,748, down 1.38%, and the Top 40 fell 1.55%, with DRDGOLD, AngloGold Ashanti and Impala Platinum the biggest fallers
  • The rand was slightly firmer at about R17.52 to the dollar on Thursday (Trading Economics)
  • US stock and bond markets are closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving, so trading in metals and currencies is expected to be thin

Update, 23:30 SAST: The JSE made up all of Wednesday's fall and more on Thursday. The All Share closed at 106,631.52, up 1,883 points or 1.80%, and the Top 40 at 98,596.15, also up 1.80%, according to Sharenet's delayed index page at 17:59. In dollar terms the Top 40 rose 3.0%. Mining shares that were sold off on Wednesday led the bounce: by 15:57, shortly before the close, Sharenet showed Pan African Resources up 5.97%, Northam Platinum 5.67%, Sibanye-Stillwater 5.38%, Impala Platinum 5.00% and Harmony 4.66%, with Canal+ up 18.28% and Tiger Brands 11.52%. AB InBev, Southern Sun and Vodacom were among the few fallers. Gold, by contrast, drifted lower in thin holiday trade: Kitco showed a bid of $3,565.50 an ounce at 2:49pm New York time (21:49 SAST), down $22.50 or 0.63% on the day, which is roughly R62,500 an ounce. Silver was at $41.42, up 0.33%, and platinum at $1,294, up 1.09%. The rand was at about R17.54 to the dollar, R19.27 to the euro and R23.01 to the pound on Thursday afternoon, The National reported, after touching a low of about R17.73 overnight.

Update, 18:00 SAST: Gold held its ground through Thursday in thin holiday trade. Kitco showed spot gold at a bid of $3,576.30 an ounce at 9:49am New York time (16:49 SAST), down $11.70 or 0.33% on the day, in a range of $3,551.40 to $3,607.90. That is a little above the $3,565 we reported this morning, and works out at roughly R62,800 an ounce. The rand was little changed against the dollar at R17.555 early on Thursday, according to Reuters figures carried by Wisevoter. Thursday’s closing JSE levels were not yet available from a reliable free source at the time of this update, so the table still shows Wednesday’s close.

Update, 12:30 SAST: Gold was holding most of its recovery on Thursday morning. Spot gold was at $3,565.39 an ounce at 08:43 SAST, down 0.44% from Wednesday’s close of $3,581, in a range of $3,561 to $3,603, according to CNBC data quoted by FinanceFeeds. That is roughly R62,500 an ounce at the current rand rate. Analysts quoted there put the rebound down to traders closing bets against gold before the US holiday. Wednesday’s closing JSE figures, which were not available when this report was first published, are now in the table: the All Share fell 1.38% and the Top 40 1.55%, as gold and platinum shares were sold off before bullion recovered.

Wednesday turned out to be a round trip for precious metals. Gold plunged after US inflation figures came in hotter than expected, touching its lowest level in more than a year, and then clawed its way back as US bond yields eased and Wall Street recovered. By the end of the US session it was trading higher on the day. That means the picture in our report on Wednesday, which had gold down more than 3% at about $3,446 an ounce in the early evening, did not hold: the metal recovered all of that loss and more before the close.

Kitco’s afternoon report, published at 4:55pm New York time (23:55 SAST), put gold at about $3,576 an ounce, up $21.20 or 0.6% on the day, after a session low of $3,426.40 and a high of $3,590. Silver was about $41.12, up 0.8%, after falling as low as $38.09. Kitco linked the turnaround to falling US Treasury yields, with the 10-year yield down about 5.6 basis points to 4.845%, and to a rebound in US shares as selling in AI-linked technology stocks eased, which took pressure off investors who had been selling metals to raise cash.

Early on Thursday the metal was holding most of that recovery. Reuters put spot gold at $3,573.39 an ounce at 00:31 GMT (02:31 SAST), down 0.2%. At the dollar rate of about R17.56, that works out at roughly R62,800 an ounce, about R2,000 more than the rand price in Wednesday’s report. Compared with the $3,446 we reported at 18:13 SAST on Wednesday, gold is now about 3.7% higher. Dealer JM Bullion showed silver at an ask price of about $42.40 and platinum at $1,313.90, up 0.94%, at 10:28pm New York time (05:28 SAST).

Thanksgiving: thin trading ahead

Thursday is the US Thanksgiving holiday. Kitco noted that US stock and Treasury markets are closed today and that US shares trade a shortened session on Friday. Gold futures still trade almost around the clock, so contracts will change hands today, but Upstox said volumes are expected to be low. Kitco warned that thinner liquidity could make swings in gold and silver larger if political or policy news breaks before normal trading resumes.

The recovery does not change the main pressure on gold. Core PCE inflation, the Federal Reserve’s preferred price gauge, rose 0.3% in October and reached 3.3% a year, and US third-quarter growth was revised up to an annualised 4.3%, Kitco reported. Futures priced more than a 95% chance of a quarter-point Fed hike at the 8 to 9 December meeting. A Reuters report carried on Thursday said markets fully expect the December hike and price about a 60% chance of a half-point rise in January. Higher interest rates make gold, which pays no interest, less attractive to hold.

Futures told the same story as spot prices. Front-month gold futures fell to a low of $3,453.80 on Wednesday morning in New York before reaching $3,617.80 in the late afternoon, The Company Chronicle reported, using Yahoo Finance data. Upstox put COMEX December gold at about $3,580 at 10:21pm New York time on Wednesday, up about 1.25% from its previous settlement of $3,535.80, with COMEX silver up about 3.4% at $41.45. Kitco also noted oil near $84 a barrel for WTI and $89.62 for Brent, with shipping risks around the Strait of Hormuz unresolved and OPEC+ due to meet on 29 November.

The rand and the JSE

The rand ended Wednesday slightly weaker, and firmed a little on Thursday to about R17.52 to the dollar, according to Trading Economics. Trading Economics’ latest quote for 25 November had the dollar at R17.5649, up 0.18% on the day, with the rand at R19.26 to the euro and R23.07 to the pound; the page does not give a time. That is a little firmer than the R17.64 to the dollar that Sharenet showed at 11:26 on Wednesday morning, and earlier on Wednesday a First National Bank market note quoted by Market Forces had the rand at R17.59 to the dollar, R19.25 to the euro and R23.02 to the pound.

The JSE had a bad day on Wednesday. The All Share closed at 104,748.25, down 1,468 points or 1.38%, and the Top 40 at 96,852.23, down 1.55%, according to Trading Economics and The Daily Rand. Resources were hit hardest, down 4.04% according to Market Forces, because the sell-off in gold and platinum came during JSE trading hours and bullion only recovered later in the US session. DRDGOLD fell 6.06%, AngloGold Ashanti 5.79%, Impala Platinum 5.28%, Gold Fields 4.55% and Anglo American Platinum 3.19%. Naspers dropped 4.35% on the day it reported results. Financials were flat, with Standard Bank and Capitec edging up less than 0.1%. Thursday’s intraday JSE levels were not available from a reliable free source at the time of this update.

Diamonds: no new price signal

Diamonds do not trade on an exchange with a single daily price, and we found no fresh diamond price data or major De Beers news this morning. The latest picture is unchanged from our earlier reports: Rapaport’s weekly comment of 19 November said smaller stones of 0.30 to 0.59 carats were still falling while larger diamonds held steady, and Anglo American is still working towards binding agreements to sell its 85% stake in De Beers, with Botswana, Angola and Namibia all seeking bigger holdings.
