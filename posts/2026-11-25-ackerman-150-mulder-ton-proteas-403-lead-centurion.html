Ackerman’s record 150 and Mulder’s century put the Proteas 403 runs ahead at Centurion

South Africa scored 406 runs on day two of the second Test against Bangladesh and closed on 494 for 7. Marques Ackerman made the fastest Test 150 by a South African.

Sport Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • South Africa closed on 494 for 7, a lead of 403 over Bangladesh’s 91
  • Marques Ackerman made 150, the fastest Test 150 by a South African
  • Wiaan Mulder scored his fifth Test century, and Tony de Zorzi made 66 on his return
  • Marco Jansen is 67 not out going into day three
  • Bangladesh wicketkeeper Litton Das left the field with a groin injury

South Africa batted Bangladesh out of the second Test on Tuesday, 24 November. Starting the day on 88 for 1 at SuperSport Park, three runs behind Bangladesh’s first-innings 91, they added 406 runs to close on 494 for 7, a lead of 403.

Marques Ackerman, who was 46 not out overnight, set the tone with 150 before he was trapped lbw by Shoriful Islam. The Citizen and SuperSport said it was the fastest Test 150 by a South African. It was his second big score of the series after his 157 at the Wanderers, and only his fourth Test.

Wiaan Mulder added a patient 100, his fifth Test century, and Marco Jansen will resume on 67 not out on Wednesday, 25 November. South Africa already lead the two-match series 1-0, and with three days left they are well placed to win 2-0.

South Africa won the first Test at the Wanderers by nine wickets. Aiden Markram is captain for this match in place of the injured Temba Bavuma. On a rain-shortened first day on Monday, Marco Jansen and Kagiso Rabada took three wickets each to bowl Bangladesh out for 91.

A fast morning and a slow grind

Ackerman reached his century from 111 balls, according to Fullview, which said he was only the fourth South African to score a Test hundred in a morning session. He had a let-off on 1, when Bangladesh decided not to review an lbw appeal. He and Zubayr Hamza put on 96 before Hamza was caught at gully for 27, and South Africa reached lunch on 228 for 3.

Tony de Zorzi, playing his first Test in a year after hamstring trouble, made 66 off 137 balls. He was content to let Ackerman score during their stand of 74. “I’ve just got to get off strike and then they’ll entertain everyone else,” he told Fullview. He survived two lbw reviews against Mominul Haque and was limping late in his innings.

Ryan Rickelton and Matthew Breetzke both made 20, and South Africa were 356 for 5 at tea. Mulder and Jansen then put on 106. Fullview noted that five of Mulder’s first six Test fifties have become centuries. The Citizen reported that he fell for 100 on the second-last ball of the day, caught by Jaker Ali off Towhid Hridoy, leaving Simon Harmer with Jansen at the close.

Bangladesh stretched

Shoriful Islam and Khaled Ahmed took two wickets each for Bangladesh. They picked four fast bowlers and left out spinner Mehidy Hasan Miraz, so they had to use part-time bowlers for 36 overs, Fullview reported. “We couldn’t bowl in a disciplined way,” Shoriful admitted.

Things got worse when wicketkeeper Litton Das left the field with a groin injury and Jaker Ali took over the gloves. His fitness for Bangladesh’s second innings is uncertain. Jansen, whose highest Test score is 85 against Australia in Gqeberha, will be chasing a first Test century on Wednesday. With thunderstorms forecast over Gauteng, rain may be Bangladesh’s best hope of saving the match.
