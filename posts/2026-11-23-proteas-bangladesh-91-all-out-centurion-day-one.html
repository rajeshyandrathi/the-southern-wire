Bangladesh bowled out for 91 at Centurion as the Proteas’ quicks take charge of the second Test

Marco Jansen and Kagiso Rabada took three wickets each on a rain-shortened first day. South Africa closed on 88 for 1, just three runs behind.

Sport Desk·Monday, 23 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Bangladesh were all out for 91 in 33.3 overs after rain delayed the start by almost four hours
  • Jansen took 3/17, Rabada 3/23 and Nortje 2/10
  • South Africa reached 88/1 at stumps; Marques Ackerman is 46 not out
  • Aiden Markram is captain with Temba Bavuma injured; SA lead the series 1-0

South Africa’s fast bowlers took just 33.3 overs to bowl Bangladesh out for 91 on the first day of the second Test at SuperSport Park, Centurion, on Monday 23 November, The Citizen reported.

Rain delayed the start by 3 hours 45 minutes, and play only began at 1.40pm, Fullview reported. Stand-in captain Aiden Markram, leading the side with Temba Bavuma injured, won the toss and chose to bowl on a pitch with an obvious green tinge.

The wickets

Marco Jansen took 3/17, Kagiso Rabada 3/23 and the recalled Anrich Nortje 2/10. Mahfijul Islam top-scored for Bangladesh with 23 off 40 balls, and the last four wickets fell for six runs. Fullview reported that 91 is the lowest total by a side batting first at SuperSport Park.

South Africa’s reply started quickly. Markram and Marques Ackerman put on 49 for the first wicket before Markram, on 21 off 23 balls, was caught behind by Litton Das off Ebadot Hossain. Bad light ended play early with South Africa on 88 for 1, three runs behind, with Ackerman on 46 and Zubayr Hamza on 9.

Team news

South Africa made two changes from the first Test: Tony de Zorzi came in for Bavuma and Nortje replaced Dane Paterson. Bangladesh were without the injured Mushfiqur Rahim and Hasan Mahmud, and left out spinner Mehidy Hasan Miraz to field an all-pace attack, Fullview reported.

What it means

South Africa won the first Test at the Wanderers by nine wickets and lead the two-match series 1-0. After day one they are in complete control, as The Citizen put it, and a 2-0 series win looks likely if the weather allows enough play.

With storms forecast over Gauteng this week, rain may be Bangladesh’s best hope of saving the match.
