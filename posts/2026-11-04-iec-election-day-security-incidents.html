IEC vows legal action after ballot theft, a stormed storage site and a string of election-day incidents

Crime Desk·Wednesday, 4 November 2026

Beyond the overnight killing of an MK Party official, the Electoral Commission spent election morning fielding a separate and wider problem: a cluster of security breaches that it says are now the subject of criminal and legal action. Chief Electoral Officer Sy Mamabolo told reporters the commission had recorded a sharp rise in prohibited conduct by candidates and party supporters in the final stretch of campaigning, ranging from intimidation of election officials to the tampering of voting material, and said the IEC would pursue both the individuals and the parties implicated.

In Mpumalanga, a ward candidate allegedly stormed a voting station and damaged electoral materials before being arrested by police, one of several incidents the commission is treating as a direct assault on the voting process rather than ordinary campaign friction. In KwaZulu-Natal, MK Party supporters forced their way into a satellite storage facility at the Dassenhoek Library in Pinetown; police dispersed the crowd, and the IEC says no election material was tampered with or compromised in the breach, though officials at the site were briefly surrounded.

A second, separate KZN incident saw three empty ballot boxes and other voting equipment stolen from the Amahlongwa Community Hall in the Umdoni Municipality during the special-votes period. The commission was quick to stress that the stolen boxes were empty and that the ballot papers and validating stamps used to authenticate them were held elsewhere in secure storage, meaning the theft does not compromise the special vote count. A case has been opened at the Umkomaas police station and remains under investigation.

In the Eastern Cape, two people allegedly posing as IEC officials attempted to collect election material from a police station and were stopped before they could do so, an attempted impersonation the commission flagged as evidence that bad actors are probing the system's weak points rather than attacking it head-on. Separately, the IEC's Real411 platform, used to flag election-related misinformation, recorded 34 complaints covering false claims, incitement and harassment in the run-up to polling day.

The National Joint Operational and Intelligence Structure said it had activated its full election security plan, with police coverage extended to more than 26,000 voting stations nationwide, and warned that attempts to intimidate officials or disrupt voting "will not be tolerated." The IEC, for its part, has sought to draw a clear line between isolated criminal incidents and the integrity of the count itself, repeating its assurance that none of the breaches reported so far have affected ballot security or the validity of votes already cast.

Taken together, the incidents add a harder edge to what was already being billed as the most tightly contested local election in a generation, and they are likely to feature heavily in post-election disputes over individual ward results, particularly in the KwaZulu-Natal municipalities where tensions have run highest. For now, the commission says its priority is finishing the count it started at 7am, not refereeing the fights breaking out around its edges.
