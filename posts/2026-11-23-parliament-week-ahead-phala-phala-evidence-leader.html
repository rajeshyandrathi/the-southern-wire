Phala Phala inquiry returns this week as MPs look for a new evidence leader

The Section 89 committee meets on Thursday for the first time since a court upheld the withdrawal of its chosen evidence leader. Ramaphosa also faces questions in the NCOP on Tuesday.

Politics Desk·Monday, 23 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • The Section 89 impeachment committee meets virtually on Thursday 26 November from 17:00
  • It will adopt draft terms of reference and consider names for a new evidence leader
  • The Western Cape High Court dismissed a challenge to the withdrawal of Adv Thandazani Madonsela SC
  • President Ramaphosa answers oral questions in the NCOP on Tuesday

The parliamentary inquiry into whether President Cyril Ramaphosa should face impeachment over the Phala Phala affair is set to resume this week. Parliament’s programme for 22 to 27 November lists the Section 89 impeachment committee to meet virtually on Thursday 26 November from 17:00 to 20:00.

On the agenda: adopting draft terms of reference and considering proposed names for an evidence leader, the lawyer who leads the questioning of witnesses.

Why the committee needs a new evidence leader

It is the committee’s first meeting since the Western Cape High Court dismissed a challenge to its decision to rescind the nomination of Advocate Thandazani Madonsela SC as chief evidence leader, eNCA reported. Madonsela was withdrawn over a conflict of interest. The EFF and the African Legal Professionals Association went to court over the withdrawal and lost.

Committee chair Makashule Gana said the committee would meet to consider the appointment of a new evidence leader. The delay means the inquiry has not yet started hearing evidence.

The rest of Parliament’s week

On Tuesday 24 November, Ramaphosa answers oral questions in the National Council of Provinces, and the National Assembly holds a debate on the 16 Days of Activism against gender-based violence, along with reports on the Division of Revenue Amendment Bill and the Special Appropriation Bill.

On Wednesday, ministers in the peace and security cluster answer questions in the National Assembly. On Thursday, Deputy President Paul Mashatile is listed for questions in the NCOP, and the finance committee is briefed on an anti-money-laundering amendment bill. Friday brings first-reading debates on the Superior Courts Amendment and Statistics Amendment bills.

Speaker Thoko Didiza is leading a delegation to the SADC Parliamentary Forum in Eswatini from 22 to 27 November.

What it means

Thursday’s meeting will show whether the committee can finally move from procedure to evidence. It comes at a busy political moment, with the ANC negotiating coalitions in councils across the country this week.
