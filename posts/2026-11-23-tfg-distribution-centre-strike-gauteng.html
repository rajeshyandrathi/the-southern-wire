Workers at TFG’s Gauteng warehouses strike for higher pay, weeks before the festive rush

Staff at the Foschini Group’s distribution centres in Kempton Park and Midrand downed tools on Monday after year-long wage talks deadlocked. They want pay of R12,000 to R17,500 a month.

Business Desk·Monday, 23 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Workers at TFG distribution centres in Kempton Park and Midrand went on strike on Monday 23 November
  • Wage talks that began in November 2025 deadlocked and the CCMA certified the dispute unresolved
  • Workers want R12,000 a month for crew and pickers, rising to R17,500 for clerks
  • TFG says it respects the right to strike and has contingency plans for festive trading

Warehouse and logistics workers at the Foschini Group’s distribution centres in Gauteng went on strike on Monday 23 November, standing in cold and rainy weather outside sites in Riverfields, Kempton Park, and Lords View, Midrand, GroundUp and The Citizen reported.

The workers served 48 hours’ notice on 20 November after wage talks that began in November 2025 reached deadlock, and the CCMA certified the dispute as unresolved. GroundUp reported that 296 workers at Kempton Park backed the strike and about 100 struck at Midrand.

What the workers want

The workers are advised by the Simunye Workers Forum, an unregistered organisation, which says TFG will not engage with it. Their demands include a 45-hour week from Monday to Friday with overtime pay for weekends, pay graded by job from R12,000 a month for crew and pickers up to R17,500 for clerks, and an end to long labour-broker contracts.

GroundUp reported that some permanent workers earn as little as R6,200 a month, and that workers employed through labour brokers earn R500 to R1,400 a week. The Citizen described the demand as a pay rise of at least 94%.

TFG’s response

TFG said it respects employees’ right to take part in lawful industrial action and has contingency plans for the festive trading period. It said the workers had declined facilitation through the CCMA and that its door remains open.

Why it matters

TFG owns some of the country’s best-known clothing and homeware brands, including Foschini, Markham, Jet and Exact. A strike at its distribution centres just before Black Friday and the December season puts pressure on both sides, as the busiest weeks of the retail year approach.

The Cape Times reported that TFG workers in the Western Cape were considering a solidarity strike, which would widen the dispute if it goes ahead.
