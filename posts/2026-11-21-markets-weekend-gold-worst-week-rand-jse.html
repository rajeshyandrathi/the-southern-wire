Gold’s worst week in eight months, a weaker rand and a JSE at a 14-month low: the markets at the weekend

Gold was trading near $3,544 an ounce late on Friday, down almost 9% for the week, as bets on a US rate hike lifted the dollar. The rand was around R17.49 to the dollar and the JSE ended the week near its lowest level since September 2025. Markets are closed until Monday.

Business Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·5 min read·Updated 7:00am SAST

Key points

  • Gold about $3,544/oz on Friday afternoon, down about 8.7% on the week; silver near $41.90 and platinum near $1,317 earlier on Friday
  • Rand about R17.49 to the dollar on Friday morning, after Thursday’s weakest close in more than a year
  • JSE All Share closed Thursday at 106,216, its lowest since 25 September 2025; Top 40 at 98,385
  • Markets are closed for the weekend; the next session is on Monday

It has been a bruising week for anyone holding gold, platinum or South African shares. Gold was trading at about $3,544 an ounce on Friday afternoon, according to IndexBox, down roughly 2.3% on the day and about 8.7% on the week. It touched a low of about $3,503 shortly before 2pm New York time. The week had opened near $3,869.

Silver and platinum were also lower. A Reuters-based report on Friday put silver at about $41.90 an ounce, down 2%, and platinum at about $1,317, down 2%. Palladium was near $889. These were earlier readings on Friday; metals kept falling later in the day, and our Friday evening report put silver and platinum lower still.

The rand was trading at about R17.49 to the dollar on Friday morning, according to Sharenet, after closing Thursday at R17.45, its weakest close since early November 2025. Business Day reports that the currency is down 4.4% so far this month and 9.5% since late February.

On the JSE, the All Share index closed Thursday at 106,216 points, down 0.33% and its lowest close since 25 September 2025. The Top 40 stood at 98,385. That followed a 3.66% fall on Wednesday, the biggest one-day drop since March. The index is now about 17% below its record close in February.

Among individual shares, automated market reports showed Sanlam down 2.04%, Nedbank down 2.47% and cement maker PPC down 2.16% in Friday’s session, a sign that the selling spread to banks and insurers, not only miners.

Why markets are falling

The main driver is the United States. Hot inflation data and minutes from the Federal Reserve’s last meeting have convinced traders that the Fed will raise interest rates again in December; CME FedWatch showed about a 94% chance of a quarter-point hike. That has pushed up US bond yields and the dollar, which is bad news for gold, which pays no interest, and for emerging-market currencies such as the rand. A sharp sell-off in US technology and AI shares added to the pressure, as some investors sold gold to cover losses elsewhere.

“Gold is facing a perfect storm of macro headwinds and forced liquidation,” said Chris Weston, head of research at Pepperstone, quoted by The National.

At home, uncertainty over who will govern Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni has added to the rand’s weakness. Stanlib’s Victor Mphaphuli told Business Day that markets were worried about what the coalition talks mean for the national unity government. Investec economist Annabel Bishop said investors want stable coalitions that last through budget cycles.

Diamonds: no daily price, but a market still under strain

Diamonds do not trade on an exchange with a single daily price, so there is no closing level to report. The latest trade figures from Rapaport show prices for smaller stones still falling: its index for 0.30-carat diamonds dropped 5.5% in October, while 1-carat prices slipped 0.9% and 3-carat stones rose 0.9%. Rapaport’s mid-November market comment said small stones continued to decline while 1 to 2 carat goods were stabilising.

The big industry story remains the sale of De Beers. Botswana has picked a consortium led by former De Beers chief executive Gareth Penny as its preferred bidder for Anglo American’s 85% stake and wants to double its own share to 30%. Rapaport reports that observers expect a deal announcement in the coming weeks.
