Ireland edge the Boks 20-17 in Dublin, but a losing bonus point sends South Africa to the final

South Africa lost a second Test in a row on Saturday night, with Rassie Erasmus banned from the ground. The single point they took from the defeat was enough to win the southern table and book a place in next Sunday’s Nations Championship final at Twickenham.

Sport Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·5 min read

Key points

  • Ireland beat South Africa 20-17 at the Aviva Stadium after leading 17-10 at half-time
  • Robert Baloucoune scored twice for Ireland, and Malcolm Marx’s late try earned the Boks a losing bonus point
  • That point put South Africa top of the southern table and into the final at Twickenham on Sunday 29 November
  • England or France will be the opponents, depending on France’s result against Argentina tonight
  • Rassie Erasmus watched from the team hotel while serving a six-match World Rugby ban

The Springboks lost in Dublin on Saturday night and still got what they came for. Ireland won 20-17 at the Aviva Stadium, but South Africa stayed within seven points and took a losing bonus point. Because England had beaten New Zealand earlier in the day, that single point was enough to put the Boks top of the southern hemisphere table and into the Nations Championship final.

It was a second defeat in eight days, after the 21-19 loss to France in Paris, and it came without the head coach in the building. Rassie Erasmus is serving a six-match ban and watched from the team hotel, where he hosted an online watchalong, according to SA Rugby Magazine and The Citizen.

The Boks started well. Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu kicked an early penalty, and scrumhalf Morné van den Berg scored from close range after pressure at the scrum. Feinberg-Mngomezulu converted to make it 10-0. Ireland then took over the rest of the first half. Wing Robert Baloucoune scored two tries, and hooker Rónan Kelleher went over on the half-time hooter. Sam Prendergast converted that one, and Ireland turned around 17-10 ahead.

South Africa lost fullback Damian Willemse, who limped off around the 26th minute, and Canan Moodie came on. Soon after the restart Moodie thought he had scored, but the television match official ruled that he was in touch.

How the second half played out

Prendergast stretched Ireland’s lead to 20-10 with a penalty about 14 minutes into the second half. Malcolm Marx then scored, and Feinberg-Mngomezulu converted to make it 20-17. The Boks could not find another score. SA Rugby Magazine and RugbyPass both pointed to Prendergast’s long-range penalty as the kick that decided the game.

Both sides finished with 14 men. Ireland captain Dan Sheehan, playing at number eight, was yellow-carded for a crocodile roll on Marco van Staden. Van Staden was later shown yellow himself for a late, dangerous clear-out at a ruck. A head collision between Ireland’s Ryan Baird and Marx was ruled accidental, with no card.

There was one change to the team announced on Thursday. Lock Ruan Nortjé withdrew on Friday with a niggle, and Franco Mostert started alongside Eben Etzebeth.

Why Erasmus was not there

World Rugby suspended Erasmus on Tuesday 17 November for six international matches, with immediate effect. The ban followed comments at a press conference in Dublin on Monday, after the France defeat. He said 17 French knee-drop tackles had gone unpunished and asked whether that was not match-fixing. World Rugby said challenging a decision is not the same as questioning an official’s integrity.

Under the ban he cannot coach on match days, enter the stadium or coaches’ box, speak to match officials or do media duties. Reports say it covers the Ireland Test, the finals weekend and the Springboks’ first four matches of 2027, subject to appeal. SA Rugby has asked for a full and urgent misconduct hearing, saying it wants to put his comments in context. No hearing date has been announced.

Correction: our match-day preview this morning listed Ruan Nortjé in the starting team and said New Zealand were unbeaten. Nortjé withdrew on Friday, and New Zealand had lost to Wales the previous weekend. Our previews this week also failed to report Erasmus’s ban, which kept him away from the ground.

What happens next

The finals are played at Twickenham in London from Friday 27 to Sunday 29 November, and the top teams in each hemisphere meet in the final on the Sunday. South Africa’s opponents will be England or France. England top the northern table on 25 points. France, on 21, would need a bonus-point win over Argentina in Paris tonight to overtake them.

Ireland will most likely play in a lower placing match on the finals weekend, against Argentina or Australia, RugbyPass reports. For the Boks, the next week is about fitness, starting with Willemse, and about another big Test without their head coach on match day.
