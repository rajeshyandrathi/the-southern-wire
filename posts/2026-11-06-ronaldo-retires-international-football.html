Cristiano Ronaldo retires from international football after 23 years and a coaching fallout

Sport Desk

Cristiano Ronaldo has walked away from international football, ending a 23-year Portugal career in which he set the all-time men's record for both caps and goals, after a dispute with national coach Jorge Jesus that neither man could patch over. The announcement landed a day before Jesus was due to name his squad for this month's Nations League fixtures against Denmark and Wales, leaving the federation to plan for a first squad list in over two decades without its captain's name on it.

The break traces back to the last international window, when Ronaldo was left out of the matchday squad for Portugal's away fixture against Norway. He accused Jesus of going back on an understanding over his role and playing time, a grievance he did not keep private, and the relationship between the two men did not recover. Ronaldo has not spoken to his coach since the end of September, and the silence held even as Jesus guided the team through its remaining results unaffected by the rift playing out around his captain.

That standoff had already cost Ronaldo a 30-day suspension handed down by the Portuguese federation after he walked out on the squad during the previous international break, a punishment that hung over the past month and hardened positions on both sides rather than forcing a resolution. By the time this window arrived, the choice facing Ronaldo was not whether to play for Jesus again but whether to keep fighting for a seat on a bench he no longer trusted he would be picked from.

Jesus, for his part, said publicly that he had tried to keep his captain in the fold. "I asked him to continue with me, but I have to accept his decision," the coach said, framing the exit as Ronaldo's call to make rather than one forced on him by the federation or the dressing room. It was a notably restrained response to the departure of the player who has defined Portuguese football for a generation, and it left open the possibility that the two men's accounts of how the relationship broke down will keep being argued over long after the squad list is finalised.

Ronaldo leaves with 146 goals in 234 appearances for Portugal, both records that stood unmatched in the men's game before he retired and are likely to stand unmatched for a long time after. He is not retiring from football altogether: he is expected to see out the season at club level with Al-Nassr in Saudi Arabia, where his international exit changes nothing about his day-to-day football. The Portuguese federation has said it intends to organise a tribute for supporters once the dust from this week's announcement settles, a recognition that an international career spanning five World Cups and more than two decades does not end quietly, whatever the circumstances of its final chapter.
