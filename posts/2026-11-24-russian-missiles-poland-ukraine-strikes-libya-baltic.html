Russian missiles in Poland, a warship sunk in Libya: the Ukraine war spills far beyond its borders

Poland confirmed that three objects that came down near its border were Russian cruise missiles, and NATO held an extraordinary meeting on Monday. Ukraine says its sea drones sank a Russian corvette in Benghazi.

World Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Polish prosecutors confirmed on Sunday that three objects that fell in south-eastern Poland on Friday were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles
  • Nobody was hurt; NATO’s North Atlantic Council held an extraordinary session in Brussels on Monday
  • Ukraine says seven of its sea drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury at Benghazi, Libya, on Sunday
  • Drones hit a sanctioned Russian cargo ship between Estonia and Finland on Saturday

Over one weekend, the war in Ukraine reached Poland, the Baltic Sea and the coast of North Africa.

On the morning of Friday 20 November, during heavy Russian attacks on Ukraine, three objects came down in south-eastern Poland near the Ukrainian border, at Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik and Mołodycz. Nobody was hurt and no infrastructure was damaged. On Sunday the regional prosecutor’s office in Lublin confirmed all three were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, Notes from Poland reported.

Operational commander Ireneusz Nowak said the missiles were in Polish airspace for about three minutes. Two Swedish Gripen fighters flying NATO air policing tried to intercept them but could not identify them in time because of very poor weather. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called it another instalment in Russia’s escalation.

NATO’s response

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte visited Warsaw on Saturday and promised to defend every inch of Polish territory, while saying it was unclear whether the missiles were sent into Poland deliberately. Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski said Poland would summon the Russian ambassador. Russia had not commented.

On Monday the North Atlantic Council held an extraordinary session in Brussels. Poland’s deputy defence minister, Paweł Zalewski, went to press for stronger air defences on NATO’s eastern flank, but said there was no need to invoke Article 4, the clause for formal consultations, RBC-Ukraine reported.

Ships hit far from the front line

On Sunday, Ukrainian sea drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury at its moorings in the port of Benghazi, Libya. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed Ukraine carried out the attack using seven Ukrainian-made naval drones, Defense News reported. Libya’s eastern authorities, who are aligned with Moscow, said the ship had docked to refuel under a military agreement with Russia. Reports of casualties differ. The UN mission in Libya said the country must not become a battleground for foreign conflicts, and the Kremlin accused Ukraine of terrorism.

A day earlier, drones struck the Sparta II, a sanctioned Russian state-operated cargo ship, in the Baltic between Estonia and Finland, and the crew abandoned ship, the Kyiv Post reported. Finland said Ukraine’s military was probably responsible, and Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said the war was spilling over.

Why it matters

For NATO, the Polish incident is the latest in a series, after Russian drones entered Poland in September 2025 and a Kh-101 exploded in eastern Poland in July 2026. For Russia, the sinking in Libya shows that Ukraine is now willing and able to hit its navy far from the Black Sea. Both raise the risk that a mistake draws other countries more directly into the war.
