Record R326bn festive spend, but fewer gifts and fewer braais

A survey of more than 8,000 South Africans finds the average festive budget up 21% to R7,658. For the first time, people expect to spend more on getting home than on presents, and cash is the gift most of them want.

Lifestyle Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • Wonga’s ninth Summer Spending Survey projects a record R326bn festive spend
  • The average budget is R7,658, up 21% and the highest since the survey began in 2018
  • Local travel and transport (R1,562) has overtaken gifts (R1,465) for the first time; food and drink is still the biggest cost at R3,225
  • Only 23% plan to travel, and 53% say they cannot afford to go anywhere
  • Nearly half say they are worse off than a year ago, and 30% plan to use buy now, pay later

South Africans expect to spend more than ever this festive season, but a new survey suggests the money is buying less fun. Wonga’s ninth annual Summer Spending Survey, which polled more than 8,000 people across the country, projects total festive spending of a record R326bn.

The average planned spend per person has risen 21% from last year to R7,658, the highest figure since the survey started in 2018. Yet nearly half of respondents said they are financially worse off than they were a year ago, The National reported. Asked to rate how 2026 had felt, people gave it 3.7 out of 10, the gloomiest score since 2020.

“Despite spending more, people are getting less,” said Wonga spokesperson Bryan Smith. Published reports on the survey do not explain how the per-person figure was scaled up to the R326bn national total.

Getting home now costs more than presents

Food and drink remains the biggest single item, at an average of R3,225 a person. The notable change is in second place: local travel and transport, at R1,562, has overtaken gifts, at R1,465, for the first time in the survey’s history.

That does not mean more people are going on holiday. Only 23% plan to travel this year, four percentage points fewer than last year, and 53% say they will stay put because they cannot afford to go. Among people earning more than R50,000 a month, 49% said petrol and flight prices were limiting their plans. Of those who are travelling, two-thirds are going to see family and friends, up 14 percentage points.

KwaZulu-Natal is the most popular destination outside people’s home provinces, chosen by 20% of travellers, followed by the Eastern Cape on 18% and Limpopo on 14%. The Western Cape has slipped to just over 10%, from a high of 18% in 2022, which Wonga links to the distance and the cost of fuel and flights.

Cash instead of gifts, and fewer braais

Gift lists are shrinking. The share of people buying presents for nobody at all rose to 13%, from 8% last year. When asked what they would like to receive, 43% chose cash, and 78% wanted either cash or vouchers, the highest level since 2018. Nearly a quarter said they would like grocery vouchers.

The braai is also taking a knock. Only 39% plan to host one this December, compared with an average of 49% in previous surveys, and Wonga suggests higher meat and poultry prices may be part of the reason. The share planning to shop for gifts online or in stores fell to 85%, from 91%, while slightly more people plan to buy at markets or make presents themselves.

To pay for it all, 28% plan to dip into savings, up four points, and 30% expect to use buy now, pay later services. Neil Roets of Debt Rescue warned that “higher expenditure should not be mistaken for economic optimism,” and said households emptying their emergency savings may lean harder on credit in January, when extra costs land on top of existing debt repayments and living expenses. With Black Friday on 27 November, he advised shoppers to check whether a discount actually lowers their total household spending or just adds to it.
