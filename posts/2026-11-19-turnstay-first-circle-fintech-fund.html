A Joburg-built travel fintech just became the face of Africa’s newest $26 million bet

Tech Desk

Venture funds close every month without anyone outside the term sheet noticing. This one comes with a receipt. First Circle Capital's debut vehicle, Africa Fund I, landed at $26 million this week, and the number investors kept repeating back wasn't the size of the fund — it was the $61.5 million in cross-border travel payments that one of its portfolio companies, South African fintech TurnStay, pushed through its rails in just six months.

TurnStay's business is unglamorous in the way infrastructure usually is: it builds the payment plumbing that lets global travel merchants collect and settle money across African currencies and banking systems without each transaction becoming its own small ordeal. Travel payments are a notoriously punishing niche — multi-currency, high-value, time-sensitive, and littered with the kind of settlement delays that sink smaller operators. That TurnStay has scaled through it fast enough to become the fund's headline case study says less about one company's year and more about how much unmet demand was sitting in that corridor.

First Circle's own story is part of why the fund is drawing attention beyond the usual venture press. Built and closed by a female-led team operating out of Kampala, it backs pre-seed and seed-stage African fintechs across six specific verticals — insurtech, alternative lending, interoperability, climate fintech, SaaS and regtech — rather than chasing the single hottest category of the year. Eighteen companies now sit inside that portfolio, spread across the continent, and TurnStay is simply the one with numbers dramatic enough to lead with.

For South Africa's fintech scene, that's the more durable story here. The country's startups have spent the past two years proving they can build genuinely hard financial infrastructure — payments, remittances, lending rails — rather than lighter consumer apps, and a continental fund choosing a Johannesburg-built company as its marquee example is a signal that the thesis is landing with the people writing the cheques.

The harder test comes next. A $61.5 million six-month run is the kind of number that invites a Series A conversation, and First Circle's fund structure is explicitly built to carry its best-performing seed bets toward exactly that stage. Whether TurnStay becomes the fund's standout graduate or one of several contenders will say as much about the depth of Africa's travel-payments opportunity as it does about the company itself — but for now, it has given a brand-new fund something most debut vehicles never get this early: a number worth bragging about.
