Erasmus splits Bok squad ahead of rivalry series opener

Sport Desk·Saturday, 19 September 2026

Forty-three names. One document. And, if you know how to read a Rassie Erasmus squad list, roughly a dozen hidden messages about what he's actually planning. The Springbok coach has unveiled his squad for the newly minted Rugby Greatest Rivalry series against New Zealand, and as always with Erasmus, the selection says as much through what it doesn't do as through what it does. This is a coach who has turned squad announcements into their own form of psychological warfare, and this one is no exception.

The headline fixture is the one every South African rugby fan has circled for months: the series opener at Ellis Park, a ground that has swallowed touring sides whole for the better part of three decades. Before that, though, comes a warm-up assignment in Buenos Aires against Argentina — a fixture that looks, on paper, like a formality, but which Erasmus will use ruthlessly to fine-tune combinations before the real business begins. Nothing this coach schedules is ever just a formality.

The injury column is where this squad announcement gets genuinely exciting for Bok supporters. Eben Etzebeth, a totem in green and gold for the better part of a decade, is back. So is Lood de Jager, whose lineout work and physicality in the tight exchanges have been sorely missed. Together they restore a lock pairing that, at full strength, is arguably the most feared second row in world rugby — all boot, elbow, and unrelenting aerial dominance. Their return alone should worry a New Zealand pack that has had its own struggles up front this season.

Then there's Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu, back in the mix at flyhalf after a position that has seen more rotation this year than almost any other in the squad. His return gives Erasmus a genuine attacking pivot to pair with the more territorially-minded options already on the books — a luxury that few international sides can claim. And leading it all, as ever, is Siya Kolisi, back at the helm of a team he has carried through two World Cup campaigns and into whatever this rivalry series becomes.

What makes this squad list unmistakably Erasmus, though, is the players he's chosen to release rather than the ones he's chosen to keep. Several fringe squad members have already been sent back to their franchises for Super Rugby and URC duty — a move that on the surface looks like a downgrade in depth but is, in practice, exactly the opposite. It keeps those players match-sharp and battle-tested for when injuries inevitably strike later in the campaign, while signalling total confidence in the first-choice combinations to carry the opening rounds. It's chess, not checkers, and Erasmus has been playing this particular game longer and better than almost anyone else in the sport.

New Zealand, for their part, arrive with selection headaches of their own, and a pack that has looked vulnerable in exactly the areas South Africa is now strongest. Ellis Park has never been a forgiving venue for the All Blacks — the altitude, the crowd, the sheer physical demand of a Springbok forward pack running downhill with the wind at its back. If this opening test follows the pattern of recent history at that ground, New Zealand are in for an uncomfortable afternoon.

What's really at stake here goes beyond a single series. This is a rebranded rivalry, one both unions have leaned into as the flagship contest of the international calendar — the two most decorated nations in the sport's history, going toe to toe not once but across multiple tests, with genuine bragging rights and momentum heading into the next World Cup cycle on the line. Erasmus knows this. His squad selection reflects a coach building not just for one Saturday at Ellis Park, but for a series, and beyond that, for a legacy still being written.
