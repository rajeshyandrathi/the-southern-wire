ActionSA's Mashaba denies pushing Zille out — but Johannesburg's own overhang has already cost the ANC its mayoral pick

The Southern Wire Desk·Johannesburg

Herman Mashaba spent Sunday doing something he rarely bothers with: publicly correcting the record. A weekend report had cast ActionSA as issuing an ultimatum to the DA over Helen Zille's mayoral candidacy, and Mashaba, accusing the paper of misleading its readers, insisted his party had made no such demand. It was a curious thing to deny so forcefully, given that ActionSA chair Michael Beaumont had already told reporters the party has "major misgivings and concerns" about any coalition built around Zille, and Mashaba himself has said on record that if the DA wants to work with ActionSA, "they must remove Helen Zille." Whatever the semantic distinction Mashaba is drawing, the practical message landing in DA headquarters has been identical for a week: bring Zille, and the deal gets harder.

Zille, for her part, has not been shy about what she thinks of her old rival. Asked whether she could work with Mashaba, she deflected into a pointed counter-jab, accusing him of having "sold out" the DA to the Economic Freedom Fighters during his own turbulent spell running Johannesburg. It is the kind of line that plays well with a DA base nervous about losing its best-known figure, and it does nothing to narrow the actual gap between the two parties. With 99.9% of voting districts counted, the DA sits on 34.8% of the Johannesburg vote, the ANC on 20.3%, and ActionSA on 16.3% — arithmetic that leaves the DA needing exactly the partner currently trying to amputate its own candidate.

While that fight plays out in public, a quieter and arguably more consequential one has been settled by the numbers themselves. An electoral overhang — created when a party wins more ward seats on the ground than its proportional vote share would normally entitle it to — has stripped the ANC's own proportional representation list of any effect in Johannesburg. The ANC won 63 seats on a vote share of roughly 20%, enough to trigger the mechanism, and the practical result is that neither Loyiso Masuku, the party's sitting deputy mayor and presumptive mayoral candidate, nor outgoing mayor Dada Morero will return to the new council at all. Masuku was first on the ANC's PR list; Morero was fourth. Neither list position now matters.

It is a strange, almost accidental way for a party to lose its two most senior local figures in one sitting, and it leaves the ANC needing to find a new face to lead its Johannesburg negotiating position at the exact moment its leverage over the DA and ActionSA matters most. News24's own analysis found the Johannesburg case is not unique: 33 of the country's 213 councils emerged from last week's election with an overhang, affecting 71 seats in total, and 93% of those seats went to the ANC nationally. Seven councils now have outright majorities that no party actually won at the ballot box.

None of this changes the basic shape of Johannesburg's problem. The Municipal Structures Act gives the new council fourteen days from its formal declaration to hold its first sitting and elect a mayor, and that clock is already running. Whoever the ANC now puts forward to negotiate will be doing so without the two people voters and the party itself had expected to lead that conversation, while the DA and ActionSA continue trading insults over a mayoralty that still, on paper, belongs to Zille. Johannesburg has cycled through a revolving cast of mayors for five years running. Nothing in this week's opening exchanges suggests that pattern is about to break.
