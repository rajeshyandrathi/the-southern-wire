A three-storey collapse in Flatbush, and a death toll that keeps climbing

World Desk

A three-storey building on Flatbush Avenue, near its intersection with Bedford Avenue in Brooklyn, came down shortly after 4pm on Wednesday, and by Thursday morning New York officials were still revising the toll upward. At least six people have been confirmed dead and at least nineteen injured, among them seven children, with fire department officials warning overnight that both numbers could still rise as crews continue working through the rubble.

The building stood next to a lot where an older, single-storey structure had recently been demolished to make way for new construction, and investigators are now examining whether that excavation work next door played any part in the collapse. City officials have not yet said what triggered the failure, only that the inquiry is active and that structural engineers have been brought in alongside emergency crews.

The search itself has been as fraught as the cause is unclear. Fire department officials said overnight that they could not yet say with confidence how many people remain unaccounted for inside the collapse zone, a gap that has kept rescue crews working in slow, careful stages rather than racing to clear debris outright. Heavy equipment has been brought in to lift sections of the structure, but much of the work close to where people may still be trapped is being done by hand.

Neighbours described a street that filled with dust and noise in seconds, followed by the longer, quieter work of pulling people from the wreckage through the evening and into the night. Several of the injured were taken to hospital in critical condition, and residents of nearby buildings were evacuated as a precaution while engineers assess whether adjoining structures were destabilised.

For a digest built around South African and African news, a building collapse in Brooklyn earns its place for what it says about a recurring and uncomfortable pattern: ageing infrastructure sitting beside aggressive new construction in dense cities, with the margin for error measured in how carefully an excavation next door is managed. New York's own building safety record has been tested by exactly this combination before, and the coming days of investigation will turn on whether this was simply a tragic structural failure or whether the work happening in the empty lot alongside it had something to do with triggering it.

No final toll had been given by Thursday morning, and city officials have been careful to describe search operations as still active rather than shifting to recovery. That distinction matters to the families still waiting for news, and it is the reason the number of dead reported this morning is already being treated, by the officials giving it, as provisional.
