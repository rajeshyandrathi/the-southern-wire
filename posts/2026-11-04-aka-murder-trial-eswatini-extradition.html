The men accused of killing AKA inch closer to facing a South African court

Crime Desk·Wednesday, 4 November 2026

Nearly three years after rapper Kiernan "AKA" Forbes and his friend Tebello "Tibz" Motsoane were gunned down outside a Durban restaurant, the case has taken what prosecutors hope is a decisive step forward: extradition proceedings against suspects held in Eswatini are moving through that country's courts, raising the prospect that men long out of reach of South African justice could finally be handed over to face trial.

The case has been defined almost as much by delay as by the crime itself. The domestic trial against the men already in South African custody has advanced in fits and starts, repeatedly pushed back by adjournments tied to legal arguments, scheduling conflicts and the sheer complexity of a case with multiple accused facing murder and attempted murder charges. Each postponement has reopened old wounds for two families who watched a double killing captured on security footage become one of the country's most-discussed unsolved cases almost overnight.

Extradition from a neighbouring country is rarely swift, and Eswatini's legal process gives the suspects there room to challenge their transfer before any handover becomes final. South African authorities have pushed the request through diplomatic and judicial channels for months, treating it as central to building a complete case rather than proceeding with a partial one. Investigators have been candid that bringing every alleged participant before a single court matters for establishing the full picture of what happened outside Mosi Lounge that night in February 2023.

For AKA's fans and for an entertainment industry that has repeatedly had to process the violent deaths of its own, the extradition news lands as a rare marker of forward motion in a case that has tested patience more than it has delivered answers. The rapper's family has used public platforms sparingly but pointedly over the trial's life, pressing for the case to move at a pace that matches the scale of what was lost.

Whether this extradition push closes the gap between the suspects already on trial and those still beyond South Africa's reach will depend on how quickly Eswatini's courts rule, and on whether any further legal challenges emerge once a decision is made. For now, the development is being treated as the most concrete sign in months that the full cast of this case may eventually stand in the same dock.
