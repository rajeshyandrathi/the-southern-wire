Toy Story 5 and a Supergirl reboot headline September streaming

Culture Desk·Saturday, 19 September 2026

Streaming calendars are usually a scattershot affair — a few decent options buried among filler content designed mostly to pad out a subscription's perceived value. September breaks that pattern entirely. This is one of those rare months where the release slate reads less like a schedule and more like a statement of intent from every major studio at once, and two titles in particular are doing most of the heavy lifting for South African audiences plugged into global platforms.

The first is the one an entire generation has been waiting three decades for without quite realising it: Toy Story 5, landing on Disney+ later this month. The premise — a rescue mission built around Jessie — sounds simple on paper, but simplicity has always been this franchise's secret weapon. Pixar built an empire on the idea that the smallest, quietest emotional beats land hardest when they're earned properly, and everything about the marketing for this instalment suggests a studio that knows exactly what audiences are showing up for: not spectacle, but the specific, aching nostalgia of watching characters we grew up with grow older alongside us. If the trailers are any indication, bring tissues.

On the opposite end of the emotional spectrum sits the month's other genuine event: a new Supergirl film with Milly Alcock stepping into the cape as the last survivor of Krypton. The casting alone generated the kind of internet buzz studios spend millions trying to manufacture organically, and early reactions suggest the film is deliberately swimming against the current tide of grim, self-serious superhero cinema. Where recent entries in the genre have leaned into darkness and moral ambiguity almost as a reflex, this Supergirl appears to be betting on something audiences have been quietly starving for: a lighter, more character-driven take that remembers superhero stories are supposed to be fun. If the gamble pays off, expect a wave of imitators within the year.

But the real strength of this month's slate is its depth beyond the two headline acts. Damien Chazelle's sprawling, maximalist Hollywood epic finally finds a streaming home, bringing a theatrical-scale production into living rooms for audiences who missed its original run. Netflix, meanwhile, adds a documentary profile that's already generating conversation in film circles for how it handles its subject. And in one of the stranger but more charming programming decisions of the month, a beloved romantic comedy franchise gets a sequel relocated entirely to Italy — trading its original setting for sun-drenched coastlines and cobblestone streets, betting that a change of scenery is exactly what long-time fans want from a franchise revival.

Taken as a whole, this is a month built for genuine range: animated heartbreak, superhero optimism, prestige drama, documentary intrigue, and breezy romantic comedy, all landing within weeks of each other. The timing isn't accidental either. As the Southern Hemisphere eases into cooler evenings better suited to staying in than heading out, streaming platforms are clearly counting on exactly this kind of embarrassment of riches to keep subscribers glued to the couch rather than shopping around for something better elsewhere. Judging by this slate, they might just get their wish.
