Amazon buys an Israeli start-up’s AI chip software in a deal reported at $7.5 billion

The technology from Decart squeezes more performance out of each chip. Amazon wants it for the AI chips it plans to sell to other companies, as well as use itself.

Tech Desk·Monday, 23 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Amazon confirmed it will acquire Decart’s AI optimisation software and hire its founders and researchers
  • Bloomberg reported a price of about $7.5 billion; Amazon did not disclose terms
  • The software will be used on Amazon’s Trainium and Inferentia AI chips
  • The deal runs through Amazon’s chip unit, Annapurna Labs, and needs regulatory approval

Amazon has confirmed it will acquire the AI optimisation technology of Israeli start-up Decart and hire its founders and researchers, GeekWire reported on Monday 23 November.

Amazon did not disclose the terms. Bloomberg reported a price of about $7.5 billion, and Israel’s Globes reported that payment is tied to performance targets over five years. According to Bloomberg, Decart’s video models are not part of the deal.

Why Amazon wants it

Decart’s software is designed to get more performance out of each AI chip. Amazon plans to use it on its own Trainium and Inferentia chips, which it intends to sell to outside companies as well as use in its own data centres. Amazon chief executive Andy Jassy has said the company expects to build a very significant business selling chips externally.

The deal runs through Annapurna Labs, Amazon’s chip design unit, and needs regulatory clearance.

The people behind it

Decart is about three years old. Its founders, Dean Leitersdorf, Orian Leitersdorf and Moshe Shalev, own about 64% of the company and have committed to five years at Amazon. Globes reported they could receive about $4.8 billion between them. Anthropic reportedly discussed buying Decart for about $6 billion in August before walking away, and Nvidia invested in Decart’s $300 million funding round in May.

What it means

The reported price shows how much big technology companies are now willing to pay for software that makes AI chips run faster. Amazon executive Peter DeSantis summed up the logic: every leap forward in AI, he said, starts with getting more out of every chip.
