Rassie Erasmus blood six new Springboks for November's Nations Championship assault

Sport Desk·Monday, 2 November 2026

Rassie Erasmus has never been shy about blooding new talent at the exact moment conservative coaches would play it safe, and his 37-man squad for November's Nations Championship Tests is true to form: six uncapped players — Yaqeen Ahmed, Sebastian de Klerk, Luan Giliomee, Markus Muller, Riley Norton and Emmanuel Tshituka — have all been handed their first shot at the green and gold, with Italy waiting in Turin on 7 November to christen whichever of them get the nod.

It is a squad shaped as much by absence as by selection. Cheslin Kolbe, Eben Etzebeth, Andre Esterhuizen and Handre Pollard — four players with World Cup-winning medals between them — are all unavailable through injury, stripping out a huge amount of experience from the group that flies to Europe. For most national sides, losing four starters of that calibre would mean retrenchment and caution. For Erasmus, it has instead opened the door wider than usual, and the uncapped names in this squad will get opportunities that a fully fit group would never have afforded them.

The itinerary is unforgiving by design. After Italy in Turin, the Springboks face France on 13 November and finish the series against Ireland on 21 November — three Tests against sides ranked among the best in the world, each demanding a different tactical approach, and each a legitimate examination of whether this reshaped squad can hold its own without several of the players who carried South Africa to its back-to-back World Cup triumphs.

What makes the gamble more interesting than reckless is the track record behind it. Erasmus has built a habit, over several seasons now, of trusting rotation and squad depth as a genuine selection philosophy rather than a necessity forced on him by injuries alone, and the Springboks have rarely been punished for it. The newcomers named this week arrive into a system that has already proven it can absorb unfamiliar faces without losing its identity.

Still, Turin, Paris and Dublin in successive weeks is as hard a November as international rugby offers, and the margin for the kind of chaotic selection experiment Erasmus sometimes favours is thinner against opposition of this calibre. How the six uncapped players respond under that pressure will shape far more than one tour — it will tell South African rugby which of them belong in the conversation for the next World Cup cycle.
