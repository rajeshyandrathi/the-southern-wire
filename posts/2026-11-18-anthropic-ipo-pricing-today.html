Anthropic prices its IPO today. Nvidia and Alphabet just bet $10 billion each that it's worth it.

Tech Desk

Anthropic is set to price its initial public offering today, the last step before the AI company begins trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT tomorrow. The terms locked in for the roadshow call for 825 million shares priced between $70 and $78 each, split between roughly 801 million new shares sold by Anthropic itself and about 24 million sold by existing stockholders cashing out a portion of their stakes. At the top of that range, it is one of the largest technology listings in years; at the bottom, it is still large enough to reshape how public markets price generative AI.

The detail that has drawn the most attention from analysts is not the share count but who is standing behind it. Alongside the public offering, Anthropic has arranged a $26 billion private placement with seven strategic partners, and two of the commitments inside that placement are striking simply for who made them: Nvidia and Alphabet have each pledged $10 billion. Both companies sell Anthropic the infrastructure and cloud capacity its models run on, and both also build competing AI systems of their own, which makes a combined $20 billion vote of confidence from two rivals-cum-suppliers an unusually loaded signal for the rest of the market to read.

Exactly what that signal says about Anthropic's value depends entirely on which estimate you believe. Analyst commentary around the roadshow has ranged from a valuation near $1 trillion at the midpoint of the pricing range to figures exceeding $1.4 trillion once the private placement and strategic commitments are factored in. That spread, on the eve of an actual price being set, says less about Anthropic specifically than about how unresolved valuations remain across the entire AI sector, where revenue multiples that would be considered reckless in any other industry have become the market's working assumption.

Retail investors have been given an unusually generous slice of this one. Up to 20% of the offering has been set aside for retail allocation, a share far higher than most mega-cap technology listings typically offer individual investors, who are usually left bidding for scraps once institutional buyers have taken their fill. Whether that generosity reflects confidence in strong demand or a deliberate attempt to build broad public goodwill around a company whose products are now woven into everyday consumer and enterprise software, is a question the market's reaction over the following days will begin to answer.

What happens when trading opens tomorrow will matter well beyond Anthropic's own balance sheet. A listing of this size, backed by commitments from the companies that sell it chips and compete with it on models, sets a reference point for every AI company still weighing whether to go public, and for every investor trying to work out whether the sector's valuations reflect a genuine platform shift or a financing cycle that has simply not yet been tested by a market downturn.
