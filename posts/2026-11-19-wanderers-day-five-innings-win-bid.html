Five wickets from history: the Proteas chase an innings win to close out Bangladesh

Sport Desk

There is a particular cruelty built into the final day of a Test match that is already lost, and Bangladesh wake up to it this morning at the Wanderers. They resume on 100 for 5, still 57 runs shy of making South Africa bat again, with a surface that has started to turn square and a pace attack that has lost none of its bounce for the bowlers' having already worked through four days of it. South Africa need five wickets. Bangladesh need a miracle they have never once produced in a Test on South African soil: a rearguard draw. Both sides know which of those two outcomes the first session is likely to settle.

The platform for this was built on day three, when Ryan Rickelton's century and a startling, career-best cameo from Kagiso Rabada dragged South Africa's first innings past Bangladesh's 400 and into a 156-run lead the tourists have never looked capable of overturning. Rabada batting like a top-six player who had simply wandered into the wrong changing room was the detail that turned a competitive match into a rout: it is one thing to lose your bowling battle to Simon Harmer and Rabada with the ball, another entirely to lose it to Rabada with the bat as well. Bangladesh's response, 14 unclaimed runs added to their first innings before the ball was even new, barely dented the gap.

What has followed since has been attrition dressed up as cricket. Bangladesh's top order has not so much been dismissed as dismantled, five wickets down with the required runs still not on the board, and the asymmetry of the contest is now total: South Africa are playing for the extra points and scalps an innings win carries, Bangladesh are playing for pride and little else. Litton Das and Mushfiqur Rahim, not out overnight, carry the weight of a lower order that has shown no sign all series of holding out a match South Africa have already decided to win.

The context matters as much as the scoreline. This is the first Test of a two-match series that moves to Centurion next Monday, where Temba Bavuma's 15-man squad — Tony de Zorzi and Zubayr Hamza both retained, Lungi Ngidi still out, Anrich Nortje's fitness still a live question — will try to make it two from two. A result today, by whatever margin, sets the tone for that trip in a way a drawn opener never could have. Harmer's control and Rabada's venom have both players walking into Centurion with exactly the kind of form a home summer is built around.

For Bangladesh, there is a harder conversation waiting once this match ends, whichever way the final session breaks. A touring side that cannot survive a fifth-day pitch against spin and bounce, after collapsing in both this innings and effectively ceding the game by day three, arrives at Centurion already needing to solve problems with technique and temperament that five days at the Wanderers have only exposed further. South Africa, by contrast, get to walk into the second Test with the one thing every home team wants heading into a series decider: the other side already out of answers.
