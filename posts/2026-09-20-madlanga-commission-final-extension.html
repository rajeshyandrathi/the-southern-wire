Ramaphosa grants the Madlanga Commission one more — and reportedly final — extension

Politics Desk·Sunday, 20 September 2026

President Cyril Ramaphosa has granted the Madlanga Commission a third extension, pushing the deadline for its final report to 25 January 2027. Officials have described it as the final extension, though that description has arguably been applied before — and the commission's own history of recesses and expanding scope suggests the process remains far from finished.

The commission, chaired by Justice Mbuyiseli Madlanga alongside advocates Sesi Baloyi SC and Sandile Khumalo SC, is investigating allegations first raised by KwaZulu-Natal Provincial Police Commissioner Lieutenant-General Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi — claims of collusion and corruption reaching into politicians, senior police officers, prosecutors, intelligence operatives and even judges. It is, in other words, not a narrow inquiry, and the scope of what it's being asked to untangle goes a long way toward explaining why its timeline keeps slipping.

The commission went into recess for over a week in early September before resuming public hearings on the 14th, part of a process that has moved in fits and starts as it works through additional evidence and expert testimony relevant to its terms of reference. Each pause and extension has its own procedural justification. Collectively, they've also tested public patience with an inquiry whose findings many South Africans are waiting on as a genuine bellwether for whether accountability at the highest levels of the criminal justice system is actually achievable.

That tension — between doing the investigation properly and doing it within a reasonable timeframe — sits at the heart of how this extension is being received. A Times Live editorial captured the split verdict well this week: for all the rot the commission has surfaced, it has also, in the same breath, shown some of the system working as it should, with witnesses willing to testify and evidence being taken seriously.

With a new deadline now set for January 2027, the commission has roughly four more months to convert testimony into conclusions. Whether that timeline holds, and whether the eventual report produces consequences rather than just documentation, remains the open question hanging over one of the most consequential inquiries South Africa's justice system has undertaken in years.
