DA signs six-party deal for Nelson Mandela Bay, but the mayoral vote slips to Friday

A coalition holding 63 of 120 seats was announced in Gqeberha on Tuesday, but the council’s first sitting was adjourned over late agendas. In Buffalo City, the ANC’s Princess Faku was re-elected mayor.

Politics Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • DA, FF Plus, UDM, ActionSA, ACDP and PAC signed a coalition worth 63 seats, two more than the 61 needed
  • The first sitting was adjourned after councillors said their agendas arrived too late
  • Council reconvenes on Friday, 27 November, at Nelson Mandela Bay Stadium
  • The ANC is using the delay to try to win over the PAC and UDM
  • In Buffalo City, Princess Faku beat the UDM’s Nqabayomzi Kwankwa by 52 votes to 47

Nelson Mandela Bay will have to wait until Friday to learn who its mayor is. The DA and five smaller parties announced a signed coalition agreement on Tuesday, 24 November, but the council’s inaugural sitting was adjourned before a Speaker or mayor could be elected, Daily Maverick and The Herald reported.

The deal brings together the DA, the Freedom Front Plus, the UDM, ActionSA, the ACDP and the PAC. According to Daily Maverick, the DA holds a record 53 seats in the 120-seat council and its partners add 10, for a total of 63. A majority is 61. The Democratic Movement’s single councillor supports the arrangement without formally joining it.

The parties’ joint statement said they intend to govern together for five years, and that residents had lived with the effects of “unstable local government leadership”. The National reported that their first priorities are stabilising water and electricity supply, dealing with sewage spills and restoring basic maintenance, along with tighter financial controls.

The DA’s planned line-up has Retief Odendaal as mayor and the ACDP’s Lance Grootboom as deputy mayor, with the DA’s Marshall von Buchenroder as Speaker and Rano Kayser as chief whip, according to Daily Maverick. Odendaal was the city’s last DA mayor, serving from September 2022 until a motion of no confidence removed him in May 2023, EWN reported.

Why Tuesday’s sitting stalled

Trouble began as councillors prepared to nominate a Speaker. Several objected that they had received the agenda only on Monday or Tuesday, while council rules require five working days’ notice. After about two hours, the presiding officer, Eastern Cape acting deputy director-general for local governance Charity Sihunu, took legal advice and adjourned the meeting until 10:00 on Friday, Daily Maverick reported.

The ANC, which has 32 seats, pushed for the delay. An unnamed ANC insider told Daily Maverick the party needed about a day to persuade the PAC’s national leadership to switch sides. The UDM signed the DA agreement but stayed away from the DA’s briefing because it was still talking to the ANC. Outgoing mayor Babalwa Lobishe said the ANC was confident its numbers were adding up.

DA provincial chair Yusuf Cassim said the agreement stands unless a party formally pulls out. Odendaal accused “certain parties” on Facebook of using “frivolous grounds” to force the postponement, The National reported. ActionSA’s Eastern Cape chair, Athol Trollip, said his party had negotiated over how to fix the metro, not over positions.

Faku returns in Buffalo City

In Buffalo City, the council did elect its leaders on Tuesday. The ANC’s Princess Faku, who chairs the party’s Dr WB Rubusana region, was re-elected executive mayor with 52 votes to 47 for the UDM’s Nqabayomzi Kwankwa, with one spoilt ballot, EWN reported. The ANC holds 42 of the 100 seats and relied on support from the EFF, the Patriotic Alliance and the African Independent Congress, while the SACP backed the UDM.

The National listed Bongiwe Sauli as Speaker, Yomelela Tyali as deputy mayor and Malibongwe Mfazwe as chief whip. EWN spells the Speaker’s surname Saule. Kwankwa indicated he could return to Parliament if the UDM does not secure a working arrangement in the metro.

Friday, 27 November, is the deadline for councils across the country to elect their office-bearers. Nelson Mandela Bay is the one metro outside the Western Cape where the ANC, ActionSA, EFF and MK bloc cannot count on a majority, so Friday’s vote will show whether the DA’s smaller partners hold firm.
