Joburg mops up after flooding storms as fire danger and heat take over in the west and south

Wednesday’s Level 4 storms flooded roads and overtopped bridges in Johannesburg. Today the Weather Service warns of extremely high fire danger on the West Coast and in Namakwa, with the Eastern Cape next on Friday.

Weather Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Wednesday’s storms flooded roads in Johannesburg, and Belgrave Bridge in Bryanston was closed after it overtopped
  • Extremely high fire danger today in Namakwa (Northern Cape) and the Matzikama and Cederberg municipalities (Western Cape)
  • Friday: fire danger moves to Dr Beyers Naudé, Blue Crane Route and Chris Hani in the Eastern Cape
  • Extreme heat on Friday in Beaufort West, Prince Albert, Oudtshoorn and parts of the Eastern Cape, with humid heat along the Eastern Cape coast
  • Gauteng expects only isolated showers and thundershowers today, with an extreme UV index

Johannesburg is cleaning up after a second day of severe storms. Wednesday’s Yellow Level 4 warning for Gauteng proved accurate: heavy rain flooded streets and overtopped bridges across Johannesburg and Ekurhuleni, The Citizen reported. The Johannesburg Roads Agency closed Belgrave Bridge in Bryanston after water poured over it.

Flooding was reported on roads including the M1 North near Corlett Drive, Marlboro Drive in Kelvin, the R55 in Midrand, the N12 near Daveyton and Joe Slovo Drive in Doornfontein. In Alexandra, roads were under water, and there were unconfirmed reports of cars being swept away. At Parkview Golf Club, floodwater washed away two bridges. Joburg ETC reported thick hail covering the ground in Brakpan. No injuries or deaths were reported in the sources we checked.

The Johannesburg Metro Police warned that even shallow-looking water can hide washed-away roads, potholes and open manholes, and asked people not to drive or walk through flooded bridges or underpasses.

Fire danger on the West Coast today

The weather pattern now changes. In its forecast for Thursday, 26 November, the South African Weather Service warns of extremely high fire danger in the Namakwa District of the Northern Cape and in the Matzikama and Cederberg municipalities on the Western Cape’s West Coast, The Citizen reported. This is new since our Wednesday report.

The heat advisory we reported on Wednesday is still in place. Extremely hot and uncomfortable conditions are expected today in Nama Khoi, Kamiesberg, Khâi-Ma and Hantam in the Northern Cape, and in Matzikama and Cederberg. Most of the Northern Cape and Western Cape will be fine and hot to very hot, with a very high UV index in the Western Cape.

Elsewhere the storms ease. Gauteng should be cloudy and cool to warm with isolated showers and thundershowers, and an extreme UV index. Limpopo, Mpumalanga, North West and the Free State can expect isolated showers and thundershowers, while KwaZulu-Natal has morning and evening fog over the central and western interior.

The Eastern Cape on Friday

On Friday, 27 November, the fire danger moves east. The Weather Service expects extremely high fire danger in the Dr Beyers Naudé and Blue Crane Route local municipalities and the Chris Hani District in the Eastern Cape.

The heat spreads too. Besides Beaufort West, Prince Albert and Oudtshoorn in the Western Cape, which we reported on Wednesday, extreme heat is now also expected in the Sarah Baartman District, Inxuba Yethemba and the Amathole District. Hot and humid weather will be extremely uncomfortable along parts of the Eastern Cape coast and nearby interior.
