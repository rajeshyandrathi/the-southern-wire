No more rotation: Erasmus sends out a near full-strength Springbok side to open the Nations Championship's second phase

Sport Desk

The experimentation is over. Having used last weekend's warm-up to blood uncapped talent and rest frontline names, Rassie Erasmus has reverted to something close to his first-choice Springbok side for today's Nations Championship fixture against Italy in Turin, a clear signal that this phase of the autumn campaign is being treated as business rather than preparation. The bench named alongside the run-on side includes Jan-Hendrik Wessels, Gerhard Steenekamp, Wilco Louw, Lood de Jager, Cameron Hanekom, Paul de Villiers, Grant Williams and Manie Libbok, a mix of proven Test-match impact players rather than the development prospects given game time a week earlier.

The shift in selection philosophy reflects what is now at stake. Saturday's match opens the second phase of the Nations Championship, the format that has replaced the traditional November international calendar with something closer to a structured league table, and results here carry weight toward seedings and prestige that a loosely-structured autumn tour never did. Erasmus, never shy about using the earliest fixtures of a northern-hemisphere swing to test squad depth, has clearly decided that the calculus changes once the matches start to count toward something more permanent.

Some of the squad's build-up has had an unusually international flavour even by Springbok standards. Several Japan-based players linked to the squad reached Italy ahead of the rest of the group, a reminder of how thoroughly globalised the modern Springbok player pool has become, with contracts in Japan's League One now a normal part of the career path for established internationals rather than the retirement-home posting it was once seen as. Managing those players' travel and conditioning schedules around a tight Test calendar has become one of the quieter logistical challenges Erasmus's management team now handles every November.

Italy, for their part, arrive with the kind of improving reputation that makes this fixture less of a formality than it would have been a decade ago. The Azzurri have beaten high-ranked opposition with increasing regularity in recent seasons, and a Springbok side that takes Turin lightly has been punished for it before. Erasmus's team talk in the build-up has reportedly emphasised exactly that history, framing today's match less as a routine away fixture and more as the first real test of whether this phase of the Nations Championship campaign starts on the right foot.

For South African fans, the appeal of a settled, experienced side needs no explanation: after a year in which injuries and rotation have made it hard to watch the same Springbok team twice, today's fixture offers something closer to the real thing. What it produces on the scoreboard in Turin will set the tone for the tougher assignments against France and Ireland still to come in this Nations Championship cycle, against opponents who will be paying very close attention to how this settled side performs under the new format's pressure.
