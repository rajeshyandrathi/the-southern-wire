Governance strains surface across municipalities

Politics Desk·Saturday, 19 September 2026

A cluster of stories from municipalities around the country this week point to a common thread: local government accountability structures are under real strain, even as national political attention stays fixed elsewhere. In Ekurhuleni, the municipal manager was dismissed following his implication in multiple criminal cases, a move that removes one of the metro's most senior administrators mid-term.

In Nelson Mandela Bay, the picture is murkier. A suspended city manager there continues to draw a salary understood to run into the millions of rand while a disciplinary process meant to resolve his status remains stalled, apparently for procedural reasons rather than any resolution on the underlying allegations. Ratepayers in the metro have grown increasingly vocal about the cost of a suspension process that produces no outcome.

Party politics is adding its own layer of friction. In the Eastern Cape, the ANC's provincial secretary has threatened legal action over how the party's candidate list was compiled, a dispute that, if it escalates, could complicate the party's positioning ahead of future local contests in the province.

None of these stories individually would dominate a news cycle, but together they describe a pattern that has become familiar in South African local government: administrative dysfunction that persists not because problems go unnoticed, but because the mechanisms meant to resolve them — disciplinary hearings, internal party processes, oversight committees — move too slowly to keep pace with the problems themselves.
