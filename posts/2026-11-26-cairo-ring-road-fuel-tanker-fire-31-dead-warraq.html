Fuel tanker fire on Cairo’s Ring Road kills at least 31

A tanker overturned and burst into flames in Giza’s crowded Warraq district on Wednesday evening, engulfing more than two dozen vehicles. Egypt’s health minister warned the toll could still rise.

World Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • At least 31 people were killed and 21 injured when a fuel tanker crashed and caught fire on Cairo’s Ring Road on Wednesday evening, the Health Ministry said
  • The fire hit a stretch of highway in Warraq, a densely populated district of Giza, and burnt more than two dozen cars and motorcycles
  • State media reported that a high-voltage cable fell in front of the tanker and the driver lost control; prosecutors are investigating
  • DNA tests are under way to identify the dead, and the health minister called the toll preliminary
  • Egypt loses thousands of people on its roads every year; in January a pile-up near Beni Suef killed 21

At least 31 people were killed and 21 injured when a fuel tanker crashed and burst into flames on Cairo’s Ring Road on Wednesday evening, 25 November, Egypt’s Health Ministry said. The fire spread across a stretch of the highway in Warraq, a densely populated district in Giza on the western edge of the capital, and swept through the vehicles stuck around the tanker.

More than two dozen cars and motorcycles were burnt, the Associated Press reported. Rescue workers pulled people from burning cars while firefighters fought to stop the blaze spreading, and hundreds of people gathered near the scene. One driver caught in the traffic told AP he saw the tanker and many cars on fire, and that “the cars got stuck together because of the fire”.

The toll rose through the evening. Early reports from the Health Ministry, carried by The National, put the number of dead at 21 with 15 injured; later figures raised that to at least 31 dead. Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar said the count was preliminary and could rise further.

What caused the crash

Egyptian state media reported that a high-voltage power cable fell into the path of the tanker and that the driver lost control, according to reports summarised by allAfrica and Globe TV. The tanker belonged to a private contractor, allAfrica reported. Officials have not yet published a full account, and the regional public prosecutor has opened an investigation.

The National placed the fire above the Ten Tunnel in Warraq and said 39 ambulances took part in the rescue. The injured were taken to Zayed Specialised, Imbaba General and Warraq Central hospitals, and the minister ordered hospitals in the area to raise their readiness. By Thursday morning the wreckage had been cleared and the road reopened, allAfrica reported.

The public prosecutor’s office said DNA testing was under way to identify the dead, many of whom were badly burnt. President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi sent his condolences to the families of the victims in a statement.

A long record of deadly roads

Egypt has a poor road-safety record, with thousands of people killed on its roads every year. AP noted that speeding, poorly maintained roads and weak enforcement of traffic laws are the main causes. In January this year, 21 people were killed and 16 injured in a multi-vehicle pile-up near the southern province of Beni Suef.

The Ring Road encircles Cairo and runs past crowded neighbourhoods such as Warraq, a working and middle-class area on the west bank of the Nile, The National noted. When a loaded fuel tanker overturns in heavy traffic, burning fuel can spread across several lanes before drivers have time to get clear, which is why so many vehicles were caught on Wednesday.

Investigators will now look at how the cable came down and whether the tanker and its load met safety rules. Families of the dead were waiting for identification results on Thursday, and the Health Ministry said it would keep updating the casualty figures.
