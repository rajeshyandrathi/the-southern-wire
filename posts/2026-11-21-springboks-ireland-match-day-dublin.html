Match day in Dublin: the Boks need to bounce back from Paris, and Ireland need a win to reach the final

South Africa face Ireland at the Aviva Stadium at 6.40pm SA time, a week after losing 21-19 to France. Eben Etzebeth returns to win his 148th cap, and the result could decide who plays in the Nations Championship final.

Sport Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Kick-off 4.40pm in Dublin, 6.40pm in South Africa, at the Aviva Stadium
  • Referee is Christophe Ridley of England
  • Five changes to the starting team that lost 21-19 to France, with Eben Etzebeth back for a record 148th cap
  • South Africa top the southern hemisphere table on 21 points but New Zealand are unbeaten and close behind

The Springboks play their last match of the Nations Championship pool stage in Dublin this evening, and they arrive with something to fix. Last Friday France beat them 21-19 at the Stade de France, France’s first home win over South Africa since 2005 and the Boks’ first defeat of the competition. Coach Rassie Erasmus did not dress it up. “We let ourselves and the country down,” he said this week.

Kick-off at the Aviva Stadium is at 4.40pm Irish time, which is 6.40pm in South Africa. England’s Christophe Ridley will referee, with Angus Gardner of Australia and Anthony Woodthorpe of England as his assistants and Ian Tempest as television match official.

Erasmus has made five changes to the starting team and seven on the bench. The most important is in the second row, where Eben Etzebeth returns to extend his record as South Africa’s most-capped player to 148 Tests. Cameron Hanekom starts at number eight, and the first-choice front row of Ox Nche, Malcolm Marx and Wilco Louw is back together.

The team

South Africa: Damian Willemse; Kurt-Lee Arendse, Jesse Kriel, Damian de Allende, Ethan Hooker; Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu, Morne van den Berg; Cameron Hanekom, Pieter-Steph du Toit, Siya Kolisi (captain); Ruan Nortje, Eben Etzebeth; Wilco Louw, Malcolm Marx, Ox Nche.

Replacements: Jan-Hendrik Wessels, Gerhard Steenekamp, Thomas du Toit, Lood de Jager, Paul de Villiers, Marco van Staden, Grant Williams, Canan Moodie. Cobus Reinach and Manie Libbok, who both played in Paris, are not in the matchday 23, and number eight Jasper Wiese is missing after his red card against France.

Paris was a game of fine margins. France led 21-7 at half-time after tries by Aaron Grandidier-Nkanang, Theo Attissogbe and Louis Bielle-Biarrey, and a try by Arendse was ruled out for offside just before the break. The Boks fought back with tries by Wessels, Reinach and Marx and won four scrum penalties in a row late on, but a missed conversion with about seven minutes left proved costly.

What is at stake

The Nations Championship ranks the northern and southern hemisphere teams in separate tables, with the top sides meeting in a final. After Paris, France top the northern table on 21 points and South Africa top the southern table, also on 21. New Zealand are unbeaten, and Daily Maverick warned this week that the Boks were in danger of missing the final if they slip again.

Ireland are still in the running for the final too, alongside England and France, so neither side can afford to ease off. Erasmus said it would take an accurate 80-minute performance against a team that always gives everything against South Africa.

Ireland have had the better of this fixture in recent years and are hard to beat at home, so the Boks will need to be sharper than they were in Paris. A win would keep South Africa’s hopes of a final in their own hands. The result will be known by about 8.30pm SA time.
