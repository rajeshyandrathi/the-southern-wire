A Cape Town fintech bets $4.6 million that stablecoins are South Africa's next payments frontier

Tech Desk·Tuesday, 3 November 2026

South African fintech WalaPay has raised $4.6 million in fresh funding to expand its stablecoin-based payments platform, joining a small but growing cluster of African financial technology firms betting that dollar-pegged digital tokens can solve cross-border payment problems that conventional banking rails have struggled with for years. The round positions WalaPay to scale a service aimed squarely at the friction points that have long made moving money across African borders slower and more expensive than it should be.

Stablecoins — cryptocurrency tokens designed to track the value of a reference asset, typically the US dollar, rather than fluctuate like bitcoin or ether — have found an unlikely niche use case in African cross-border commerce and remittances, where traditional correspondent banking can take days and layer on fees that eat meaningfully into smaller transactions. For businesses and individuals moving money between African currencies that are themselves volatile against the dollar, a stable intermediary asset settled near-instantly on blockchain rails offers a genuine efficiency gain over the status quo.

The raise lands amid a broader, if uneven, rebound in African fintech funding. Continental startups have pulled in just over $2 billion across the first nine months of 2026, trailing slightly behind the same period last year but outperforming the sharper funding droughts seen across the sector in 2023 and 2024. South African fintechs specifically have drawn a wave of recent interest, from earned-wage-access platforms to card-linked instalment lenders, suggesting investors still see the local payments and lending landscape as underserved relative to its market size.

Regulatory clarity remains the biggest swing factor for any South African stablecoin business. The South African Reserve Bank and the Financial Sector Conduct Authority have moved cautiously but steadily toward formalising crypto-asset oversight in recent years, and how that framework eventually treats stablecoin-settled payments specifically could determine whether WalaPay's bet pays off at genuine scale or remains a niche product for a narrow set of use cases.

For now, the funding gives WalaPay runway to prove the model works at a meaningfully larger volume than it has operated at so far — the real test for any payments startup, long after the initial pitch deck has done its job of attracting capital.
