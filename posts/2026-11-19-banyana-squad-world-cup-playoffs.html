Banyana Banyana name their World Cup playoff squad — and a WAFCON heartbreak is still the backdrop

Sport Desk

Desiree Ellis has had a difficult few months to coach her way through, and it shows in the squad she has just named. Twenty-three players will travel to Rabat for the FIFA Women's World Cup play-offs running from 28 November to 5 December, a tournament South Africa should never really have needed to enter. Automatic qualification was there for the taking at this year's Women's Africa Cup of Nations, until Banyana Banyana lost 2-1 to hosts Morocco in the quarter-final and watched a route to 2027 close in front of a crowd that had expected a very different outcome.

That defeat sits underneath everything about this squad announcement. Ellis has recalled veteran midfielder Refiloe Jane, 34, leaning on exactly the kind of composure and big-match nous a team still working through the sting of a home-soil exit needs in its engine room. The coach has been candid about how hard the selection itself was — injuries, passport complications and players' academic commitments all narrowed her options in ways that had nothing to do with form or merit, forcing late changes to a group she would otherwise have settled on weeks ago.

The format ahead is unforgiving in its simplicity. South Africa face Chinese Taipei, Ecuador and Uzbekistan in a preliminary round-robin phase, and only the top two sides advance to a further playoff round in February 2027 that will decide the last places at the World Cup itself. There is no margin for the kind of slow start that cost Banyana Banyana their continental qualification; every match in Rabat effectively carries knockout weight, compressed into little more than a week of football.

What makes this squad worth watching closely is less the individual names than the psychology Ellis has to manage. This is a group that has tasted real continental success in recent years — a reminder of just how far South African women's football has climbed — and now has to prove that the WAFCON exit was an aberration rather than a sign of a wider slide. Jane's recall, and the emphasis Ellis has placed on experience over raw form in these selections, suggests a coach deliberately prioritising composure under pressure over everything else this time around.

Morocco, fittingly, is where the wound was opened, and Morocco is now where the recovery has to begin. A squad this experienced, this battle-tested, and this aware of exactly what slipped away in the WAFCON quarter-final arrives in Rabat with something to prove rather than merely a ticket to punch — and for Ellis, getting that balance of hunger and composure right over nine days of football may matter more than any single tactical tweak she makes.
