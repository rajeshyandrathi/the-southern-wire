Pretoria pushes revised offer to cut 30% US tariff

Business Desk·Saturday, 19 September 2026

South Africa's cabinet has signed off on an enhanced trade proposal aimed at chipping away at the 30% tariff Washington has applied to South African exports since last year. Trade officials say the revised documents are being couriered to Washington immediately, part of a renewed push to get relief before the tariff does further damage to export-reliant sectors.

The stakes are significant. Government estimates put roughly 30,000 jobs at risk across the automotive and agricultural sectors alone, industries that depend heavily on preferential access to the US market to remain competitive. Exporters in these sectors have been quietly absorbing margin pressure since the tariff took effect, and many say they cannot sustain that indefinitely without either relief or a pivot to other markets.

That pivot is already underway in parallel. Pretoria has been courting alternative buyers in China and Japan, part of a broader diversification strategy that predates the tariff dispute but has taken on new urgency because of it. Trade analysts describe this as a hedge rather than a substitute — the US remains too large a market to walk away from, but reducing dependence on any single trading partner has become a stated policy priority.

Whether the revised offer moves the needle in Washington remains to be seen. Previous rounds of engagement produced incremental progress at best, and officials on both sides have signalled that a full resolution is unlikely to come quickly. For now, the government's approach is to keep pressing on multiple fronts at once: formal trade diplomacy, market diversification, and domestic support measures for the industries most exposed.
