ActionSA tells the DA it won't accept Helen Zille as Johannesburg's mayor — and the clock is already running

The Southern Wire Desk·Johannesburg

As the Independent Electoral Commission moves to formalise last week's results on Sunday, Johannesburg's coalition arithmetic has produced exactly the standoff the city's political class had been dreading. Provisional figures give the DA 35.98% of the vote, well short of a majority, with ActionSA a distant second at 16.2%. On paper that should make ActionSA a natural junior partner. In practice, the party has used that leverage to issue an ultimatum: it will not help install Helen Zille as mayor, and if the DA insists on her, ActionSA says it would rather see the city's biggest council stay ungoverned than hand her the chains of office.

The demand that has emerged instead is for ActionSA's own leader, Herman Mashaba, a former Johannesburg mayor himself, to take the post in any coalition arrangement. For the DA, that is a hard sell. Zille remains one of the party's most recognisable figures nationally, and benching her in favour of a rival party's leader would be read by its own base as a capitulation rather than a compromise. Party insiders have so far publicly pushed back on the ActionSA demand rather than entertaining it, setting up a negotiation where neither side has an obvious path to retreat without looking like it lost.

The stakes are sharpened by the calendar. Once the IEC's declaration is official, the Municipal Structures Act starts a strict countdown for the new council to hold its first sitting and elect a speaker and a mayor. Johannesburg has lived through this exact scenario before, cycling through a rotating cast of mayors during the last term as shifting coalitions collapsed under their own contradictions, and both ActionSA and the DA know that a repeat would be blamed squarely on whichever party the public decides was being the more intransigent.

That leaves the ANC, bruised everywhere else in this election but still numerically necessary in a council this fragmented, in an unusually comfortable position. Neither the DA nor ActionSA can assemble a majority without either the ANC's support or its abstention, and a party that spent the campaign being written off as a diminished force now finds itself able to extract concessions simply by waiting. Smaller parties holding a handful of seats each are watching the same dynamic and positioning themselves for whatever a prolonged stalemate might be worth to them.

What happens over the coming days will test whether Johannesburg's political parties learned anything from the instability of the last five years, or whether the city is about to relearn the same lesson from scratch. For residents who have watched service delivery falter through successive changes of mayor, the question is no longer really about Zille or Mashaba specifically, but about whether any coalition assembled this way can actually hold together long enough to govern.
