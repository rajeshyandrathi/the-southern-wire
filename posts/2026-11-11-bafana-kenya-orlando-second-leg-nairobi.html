Bafana's old striker is now Kenya's coach, and Friday's AFCON qualifier is personal

Sport Desk

Pitso Mosimane's Bafana Bafana go into Friday's AFCON qualifier against Kenya at Orlando Amstel Arena sitting exactly where they want to be: top of Group D, unbeaten so far in the campaign, and defending that position on home soil before a tighter second leg in Nairobi five days later. The qualifying run started with a 2-1 win over Guinea at the same venue back in September, Ime Okon opening the scoring before Samukele Kabini settled it with a 77th-minute winner, and South Africa have carried that early momentum into the double-header against the Harambee Stars.

What gives Friday's match its edge is the man in Kenya's dugout. Benni McCarthy, one of the most prolific strikers South African football has produced, is now the coach trying to beat his own country on its own turf, and he has not been shy about the task ahead of him — vowing publicly that Kenya will challenge Bafana rather than simply absorb pressure at Orlando. For a coach who built his name scoring goals in a Bafana shirt, there is an obvious irony in trying to end his old team's unbeaten start.

The format works against the away side in theory and for drama in practice. South Africa will have home advantage and a vocal Soweto crowd for the first leg, then travel to Nairobi just five days later for the return fixture — a short turnaround that leaves little time to recover from travel, let alone correct any mistakes made at Orlando. Mosimane's side will want the tie effectively settled by the time they board the flight to Kenya.

Ticket pricing for Friday has been set deliberately low, at R100 for adults and R40 for children, a decision that reads as much as a statement about filling Orlando Stadium as it does about accessibility — South African football's administrators have learned, through painful experience with half-empty qualifiers in the past, that AFCON campaigns live or die on the atmosphere fans provide as much as on results.

Group D's table makes the stakes plain without needing embellishment: a win on Friday all but secures Bafana's position heading into the return leg, while a slip gives Kenya and McCarthy exactly the opening they are looking for to turn an away tie into a genuine contest. For a federation that has had more than its share of qualifying heartbreak in recent cycles, protecting an unbeaten start against the country's own former hero is this week's entire assignment.
