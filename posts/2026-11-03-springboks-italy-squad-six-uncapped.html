Rassie bloods six newcomers but benches them all for the Springboks' Italy opener

Sport Desk·Tuesday, 3 November 2026

Rassie Erasmus has named a full-strength Springbok side for Saturday's opening Test of the November tour against Italy in Turin, confirming that six uncapped players included in the wider squad will have to wait at least one more week for their international debuts. It is a selection that signals exactly how seriously South Africa is treating the first fixture of a gruelling three-match European swing that continues against France in Paris and Ireland in Dublin.

The Springboks arrive in Turin sitting top of the Southern Hemisphere standings, locked on points with New Zealand but holding the number one ranking by virtue of a superior points difference — a margin thin enough that Erasmus has little appetite for experimentation against an Italian side that has steadily closed the gap on the game's traditional powers in recent seasons. Four players from South Africa's World Cup-winning core have been left out of this opening Test entirely, part of a now-familiar rotation strategy designed to manage bodies across a long, physically brutal end-of-year tour.

The six uncapped players named in the squad, even sitting out the Italy match, represent Erasmus's continued willingness to blood fresh talent even at the peak of a World Cup cycle, a policy that has repeatedly paid off in depth when injuries have struck during previous tours. Their moment is likely to come against either France or Ireland, assuming results in Turin go as comfortably as South Africa's ranking suggests they should.

Hovering over the French leg of the tour is a refereeing subplot that has needled Springbok fans since South Africa's narrow 42-38 defeat to Australia earlier this year: Andrea Piardi, whose calls in that match remain a sore point in Bok circles, has been appointed to officiate the Paris Test. Erasmus has so far declined to be drawn into controversy over the appointment, focusing his public comments squarely on preparation for Italy.

A results-first approach to this November series would see South Africa banking points in Turin before the tour's true tests begin in Paris and Dublin — fixtures that will say far more about where this Springbok side stands heading into next year than anything likely to happen against Italy this weekend.
