Airlines are already stacking on seats for a festive season that starts earlier every year

Lifestyle Desk·Monday, 2 November 2026

It's still only the first week of November, but South Africa's festive travel season is already being built out in flight schedules and seat counts. Emirates has announced fourteen additional flights between Dubai and Durban spanning December 2026 and January 2027, a capacity boost aimed squarely at the stretch of the calendar when demand for that route reliably outstrips what the regular schedule can comfortably absorb.

The timing reflects a travel pattern that has become one of the most dependable features of the South African calendar: a long, concentrated surge of both outbound holidaymakers and inbound diaspora and international visitors converging on the country's airports in a window that effectively runs from mid-December through to early January. Airlines that get their capacity planning wrong in either direction either leave money on the table or leave passengers stranded — which is exactly why route announcements like this one tend to land as early as they do.

Domestic carriers are bracing for the same surge from a different angle. Local airlines have previously flagged jumps of as much as 40% in festive-season passenger volumes compared with quieter months, with the peak travel window running from mid-December into the first weeks of the new year. That kind of swing puts real pressure on everything from check-in queues to baggage handling, and airports typically respond with extended hours and additional staffing to absorb it.

Banking data from the previous festive season told a more nuanced story about how South Africans actually travel in December than the simple "everyone goes away" narrative suggests. Rather than skipping the season or cutting back, spending patterns showed people adjusting when and how they travelled — shifting dates, choosing different destinations, or spacing trips differently — without abandoning the tradition of a December getaway altogether. It's a reminder that cost-of-living pressure changes travel habits more often than it cancels them outright.

With just over six weeks left before the peak window opens, the early moves from international carriers like Emirates suggest the industry is backing a strong season regardless of the broader economic pressures weighing on household budgets. For travellers hoping to lock in seats before prices climb further, the message from the airlines is now unmistakable: the early planning window is already open, even if the holidays themselves still feel a long way off.
