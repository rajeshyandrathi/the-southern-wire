Crime & Courts

A fourth arrest, a hitman charge, and the Knysna assassination case finally finds a shape

Crime Desk

Nearly three months after former Knysna ward councillor and executive mayor Aubrey Tsengwa was shot dead in his car in Joodsekamp, investigators have made the arrest that has done the most to clarify who they believe actually pulled the trigger. A 44-year-old man, picked up in an operation in Peddie in the Eastern Cape, has been added to the case as the alleged hitman, facing one count of murder and three counts of attempted murder. He appeared in the Knysna Magistrates' Court this week and is expected before the Mossel Bay court separately on unrelated murder charges.

The arrest was carried out by the Political Killings Task Team, the specialised unit that has spent years working through South Africa's backlog of politically linked assassinations, and its involvement here is itself a signal of how the case is being read. Tsengwa was a sitting ward councillor at the time he was killed in August, and the task team's focus on the file from early on reflected suspicion that the shooting was connected to local political or municipal disputes rather than ordinary criminality.

This is now the fourth person linked to the killing to pass through a courtroom, after three other men, aged in their thirties and forties, were arrested and first appeared in court within days of the shooting in August. The case against one of those earlier suspects has since been provisionally withdrawn by the state, while charges against the other two continue to move forward. The newest suspect's arrest, with an alleged hitman now named and charged, gives prosecutors their clearest working theory yet of how the killing was carried out, even as the question of who ordered it remains unanswered in public.

Knysna's municipal politics have been under strain for months, and Tsengwa's death landed in the middle of that tension. Provincial officials welcomed the earlier arrests as evidence that police were taking the case seriously, language they are likely to repeat now that a suspected shooter has been formally charged. But for a council and a town still absorbing the loss of a sitting councillor to an apparent contract killing, an arrest is a step toward accountability, not the end of the story.

What happens next will test how far this prosecution can actually reach. Alleged hitmen are, by the nature of the crime, rarely the people with the most to answer for; the harder work for the Political Killings Task Team is tracing the money and the motive back to whoever commissioned the killing in the first place. Three suspects already in the system and a fourth now charged as the alleged shooter gives investigators more to work with than they had in August, but it does not yet tell Knysna who wanted Aubrey Tsengwa dead, or why.
