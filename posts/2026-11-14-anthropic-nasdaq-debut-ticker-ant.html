Anthropic sets November 19 as its Nasdaq date, with Google, Nvidia and TSMC already in as anchors

Tech Desk

What was a price range a day ago is now a date. Anthropic's amended filing with US regulators sets 19 November as the target for its Nasdaq debut, trading under the ticker ANT on the exchange's Global Select Market, and fixes an indicative price range of $70 to $78 a share across 825 million shares on offer. For a company whose IPO timeline had drifted across several quarters of speculation, the specificity of an actual trading date marks the point where this listing stops being a rumour and starts being a transaction.

What has changed most since the price range alone was known is the list of investors willing to commit before the stock opens. Google, Nvidia and TSMC have all been named as cornerstone investors in the offering, three of the handful of companies whose participation in a listing functions as a signal to every other investor watching: the infrastructure and chip supply chain that AI companies depend on is willing to put its own capital behind this one specifically, rather than simply selling into the broader AI boom from the sidelines.

The filing also discloses that Anthropic will list as a "controlled company" under Nasdaq's corporate governance rules, a designation that exempts it from some of the board independence requirements that apply to widely held public companies. That status is common among technology companies whose founders or early investors retain concentrated voting power after listing, and it tells prospective shareholders something concrete about how much influence they will actually have over the company's direction once they own a piece of it.

Valuation estimates attached to the offering have varied by hundreds of billions of dollars depending on the source, with figures ranging from roughly $1.3 trillion to $1.4 trillion depending on how the final pricing lands within the indicated range. That spread is itself a reminder of how unsettled the market's view of frontier AI company valuations remains, even for a company with the kind of institutional backing Anthropic has secured ahead of its debut.

None of this guarantees a smooth first day of trading. Markets have been choppy for technology stocks more broadly in recent weeks, and a listing of this size inevitably invites scrutiny of whether the price reflects genuine revenue and usage fundamentals or simply the premium currently attached to anything adjacent to artificial intelligence. What is no longer in question is whether the listing happens. Barring a last-minute reversal, 19 November now has a date attached to it that the market can actually trade against.
