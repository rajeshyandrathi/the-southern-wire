DRC's crisis deepens as M23 tightens its grip and Ebola disrupts the new school year

World Desk·Sunday, 20 September 2026

The Democratic Republic of Congo is facing a compounding crisis rarely captured in a single headline: an active Ebola outbreak spreading across six provinces, an ongoing conflict that has seen M23 rebels seize and hold major cities including Goma and Bukavu, and, arriving in the middle of both, millions of students returning to school for a new academic year that almost nothing about the current moment makes easy.

The rebel presence in Goma and Bukavu has moved well beyond military control into de facto governance, with M23 now reportedly imposing fees on residents, local businesses, transporters and even humanitarian organisations operating in areas under its control. That shift — from occupying force to functioning, fee-collecting authority — signals a conflict that has settled into something more entrenched than a temporary military campaign, with implications for how long any political resolution might realistically take.

Layered on top of the conflict, the Ebola outbreak adds a public health emergency to a humanitarian situation already stretched thin. An epidemic spreading across six provinces during the same period that schools are reopening creates exactly the conditions health officials dread most: population movement, crowded classrooms, and limited healthcare infrastructure in areas where conflict has already degraded basic services.

For a country this size and this central to regional stability, a crisis unfolding on three fronts simultaneously — military, medical and educational — is more than a series of unfortunate coincidences. It's a snapshot of how quickly a fragile state can be pulled in multiple directions at once, with ordinary Congolese families left navigating rebel checkpoints, disease containment measures and a fragile school system all in the same difficult season.
