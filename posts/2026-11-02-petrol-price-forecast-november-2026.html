Motorists may finally get a break at the pump as petrol price set for a dip

Business Desk·Monday, 2 November 2026

For the first time since July, South African motorists are looking at a fuel price cut rather than another increase. Wednesday's adjustment, which resets inland 95 unleaded from its current R30.25 a litre, is now tracking toward a decline of roughly 33 to 49 cents a litre depending on grade — a modest number on its own, but a genuinely welcome reversal after a run of increases that had squeezed household budgets through the back half of the year.

The shift didn't happen by accident. Daily under-recovery data — the running tally that determines how much the price needs to move — has come in far softer than expected over the past fortnight, swinging projections that had pointed toward increases as large as R2 a litre just weeks ago into the opposite direction entirely. It is a reminder of how volatile these monthly calculations can be, and how quickly a forecast that looked locked in can flip in the final stretch before the Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources publishes the official number.

Government has also lent a hand. Treasury's mid-term budget policy statement last month included fuel levy relief specifically designed to cushion consumers, with a second, larger phase of relief — R1.50 a litre for diesel and R1 for petrol — set to kick in from 1 December and run through to early January. Coming into effect just as the festive season travel period begins, the timing looks deliberate: a government under pressure on cost-of-living issues choosing to let relief land exactly when households are filling up for long-distance trips home.

Whether a few cents off the pump price actually registers with households already weighed down by months of accumulated increases is a separate question. Fuel costs ripple through almost everything else in the economy — transport, food logistics, the cost of getting goods from port to shelf — and a single month's relief does little to undo the cumulative squeeze of a year in which petrol has, more often than not, gone up rather than down.

Still, after a string of months where South Africans braced for bad news every time a new fuel forecast landed, Wednesday offers something different: an adjustment that, for once, is expected to move in the right direction. Confirmation from the Department remains the final word, but the trajectory of the data over the past two weeks leaves little doubt about which way this one is headed.
