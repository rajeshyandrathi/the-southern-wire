Lifestyle

Cape Town keeps all eight Blue Flags. The real story is the four chasing a ninth.

Lifestyle Desk

Eight is becoming a habit. For a fifth consecutive season, Cape Town has retained full Blue Flag status on eight of its beaches, the gold standard international accreditation that checks everything from water quality and lifeguard coverage to environmental management and beach safety signage. In a summer when the city's tourism machine needs every point of differentiation it can get, eight unbroken Blue Flags is the kind of unglamorous statistic that quietly does a lot of marketing work on its own.

But the more interesting number sits just behind the headline: four additional Cape Town beaches have been named pilot sites for 2026/27, the probationary status WESSA uses before a beach is trusted with full accreditation. Pilot sites are assessed against the same criteria as full-status beaches but without the multi-season track record regulators want before making the designation permanent. Which four beaches cross that line first will say a lot about where the City is investing in coastal maintenance beyond the usual showpieces.

Elsewhere on the coast, Nelson Mandela Bay held its own three Blue Flag beaches, a result that matters more in Gqeberha than the number suggests. The Eastern Cape's tourism pitch has long struggled against the perception that its beaches are an afterthought next to the Cape's, and three consecutive-season retentions give the metro's tourism office something concrete to point to.

KwaZulu-Natal's own list matters just as much given how central beach tourism is to Durban's summer economy, and how aggressively Durban's municipality has leaned into Blue Flag status as shorthand for "safe to swim" after a string of sewage-related water quality scares in recent years.

None of this moves the needle on its own — a flag on a pole does not fill a hotel room. But as South Africa's domestic and inbound tourism numbers both post real growth this year, the beaches doing the accrediting work are the ones that convert a scroll through holiday listings into an actual booking. Eight flags, four more waiting in the wings: Cape Town's coastline is not resting on its reputation this season, it is quietly trying to expand it.
