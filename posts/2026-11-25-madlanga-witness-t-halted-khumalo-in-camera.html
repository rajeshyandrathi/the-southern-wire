Madlanga commission halts drug-syndicate witness over safety fears as crime intelligence boss testifies behind closed doors

Witness T was due to describe a heroin network he alleges is protected by police and prosecutors, but the hearing was adjourned after two security sweeps. Lt-Gen Dumisani Khumalo, who is claiming R28m from the state, testifies in private today.

Crime Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Justice Mbuyiseli Madlanga adjourned on Tuesday before Witness T began, citing security concerns
  • Witness T’s untested affidavit alleges a whoonga syndicate earning at least R1 million a day
  • Lt-Gen Dumisani Khumalo gives evidence in camera on Wednesday, 25 November
  • Khumalo and three generals are claiming R112m from the state over withdrawn Idac charges
  • Open hearings are expected to resume on Thursday

The Madlanga commission’s much-anticipated protected witness never took the stand on Tuesday, 24 November. Witness T was due to begin testifying at 09:30, but commission chair Justice Mbuyiseli Madlanga adjourned the hearing because of concerns about the witness’s safety, The Witness and eNCA reported.

Security had been visibly tightened at the Brigitte Mabandla Justice College auditorium in Pretoria, where the commission sits. A sweep was carried out after 07:00, and at about 08:45 journalists and members of the public were asked to leave so a second sweep could be done.

Madlanga told the room the commission had not yet decided how best to deal with the situation, and would not say when it would return. Daily Maverick later reported that the testimony had been postponed to a date still to be set.

On Monday, we reported that Witness T was due to follow Witness C, whose evidence linked a suspended Johannesburg metro police officer to alleged hitmen. Witness T’s evidence has now been delayed.

What Witness T’s affidavit alleges

A Monday order by Madlanga bars publication of anything that could identify Witness T. He had been due to testify remotely, with his face hidden and his voice distorted. His redacted affidavit, reported by The Witness and Daily Maverick, describes a syndicate wholesaling whoonga, a low-grade heroin, which he says is imported from Afghanistan, Tanzania and China.

He alleges the network operates behind seemingly legitimate businesses, and that its leading figures earn “no less than R1 million a day”. He claims they use that money to pay hitmen, police officers and prosecutors. According to Daily Maverick, the affidavit alleges that a lieutenant colonel and a senior public prosecutor tip off the syndicate. It also alleges that rivals are removed through arrests or through killings disguised as legitimate operations.

None of these allegations has been tested in testimony, and no findings have been made against anyone. Daily Maverick also reported that further evidence from businessman Tumelo Nku, who was earlier named as a confidential police source, has been postponed for security reasons.

Khumalo’s private evidence and his R112m claim

On Wednesday, 25 November, Lt-Gen Dumisani Khumalo, the SAPS divisional commissioner for crime intelligence, is due to testify in camera. Madlanga ruled that only commissioners, evidence leaders, the commission’s attorneys, designated officers and IT staff may attend, Daily Sun reported. Evidence leaders argued that discussing the rules for handling informants and agents in public could expose intelligence methods and put sources at risk. Open hearings are expected to resume on Thursday.

Khumalo is also in a legal fight with the state. TimesLIVE reported on Monday that he and Major-Generals Philani Lushaba, Josias Lekalakala and Nosipho Precious Madondo have sent letters of demand for R28m each, R112m in total. The claims are for malicious arrest and prosecution and for impairment of dignity. Idac, the Investigating Directorate Against Corruption, had charged them over the allegedly irregular appointment of a brigadier. Those charges were later withdrawn after evidence at the commission raised questions about Idac’s conduct.

The officers’ lawyers allege that Idac fabricated the case for an ulterior purpose. That is an allegation, not a finding. TimesLIVE reported that Khumalo suffered a heart attack and a mild stroke and needed heart surgery, and that Lushaba spent seven nights in detention. The commission is investigating claims, first made public in July 2025, that a criminal cartel infiltrated the justice system, politics and the private sector.
