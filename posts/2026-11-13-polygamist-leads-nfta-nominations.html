'The Polygamist' leads South Africa's newest screen awards into their fourth year

Culture Desk

South African television does not often get to simply enjoy itself, and this week it got the chance. The National Film & TV Awards South Africa released its 2026 nominations with The Polygamist, from Stained Glass Productions, leading the field by a wide margin, picking up nominations across Best Scripted TV Series, Best TV Drama Series and acting categories for four members of its cast, including lead Sdumo Mtshali in contention for Best Actor in a TV Series. The fourth annual ceremony is expected later this month at the Theatre of Marcellus at Emperors Palace in Johannesburg, with public voting open ahead of the night.

The show's dominance in the nominations list reflects something South African broadcasters have leaned into hard over the past two years: polygamy, and the domestic drama it generates, has become one of the most reliable subjects in local scripted television, running in a direct line from reality formats built around real polygamous families to fiction that borrows the same tension for narrative plotting. The Polygamist's nomination haul suggests audiences and now industry voters alike are rewarding the fictionalised version with the same appetite they've shown the real thing.

That overlap between reality and drama runs through the rest of the nominations too. Musa Mseleku, the reality television figure whose polygamous family life has anchored one of Mzansi Magic's longest-running unscripted hits, picked up two nominations of his own at the same ceremony — a reminder that the NFTA, in only its fourth year, has positioned itself as a home for the kind of broad, audience-driven South African television that the more established SAFTAs, held earlier in the year, treat as a smaller part of a wider brief.

The timing matters beyond the red carpet. South Africa's scripted production sector has spent 2026 absorbing the consequences of a production incentive scheme that has barely functioned since 2024, with new applications stalled and the industry's coalition now appealing directly to the Presidency after a third round of talks with the DTIC collapsed. Award nominations do not fix a dormant rebate programme, but they are a visible, publicly celebrated counterpoint to a year defined mostly by funding paralysis — proof, however partial, that local production is still capable of making work audiences and critics want to notice.

For an industry whose loudest recent headlines have been about job losses and unanswered ministerial letters, a nominations list topped by a hit domestic drama is a small mercy. It changes nothing about the incentive scheme's dormant claims backlog. It does, for one ceremony night in Johannesburg, give the people who kept making television anyway something to put on a stage.
