Eisenkot and Netanyahu’s parties hold four hours of unity talks in Israel, but no deal

Prime Minister-designate Gadi Eisenkot invited Likud to coalition talks after an inconclusive October election. The two sides remain split over who should lead and whether Itamar Ben-Gvir can join.

World Desk·Monday, 23 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Negotiators from Eisenkot’s Yashar party and Likud met for about four hours on Sunday without agreement
  • Yashar wants Ben-Gvir kept out and Eisenkot as prime minister for the full term
  • Eisenkot’s mandate to form a government expires on 6 December
  • If talks fail, Eisenkot says he will seek a minority “national emergency” government

Israel’s attempt to form a new government has hit its first wall. Negotiators from Prime Minister-designate Gadi Eisenkot’s centrist Yashar party and Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud met on Sunday 22 November for about four hours at a hotel in Herzliya, but broke up without a deal, Middle East Monitor reported, citing Israel’s Channel 12.

Yashar had invited Likud to talks on Saturday, saying it was committed to forming the broadest possible government, Ireland’s RTÉ reported. Haaretz reported that the two parties agreed to keep talking.

What divides them

Yashar wants far-right minister Itamar Ben-Gvir kept out of government, and wants Eisenkot to serve as prime minister for the full term. Likud wants Netanyahu in the post from the start, and wants other parties to end their boycott of him. Earlier on Sunday, Netanyahu met Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich.

Other players have drawn their own lines. Naftali Bennett backs a unity government led by Eisenkot and says he is committed to ensuring Netanyahu is not the next prime minister. Avigdor Lieberman rules out any government that includes Netanyahu.

The numbers

According to Middle East Monitor, Likud holds 27 seats in the 120-member Knesset and Yashar 21, with Otzma Yehudit on 7 and Religious Zionism on 6. The Joint List and the United Arab List hold 19 between them.

The October election gave neither bloc a majority. President Isaac Herzog gave Eisenkot the mandate to form a government on 8 November, and it expires on 6 December. Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister while on trial for corruption.

What happens next

If the unity talks fail, Eisenkot says he will put a minority “national emergency” government to a confidence vote, with the Joint List and the United Arab List supporting it from outside the cabinet. With the mandate running out on 6 December, the next two weeks are decisive.
