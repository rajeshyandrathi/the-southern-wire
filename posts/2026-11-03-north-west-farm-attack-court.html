Five face court after a North West farmer was shot and mutilated in a brutal farm robbery

Crime Desk·Tuesday, 3 November 2026

A violent robbery on a North West farm has reignited fury over rural safety after a 50-year-old farmer was shot and had both his ears cut off by his attackers. Police say the assault happened during a robbery at the farmer's property over the weekend, with five suspects allegedly involved in an attack that combined armed robbery with a level of deliberate brutality that has shocked even hardened observers of South Africa's long-running rural crime debate.

The five men were arrested over the weekend and are due to appear before a local magistrate's court on Tuesday, facing charges that include house robbery, attempted murder, and illegal possession of firearms and ammunition. Agriculture Minister Willie Aucamp condemned the attack in unusually direct terms, adding his voice to a chorus of farming organisations that have long argued rural crime deserves dedicated policing resources distinct from urban crime strategy.

The mutilation detail has driven much of the public reaction, but farm attacks of this kind rarely occur in isolation from a broader pattern: robberies targeting farm homesteads for cash, firearms and vehicles, frequently accompanied by violence disproportionate to what is actually taken. Rural advocacy groups have renewed calls for better resourcing of the SAPS's rural safety units and faster response times in areas where the nearest station can be a considerable drive away.

The surviving farmer's condition has not been detailed publicly beyond confirmation that he survived the shooting and the assault, and police have said the investigation remains open as they work to establish whether the five arrested represent the full group involved or whether others remain at large.

Tuesday's court appearance will be the first real test of how the state intends to frame the case — as a robbery that turned violent, or as a premeditated act of torture carried out alongside it — a distinction that is likely to shape both the charges that stick and the sentences that follow if convictions are secured.
