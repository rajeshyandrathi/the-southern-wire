Rickelton's century, Bavuma's return and 557 all out: the Proteas seize the Wanderers Test

Sport Desk

The question hanging over the Wanderers at the start of day four was whether Temba Bavuma could bat at all. By the end of a session cut short twice by rain, South Africa had an answer far better than the worried version going around the ground that morning: not only did Bavuma return to the crease, he finished unbeaten on 68 as the Proteas were eventually bowled out for 557, a first-innings total that leaves them 157 runs ahead of Bangladesh with time still to bowl.

The innings' defining knock belonged to Ryan Rickelton. His 102 anchored a middle order that had looked vulnerable a day earlier, when Bavuma's injury scare triggered a clatter of wickets as the Proteas closed day three still 14 runs behind Bangladesh's first-innings 400. Rickelton batted through the uncertainty around his captain to post a century that turned a tight match back in South Africa's favour, finding support from Kagiso Rabada, whose own half-century down the order stretched a total that could easily have stalled closer to 450.

Bavuma's contribution carried weight well beyond the scoreboard. A team that had spent the previous evening debating whether its captain would need to retire hurt for a second time instead watched him walk out, play through the discomfort that forced him off the field on day three, and bat his side past Bangladesh's total and deep into a match-winning lead. Whatever assessment the medical staff make before the Proteas take the field to bowl, Bavuma's 68 not out has already done the job of settling a dressing room that woke up this morning unsure what to expect from him.

Bangladesh's bowlers were left with little room to work once the rain allowed play. Two sessions were washed out entirely, compressing what should have been a full day's cricket into a shortened window that still somehow produced 157 runs of daylight between the sides. Litton Das's six catches during Bangladesh's own first innings had already set a record for the tourists in this series; nothing in his side's bowling effort on day four offered a similar highlight, as the required breakthroughs came too slowly to prevent South Africa building a lead that now puts the match firmly in the Proteas' control.

Why a captain's fitness changes a Test

Cricket is unusual among team sports in how directly a captain's physical state can alter the tactical picture, since the captain is also almost always a specialist batter or bowler whose absence from the XI is a genuine on-field loss, not just a leadership gap filled by a deputy. A side that had spent the previous evening planning for the possibility of batting out the rest of its first innings without its captain instead got both his runs and his on-field leadership back in one session — a swing in available options, over the two remaining innings of the match, that goes well beyond the 68 runs on the scoreboard. How much workload the medical staff allow him to carry with the ball or in the field over the rest of the Test will be one of the details worth watching as the match moves into its final stages.

What a 157-run lead means from here

A first-innings lead of this size does not guarantee a result; rain has already shown this match it can take sessions away without warning, and a draw remains squarely in play if the weather that disrupted days three and four returns. But a lead approaching 160 runs gives South Africa's bowlers room to attack rather than simply contain when they take the field again, since even a moderately below-par bowling effort in Bangladesh's second innings would likely still leave the tourists needing something close to a record chase to win outright. That changes the calculus for Bangladesh's lower order too: batting to save a draw against a bowling attack with a healthy cushion behind it requires a different mindset than batting to set a target, and how quickly Bangladesh's top order adjusts to that reality once South Africa take the field will shape the rest of the Test.

With the first innings finally settled, the contest shifts to whether South Africa's attack can turn a 157-run cushion into victory before the match runs out of time. Bangladesh will resume knowing they conceded the initiative during the same session that was meant to be their best chance to claw it back, and South Africa's bowlers now have the lead, the conditions and a settled captain behind them to press the advantage.
