Ramaphosa comes home from Silicon Valley with a R100 million plan for AI start-ups

At the end of a five-day visit with almost 300 South Africans, the President announced a venture capital fund for AI and tech start-ups and a credit guarantee fund. Critics say the sums are small and the details thin.

Tech Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Government plans a R100 million venture capital fund for AI and tech start-ups, plus a credit guarantee fund
  • Ramaphosa announced it on Sunday at the end of a five-day working visit to Silicon Valley
  • A team must produce implementable proposals within 90 days, feeding into a national AI policy
  • No launch or application date has been set

South Africa will set up a R100 million venture capital fund for artificial intelligence and technology start-ups, President Cyril Ramaphosa said on Sunday 22 November, speaking to the media in Silicon Valley at the end of a five-day working visit, SAnews reported. A separate credit guarantee fund will sit alongside it.

Government intends to match money committed by local entrepreneurs and will invite the private sector to take part. Ramaphosa said the idea was proposed by entrepreneurs who travelled with him.

The delegation numbered almost 300 South Africans, including business leaders, ministers, officials, students, public entities and researchers. They met AI developers, chip and semiconductor makers, infrastructure providers, investors and academics. Scrolla, published via allAfrica, reported meetings with Google, Microsoft and Cisco. Ramaphosa said South Africa hosts more than 70% of Africa’s data centres.

What the government wants

Ramaphosa said South Africa refused to become merely a consumer of the technology, and that the country needed to harness it for the benefit of South Africans. A dedicated team will draw up an action plan, and he expects implementable proposals within 90 days. The visit is meant to feed into a national AI policy, and government plans a facility to reconnect South African tech professionals abroad with opportunities at home, SAnews reported.

He also flagged the risks, especially job losses and cybersecurity, and stressed the need to reskill workers. Further delegations to other regions are expected in the new year, this time including labour and civil society.

The doubts

Not everyone was impressed. Scrolla reported criticism that Ramaphosa did not meet some of the biggest names in AI, including OpenAI’s Sam Altman. The fund has not been launched, there is no application date, and government has not said how many jobs it could create.

The scale is modest. Business Tech Africa put R100 million at about $5.9 million, a modest sum for a venture fund. The need is large: Stats SA puts unemployment at 33.7%, or 8.6 million people, for July to September 2026, and a World Bank report in August found shortages of programming, networking and cybersecurity skills.

What happens next

The next milestone is the 90-day action plan, due around February. Whether the fund attracts serious private money, and how quickly it can start backing companies, will show whether the trip produces more than headlines.
