ANC turns to the DA in Polokwane as MK moves its MPLs into KZN councils

Away from the big metros, John Mpe was re-elected Polokwane mayor with DA backing, Msunduzi pushed its leadership vote to Friday, and the MK Party has started moving provincial legislators into council seats.

Politics Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • The ANC's John Mpe was re-elected Polokwane mayor by 55 votes to 35 under an ANC-DA agreement
  • The deal is an exception to the ANC's national preference for MK and other partners over the DA
  • Msunduzi's councillors were sworn in on Wednesday, but the votes for mayor and Speaker were moved to Friday
  • MK says KZN legislator Mervyn Dirks has resigned to take a Msunduzi council seat, and others are following

While Johannesburg, Ekurhuleni and Durban wait for Friday, Polokwane has already chosen its leaders, and it did so with a partnership that the ANC's national leadership has been playing down. ANC councillor John Mpe was re-elected executive mayor of the Limpopo capital on Tuesday, 24 November, after an ANC-DA coalition agreement, Jacaranda FM reported.

In a secret ballot of 90 councillors, Mpe won 55 votes to 35 for the EFF's Lawrence Mapoulo. Jacaranda FM said Mpe was nominated by the DA and Mapoulo by ActionSA. The ANC's Lizell Whitehead was elected Speaker and Adolf Rapetswa was re-elected chief whip. Inside Metros spells the Speaker's first name Lizel.

The ANC lost its majority in Polokwane in the 4 November elections. According to Inside Metros, it won 37 seats, nine short of the 46 needed, and the DA's 11 seats take the two parties to 48. The council's first sitting, on 18 November, had collapsed after parties failed to agree on candidates for mayor, Speaker and chief whip.

An exception to the national line

The Polokwane deal stands out because, as we reported on Tuesday, Business Day's Luthuli House sources said President Cyril Ramaphosa had told the ANC's national executive committee to treat a partnership with the DA as a last resort. Polokwane shows that the option has not been ruled out everywhere. ANC Limpopo provincial secretary Reuben Madadzhe said the party expected its deployees to serve with "discipline, integrity, humility", Inside Metros reported.

Mpe promised a 100-day push on services. He said water was the main priority and that the administration would now focus on rural areas such as Moletjie, Matlala, Molepo, Maja, Chuene and Mankweng, Jacaranda FM reported. His plan includes filling the vacant executive director posts for water and sanitation and for energy, completing six rural water projects for 1,442 households, installing 1,500 smart meters and grading 500km of roads. Inside Metros reported that 42 of the municipality's 45 wards have had water supply problems.

Msunduzi waits for Friday

In Pietermaritzburg, Msunduzi's newly elected councillors were sworn in on Wednesday, 25 November, at the start of the 2026–2031 term. The election of the Speaker, mayor, deputy mayor and chief whip was then moved to the next sitting on Friday, 27 November, Daily News reported. The municipality said its administration remained fully operational and that services would continue without interruption.

No party can govern alone. The MK Party won 31 of the council's 81 seats on 4 November, 10 short of a majority, Scrolla reported. Business Day reported that a prospective ANC-MK coalition in the city had stalled over the allocation of leadership posts. Msunduzi is one of 13 KwaZulu-Natal municipalities, among them eThekwini, uMhlathuze, Newcastle and KwaDukuza, whose first sittings were postponed at the provincial government's request, according to Business Day.

MK's legislators head for councils

The MK Party is also reshuffling its people to fill the posts it hopes to win. Its chief whip in the KwaZulu-Natal Legislature, Bonginkosi Mngadi, said former ANC MP Mervyn Dirks, a former Msunduzi deputy mayor, had filed his resignation from the legislature to take up a council seat in Msunduzi, Scrolla reported. The Sunday Tribune, citing MK sources, has reported that Dirks could become mayor.

Mngadi said the same process was under way for Nathi Mthethwa, whom the party wants as uThukela District mayor, and for Cebile Zuma-Dube, who is in line for a seat in the uMgungundlovu District. He said all due processes had been followed. Not everyone in the party is happy: an MK member who asked not to be named told Scrolla that council seats should go to people who campaigned on the ground. Dirks, Mthethwa and Zuma-Dube could not be reached by Scrolla.

District councils come next. Cooperative Governance Minister Velenkosini Hlabisa has said they must hold their first meetings within 14 days after all the local councils in each district have been formed and have chosen their district representatives.
