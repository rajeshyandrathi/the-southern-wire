Karoo wire-car film wins South Africa an International Emmy

The Philipstown WireCar Grand Prix, a documentary about children who build and race cars made of wire in a small Northern Cape town, won Best Sports Documentary at the 54th International Emmy Awards in New York.

Culture Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • The Philipstown WireCar Grand Prix won the Sports Documentary award at the International Emmys in New York on Monday, 23 November
  • It is the first South African winner in that category, and the country’s first documentary win since Miners Shot Down in 2015
  • The 75-minute film follows eight young racers who build cars from wire and scrap and race them over a rocky 3km course
  • It was directed by Paul Ward, produced by Giant Films and created by Accenture Song for the Philipstown WireCar Foundation
  • The full film streams on Prime Video and has also been shown on kykNET and SuperSport

A documentary about home-made wire cars in a tiny Karoo town has won South Africa an International Emmy. The Philipstown WireCar Grand Prix took the Best Sports Documentary award at the 54th International Emmy Awards, held in New York on Monday, 23 November. News of the win reached most South African audiences on Tuesday and Wednesday.

Cape Town ETC and The National both report that it is the first South African winner in the sports documentary category. It is also the first South African documentary to win an International Emmy since Rehad Desai’s Miners Shot Down, about the Marikana massacre, in 2015. According to TimesLive, the film beat Tom Daley: 1.6 Seconds from the UK and nominees from Brazil and Spain.

Director Paul Ward accepted the award on stage. The National reports he was joined by Cindy Gabriel, whom it credits as a director and producer of the film.

Barefoot racing in the Karoo

Philipstown is a small town in the Karoo in the Northern Cape. Its wire-car race has run for more than 14 years and has become the town’s biggest social event, according to The National. Children build their own cars from wire and discarded materials, then race them barefoot around a rocky three-kilometre course.

The 75-minute film follows eight young racers through the build-up and the race itself. It pays as much attention to the children’s creativity and preparation as to the result on race day. Stylised computer-animated sequences, directed by Dima Lochmann of Accenture Song Germany, show the imaginary worlds the racers picture as they drive. Jason Prins was director of photography.

Tseliso Rangaka, chief creative officer of Accenture Song South Africa, put the credit with the town. “This one belongs to Philipstown,” he said after the ceremony.

From brand project to Emmy winner

The film did not start life as a conventional documentary commission. It was created by the advertising and creative agency Accenture Song for the Philipstown WireCar Foundation, which runs youth development work in the town, and produced by Giant Films. It was the centrepiece of a wider campaign to raise the town’s profile, which also included a mobile game that recreates Philipstown as a digital copy using 360-degree video and GPS data recorded during the race.

That campaign had already won a long list of advertising and digital awards this year before the Emmy. TimesLive lists silver and bronze at Cannes Lions, three Gold Pencils at The One Show, the Social Impact Award at Tribeca X and a Webby for best documentary storytelling. At South Africa’s IAB Bookmark Awards it won eight golds, and the foundation was named Digital Brand of the Year.

The documentary has been broadcast on kykNET and SuperSport, and the full film is streaming on Prime Video, according to Broadcast Media Africa and Cape Town ETC. The win adds to a strong run for South African non-fiction on the world stage, five years after My Octopus Teacher won the Academy Award for best documentary feature in 2021.
