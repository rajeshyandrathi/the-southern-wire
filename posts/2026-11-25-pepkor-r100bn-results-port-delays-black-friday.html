Pepkor passes R100bn in sales but warns port delays are holding up summer stock

The owner of PEP and Ackermans grew revenue by 11.9% to R105.9bn and raised its dividend. It also warned that bad weather and congestion at the ports have disrupted deliveries just before Black Friday.

Business Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Revenue rose 11.9% to R105.9bn in the year to 30 September, the first time above R100bn
  • Headline earnings per share rose 6.9% to 171.3c, and the dividend rose 9.3% to 57.9c
  • Like-for-like sales grew only 2.6%, and Ackermans sales were almost flat
  • Financial services revenue jumped 35.2% to R6.2bn
  • Pepkor says summer stock deliveries were disrupted by bad weather and port congestion

Pepkor, the group behind PEP, Ackermans, Refinery, Legit, Incredible and Bradlows, has passed R100bn in annual revenue for the first time. In results released on Tuesday, 24 November, for the year ended 30 September 2026, revenue rose 11.9% to R105.9bn. Organic growth, which excludes acquisitions, was 7.3%.

Headline earnings per share rose 6.9% to 171.3 cents. Without the start-up costs of PlusB, the digital bank Pepkor is building, the increase was 9.7% to 177.5 cents. The dividend rose 9.3% to 57.9 cents a share, and the share price jumped on the day: Investing.com reported a rise of about 6.6%, and Sharenet’s closing list showed Pepkor as the JSE’s biggest riser among the top 100 shares, up 7.55%.

The results also came with a warning. Pepkor’s results booklet says “summer inflows have been disrupted by adverse weather and domestic port congestion”, The Rio Times reported. Black Friday is on Friday, 27 November, and management says it has put its most important products first.

Strong cash, slow shoppers

The cash numbers were the standout. According to Bizcommunity, cash generated from operations rose 40.6% to R15.3bn and free cash flow reached R4.8bn, up from R1.7bn a year earlier. Gross margin improved by 170 basis points to 41.5%, and debt fell to 0.67 times earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, from 0.77 times.

Underlying demand was weaker. Sales in stores open for both years grew just 2.6%. PEP grew sales by 5.1%, but Ackermans managed only 0.2%, which the group put down to tighter credit and weak baby and kids ranges. The speciality division, which includes Legit, Swagga and Style, grew 51% after adding hundreds of stores.

Chief operating officer Sean Summers told analysts that market growth had slowed to “just around 2%”, according to an Investing.com transcript. Chief executive Pieter Erasmus said “times remain tough for our customers”, and the group expects household budgets to stay under pressure from high living and transport costs.

Pepkor ended the year with 6,744 stores across southern Africa and Brazil, a net increase of 699, Sunday World reported. Online sales rose 42.2% but are still only about 1.5% of group sales. The +more loyalty programme has 18.5 million members.

Banking and informal traders

Financial services is growing much faster than clothing. Revenue in the division rose 35.2% to R6.2bn and operating profit rose 37% to R1.5bn. Insurance contributed more than R1bn in profit, and the FoneYam cellphone business has 2.7 million active customers.

Flash, Pepkor’s platform for spaza shops and other informal traders, handled about R71bn in transactions for 176,000 traders and made more than R1bn in operating profit for the first time. Pepkor plans to combine Flash with fintech company Shop2Shop, a deal it expects to complete in the second half of the 2027 financial year.

PlusB is due to launch in the second half of the 2027 financial year, if regulators approve it. Pepkor says its banking licence application is at the final stage and that spending is within a R920m budget. The group also plans to open more than 200 new stores in the coming year.
