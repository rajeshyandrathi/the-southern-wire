Ekurhuleni puts off its mayor vote as partners wait for the ANC to sign

Ekurhuleni's 224 councillors were sworn in on Wednesday, but the election of a mayor was moved to Friday. ActionSA and the EFF say they are ready and are waiting for the ANC, whose secretary-general is due to announce the party's coalition decisions today.

Politics Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • Ekurhuleni councillors were sworn in on 25 November; the votes for Speaker, chief whip and mayor were moved to Friday, 27 November
  • The DA proposed the delay, and the motion had unanimous support
  • ActionSA and the EFF say their side of the deal is done and the ANC has yet to sign
  • EWN reports the ANC will field ward 56 councillor Mziyanda Mketsu for mayor, though this is not official
  • Tshwane's council sits today, and ANC secretary-general Fikile Mbalula is due to brief the media

A second Gauteng metro has sworn in its councillors without electing a mayor. Ekurhuleni's 224 newly elected councillors took their oaths at the OR Tambo Government Precinct Civic Centre in Germiston on Wednesday, 25 November, but the council then deferred the election of a Speaker, chief whip and executive mayor to Friday, The National and Business Day reported.

The motion to postpone came from DA caucus leader Tania Campbell, who argued that going ahead could cause instability and hurt service delivery, according to Business Day. Councillors from the EFF, MK, the Freedom Front Plus and ActionSA backed her, and The National reported that the motion passed with unanimous support. City manager Kagiso Lerutla, who presided, said Friday was the deadline for councils to elect their new leaders.

That makes Friday, 27 November, a crowded day. Johannesburg's council, which was sworn in on Monday, is also due to reconvene then to elect a Speaker and mayor, as we reported on Tuesday. Briefly News reported that after the deadline the provincial department of cooperative governance could step in.

Waiting for the ANC

The numbers explain why nobody wanted to vote yet. According to Business Day, the DA won 66 seats in Ekurhuleni, the ANC 59, ActionSA 40, MK 15, the EFF 14 and the Freedom Front Plus 11. A majority in the 224-seat council is 113. On those figures, the ANC, ActionSA, MK and the EFF together hold 128 seats, enough to govern without the DA, which won the most seats.

The ANC's partners say the hold-up is on its side. ActionSA leader Herman Mashaba told Business Day: "We are ready to sign; we are just waiting for the ANC." He said the broad agreement gives ActionSA the mayoralties of Johannesburg and Tshwane and the ANC the Ekurhuleni mayoralty, but that the ANC had failed to settle finer details at a meeting on Tuesday. He told The National he expects ActionSA's Xolani Khumalo to become deputy mayor and the member of the mayoral committee for community safety, although the city's organogram does not yet provide for a deputy mayor.

EFF secretary-general Marshall Dlamini said his party had concluded its part of the talks and was ready to elect a mayor, but would respect its partners. "This is a joint programme; it is not an EFF programme," he said, according to The National. The DA's mayoral candidate, Khathutshelo Rasilingwane, said talks were "still in progress", Briefly News reported.

Who the ANC might field

EWN reported that the ANC will put forward Mziyanda Mketsu, who was sworn in as the ward 56 councillor on Wednesday, for mayor, though it stressed this has not been officially confirmed. The outgoing mayor and ANC regional chairperson, Nkosindiphile Xhakaza, declined to confirm the name. He said the region had sent its proposed mayor, Speaker and whip to the national leadership, and that "the negotiations and coalitions are handled at the national level".

The stakes are high. Business Day noted that Ekurhuleni produces about 8.1% of the national economy and 23.4% of the province's.

Tshwane sits, Mbalula speaks

Tshwane's new council is due to hold its first sitting at 10:00 today, Thursday 26 November, at Tshwane House, Pretoria News reported. The DA is the largest party there with 74 of 214 seats, followed by ActionSA with 48 and the ANC with 44. ActionSA's Nasiphi Moya is tipped to return as executive mayor under the ANC-ActionSA arrangement, but Pretoria News said it was still unclear whether a mayor would be elected today because talks had not been finalised.

Also today, ANC secretary-general Fikile Mbalula is expected to announce the outcome of the national executive committee's deliberations on pacts in more than 130 hung councils, and EWN reported that he is expected to confirm agreements with parties including ActionSA and the EFF. ANC caucuses in the metros are understood to be waiting for his direction on which candidates to back. Until the votes are counted in each chamber, the Gauteng deal remains an agreement on paper.
