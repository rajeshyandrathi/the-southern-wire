Gold has its worst week since March and the JSE slides again: today’s markets in one place

Gold fell below $3,600 an ounce, silver lost more than 2% and platinum held up better as bets on a US rate hike lifted the dollar. The JSE All Share dropped another 1.7% to around 104,400 points.

Business Desk·Friday, 20 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Gold about $3,558/oz, down 1.9% on Friday
  • Silver about $41.86/oz, down 2.4%
  • Platinum about $1,327/oz
  • JSE All Share closed near 104,443, down 1.7%
  • Rand around R16.6 to the dollar

It was a rough end to the week for anyone holding precious metals or South African shares. Gold slid to about $3,558 an ounce on Friday, down almost 1.9% on the day, as traders priced in near-certain odds of an interest rate hike by the US Federal Reserve and the dollar firmed. That leaves the metal roughly 36% below the record of about $5,589 it set in January.

Silver fell harder, down about 2.4% to around $41.86 an ounce, while platinum, which matters a great deal to South Africa’s mining industry, held up comparatively well at around $1,327 an ounce on Friday morning, US time.

On the JSE, the All Share index fell about 1.7% to close near 104,443 points, after sliding 3.7% on Wednesday. That puts it below its previous 52-week low and roughly 7% lower than a week ago, a sharp sell-off for South Africa’s main share index.

The rand has also been under pressure, trading around R16.6 to the US dollar this week, its weakest level since early November, as a firmer dollar and weaker metals prices weighed on the currency.

Why gold is falling

Gold pays no interest, so it tends to lose appeal when interest rates rise or are expected to rise. With markets now betting heavily on a US rate hike, the yields on US government bonds have climbed and the dollar has strengthened. Both make gold more expensive for buyers using other currencies and less attractive compared to assets that pay a return.

The fall this year has been steep. Gold reached record highs in January, partly as a safe haven during the US-Iran war and its oil price shock. As that risk has eased and rate expectations have turned, much of that safe-haven premium has unwound.

What it means for South Africa

South Africa is one of the world’s biggest producers of platinum and a major gold miner, so metal prices feed straight into mining company profits, tax revenue and the rand. Falling gold and silver prices, combined with a firmer dollar, help explain why both the JSE and the rand have weakened this month.

There is a small upside for consumers. A weaker rand makes imports dearer, but lower global gold prices can ease jewellery prices, and the expected December fuel price cut should offer some relief at the pump.

Diamonds: a market still searching for a floor

Diamonds do not trade on an exchange with a single daily price the way gold and platinum do. Prices are set through private sales and tracked by industry indexes. The latest trade reports show prices for smaller stones, between about 0.3 and 0.7 carats, still falling, while prices for 1 to 2 carat stones are starting to stabilise and larger stones are holding steady.

The biggest story in the industry is the sale of De Beers. Botswana has picked a consortium led by former De Beers chief executive Gareth Penny as its preferred bidder, and wants to double its own stake in the company to 30%. De Beers sold far more rough diamonds in its third quarter after cutting prices in July, but the average price it received fell sharply, a sign of how hard the market has been squeezed by weak demand and competition from lab-grown stones.
