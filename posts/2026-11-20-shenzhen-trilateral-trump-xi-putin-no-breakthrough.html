Trump, Xi and Putin met in Shenzhen. Everyone called it historic. Nobody announced a deal.

The first-ever three-way meeting of the US, Chinese and Russian leaders produced a joint declaration and warm words, but no breakthrough on Ukraine. Trump is due to brief reporters at the White House on Friday.

World Desk·Friday, 20 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • First-ever three-way meeting of US, Chinese and Russian leaders
  • Joint declaration on peace, but no breakthrough on Ukraine
  • Kremlin: “no particularly important statements” in Trump-Putin talks
  • Trump to brief reporters at the White House on Friday

The images from Shenzhen were striking: Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin arriving together for the first three-way meeting between the leaders of the United States, China and Russia, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. All three described it as historic. What it actually changed is much less clear.

No diplomatic breakthrough was announced. The three issued a joint declaration on peace, and Xi said the talks were positive and fruitful. Trump, who greeted Putin as “my friend”, later gave the summit a score of 12 out of 10 and said Putin wants a deal on Ukraine.

The Kremlin’s account was cooler. Putin and Trump spent about 40 minutes on Ukraine in their own meeting, and Putin’s foreign policy aide Yuri Ushakov said neither side made any particularly important statements. Moscow has already ruled out a ceasefire while talks continue, and Putin used his press briefing to repeat his justifications for the invasion, now nearing its fifth year.

After the talks, Trump dialled into a meeting in Berlin with European officials, where his envoys said he had raised a peaceful end to the war with both leaders. US officials say Trump has asked Jared Kushner to draft security guarantees for Ukraine, and that Putin has signalled he could live with NATO-style guarantees. None of this has been confirmed by Kyiv.

Several analysts noted that Putin got the biggest win simply by being there: a seat at the top table with the US and Chinese leaders, without having to offer anything in return. Trump has scheduled a White House press conference for Friday, which may say more about what, if anything, was agreed.

What Europe and Kyiv are watching

European governments have worried for months that a deal on Ukraine could be shaped by the US and Russia without Kyiv or Europe at the table. Trump’s decision to dial into a Berlin meeting with European officials straight after Shenzhen was clearly meant to address that concern.

Ukraine has not commented in detail on the reported plans for security guarantees. Any agreement would need its consent, and Kyiv has repeatedly said it will not accept a settlement imposed from outside.

Why it matters for South Africa

South Africa has tried to position itself as a neutral voice on the war and has close ties with both China and Russia through BRICS, while also depending on trade with the US and Europe. A shift towards a world shaped by deals between the biggest powers raises questions about how much room middle-sized countries will have to steer their own course.

For now, the summit produced more pictures than policy. Friday’s White House briefing is the next place to look for detail.
