Showmax beats Netflix to the title of best television streaming network

Culture Desk·Wednesday, 4 November 2026

For a platform that spent its early years being measured, unfavourably, against Netflix's budgets and catalogue depth, Showmax's win as best television streaming network lands as a genuine turning point in how South African streaming is perceived both locally and abroad. The MultiChoice-owned service built its case less on matching Netflix's global scale than on doing something Netflix has never quite prioritised: treating South African and broader African storytelling as the main attraction rather than a supplementary category buried several menus deep.

That strategy has paid off in ways that are now showing up in industry recognition rather than just subscriber numbers. Local dramas, reality formats and sports coverage, particularly football and rugby content that international platforms have historically under-served, have given Showmax a library that feels distinctly made for its audience rather than localised after the fact. Industry observers tracking the award say the judging panel singled out exactly that distinction, rewarding depth and cultural specificity over sheer catalogue size.

The win carries particular weight given how directly it positions Showmax against Netflix, a company whose African content investment has grown substantially but still operates as one piece of a global strategy rather than a continent-first one. Netflix has backed a growing slate of South African films and series over the past two years, evidence that the streaming giant takes the market seriously, but Showmax's recognition suggests local audiences and industry voters still see something irreplaceable in a platform built from the region outward.

For South Africa's broader film and television industry, the award functions as more than a trophy on a shelf. It reinforces an argument producers and commissioning executives have been making for years: that investment in distinctly local stories, rather than imitations of imported formats, is what actually builds a loyal, defensible audience in a market where international platforms can otherwise outspend almost anyone on marketing and acquisition.

Whether this recognition translates into deeper investment from Showmax's parent company, at a moment when MultiChoice itself has faced pressure on subscriber growth and margins, remains the open question hanging over the win. For now, the industry is treating it as proof that a platform built around African stories can hold its own against the biggest name in global streaming, on merit rather than nostalgia.
