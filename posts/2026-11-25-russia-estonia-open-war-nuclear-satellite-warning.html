Moscow warns Estonia of “open war” as allies weigh reports of a Russian nuclear satellite

Russia says Estonia’s plan to close its border is pushing the two countries towards open war. German media, meanwhile, report Western intelligence warnings that Moscow could put a nuclear-armed satellite into orbit within weeks, claims that have not been verified.

World Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Russia summoned Estonia’s top diplomat in Moscow over Tallinn’s plan to shut road crossings from 1 December
  • Estonia blames a foiled arson plot at a drone factory on Russian services, which Moscow denies
  • Estonia’s foreign minister says the closure will limit hostile intelligence activity
  • German media report Western intelligence warnings of a Russian nuclear-armed satellite launch, possibly in December
  • The satellite claims are unverified and the Kremlin has dismissed them

Tension between Russia and its small Baltic neighbour Estonia has sharpened sharply this week. On Monday, 23 November, Russia’s foreign ministry summoned Estonia’s chargé d’affaires in Moscow, Marek Uhtegi, and told him Tallinn’s actions were “on the verge of triggering open war with Russia”, Al Jazeera reported.

The trigger is Estonia’s decision to close its road border crossings with Russia, including the crossing over the Narva River, from 1 December for at least six months. Estonia says the move follows a foiled arson plot at a drone factory, which it blames on Russian security services. Moscow denies any involvement.

Russia also accused Estonia of quietly letting Ukrainian drones use its airspace to attack north-western Russia, of obstructing Russian ships in the Baltic and of banning the transit of Russian and Belarusian grain. It warned the border closure “will inevitably backfire” on Estonia and its allies, according to Al Jazeera.

Tallinn hits back

Estonia’s foreign ministry said the country “has never allowed, and will never allow” its territory to be used for another country’s military operations. Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna wrote on X that Russia was “flooding the airwaves with baseless accusations”, TIME reported.

In an opinion piece published by Politico on Tuesday, Tsahkna said the closure was a response to a growing number of Russian acts of sabotage and intimidation, and that it would “limit opportunities for hostile intelligence activities”, according to TIME. Relations between the two countries have been at chargé d’affaires level since ambassadors were expelled in 2023.

Nato Secretary-General Mark Rutte, speaking in Berlin, said the alliance was monitoring the situation in Estonia, alongside last week’s Russian missile incursion into Poland, and had options available if needed.

An unverified warning about space

At the same time, a joint investigation by German broadcasters NDR and WDR and the newspaper Süddeutsche Zeitung reported that Western intelligence services warned in early November that Russia could launch a nuclear-armed satellite within 60 days. Hromadske, citing the reports, said the satellite was believed to be stored near Moscow and that German military space officials thought a December launch was possible. None of this has been independently verified.

If true, it would breach the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, which bans nuclear weapons in orbit. Hromadske reported that the head of US Space Command has warned a nuclear blast in orbit could disable about a third of satellites in low Earth orbit within hours or days, hitting navigation and communications far beyond Europe, including the satellite services South Africans rely on.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov dismissed the reports as based on unidentified sources, IBTimes UK reported. Germany’s government said it was taking them seriously but warned against “excessive nervousness” over hypothetical dangers, while Rutte said Nato would defend its territory, including in space.
