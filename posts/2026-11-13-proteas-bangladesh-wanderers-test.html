The Proteas assemble in Johannesburg as cricket's top-ranked Test side turns to Bangladesh

Sport Desk

There is a particular kind of pressure that comes with being the best team in the world rather than the team trying to prove it belongs there, and South Africa's cricketers arrive at the Wanderers this week carrying exactly that weight. The Proteas assembled in Johannesburg to begin preparations for a two-Test series against Bangladesh, the first since they completed a dramatic 2-1 series win over Australia having lost the opening Test, and they do so still holding the number one Test ranking that result helped secure.

The squad named for the series tells its own story about a side still adjusting its combinations even while winning. Tony de Zorzi returns to the top order after a period out of favour, a recall that suggests the think tank wants a steadier head opening the innings than recent experiments have provided. Anrich Nortje's return is the more significant selection story, a fast bowler whose body has denied South African cricket more Test cricket than his talent ever should have allowed, brought back into a pace attack that has looked formidable on its best days and fragile whenever injury has forced changes.

Zubayr Hamza's recall at the expense of David Bedingham and Kyle Verreynne is the selection call likely to generate the most debate among a cricketing public that has watched both dropped players contribute to recent results. Temba Bavuma continues as captain of a side that, for all the changes around its edges, has kept a remarkably stable core through a period in which South African cricket has had every reason to be distracted by administrative turmoil elsewhere in the sport. That stability, more than any single personnel change, is what has carried this team to the top of a format where consistency is harder to manufacture than in any other.

Bangladesh arrive as the kind of opponent that creates a different sort of danger than Australia did. There is no reputational cushion in beating a side ranked well below South Africa, and all the pressure in a short two-match series sits on the favourites rather than the visitors; a slip in either Test invites exactly the scrutiny that a hard-fought series win over Australia was supposed to have bought the Proteas some relief from. The first Test runs at the Wanderers from Saturday, the second at SuperSport Park in Centurion later this month, giving South Africa two different surfaces in which to show whether their new ranking is a genuine level or a high-water mark.

For a squad that has spent years being asked when its talent would finally translate into global stature, arriving at a home series as the top-ranked Test team in the world is itself the answer to a question South African cricket has carried for a generation. What happens against Bangladesh over the next fortnight will say less about whether the Proteas deserve that ranking than about whether they can hold it.
