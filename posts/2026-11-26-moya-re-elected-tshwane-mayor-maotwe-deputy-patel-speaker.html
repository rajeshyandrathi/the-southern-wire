Moya is back as Tshwane mayor as ActionSA, the ANC, the EFF and MK shut the DA out

ActionSA's Nasiphi Moya beat the DA's Cilliers Brink by 115 votes to 87 at the new council's first sitting on Thursday. The EFF's Omphile Maotwe is her deputy and the ANC's Naeem Patel is Speaker.

Politics Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·5 min read

Key points

  • ActionSA's Nasiphi Moya was re-elected executive mayor of Tshwane on Thursday 26 November, with 115 votes to 87 for the DA's Cilliers Brink
  • The EFF's treasurer-general, Omphile Maotwe, was elected deputy executive mayor unopposed, and the ANC's Naeem Patel won the Speaker vote with 119 of 209 votes
  • ActionSA, the ANC, the EFF and MK signed a co-operation accord before the vote; the DA won the most seats, 74 of 214, but not a majority
  • Johannesburg and Ekurhuleni are due to elect their mayors on Friday 27 November, the deadline for new councils to choose their leaders

Nasiphi Moya will lead South Africa's capital city for a second term. The ActionSA mayor was re-elected executive mayor of the City of Tshwane on Thursday, 26 November, at the first sitting of the council chosen in the 4 November local government elections, beating the DA's Cilliers Brink by 115 votes to 87 in a secret ballot, eNCA and The National reported.

The vote ended weeks of uncertainty in Tshwane. When the council met at Tshwane House on Thursday morning, Pretoria News had reported that it was still unclear whether a mayor would be chosen at all, because coalition talks had not been finalised, as we noted in our earlier report on Ekurhuleni. In the end the meeting went ahead, and the DA's proposal to postpone it to allow more talks was voted down by 119 votes to 87, The National reported.

The EFF nominated Moya and the ANC seconded her, according to Inside Metros, while the DA put Brink forward with the Freedom Front Plus seconding. Earlier in the sitting the ANC's Naeem Patel was elected Speaker with 119 of the 209 votes cast, ahead of the DA's candidate Mthimunye on 71 and the Freedom Front Plus's Willie Spies on 19. The EFF's treasurer-general, Omphile Maotwe, was elected deputy executive mayor unopposed, and the ANC's Mabona became chief whip.

How the numbers worked

No party won a majority in Tshwane. According to The National, the DA holds 74 of the council's 214 seats, ActionSA 48, the ANC 44, the EFF 13 and MK four. Together, ActionSA, the ANC, the EFF and MK have 109 seats, just over half. Before the vote the four parties signed what ActionSA national chairperson Michael Beaumont called a municipal co-operation accord, a written deal setting out how they will share power in the city.

Moya's 115 votes were six more than those four parties' seats, which means she drew some support from smaller parties. Inside Metros reported that the Afrika Mayibuye Movement and the SACP also backed her, though the ballot was secret and no party has said exactly how its councillors voted.

ActionSA's own growth is a big part of the story. Beaumont said the party had grown from about 7% to 23% of the vote in Tshwane and had gone from holding no wards to 26. ActionSA had said before the elections that it would not work with the DA while Brink and Helen Zille were the DA's mayoral candidates in Tshwane and Johannesburg.

What the parties said

The DA reacted angrily. Its federal leader, Geordin Hill-Lewis, called the new arrangement a "coalition of chaos" and said voters in Tshwane must know that party leaders had ignored their voice, Inside Metros reported. Brink said the DA would be the strongest opposition the city had seen.

ActionSA leader Herman Mashaba said "no one is going to govern us without getting our mandate," according to The National. Beaumont acknowledged the size of the task, saying the city's finances are badly broken. The arrangement brings old tensions inside the coalition: Newsnote noted that the EFF had earlier called on Moya to resign over the water contamination crisis in Bronkhorstspruit. The ANC's regional secretary, George Matjila, said the talks had been difficult and that the party's national leadership had made the final decision.

What happens next

Attention now moves to the province's two other metros. Johannesburg's council, sworn in on Monday, and Ekurhuleni's, sworn in on Wednesday, are both due to elect their mayors on Friday 27 November, which is the 14-day deadline for new councils to choose their leaders. The National reported that Mashaba is tipped to become mayor of Johannesburg, with MK's Bongani Baloyi, a former Midvaal mayor, as his deputy, and that the ANC is expected to field Mziyanda Mketsu in Ekurhuleni. The deals for both cities were still unsigned on Thursday evening.

ANC secretary-general Fikile Mbalula, who had been expected to announce the party's coalition decisions on Thursday, is now due to brief the media on Friday, The National reported. eThekwini's first council sitting was also postponed. Tshwane's vote shows the four-party arrangement can hold in a secret ballot; Friday will show whether it holds in Johannesburg and Ekurhuleni as well.
