‘Nigeria is not your laboratory’: five Mexicans held after a $227m meth lab is found in Enugu

Anti-drug officers seized more than 1.5 tonnes of methamphetamine at a hidden lab in south-east Nigeria, the fourth with suspected Mexican cartel links found in the country in recent months.

World Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • Nigeria’s NDLEA arrested five Mexicans and a Nigerian businessman at an industrial-scale meth lab in Enugu State
  • More than 1.5 tonnes of methamphetamine, worth about $227 million, and nearly seven tonnes of chemicals were seized
  • The agency says it let the Mexicans into the country on purpose on their second attempt, using forged passports, and watched them
  • More than 200 officers raided the lab on 11 November after a seven-month, multi-country investigation
  • It is the fourth cartel-linked meth lab found in Nigeria in recent months; all six suspects await arraignment

Nigeria’s National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) says it has broken up an industrial-scale methamphetamine laboratory in Enugu State in the country’s south-east, arresting five Mexican nationals and a Nigerian businessman and seizing more than 1.5 tonnes of the drug. The agency put the street value at about $227 million, roughly R4 billion at current exchange rates.

NDLEA chairman Mohamed Buba Marwa announced the bust at a news conference in Abuja on Wednesday, 25 November. Officers also seized nearly seven tonnes of chemicals used to make meth, along with industrial equipment. It is the fourth industrial-scale meth lab with suspected Mexican cartel links found in Nigeria in recent months.

“Nigeria is not your laboratory,” Marwa warned the networks behind the operation, adding that the country would not serve as a warehouse or transit route either.

Let in on purpose

The NDLEA says the businessman, Chuks Enoch Ezeike, 63, set up the lab behind an oil company’s premises in Enugu, with living quarters so the cooks could work without drawing attention. The agency had been tracking him since April, after intelligence linked him to Oscar Ramos-Cardenas, whom it described as a suspected large-scale meth specialist and the alleged leader of the Mexican group.

According to the agency’s account, reported by the BBC and GBC, the five Mexicans were refused entry at Abuja airport in August and sent home. Less than a month later they tried again, travelling through Colombia and France to Benin, where they allegedly obtained forged Argentine, Colombian and Chilean passports. This time Nigerian officials let them in deliberately and followed them. Marwa said sending them home a second time “would only have postponed the problem”.

More than 200 officers raided the lab and a hotel where Ezeike was staying on 11 November, after a seven-month investigation across several countries. The five men were caught producing the drug, the NDLEA said, and Ezeike told investigators they had been due to leave two days later. He admitted owning the lab and bringing the men in, according to the agency. The other four Mexicans were named as Juan Antonio Sarabia-Cabrera, Luis Enrique Garcia-Amador, Jose Alejandro Renteria-Reyes and Salomon Cabanillas-Torres. All six are in custody awaiting arraignment; the agency has not named the cartel involved.

Why it matters beyond Nigeria

Investigators believe a Mexican cartel recruited and paid the specialists while Nigerian partners provided the site and logistics. Earlier this year similar labs were found in Ogun, Oyo and Ebonyi states, and the NDLEA says 10 suspected Mexican meth specialists have now been arrested in Nigeria in recent months. Analysts quoted by GBC say Latin American groups are moving from using West Africa only as a transit route to running production hubs there, which saves them shipping bulk drugs across the Atlantic.

Some of the output feeds local markets in West Africa, while traffickers also target Europe and Asia. Neighbouring Ghana has tightened checks at its borders and ports after hard-drug seizures at Tema harbour, GBC reported.

The case comes as South African police report their own run of drug-lab busts. Earlier today we reported on a mandrax lab found hidden in a De Deur tavern south of Johannesburg, a day after the Hawks shut down another lab near Richards Bay. Police say they have dismantled about 15 major labs in the past year. Marwa urged landlords to check what their tenants are doing and to report suspicious industrial activity.
