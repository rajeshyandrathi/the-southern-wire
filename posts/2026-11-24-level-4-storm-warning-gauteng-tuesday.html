Level 4 storm warning for Gauteng and parts of four provinces today, with hail, flooding and damaging winds

The Weather Service has issued a Yellow Level 4 warning for severe thunderstorms over Gauteng, southern Mpumalanga, parts of the Free State, North West and northern KwaZulu-Natal. Strong winds are expected along the east coast.

Weather Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Yellow Level 4 warning for severe thunderstorms over Gauteng, southern Mpumalanga, the eastern Free State and North West, and far northern KZN
  • Risks include flooded roads, damage to infrastructure, damaging winds, hail and excessive lightning
  • Level 2 warning for damaging winds along the coast from Algoa Bay to KwaZulu-Natal
  • Extremely high fire danger in the eastern parts of the Northern Cape

Another round of severe storms is expected over the interior today, Tuesday 24 November. The South African Weather Service has issued a Yellow Level 4 warning for severe thunderstorms over extreme northern KwaZulu-Natal, southern Mpumalanga, Gauteng, and the eastern parts of the Free State and North West, The Citizen reported.

The warning covers heavy downpours that could flood roads and settlements, damage to infrastructure, damaging winds, hail and excessive lightning. A wider Level 2 warning for severe thunderstorms covers Limpopo, northern Mpumalanga, central North West, the Free State, and central and southern KwaZulu-Natal.

Wind on the coast and fire in the west

A Level 2 warning for damaging winds applies from Algoa Bay to East London in the morning, spreading to Port Edward and the KwaZulu-Natal coast in the afternoon, Snow Report and The Citizen reported. Port operations could be disrupted and conditions at sea may be difficult for small vessels.

Further west, the Weather Service expects extremely high fire danger conditions over the eastern parts of the Northern Cape, with some areas of North West and the Free State also affected. The UV index is high in Gauteng and very high in the Western Cape.

What to do

Johannesburg EMS spokesperson Robert Mulaudzi urged motorists not to drive through flooded roads or over low-lying bridges, and asked residents to stay away from rivers and dams. He also asked faith groups not to hold baptism or cleansing rituals in rivers during the storms. The EMS emergency line is 011 375 5911, The Citizen reported.

Last week’s heavy rain is still being felt. On Monday the Gauteng health department said three facilities in Soweto had been damaged: Bheki Mlangeni District Hospital, where the roof has been patched, Zola Gateway Clinic, where repairs are under way, and Zola Community Health Centre, where a structural engineer has been sent to assess what the department called extensive damage.

Storms like these can develop quickly in the afternoon. Check the Weather Service’s latest warnings before travelling, and keep away from open ground and tall trees when lightning is close.
