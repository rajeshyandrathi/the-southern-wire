Cape Town just got the date for its first global food festival, and it's bringing its own TV deal

Lifestyle Desk

For most of this year, Cape Town's food calendar has carried an asterisk next to one name: the Taste Festival, a global brand that has run in London, Paris and Dubai but never once in Africa. That asterisk is gone. The festival's South African debut is now locked in for Saturday 28 and Sunday 29 November, pitched inside the Green Point Cricket Club grounds a few hundred metres from the stadium, and organised locally under the banner "Feed Every Sense."

What sets this edition apart from the usual festival-season crowd of pop-up tents and craft-beer stalls is the sheer density of what's being asked to share one patch of grass. The programme brings together the city's most sought-after restaurants, its leading wine estates and a run of specialist producers, all serving signature dishes rather than festival-sized approximations of their menus — the premise being that a diner can eat their way through a chef's actual reputation in a single open-air afternoon, a few minutes at a time between stalls.

The broadcast deal is the detail that separates this from a one-off ticketed weekend. The SABC has signed on as the festival's official broadcast partner, a commitment that turns a two-day culinary event into something closer to a televised showcase of the Cape's food scene for an audience well beyond whoever can get a ticket. It's a reversal of the usual traffic for food festivals in South Africa, which tend to be covered after the fact rather than broadcast as they happen.

The timing is deliberate. Landing on the same weekend the city's festive season properly switches on, the festival sits at the head of a run of Cape Town events — cocktail weeks, coffee showcases, outdoor concert series — that have turned November into the Cape's busiest month for anyone whose idea of a holiday involves eating well. Tickets are moving steadily, with the listing describing the event as family-friendly and carrying some free-entry options alongside paid tiers.

What the festival is really testing is whether Cape Town's food identity — built over two decades of award ceremonies, Michelin-adjacent ambition and a steady stream of international "best restaurant" lists — can draw a crowd on its own terms, without a wine estate backdrop or a jazz festival to piggyback on. If the inaugural weekend lands the way its backers are betting, the city will have a signature food festival of its own, the kind London, Paris and Dubai have each had for years.
