Senegal’s ruling party has thrown out the president it put in power

Pastef expelled President Bassirou Diomaye Faye and 31 others this week, completing a bitter split with party leader Ousmane Sonko. The rift threatens a budget deadlock just as Senegal needs IMF help.

World Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Pastef’s national council expelled President Faye and 31 other members on Tuesday
  • The party holds 130 of 165 seats in the National Assembly; Faye’s new party holds none
  • Faye fired Sonko as prime minister in May; Sonko is now Speaker of parliament
  • The standoff threatens IMF support and next year’s budget

Two years ago they were the faces of a political revolution in Senegal. This week the party that carried Bassirou Diomaye Faye to the presidency formally expelled him. Pastef’s national council announced on Tuesday that Faye and 31 other members were out, according to Africanews and Benin Web TV.

The party said the expulsions were automatic under its statutes, a permanent removal rather than a suspension. Benin Web TV reports that several of those expelled had joined the government even though Pastef had announced it would not take part in it. Africanews described the others removed as inactive members.

The decision completes a split between Faye and Ousmane Sonko, the charismatic Pastef leader who was the party’s original presidential candidate. When Sonko was barred from the March 2024 election, Faye stood in his place and won, then appointed Sonko as prime minister.

How the alliance broke

Relations soured over how the state should be run. In May 2026 Faye dismissed Sonko as prime minister and dissolved the government after months of tension. Sonko then became Speaker of the National Assembly, where Pastef holds 130 of the 165 seats. In July, Faye launched his own movement, which Africanews calls the Kiiray Republican Patriots. It has no seats in parliament.

Benin Web TV reports that Sonko described the split as definitive in mid-November. Neither Faye nor Sonko had commented publicly on the expulsion itself in the reports we have seen.

The arrangement now is unusual: a president without a party in parliament, facing a legislature controlled by his former ally and the party that once backed him.

Why it matters

The political fight has direct economic consequences. Senegal is carrying a heavy debt burden, and the government and the Pastef majority are locked in a dispute over the budget. Africanews reports that lawmakers rejected a key budget measure earlier this month, and that the deadlock threatens the release of significant IMF financial aid and could freeze new spending in the 2027 budget.

Senegal has long been seen as one of West Africa’s most stable democracies, and the peaceful handover in 2024 strengthened that reputation. How Faye and the Assembly manage to pass a budget in the coming weeks will be an early test of whether that stability holds under a divided government.

For now, the next steps are unclear. Faye remains president until his term ends, and Pastef’s majority in parliament means he will need to negotiate with the party that has just shown him the door.
