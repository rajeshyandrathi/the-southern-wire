Tencent carries Naspers again as food-delivery spending bites, while Takealot holds off Amazon

Naspers grew adjusted earnings by 12% on the back of its Tencent stake, but heavy spending at iFood and Just Eat Takeaway cut into profit and cash. At home, Takealot broke even in its first half-year since Amazon Prime arrived.

Business Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • Naspers adjusted earnings per share rose 12% to 240 US cents for the six months to 30 September, mainly thanks to Tencent
  • Revenue rose 55% to $6.4bn after the Just Eat Takeaway and Despegar deals, but adjusted EBITDA fell about 6% to $405m
  • About $344m went into iFood and Just Eat Takeaway, where iFood’s adjusted EBITDA dropped to $28m from $184m
  • Takealot Group grew revenue 16% in rand terms and reached break-even at adjusted operating level
  • Prosus shares fell 3.66% on the JSE on Wednesday

Naspers and its Amsterdam-listed subsidiary Prosus reported interim results on Wednesday, 25 November, and the story was a familiar one: the stake in Chinese internet giant Tencent did the heavy lifting, while the group’s own businesses spent heavily to defend their markets.

For the six months to 30 September, Naspers said adjusted earnings per share, the measure it used to call core headline earnings, rose 12% to 240 US cents from 215 US cents, Reuters reported via CNBC Africa. Revenue from continuing operations jumped 55% to $6.4bn, but most of that came from buying food-delivery group Just Eat Takeaway.com (JET) and travel company Despegar. Without acquisitions and currency effects, growth was 11%, TechCentral reported.

Profit from the operating businesses went the other way. Consolidated adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) fell about 6% to $405m, after $344m was put into iFood in Brazil and JET in Europe. Excluding the Tencent dividend, free cash flow was an outflow of $379m, TechCentral said, including $342m on iFood incentives for customers and restaurants.

Paying to defend iFood

The Prosus figures, reported by The National, show where the money went. iFood grew revenue by 20%, but its adjusted EBITDA dropped to $28m from $184m after $278m of investment. In May, chief executive Fabricio Bloisi had warned that iFood would step up spending after rivals committed more than $1.5bn to win market share in Brazil.

Bloisi defended the plan. “We said FY27 would be a year of execution,” he said, adding that the spending was the right choice for sustainable growth. Prosus expects profitability and cash flow to improve in the second half of the financial year and is aiming for a return to EBITDA growth in the 2028 financial year. There was some good news from JET, whose orders grew in September for the first time in 55 months, and from classifieds business OLX, which lifted its margin to 51%.

Investors were not convinced on the day. Prosus shares fell 3.66% on the JSE on Wednesday and were trading at R575.47 in the afternoon, more than 47% below their level a year ago, The National reported. The group says its $5bn share buyback programme is continuing. Before the results, on 19 November, Naspers had closed at R658, about 45% lower than a year earlier, TechCentral noted. That leaves Bloisi a long way from the target in his $100m share award, which requires the combined value of Naspers and Prosus to double to $168bn by June 2028.

Takealot breaks even

The South African business that most local shoppers know had a better half. Takealot Group revenue rose 16% in rand terms to $571m, or about R9.4bn, TechCentral reported; measured in dollars, Business Day put the increase at 27%. Adjusted EBITDA rose 21% to $37m despite higher fuel costs, and the adjusted operating result reached break-even, from a $4m loss a year earlier.

This was the first full half-year since Amazon launched its Prime membership in South Africa on 3 June at R59 a month. The Naspers results do not mention Amazon, but Takealot.com grew revenue 17% in rand terms to $493m, and revenue from its paid TakealotMore membership rose 66%. Naspers said growth was coming from more orders and better conversion, not price increases. Black Friday is on Friday, 27 November.

Food delivery service Mr D grew revenue 11% in rand terms to $78m. Its grocery sales grew 31% and now make up 27% of the value of goods sold on the app, where it competes with Checkers Sixty60 and Pick n Pay. Media24, the group’s news and publishing arm, widened its adjusted EBITDA loss to $11m from $9m on revenue of $48m.
