Fear grips Ekurhuleni as ninth body found in serial killings probe

The Southern Wire Desk·Johannesburg·Saturday, 19 September 2026

A ninth body has been recovered in Ekurhuleni, east of Johannesburg, deepening a serial killings investigation that has gripped the East Rand since the first victim was found in Kempton Park in mid-July. The latest discovery, made in Dawn Park, extends a pattern investigators say spans several suburbs and more than two months, with no clear geographic boundary to the killer or killers' movements.

Police have formally identified four of the nine victims: Dineo Motapane, 38; Gracious Nkomo, 28; Elizabeth Moselakgomo, 38; and Itumeleng Kekana, 32. Detectives are still working to confirm the identities of the remaining five women, a process complicated by the condition in which several bodies were found and by the need to trace next of kin across different parts of the country.

At least one suspect has appeared in court in connection with the case, though investigators have been careful not to rule out the possibility that more than one person is responsible. The pattern of the killings — the age range of the victims, the method, and the disposal sites — has led some detectives to describe the case privately as one of the most complex serial investigations the province has handled in years.

The case has drawn national attention. President Cyril Ramaphosa has publicly instructed police leadership to leave "no stone unturned," a directive that has translated into an expanded task force and additional forensic resources being funnelled into the investigation. Authorities have also put up a reward exceeding R400,000 for information leading to further arrests, a figure large enough to signal how seriously the case is being treated at a national level.

For residents of Ekurhuleni, the toll has been less about statistics and more about a creeping sense of unease. Community patrols have grown more active in some of the affected suburbs, and local safety forums say they have seen a noticeable shift in how women in the area plan their movements after dark. One officer involved in the operation put it bluntly: this is being treated internally not as a routine murder docket, but as a manhunt.

The Southern Wire will continue following developments in this case as they unfold.
