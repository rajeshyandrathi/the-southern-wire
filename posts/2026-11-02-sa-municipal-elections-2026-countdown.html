South Africa counts down to high-stakes municipal elections as GNU tensions boil over

The Southern Wire Desk·Monday, 2 November 2026

In two days, more than 26,000 voting stations across South Africa's nine provinces will open their doors at seven in the morning and won't close until nine that night, putting 10,526 council seats up for grabs in the biggest single test of public opinion since the 2024 general election that birthed the Government of National Unity. The Electoral Commission has certified 136,790 candidates to contest Wednesday's local government poll, and by the time counting finishes, South Africans will have a far clearer picture of whether the coalition experiment governing them nationally still has the public's confidence at the municipal level, where potholes, water outages and billing disputes decide elections far more than grand political theory does.

Getting to polling day has been its own fight. A wave of disqualifications — 1,596 proportional-representation candidates and 3,500 ward candidates, most struck off for failing to meet signature thresholds required of independents — triggered a legal scramble that ran all the way to the Constitutional Court. The Electoral Court had upheld several of the exclusions, but a later Constitutional Court ruling reversed course for the ANC specifically, confirming that all 181 of the party's disputed candidates will now appear on ballots in six municipalities: Ngquza Hill, Port St Johns, Walter Sisulu and Sundays River Valley in the Eastern Cape, uMshwathi in KwaZulu-Natal, and the Mangaung Metro in the Free State. It was a result the ANC fought hard for and one that has left scars inside the coalition that governs the country.

Those scars are now visible at the very top of the GNU. ANC secretary-general Fikile Mbalula has called for the DA's continued role in the national coalition to be reviewed, after the DA publicly opposed the ANC's court bid to have those 181 candidates reinstated — a position the ANC reads as a party willing to use the courts against a governing partner when it suits a local contest. President Cyril Ramaphosa has moved quickly to contain the fallout, rejecting any suggestion that a municipal-level dispute should be allowed to destabilise the national arrangement his office has spent two and a half years trying to hold together. Whether that containment holds past Wednesday night, when real results start replacing political posturing, is an open question.

Nowhere will the stakes be sharper than in Tshwane, the capital metro that has become this election's marquee contest. A field stacked with political heavyweights — Helen Zille, Herman Mashaba, Mkhuleko Hlengwa and incumbent Geordin Hill-Lewis all have skin in the race — means no single outcome looks obvious, and no analyst is confidently predicting an outright majority for anyone. It is shaping up as a near-perfect illustration of what this election has become nationally: a contest less about which party wins outright and more about who ends up holding the balance of power once the horse-trading starts.

That horse-trading will have a new player at the table. The MK Party, formed too late to properly contest the 2021 local polls, is fighting its first nationwide municipal election as the country's third-largest political force, and how it performs in councils from KwaZulu-Natal to the metros will say a great deal about whether its 2024 breakthrough was a protest vote or something more durable. Smaller parties, too, are positioning themselves as kingmakers in the dozens of councils where no single party is expected to cross 50%.

By Thursday morning, South Africa will likely still be counting — but the shape of the result should already be visible: a patchwork of hung councils stitched together by coalition deals, a verdict on the ANC's drawn-out mayoral strategy of naming candidates only after the fact, and a fresh data point on whether a government built on compromise at the national level can survive contact with the far more local, far more personal politics of who fixes the streetlights.
