Eight border and police officials arrested at Van Rooyen’s Gate over alleged smuggling of people from Lesotho

Three Border Management Authority officials and five police officers were picked up before dawn on Friday, accused of helping Lesotho nationals enter South Africa illegally. Three Lesotho nationals were arrested earlier.

Crime Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Three BMA officials and five SAPS officers arrested early on Friday at the Van Rooyen’s Gate port of entry
  • They are accused of helping Lesotho nationals cross into South Africa illegally
  • Three Lesotho nationals were arrested earlier, bringing the total to 11; another officer has been subpoenaed
  • Suspects are expected to face corruption and Immigration Act charges

Eight officials who were supposed to guard one of South Africa’s busiest crossings with Lesotho were arrested in the early hours of Friday, accused of doing the opposite. Three Border Management Authority (BMA) officials and five police officers working at the Van Rooyen’s Gate port of entry, near Wepener in the Free State, were taken into custody in a joint operation.

The BMA says the group allegedly helped Lesotho nationals enter South Africa illegally. According to TimesLIVE, three Lesotho nationals had already been arrested earlier in the same investigation, which brings the number of people arrested to 11. One more police officer has been subpoenaed to appear in court.

The operation brought together the BMA’s own integrity unit, police crime intelligence, the Free State organised crime unit, the Department of Home Affairs and the Tactical Response Team from Bloemfontein. The suspects were being held for further processing on Friday and are expected to face charges of corruption and contravening the Immigration Act. A court date had not been announced.

A pattern at the Lesotho border

This is not the first case of its kind at the Lesotho border this year. In October a BMA official was arrested with 22 stamped Lesotho passports, and in July another was arrested over an alleged R600 bribe to stamp a Lesotho national’s passport illegally. Lesotho is entirely surrounded by South Africa, and many people cross daily for work, trade and family.

BMA Commissioner Dr Michael Masiapato said the authority would not tolerate conduct that damages its integrity. “If you see something, say it,” he said, appealing to the public and to other officials to report corruption.

The authority, which took over border policing functions from several departments in 2023, has been trying to show that it can clean up ports of entry. It says it has stopped about 500,000 attempted illegal entries, a figure The Citizen reported alongside news of Friday’s arrests.

Why it matters

Corruption at a border post undermines more than immigration control. Officials who can be paid to let people through can also be paid to look away from stolen vehicles, drugs, cash or weapons. That is why the case involves organised crime detectives and crime intelligence, not only immigration officers.

It also matters for the many honest Basotho and South Africans who use Van Rooyen’s Gate legally every day. Long queues and delays at the crossing are a familiar complaint, and the BMA says rooting out officials who sell access is part of making the system work for people who follow the rules.

The suspects are expected to appear in court in the coming days. The Southern Wire will report on the charges once they are formally put to the accused. They are presumed innocent until proven guilty.
