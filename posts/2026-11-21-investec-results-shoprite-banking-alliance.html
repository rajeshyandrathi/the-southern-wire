Investec’s South African profits jump 14%, and it will run the bank behind Shoprite’s new money services

The banking group’s local business carried its half-year results, and it has finalised a partnership that will let Shoprite offer banking services in close to 3,000 stores under its own brand. Investors were unimpressed by rising costs.

Business Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Southern Africa operating profit rose 14% to £270.6 million in the six months to September
  • Group adjusted earnings per share rose 7.4% to 43.5 pence; interim dividend of 18.5 pence
  • Investec has finalised an alliance banking deal with Shoprite covering close to 3,000 stores and digital channels
  • Shares fell as much as 9% on Thursday as costs grew faster than revenue

Investec’s South African business did the heavy lifting in the banking group’s half-year results, released on Thursday. Operating profit from Southern Africa rose 14% to £270.6 million in the six months to the end of September, helped by stronger lending and lower funding costs, according to EWN and Business Tech Africa.

At group level the picture was more modest. Adjusted earnings per share rose 7.4% to 43.5 pence, which EWN described as muted, and return on equity was 13.5%, inside the group’s target range of 13% to 17%. The board declared an interim dividend of 18.5 pence a share. Business Tech Africa reported that operating costs rose 9.7% while revenue grew 6.7%, and the shares fell as much as 9% in Johannesburg and London on the day.

Group chief executive Fani Titi said Investec had stayed focused on supporting clients while investing in the business for long-term growth. Private banking client numbers grew in both South Africa and the UK.

The Shoprite deal

The bigger story for ordinary South Africans is a partnership that Investec says it finalised alongside the results. It will provide the banking licence and systems behind banking services offered by the Shoprite Group, under Shoprite’s brand rather than its own. Investec South Africa chief executive Cumesh Moodliar told EWN the arrangement covers close to 3,000 stores as well as digital channels.

Moodliar stressed that this is not Investec moving into mass-market retail banking under its own name. “Investec is not taking its brand directly into the retailer’s consumer base,” he said. Instead the bank will support Shoprite in delivering services to its own customers. Neither company has given a launch date.

Shoprite is one of the country’s biggest retailers, and its stores are often the most convenient place to shop in townships and small towns. A banking offering backed by a licensed bank would let it serve those customers in a new way, in a market where low-cost banks have grown quickly by serving working South Africans.

What it means for customers

For shoppers, the practical effect could be more banking services available where they already buy groceries, potentially at lower cost than traditional branches. Details such as fees, account types and when products will be available have not been announced, so it is too early to say how the offering will compare.

For Investec, a bank known for serving wealthier clients, the deal opens a new source of income without the expense of building its own mass-market brand. The market reaction suggests investors are more focused for now on costs, but the partnership is one to watch as South Africa’s retail banking market keeps getting more crowded.
