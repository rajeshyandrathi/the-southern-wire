Leaked records put Russia’s war dead above 700,000 as missiles hit a Kyiv mall

An analysis of leaked Russian statistics data estimates that more than 720,000 soldiers have died in the war. The Kremlin told people to ignore it, hours before ballistic missiles struck a shopping centre in Kyiv.

World Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • Independent Russian outlets Meduza and Mediazona estimate that more than 720,000 Russian soldiers had died in the war by early autumn 2026
  • About 490,000 of those deaths already appear in official records, according to the leaked statistics data
  • Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said only the Defence Ministry can publish casualty figures
  • Ballistic missiles hit a shopping centre and two other buildings in Kyiv late on Wednesday, injuring four people, two of them seriously

At least 700,000 Russian soldiers have probably died in the war against Ukraine, according to a new estimate built on leaked data from Russia’s federal statistics agency, Rosstat. The independent Russian news outlets Meduza and Mediazona published the analysis on Monday, 23 November. Their model puts the toll at more than 720,000 by the start of autumn 2026, once deaths that have not yet been registered are included.

The Kremlin brushed the figure aside on Wednesday. Asked about the reports at a briefing, spokesman Dmitry Peskov said only the Defence Ministry can release casualty numbers, and he advised people to disregard “any other data”, Meduza reported, citing Interfax. The ministry has not published a death toll since September 2022.

Hours later, Russia launched a ballistic missile attack on Kyiv. Air raid sirens sounded across the capital and much of the country at about 10.46pm local time on Wednesday, and missiles struck a shopping centre, a building in an industrial area and another non-residential building in the Desnianskyi district on the city’s left bank.

How the estimate was made

The leak gave journalists a rare look at Russia’s death records. Meduza says that deaths already on record add up to about 490,000. Other outlets working with the same data reached similar numbers: Novaya Gazeta Europe also counted about 490,000, while iStories and Proekt each counted about 474,000 clear-cut military deaths.

To estimate deaths that have not yet been registered, Meduza used a statistical model borrowed from epidemiology. It combines the leaked death records with missing-person notices posted by relatives, the national probate registry and published lists of the dead. It also accounts for the different delays in the three ways a soldier’s death can be recorded: a medical report, an official death certificate or a court ruling. Estimates from other outlets that include unregistered deaths range from 611,000 (iStories) to 670,000 (Novaya Gazeta Europe).

The leak also showed more about who is dying. The outlet Agentstvo found that the war is now the leading cause of death for Russian men aged 20 to 39, and that more than 1,000 men aged 18 and 19 may have died soon after signing military contracts. iStories found that explosions and shrapnel caused 92% of military deaths in the combat zone. Novaya Gazeta Europe estimated that 120 to 180 Russian soldiers died for every square kilometre of territory captured in 2026.

Kyiv under fire again

By early Thursday, four people had been injured in the Kyiv attack, the Kyiv Post reported. Two were in hospital in a serious condition and two were treated and sent home. Mayor Vitali Klitschko told residents on Telegram that the city was under ballistic missile attack and urged them to stay in shelters.

The strike came a day after President Volodymyr Zelensky said a deal to deliver 48 Patriot interceptor missiles, already paid for by Western allies, was still waiting for the signature of US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth. Kyiv has been hit repeatedly this month: Russian strikes on 20 November damaged three of the city’s bridges. Overnight into Wednesday, an attack killed one person in the Bila Tserkva district of Kyiv region, and a late strike on Tuesday killed two people, including a teenager, in Odesa region.

The attacks come as Poland plans a no-fly zone along its border with Ukraine and has asked NATO allies to consider shooting down Russian missiles over Ukrainian territory. No decision has been made on that request.
