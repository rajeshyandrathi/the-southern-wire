“We are restructuring Eskom”: Ramaphosa says growth reforms are on track and nothing is for sale

Answering questions in the National Council of Provinces, the president said 60% of Operation Vulindlela’s second-phase reforms are complete or on track. He also said Eskom and the rail network will stay in state hands.

Business Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • 60% of the 30 phase-two Vulindlela reforms are complete or on track
  • Ramaphosa said South Africa has gone more than 550 days without load-shedding
  • Eskom is being restructured, not unbundled or sold, he said
  • The first private train operators are expected on the rail network in 2027
  • The government does not expect to avoid losing AGOA access on 1 January 2027

President Cyril Ramaphosa told the National Council of Provinces on Tuesday, 24 November, that 60% of the 30 reform programmes in the second phase of Operation Vulindlela are complete or on track, Business Day reported. Another 30% are under way, and the remaining 10% need targeted attention.

Operation Vulindlela is the joint Presidency and Treasury programme to remove obstacles to growth in energy, logistics, water and other areas. Its first phase eased the electricity crisis, opened up access to network infrastructure and modernised the visa system. The second phase began in 2025.

The president used the session to reject claims that the reforms amount to privatisation. “Our objective is not to privatise any of our state companies,” he said, according to EWN.

Eskom and rail stay with the state

NCOP member Makuduele Mampuru asked whether the reforms would push up costs for working-class households, The National reported. In reply, Ramaphosa drew a line between unbundling and restructuring. “Unbundling means we’re taking one company and selling it off,” he said, adding, “We are restructuring Eskom.”

Under the plan, an independent state-owned transmission company will run the grid, Eskom will focus on generating power, and other generators will operate alongside it. Ramaphosa said generation, transmission and distribution would all remain state-owned. On rail, he said private operators would be brought in to invest in lines such as the iron-ore route while the state keeps ownership. “We run the rail; it is profitable, and we tax it,” he said.

Business Day reported that Ramaphosa listed several energy figures. South Africa has gone more than 550 days without load-shedding, and Eskom has made a profit for two years in a row. More than 7GW of new generation is connected to the grid, and households and businesses have installed more than 9GW of rooftop solar. Trading rules for a competitive wholesale electricity market are due in the coming months.

He said the decline at Transnet had been halted and that trade volumes were recovering. Cabinet has approved the creation of an independent state-owned national rail authority, and the first private train operators are expected on the network in 2027.

Water, municipalities and AGOA

Ramaphosa pointed to the new White Paper on Local Government, which proposes licensing municipal water and electricity providers and ring-fencing the money they collect for infrastructure. Municipalities that fail to meet licence conditions could have other providers appointed. A national water action plan has five areas of work, including infrastructure investment and action against corruption.

On trade, he confirmed that South Africa’s exclusion from the US African Growth and Opportunity Act takes effect on 1 January 2027. Talks with Washington continue, but the government does not expect a different outcome and is looking for other markets, including Chinese tariff-free access for African products. “We are working on seeing how best we can protect the jobs of our workers,” he said.
