Bafana Bafana's AFCON qualifying double-header starts at Orlando Stadium on Friday

Sport Desk

Pitso Mosimane names his teams carefully, and the 27-man squad he announced for this month's double-header against Kenya is built around a simple fact: Bafana Bafana are already sitting on top of their AFCON 2027 qualifying group, and a coach in that position generally wants to defend the advantage rather than gamble it away. South Africa goes into Friday's home leg at Orlando Stadium in Soweto having beaten both Guinea and Eritrea in earlier Group D fixtures, results that leave the squad needing points rather than miracles to keep control of qualification.

The squad Mosimane has assembled blends established internationals with a handful of names still building their reputations at this level, among them Mbekezeli Mbokazi and Siyanda Ndlovu, both included in a group the coach clearly intends to rotate through two matches in the space of five days. That scheduling detail matters as much as any single selection: after Friday's fixture in Soweto, the squad travels to Nairobi for the return leg on Tuesday, 17 November, a turnaround tight enough that squad depth, not just a starting eleven, will decide how South Africa's week actually goes.

Orlando Stadium itself is part of the story. Tickets for the home leg have been priced at R100 for adults and R40 for children aged five to twelve, pricing clearly designed to fill a stadium with deep historical ties to South African football rather than to maximise gate revenue from a single marquee fixture. Soweto crowds have a reputation for noise that travelling teams find genuinely difficult to play through, and Mosimane's squad will be counting on exactly that atmosphere to help settle the tie before the away leg complicates things.

Kenya is not an easy opponent to write off, even with South Africa currently ahead of them in the group. Co-hosting the 2027 tournament alongside Uganda and Tanzania gives Kenya an obvious incentive to arrive in qualifying with momentum, since nothing would sting more for a host nation than failing to qualify for its own tournament. That context adds an edge to fixtures that might otherwise read as routine group-stage business on paper.

For Bafana Bafana, the calculation is more straightforward: win both legs, or even split them favourably, and South Africa all but secures passage toward a tournament that will be played on the continent's eastern side for the first time in years. Lose ground now, with the group this tight, and qualification becomes a far more complicated conversation heading into 2027. Friday night in Soweto is where that conversation starts.
