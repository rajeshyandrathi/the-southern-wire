Springboks rout Italy 66-7 in Turin, the biggest win this fixture has ever produced

Sport Desk

Whatever Rassie Erasmus wanted from reverting to a near full-strength side for the Nations Championship's second phase, this went well beyond it. The Springboks demolished Italy 66-7 at the Allianz Stadium in Turin on Saturday, scoring nine tries to one and turning what had been billed as a genuine litmus test into the heaviest defeat Italy have suffered in this fixture. By half-time the scoreboard already read 45-0, and the only question left for the second forty minutes was how far South Africa were prepared to push the margin.

Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu was the game's standout performer, finishing with 26 points from a try, nine conversions and a penalty, a personal haul that on its own would have beaten most international sides outright. The fly-half's kicking was faultless throughout, and his try came amid a first-half period in which the Springbok pack simply could not be stopped at the collision, repeatedly bludgeoning gaps through an Italian defence that had come into the match already weakened by injuries in the front five.

The result is even more striking set against the week South Africa had leading into it. Lock Salmaan Moerat was ruled out in the days before kickoff, denying Erasmus one of his most experienced tight-five options at exactly the moment selection had shifted back to a frontline combination. It made no visible difference. The replacements who came into the pack performed as though the plan had never changed, and the bench Erasmus named — Jan-Hendrik Wessels, Gerhard Steenekamp, Wilco Louw, Lood de Jager, Cameron Hanekom, Paul de Villiers, Grant Williams and Manie Libbok among them — extended the damage rather than managing a lead.

For Italy, there was almost nothing to salvage. Federico Ruzza's try, which came with the Springboks already well clear, was the Azzurri's only answer on the night, and a side that has spent recent seasons building a reputation for springing upsets on bigger nations instead found themselves comprehensively outmuscled from the opening minutes. The gulf between a full-strength Springbok side and almost everyone else in the tournament, which had looked uncertain after a weekend of rotation and experimentation, reasserted itself with brutal clarity.

What the result means for the rest of the Nations Championship's second phase is now the obvious question. South Africa still face France and Ireland in the matches to come, opponents who will have taken careful note of a 66-7 scoreline and of just how little the loss of a frontline lock seemed to cost Erasmus's side on the night. For a Springbok group that spent last weekend being quietly second-guessed over its depth, Turin was as emphatic an answer as any coach could have asked for.
