A week into Johannesburg's mayoral countdown, the clock is the only thing moving

The Southern Wire Desk·Johannesburg

Six days after Johannesburg's new council was supposed to start settling into its seats, nobody has been handed the mayoral chain, and nobody looks close to it. The 4 November municipal election left the 270-seat council without a majority anywhere near reach: the DA finished first on the strength of its own vote, the ANC's long dominance collapsed to a fraction of what it once commanded, and ActionSA holds just enough seats to decide who gets to govern without actually being large enough to govern alone. The constitutional clock that is supposed to force a resolution — fourteen days from the council's first sitting to elect a mayor — is already most of the way through its countdown, and the three parties that matter are no closer to an arrangement than they were the morning after the vote.

The obstacle has a name, and it is Helen Zille. Herman Mashaba has spent the week publicly denying that ActionSA issued any ultimatum over her candidacy, even as his own chairperson, Michael Beaumont, has told reporters the party has "major misgivings and concerns" about a coalition built around her, and Mashaba himself has said on record that if the DA wants ActionSA's votes, "they must remove Helen Zille." Zille has answered in kind, accusing Mashaba of having sold the DA out to the Economic Freedom Fighters during his own turbulent run as Johannesburg's mayor. Neither insult has moved the arithmetic. The DA needs ActionSA or the ANC to reach a majority; ActionSA will not take Zille; and a week of trading jabs in public has produced no visible counteroffer from either side.

What has changed, almost by accident, is who gets to negotiate for the ANC. An electoral overhang — triggered when a party wins more ward seats on the ground than its proportional vote share would normally justify — wiped out the effect of the ANC's own party list in Johannesburg. Deputy mayor Loyiso Masuku, first on that list and the presumptive ANC mayoral candidate, and outgoing mayor Dada Morero, fourth on it, both lose their seats entirely under the mechanism, regardless of where they were ranked. It leaves the ANC needing to find and empower a new face to lead its side of the talks at the precise moment its leverage over the DA and ActionSA is most valuable, since the party that can swing either of the other two toward a majority effectively gets to pick the next mayor.

The problem is not unique to Johannesburg, which is part of why it has drawn so much attention. Thirty-three of the country's 213 municipal councils emerged from last week's vote with an overhang affecting a combined 71 seats, the overwhelming majority of them involving the ANC, and seven councils nationally now carry an outright majority that no single party actually won at the ballot box. Johannesburg's version is simply the highest-profile case, playing out in the country's largest city with the most recognisable cast of characters.

None of that changes what happens if the fourteen days run out without a vote. The Municipal Structures Act does not pause for ongoing talks; a council that fails to elect a mayor within its window risks provincial intervention, an outcome every party involved claims to want to avoid and that none of them seem to be negotiating hard enough to prevent. Johannesburg has burned through a revolving cast of mayors for five straight years. A week into this round's countdown, the only thing that has moved is the deadline itself, creeping closer with no deal in sight.
