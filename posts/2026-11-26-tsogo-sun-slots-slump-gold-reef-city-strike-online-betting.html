Tsogo Sun hit by a slot-machine slump and a Gold Reef City strike, as online betting takes off

The casino and hotel group says income and profit slipped in the half-year, and that October and November have been especially tough. Its online betting business grew revenue by 43% and is now clearly profitable.

Business Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Total income fell 2% to R5.43bn and adjusted EBITDA fell 4% to R1.65bn in the six months to September
  • Headline earnings per share still rose 7% to 79c, helped by share buybacks; the interim dividend stays at 15c
  • Casino revenue fell 3% to R4bn as slot machine takings dropped
  • A strike at Gold Reef City cost an estimated R43m in EBITDA
  • Online betting revenue rose 43% to R193m, and its EBITDA rose to R50m from R13m

Casino and hotel group Tsogo Sun is feeling the squeeze. The company, which owns casinos such as Montecasino and Gold Reef City, reported on Wednesday, 25 November, that total income for the six months to the end of September fell 2% to R5.43bn. Adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) fell 4% to R1.65bn, and headline earnings fell 2% to R757m, Business Day reported.

Shareholders fared a little better. Because the group has been buying back and cancelling its own shares, headline earnings per share rose 7% to 79 cents. The interim dividend was kept at 15 cents a share.

The main problem was slot machines. Revenue at the casino and hotel division fell 3% to R4bn, and its adjusted EBITDA, excluding the theme park, fell 6%. The company also warned that things got worse after the reporting period. The company, led by chief executive Chris du Toit, said October and November had been “particularly tough trading months”, The National reported.

Strike at Gold Reef City

Gold Reef City in Johannesburg had an especially difficult half. Members of the Transport, Retail and General Workers Union walked out at the casino and theme park after wage talks deadlocked, and the industrial action cost an estimated R43m in EBITDA, according to News24’s report. The company said the precinct was affected by both the strike and intimidation, and that the theme park’s adjusted EBITDA fell 37%.

Recovery has been slow, the group said, although upgrades to the casino gaming floor have been finished and a rebuilt 5-D cinema at the theme park is expected by June next year.

Market analysts were blunt. Simon Brown of Just One Lap called the numbers “poor in a tough market” and said online gaming was hurting land-based casinos. Wilhelm Hertzog of Rozendal Partners described a “slow bleed” in the parts of the business other than online, with casinos under pressure since the post-Covid recovery peaked in 2023.

Online is the bright spot

Tsogo Sun’s own online betting business, which runs playTSOGO and bet.co.za, is where the growth is. Gross gaming revenue after bonuses rose 43% to R193m, and adjusted EBITDA jumped to R50m from R13m, lifting the margin to 26% from 10%. The division stopped making losses in August 2025 and has hired a new chief executive with online experience.

Limited payout machines, the slot machines found in pubs and taverns, held steady, with adjusted EBITDA of R282m. The bingo business struggled with long leases, a shortage of good new sites and regulatory delays, and the group is moving to smaller, cheaper outlets. It also said illegal gambling operators remain a problem, and that it is watching proposed tobacco laws that would ban smoking areas in casinos.

The balance sheet is in better shape. Net debt fell to R5.99bn, and the group says it is in the advanced stages of refinancing R4.45bn of debt facilities that mature in 2027. It spent R344m on capital projects in the half and expects about R1.1bn for the full year. A planned new casino in Somerset West in the Western Cape is going ahead, which du Toit said was positive for the group’s future growth.
