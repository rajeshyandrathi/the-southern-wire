Israeli forces pull back from Hebron after a deadly checkpoint attack and days-long manhunt

World Desk

Israeli forces have withdrawn from positions around Hebron in the occupied West Bank, bringing to a close a multi-day operation launched after two Israeli soldiers were killed at a checkpoint on the city's outskirts. The operation, which involved an extended search for those responsible, placed much of the city under tight movement restrictions for days, disrupting daily life for tens of thousands of Palestinian residents in one of the West Bank's largest and most volatile urban centres.

The checkpoint killing itself has been claimed or praised by several armed factions operating in the West Bank, though Israeli authorities have not publicly confirmed which group, if any, directed the attack, treating it instead as part of a broader pattern of violence that has become more frequent across the territory over the past two years. Checkpoints, a near-constant feature of Palestinian life in the West Bank, have increasingly become flashpoints themselves, sites of both routine friction and, as this week showed, lethal violence.

The withdrawal does not represent any broader de-escalation. Israeli military officials have described the pullback as the natural conclusion of a specific operation rather than a shift in posture, and have signalled that the broader pattern of raids, arrests and settlement-related friction across the West Bank, already running at its highest tempo in years, is expected to continue. Palestinian residents and rights groups, for their part, describe an occupation that has intensified steadily since the war in Gaza began, with the West Bank increasingly treated by both sides as an extension of the same conflict.

The timing matters diplomatically. The incident and the ensuing operation come as the broader Gaza ceasefire framework brokered with heavy American involvement enters what officials have described as its active implementation phase, a process already complicated by sporadic violence in Gaza itself and now shadowed by renewed friction in the West Bank. Analysts tracking the broader peace process say instability in Hebron, while geographically separate from Gaza, feeds directly into the same question hanging over the entire framework: whether a ceasefire negotiated under intense external pressure can hold once that pressure is no longer the only force at work on the ground.

For now, life in Hebron is returning to its usual uneasy rhythm, checkpoints reopened, movement restrictions eased, but with a death toll on both sides that has added one more grievance to a conflict already carrying decades of them. Few in the city expect this to be the last such episode before whatever comes next in the region's wider diplomatic process becomes clear.
