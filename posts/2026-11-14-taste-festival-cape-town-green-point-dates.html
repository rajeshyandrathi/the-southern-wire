Cape Town's food festival finally has an address: Green Point Cricket Club, two weekends from now

Lifestyle Desk

After months of being described only in vague terms — "the last weekend of November," "Green Point" — Cape Town's debut edition of Taste of Cape Town finally has the specifics that turn an anticipated event into one people can actually plan around. The global Taste Festival's first South African outing runs on 28 and 29 November at Green Point Cricket Club, with sessions scheduled from 11am to 9pm across both days, giving the city a concrete date to build its last big food weekend of the year around.

The format follows the template that has made Taste Festival a recognisable brand in the cities it has already landed in: an open-air ground given over to the city's most sought-after restaurants, chefs, wine estates and specialty producers, all operating under one roof rather than scattered across a dozen separate venues. Organisers have built the 2026 edition around the theme "Feed Every Sense," promising an experience built as much around atmosphere and presentation as around the food itself.

Attendance projections put the two-day crowd at more than 12,000 guests, a number that would make this one of the larger standalone food events Cape Town has hosted in recent years. The event is being produced by the Allure Group, the organisation responsible for bringing the broader Taste Festival franchise into the South African market after what local coverage has described as a lengthy courtship between the city and the festival's international organisers.

The SABC's confirmation as official broadcast partner adds a layer of national visibility that most Cape Town food events never reach. A televised food festival, rather than one documented only through social media and local lifestyle press, signals an ambition to make Taste of Cape Town a fixture on the country's cultural calendar rather than a one-off curiosity, and gives the festival a built-in audience well beyond the 12,000 people who will actually walk through Green Point's gates.

For a city whose culinary reputation has increasingly been cited in global rankings of top food destinations, landing a festival with this kind of international pedigree and broadcast backing fits a pattern rather than arriving as a surprise. What Cape Town gets out of the actual weekend, beyond the headline numbers, will depend on whether the execution matches the ambition organisers have set for themselves — a question the city will have a definitive answer to inside the next fortnight.
