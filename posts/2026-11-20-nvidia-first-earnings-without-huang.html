Nvidia’s first earnings call without Jensen Huang: “business as usual”

Interim chief executive Colette Kress told investors the chipmaker is steady after Huang’s exit, forecast revenue above expectations, and watched the shares rise more than 4% after hours. The board says Huang is not coming back.

Tech Desk·Friday, 20 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Interim CEO Colette Kress led her first earnings call
  • Revenue forecast beat expectations
  • Shares up more than 4% after hours
  • Board chairman rules out Jensen Huang’s return

For the first time in Nvidia’s life as a giant, its earnings call did not feature Jensen Huang. Interim chief executive Colette Kress, long the company’s finance chief, led the call and described the business as business as usual. She promised disciplined use of capital and said suppliers and partners continue to back the company.

The numbers helped her case. Nvidia forecast fourth-quarter revenue above analysts’ expectations and pointed to sales growth of around 75% in its 2028 financial year. The shares rose more than 4% in extended trading.

Huang’s departure comes after the board opened an independent investigation earlier this month, which we reported on, covering a personal relationship and reports that he may have been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. Board chairman Harvey Jones has now ruled out his return, despite public calls for it from employees, industry figures and US President Donald Trump. The search for a permanent chief executive is under way, and analysts have named Nvidia executive Ian Buck among the possible candidates.

Separately, California’s attorney general has issued a subpoena to Nvidia as part of an investigation into whether its advanced chips reached China in breach of US export controls, adding to the legal questions around the company.

Nvidia’s chips power most of the world’s AI systems, so its stability matters far beyond Silicon Valley. For now, the message to markets is that demand is strong enough to carry the company through a leadership crisis that would have shaken almost any other firm.

Who could lead Nvidia next

Replacing a founder who led the company for more than three decades is one of the hardest jobs in business. The board is looking for a permanent chief executive, and analysts have named Ian Buck, a long-serving Nvidia executive closely linked to its AI software, as one possible candidate. No timeline has been given.

In the meantime, Kress, who has run Nvidia’s finances for years and is well known to investors, gives the company continuity at a delicate moment.

Why it matters beyond Silicon Valley

Nvidia’s performance is often treated as a barometer for the whole AI industry. Strong numbers suggest that big tech companies and AI labs are still spending heavily on computing power. A sudden slowdown would ripple through chipmakers, cloud providers and the companies building AI products.

For now, the forecast suggests that spending boom is still running, even as questions grow about whether the returns will justify it.
