Jilly Cooper's gloriously trashy Rivals returns for round two this week

Culture Desk

Rivals returns for its second season on Disney+ and Hulu this week, picking up the story that turned Jilly Cooper's 1988 bonkbuster novel into one of streaming's more unlikely hits. The first season, set in the gleaming, ruthless world of 1980s independent television in the fictional English county of Rutshire, built its audience on a formula that should not work nearly as well as it does: lavish period production values wrapped around scripts that take enormous, unashamed pleasure in being trashy, funny, and occasionally outrageous in roughly equal measure.

Much of that tone traces back to the source material itself. Cooper's novel was a publishing phenomenon precisely because it refused to apologise for its own excess, stacking boardroom warfare, sexual intrigue, and class comedy into a single sprawling narrative engine. Adapting that sensibility for a streaming audience in the 2020s without sanding down its edges was always the risk; the first season's reception suggested the show found a way to keep the novel's nerve intact while still working as prestige-adjacent television.

David Tennant anchored that first run as the magnetic, self-regarding television executive at the centre of the Corinium Television power struggle, in a performance that leaned fully into the character's vanity rather than softening it for sympathy. The ensemble around him did much of the same work, treating the material's heightened melodrama as an invitation rather than an embarrassment, which is a large part of why the show found an audience well beyond viewers who had read Cooper's novels decades earlier.

A second season was never really in doubt once the first had landed as well as it did commercially, and the appetite for more of Rutshire's scheming broadcasters and ambitious rivals has clearly persisted in the time since. Streaming platforms do not often get handed source material this confident in its own voice, and Disney and Hulu appear to be leaning into that rather than trying to reinvent a show that already knew exactly what kind of entertainment it wanted to be.

What the new episodes do with that confidence is the question this week answers. Period soap operas built around industry warfare live or die on whether the schemes feel like they still have somewhere new to go once the initial shock value of the premise has worn off. Rivals earned the benefit of the doubt with its first outing. Round two is where it has to prove the formula has legs beyond a single season's worth of surprises.
