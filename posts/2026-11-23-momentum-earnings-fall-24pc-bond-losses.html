Momentum’s earnings fall 24% as the bond sell-off hits insurers, and its shares drop 8%

Rising South African bond yields caused losses on Momentum’s bond portfolios in the three months to September. The group expects much of the damage to reverse as the bonds approach maturity.

Business Desk·Monday, 23 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Normalised headline earnings fell 24% to R1.34bn in the first quarter of the financial year
  • Value of new business fell 28% to R105m and the new business margin slipped to 0.5%
  • Health earnings rose 59%, while investments and corporate earnings more than halved
  • Shares fell 8.04% to about R35 in afternoon trade on Monday

Momentum Group’s earnings fell sharply in the first quarter of its financial year, as the sell-off in South African bonds caused losses on its investment portfolios, Business Day reported on Monday 23 November.

Normalised headline earnings for the three months to September fell 24% to R1.34 billion, from R1.76 billion a year earlier, and normalised headline earnings per share fell 22% to 102c. Market-related variances swung to a R244 million loss, from a R202 million gain.

The value of new business, a key measure of the profit expected from policies sold in the period, fell 28% to R105 million, The National reported, and the new business margin slipped to 0.5% from 0.7%. The present value of new business premiums rose 3% to R23.1 billion.

Where the damage was

The picture across the group was mixed. Retail earnings rose 10% to R280 million and health earnings jumped 59% to R161 million. But investments fell 51% to R152 million, corporate fell 55% to R194 million, Insure fell 29% to R112 million and Africa fell 24% to R145 million. The Africa comparison included R46 million from the sale of the Ghana business. Metropolitan Life’s earnings fell to R174 million from R248 million, The National reported.

Momentum said rising nominal and real bond yields in South Africa caused mark-to-market losses on its bond portfolios, while local equities were negative and global returns weaker. It expects the bond losses to reverse as the bonds approach maturity, and plans to reduce its exposure to interest rates.

Market reaction and outlook

Investors were not reassured. Momentum’s shares fell 8.04% to about R35 in afternoon trade on the JSE, The National reported.

The group said the local outlook remains volatile, pointing to oil prices, pressure on consumers and interest rates, and that it is harder to win new business while households are under strain.

The results show how last week’s sell-off in South African bonds and shares, driven by fears of higher US interest rates and local political uncertainty, is now feeding through into company earnings.
