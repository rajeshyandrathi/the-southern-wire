The Polygamist wins six National Film & TV Awards, and Lillian Dube is honoured for a lifetime’s work

The Polygamist dominated the fourth NFTA ceremony, with Skeem Saam taking three awards and Yoh! Bestie named best feature film. Here are the main winners.

Culture Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • The Polygamist won six awards, including Best Scripted TV Series, Best Director and Best Actress (Gugu Gumede)
  • Skeem Saam won three, including Best TV Drama Series
  • Yoh! Bestie won Best Feature Film; Thobani Nzuza and Tumi Morake took the top film acting awards
  • Dr Lillian Dube received the Lifetime Achievement Award

The Polygamist was the big winner at the fourth National Film & TV Awards, taking six of the 30 categories at a ceremony held on Thursday 19 November at the Theatre of Marcellus at Emperors Palace in Gauteng. The full results were published on Monday, The Media Online and Bizcommunity reported.

Its six awards were Best Scripted TV Series, Best Producer for Gugu Zuma-Ncube, Thuli Zuma and Pepsi Pokane, Best Director for Akin Omotoso, Rolie Nikiwe and Nthabi Tau, Best Actress in a TV Series for Gugu Gumede, Best Supporting Actor in a TV Series for Kenneth Nkosi, and Outstanding Performance for S’dumo Mtshali.

The other big winners

Skeem Saam won three awards: Best TV Drama Series for its 15th season, Best Supporting Actress in a TV Series for Lerato Marabe, and Best Newcomer for Relebogile Mothapo. Yoh! Bestie was named Best Feature Film, and Didie Makobane won Best Supporting Actress in a Film for it. Tropika Island of Treasure took Best Competition Show and Best TV Presenter for Zanele Potelwa.

In film acting, Thobani Nzuza won Best Actor for God’s Work, Tumi Morake won Best Actress for Seriously Single Too, and Abdul Khoza won Best Supporting Actor for Masinga: The Calling. Vuyo Biyela was named Best Actor in a TV Series for Kwa Baba.

SABC 2 was named Best Television or Streaming Network and Tshedza Pictures Best Production Company. Great African Crimes with Mandy Wiener won Best Documentary, Paradys won Best Afrikaans TV Series, Big Brother Mzansi won Best Non-Scripted Series, Celeste Ntuli was Best Comedian and Open Chats Podcast won Best Podcast.

Special honours

Veteran actress Dr Lillian Dube received the Lifetime Achievement Award. Vusi Kunene was honoured for his outstanding contribution to film and television, for his role as Jack Mabaso in Generations, and Leanne Manas for her contribution to news programming on SABC’s Morning Live.

Why it matters

The awards come at a difficult time for the industry. As we reported last week, producers spent much of November pressing government over funding and incentives after talks repeatedly broke down. A strong night for local drama and film is a reminder of what the sector produces, and of the audiences it still draws.
