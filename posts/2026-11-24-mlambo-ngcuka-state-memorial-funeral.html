South Africa honours Phumzile Mlambo-Ngcuka, its first woman deputy president, at a state memorial today

The former deputy president and UN Women chief died on Friday aged 71. A state memorial service is held in Rivonia this afternoon, ahead of a Category 2 state funeral in the Eastern Cape on Saturday.

Politics Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Mlambo-Ngcuka died on Friday 20 November, aged 71, after complications following a medical procedure
  • A state memorial service takes place today at 14:00 at Our Lady of Lourdes Catholic Church in Rivonia
  • The state funeral is on Saturday 28 November at Middledrift in the Eastern Cape
  • Flags fly at half-mast across the country until the evening of 28 November

South Africa says goodbye this week to one of the most influential women of its democratic era. Phumzile Mlambo-Ngcuka, the country’s first woman deputy president and later head of UN Women, died on Friday 20 November at the age of 71, from complications after a recent medical procedure, the Presidency said.

President Cyril Ramaphosa has declared a Category 2 state funeral, with ceremonial elements performed by the South African National Defence Force. A state memorial service will be held today, Tuesday 24 November, at 14:00 at Our Lady of Lourdes Catholic Church in Rivonia, Johannesburg. The funeral takes place on Saturday 28 November at Middledrift in the Eastern Cape.

The national flag is flying at half-mast at all flag stations from Sunday 22 November until the evening of Saturday 28 November, the Presidency said.

A career that ran from the classroom to the UN

Born in Clermont, Durban, Mlambo-Ngcuka was a teacher and anti-apartheid activist before she entered the first democratic Parliament as an ANC MP, the Associated Press reported. She served as deputy minister of trade and industry and then as minister of minerals and energy.

In 2005 President Thabo Mbeki appointed her deputy president after he dismissed Jacob Zuma, and she held the post until 2008. The appointment was controversial at the time because her husband, Bulelani Ngcuka, was the prosecutor who had pursued Zuma, AP noted.

From 2013 to 2021 she was a UN under-secretary-general and executive director of UN Women, where she campaigned on gender equality and violence against women around the world.

Tributes from home and abroad

Ramaphosa called it a very sad moment for South Africans, while Mbeki described her as a very important South African, The Witness reported. The ANC called her a distinguished leader of the movement, and UN Secretary-General António Guterres described her as a lifelong champion of the rights of women and girls, according to AP.

Her death comes during the 16 Days of Activism against gender-based violence, a cause she championed throughout her UN years. She is survived by her husband and five children. A family spokesperson said the family loved her dearly and would miss her deeply.
