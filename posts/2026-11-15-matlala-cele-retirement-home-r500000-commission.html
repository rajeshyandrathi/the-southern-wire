Matlala says he paid Cele for a 'retirement home.' Cele says Matlala is lying.

Crime Desk

Vusimuzi "Cat" Matlala has given the Madlanga Commission a lot of numbers over the past fortnight, and they keep changing shape depending on who is asking. The latest is R500,000, which Matlala says he handed to former police minister Bheki Cele, framed as a thank-you for help retrieving firearms that police had seized from him in December 2024. The commission heard it bluntly: Cele, in Matlala's telling, wanted a retirement home, and got half a million rand toward one. It is a vivid image for a commission that has spent months collecting vivid images, and it arrives attached to a man whose own credibility is the thing actually on trial.

The sum itself is contested before Matlala has even finished explaining it. Earlier testimony attributed to the same events put the figure at R1 million, a number Matlala now dismisses as a fabrication introduced by investigators from the Investigating Directorate Against Corruption. That is not a small discrepancy to wave away. It is the difference between two very different stories about how much influence a sitting Cabinet minister was willing to sell, and Matlala's explanation for the gap is that the people building the case against him altered it, which conveniently shifts blame from his own memory onto the state's own investigators.

Cele has not been silent through this. Matlala has accused the former minister of lying under oath about the nature of their relationship, which means the commission is now working through two mutually exclusive sworn accounts of the same alleged transaction, with no independent witness yet surfaced to break the tie. Evidence leaders have pressed Matlala on precisely this kind of inconsistency throughout his testimony, including contradictions between what he told Parliament's ad hoc committee months ago and what he has told the commission under a different, more searching form of questioning.

Layered on top of the payment dispute is a parallel claim that complicates everything else: Matlala says IDAC investigators wanted Cele arrested and pressed him to fabricate evidence to make that happen. If true, it would mean the body built to clean up policing corruption was itself manufacturing the case against a former minister, which would hand Cele's defence a gift and Matlala's own credibility another wound, since the same testimony asking the commission to believe him on the R500,000 is also accusing his original accusers of inventing a different number. Commissions built on a single witness's shifting account tend to collapse under exactly this kind of internal contradiction.

None of this resolves cleanly, and the commission is reportedly working toward a final report before the month is out, which means Chairperson Mbuyiseli Madlanga's team has limited runway to decide which version of events, if any, survives scrutiny. What is clear is that Matlala's testimony has stopped functioning as a simple account of corruption and started functioning as a test of how much a single compromised witness's word can carry when it keeps rewriting itself from one session to the next.
