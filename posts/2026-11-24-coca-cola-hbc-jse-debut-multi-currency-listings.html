Coca-Cola HBC lists on the JSE, and the exchange says dollar and euro listings are next

The bottler’s secondary listing on Monday came days before it completes a $2.6 billion deal for Coca-Cola Beverages Africa. The JSE, which has lost far more companies than it has gained this year, is planning its biggest change in more than a century.

Business Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Coca-Cola HBC joined the JSE main board on Monday, opening at R996 a share
  • The listing raised no new money; it comes ahead of a $2.6bn deal for 75% of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa
  • The JSE plans to let companies list in currencies other than the rand, Business Day reports
  • eNCA says the JSE recorded 13 delistings and only three new listings this year

One of the world’s biggest Coca-Cola bottlers is now on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange. Swiss-based Coca-Cola HBC, already listed in London and Athens, joined the JSE main board on Monday 23 November through a secondary listing by introduction, meaning existing shares were admitted to trade and no new capital was raised, Reuters reported.

The shares opened at R996, traded near R1,015 by mid-morning and peaked at R1,039.50 before easing to about R990 in the early afternoon, according to Reuters. The company’s market value is about R341 billion, or $19.5 billion.

Why it is listing in Johannesburg

The timing is linked to a large African deal. Coca-Cola HBC is buying a 75% stake in Coca-Cola Beverages Africa (CCBA) for $2.6 billion from The Coca-Cola Company and Gutsche Family Investments. Completion is expected on 27 November, when HBC will issue 21 million new shares to Gutsche Family Investments that start trading on 30 November, Reuters reported.

After the deal, the group will account for about two-thirds of Coca-Cola system volumes in Africa and will be valued at about R370 billion. Chief executive Zoran Bogdanovic said the listing underpins the company’s long-term commitment to South Africa and the wider continent.

A bigger change for the JSE

The listing comes as the exchange prepares a more fundamental shift. Business Day reported on Tuesday that the JSE plans to become a multi-currency platform, the first such change in more than 135 years. It is part of the government’s synthetic financial centre reforms, for which the Reserve Bank published draft regulations in October.

The aim is to attract back some of the more than R5 trillion of South African assets managed offshore, with bonds in euros or dollars expected to be the main early use. JSE capital markets head Helina Andhee told Business Day that at the moment the exchange is limited to listing in rand. No launch date has been given.

What it means

The JSE badly needs good news on listings. eNCA reported that it has recorded 13 delistings and only three new listings this year, about R44 billion of lost market value. SARB data cited by Reuters shows the number of listed companies fell to 271 at the end of 2025 from 284 two years earlier.

A large international company choosing Johannesburg, together with the option to list in hard currencies, could help reverse that trend. The real test will be whether other companies follow.
