Denmark expels Russia’s defence attaché over an alleged plot to kill a Ukrainian rapper

Danish intelligence says Russian security services were behind a foiled plan to attack the rapper Kyivstoner at a concert near Copenhagen. Russia calls the accusation absurd and promises a response.

World Desk·Sunday, 22 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Denmark ordered Russia’s defence attaché to leave within seven days
  • Intelligence service PET says Russian security services were behind a plot to kill the rapper Kyivstoner
  • Four men were arrested near the concert venue in September; two are charged with attempted murder and deny it
  • Denmark will stop issuing non-essential visas to Russians; Russia’s embassy calls the claims groundless

Denmark has ordered Russia’s defence attaché to leave the country within seven days, after its intelligence service concluded that Russian security services were behind a plot to kill a Ukrainian rapper in Denmark, The Copenhagen Post and Al Jazeera reported.

Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen announced the expulsion on Friday 20 November after summoning Russia’s ambassador. Danish broadcaster DR named the target as Kyivstoner, a rapper with Ukrainian and US citizenship, who was due to perform in Charlottenlund, outside Copenhagen, on 25 September. Russia’s FSB has accused him of drone smuggling.

The foiled attack

On 25 September police stopped a car in Hellerup and found two pistols and two knives. Four men were arrested, and the concert was cancelled. Al Jazeera reported that the arrests came five minutes before the concert and that one suspect had bought a ticket.

Two Danish men are charged with attempted murder and with helping a foreign intelligence service, and two others face weapons charges. Police suspect the banned gang Loyal to Familia was involved. On Friday a judge ruled that the two attempted-murder suspects could be kept in custody. Both deny the charges.

Reactions

The intelligence service said it was the first Russian-directed assassination attempt it had identified on Danish soil. Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen spoke of a cornered Russia. Denmark will stop issuing non-essential visas to Russians, wants similar limits across the EU, and plans tougher penalties for crimes carried out on behalf of foreign states. European Council President António Costa called the plot unacceptable.

Russia’s foreign ministry promised an appropriate response. In a statement to the Ritzau news agency on Saturday, the Russian embassy called the accusations groundless, said the idea of Russian involvement was absurd, and said Denmark had presented no evidence.

Why it matters

Denmark has also blamed Moscow for other hybrid attacks, including drone flights that shut Copenhagen airport in September 2025. The suspected use of a criminal gang to carry out the plot is what alarms Danish officials most. Russia has signalled it will retaliate, but no response had been announced by Sunday.
