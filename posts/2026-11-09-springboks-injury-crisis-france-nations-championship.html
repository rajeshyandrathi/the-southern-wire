Four World Cup winners down, one week to Paris: Erasmus's injury list just got the Springboks' bitterest rivalry wrong

Sport Desk

Rassie Erasmus named his 37-man squad for the second leg of the Nations Championship with a casualty list that reads like a highlight reel of the last decade of Springbok rugby. Cheslin Kolbe is out with a broken jaw. Eben Etzebeth is sidelined by a lower-limb injury. Andre Esterhuizen is managing an ankle problem, and Handre Pollard, so often the man Erasmus turns to when a match needs settling from the kicking tee, is nursing a hamstring injury. All four were part of the squad that won the 2023 World Cup. All four will be watching from outside the matchday twenty-three when the Springboks travel to Paris to face France on Friday.

The timing could hardly be worse. This fixture, which both camps and most of the European rugby press describe without much exaggeration as the sport's bitterest current rivalry, carries direct weight in the Nations Championship table: the round effectively decides which of the two sides advances toward a Twickenham date later in the series. South Africa arrive unbeaten, having followed up their Rugby Championship title with a 66-7 demolition of Italy in Turin that stretched their run in this competition to four wins from four. France, by contrast, come into the match with their own injury concerns, having lost Fabien Brau-Boirie, Marko Gazzotti and Antoine Hastoy to Top 14 knocks in the days before the squad was finalised, and without Antoine Dupont, still working his way back from an ACL rupture suffered during the Six Nations.

For Erasmus, the response to this kind of attrition has become almost a signature. South African rugby's depth has been built, deliberately and over several seasons, to survive exactly this scenario — a coaching philosophy that treats squad depth as a competitive weapon rather than an insurance policy, and that has repeatedly been vindicated in exactly the moments when it looked most threadbare on paper. The last time the Springboks travelled to Stade de France missing key personnel to injury and suspension, in November last year, they still found a way to win 32-17 with fourteen men on the field for most of the second half, a result both camps have referenced in the build-up to this fixture as evidence that absences alone do not decide this rivalry.

What Erasmus cannot simply paper over is experience at the specific positions now vacated. Kolbe's finishing and footwork at the top level of Test rugby are not easily replicated by a direct like-for-like swap, and Etzebeth's lineout work and physical presence in the tight exchanges have anchored Springbok forward play through multiple World Cup cycles. The players stepping into those roles will be doing so against a French side desperate to settle a score that has now been open, in one form or another, across several consecutive encounters.

Friday's kick-off at the Stade de France, 20:10 local time, will tell its own story regardless of what either injury list says beforehand. But the shape of this Springbok squad — four genuine match-winners unavailable, a game plan that still has to be executed at the sharpest end of a knockout-adjacent fixture — is exactly the kind of test Erasmus has spent years insisting his squad depth was built to pass. Paris will be where that claim gets its latest, and highest-stakes, examination.
