COP31 ends without a deal, the first UN climate summit to fail since Copenhagen

After 44 hours of overtime in Antalya, the final text was rejected on Sunday. The EU and at least 31 other countries refused a deal that did not clearly mention fossil fuels.

World Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • The final text was rejected on Sunday afternoon, 44 hours after the official close, the longest UN climate summit yet
  • It is the first COP since Copenhagen in 2009 to end without a deal all parties accepted
  • At least 32 countries, led by the EU, rejected a draft that only obliquely referred to moving away from fossil fuels
  • Obligations agreed at earlier summits still stand

The UN climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, has collapsed without its main agreement. The final text was rejected on Sunday afternoon, 22 November, 44 hours after the summit was due to close, making COP31 the longest UN climate conference ever, ABC News in Australia reported.

It is the first time since Copenhagen in 2009 that a COP has failed to adopt a deal accepted by all parties. Our report on Friday said the summit had gone into overtime with a draft that dropped any mention of oil, gas or coal; that dispute is what sank it.

Why it failed

The draft “Antalya Pact”, tabled by the co-presidents Türkiye and Australia, did not explicitly mention fossil fuels and only indirectly recalled the 2023 Dubai agreement to transition away from them. At least 32 countries, led by the European Union and including Panama, the Marshall Islands, Belize, Norway and Vanuatu, rejected it, Forbes reported.

Ireland’s climate minister, Darragh O’Brien, speaking for the EU, said the talks had been hijacked by some oil states. Saudi Arabia and Russia backed leaving fossil fuels out. Many poorer countries also objected because the text called for tripling adaptation finance without setting a baseline to measure it against.

Australia’s Chris Bowen, one of the co-presidents, called the result very disappointing, and said he would have accepted wording on a fossil fuel transition if other parties had supported it. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said fossil fuels were driving the climate crisis.

What happens now

The collapse does not cancel commitments made at earlier summits. But the UN environment agency confirmed in October that warming is set to exceed 1.5°C, and the failure leaves no new agreed plan for closing the gap.

Countries frustrated with the consensus rule are turning to a separate track of conferences on moving away from fossil fuels, which held its first meeting with 57 countries in April, and about 30 countries are discussing a fossil fuel treaty, Forbes reported. For countries such as South Africa that are relying on international support for their energy transition, the question is whether these smaller coalitions can deliver what the full UN process could not.
