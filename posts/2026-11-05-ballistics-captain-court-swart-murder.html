The police captain accused of burying a murder case returns to court

Crime Desk

Captain Laurance Makgotloe, the SAPS forensic ballistics analyst accused of sabotaging the investigation into the murder of Vereeniging engineer Armand Swart, was back in the dock on Thursday as the case against him edges toward trial. Swart, who ran the engineering firm Q Tech, was shot 23 times while sitting in his vehicle outside his own offices, a killing some investigators have described as a case of mistaken identity rather than a targeted hit.

Makgotloe faces charges of defeating the ends of justice, accessory after the fact to murder and unlawful possession of ammunition. The state's case is that, rather than simply analysing the ballistic evidence assigned to him, he produced findings that could have let Swart's killers walk free, and that he went further by allegedly removing a firearm and ammunition connected to the case from where they should have been secured.

The allegations against him are not confined to the Swart case alone. Makgotloe has testified that the firearms at the centre of the investigation were linked through ballistics to roughly twenty other shootings, among them the high-profile killings of DJ Sumbody, DJ Vintos and businessman Don Tindleni, raising the possibility that whatever happened to the evidence in the Swart matter had implications well beyond one courtroom.

Makgotloe has disputed the state's version of events. Testifying before the Madlanga Commission earlier this year, he said he was effectively abducted by an investigating team that pressed him over irregularities in his ballistic report, and said a senior prosecutor on the Swart case had warned him the matter would be withdrawn if the report's errors were not corrected, an account he has framed as evidence of pressure on him rather than wrongdoing by him. He was granted bail by the Pretoria Magistrate's Court in June and has since been barred from returning to his workplace in Silverton while the case proceeds.

For a police service already under scrutiny over the integrity of its forensic units, a serving ballistics captain facing prosecution for allegedly tampering with his own evidence is an uncomfortable story to keep running. Thursday's appearance produced no dramatic new disclosure, but it kept a case with implications for roughly twenty other murder investigations moving toward the regional court, where the state has indicated it believes it is ready to proceed.
