Gold and silver slide as US rate-hike bets harden, and the JSE slips before key inflation data

Monday’s relief rally faded on Tuesday. Gold fell about 2% and silver about 5%, the rand weakened to around R17.54 to the dollar, and the JSE All Share slipped 0.2%, with US inflation figures due this afternoon.

Business Desk·Wednesday, 25 November 2026·5 min read ·Updated 6:30am SAST

Key points

  • JSE All Share closed Tuesday at 106,217, down about 0.2%, and the Top 40 at 98,373, down about 0.3%
  • Spot gold about $3,545/oz early on Wednesday, after falling roughly 2% on Tuesday; silver lost about 5% and platinum nearly 4%
  • Rand weakened about 0.8% to R17.54 to the dollar by 16:03 SAST on Tuesday
  • Markets see a 96% chance of a US Fed rate hike in December; US PCE inflation data is due at 15:30 SAST today

The rebound that followed Nvidia’s reinstatement of Jensen Huang lasted barely a day. On Tuesday precious metals fell sharply, the rand gave back most of Monday’s gain and the JSE drifted lower, as traders grew more certain that the US Federal Reserve will raise interest rates next month. The next test comes at 15:30 SAST today, when the US releases its PCE inflation figures, the Fed’s preferred measure of price pressure.

On Wall Street, Nvidia fell 5.9% on Tuesday after Monday’s jump of 31.5%, its best day in more than 23 years. Reuters reported that the Nasdaq Composite closed 1.45% lower at 23,740.52 and the S&P 500 0.48% lower at 7,202.45 in preliminary figures, while the Dow rose 0.87% to 50,028.79. Investors weighed governance and political-risk questions around Huang’s return, Reuters said, and new US import duties are expected after a probe into excess industrial capacity in 16 trading partners.

Precious metals took the brunt. Reuters reported spot gold down 1.8% at $3,566.45 an ounce late on Tuesday, with silver down 5% and platinum down 3.9%, as losses in AI-linked shares again pushed some investors to sell metals to raise cash for margin calls. Early on Wednesday spot gold was down a further 0.3% at $3,544.54 at 02:24 SAST, silver was at $40.79 and platinum at $1,267.65, according to Reuters. Weaker platinum is unwelcome news for South Africa’s platinum miners.

Rate-hike fears weigh on the rand

The main pressure is coming from the US. The CME FedWatch tool shows a 96.4% chance of a quarter-point Fed hike in December and a 3.6% chance of a half-point move, Reuters reported. New York Fed President John Williams said on Monday that persistently high inflation points towards tighter policy. US consumer confidence also rose more than expected in November. Higher US rates make non-yielding gold less attractive and tend to draw money away from emerging markets such as South Africa.

The rand was at R17.54 to the dollar at 16:03 SAST on Tuesday, about 0.8% weaker on the day, even though the US dollar slipped about 0.5% against a basket of currencies, Reuters reported. At home, the composite leading business cycle indicator fell 0.3% month on month in September, another sign of a soft economy, and the 2035 government bond yield edged up to 8.665%. Sharenet’s late Tuesday rates put the rand at R19.24 to the euro and R23.01 to the pound, both weaker on the day.

On the JSE, the All Share index closed Tuesday at 106,216.57 points and the Top 40 at 98,372.69, according to Sharenet data, down about 0.2% and 0.3% from Monday’s close. Miners led the fallers: Sharenet’s list of the biggest moves among the top 100 shares showed Implats down 4.18%, Pan African Resources down 3.68%, African Rainbow Minerals down 3.57% and Anglo American down 3.26%, with Optasia also down 3.74%. On the other side, Pepkor rose 7.55%, Canal+ 6.98%, Mondi 4.42% and Netcare 3.51%.

Oil moved the other way. Brent crude settled 2.27% higher at $91.40 a barrel on Tuesday after earlier touching $88.10, its lowest since 27 August, The Nation reported. Prices turned up after more reports of attacks on shipping in and around the Strait of Hormuz, including an Iranian state media report that a container ship hit a mine. Traders are also watching signs of oversupply, which have kept a lid on prices in recent weeks.

Diamonds: Angola puts a price on De Beers

Diamonds do not trade on an exchange with a single daily price, but the sale of De Beers moved forward. Angola expects to pay about $180 million for a 10% stake, Endiama chief executive José Manuel Augusto Ganga Júnior said on 20 November, according to Reuters as reported by JCK. He called the stake “worthwhile” despite depressed diamond prices. JCK noted that this implies a value of about $1.8 billion for De Beers, well above the roughly $1.18 billion implied by the Global Diamond Consortium’s reported offer of about $1 billion for Anglo American’s 85%.

Botswana, which owns 15% of De Beers, wants to double its holding to 30%, while Angola and Namibia are expected to seek 10% each and Oman is involved in talks, JCK reported; the final split has not been announced, and Anglo American is still working towards binding agreements. On prices, Rapaport’s latest weekly comment, of 19 November, said smaller stones of 0.30 to 0.59 carats were still falling while larger diamonds were stable.
