The ANC turns to MK in hung councils and treats the DA as a last resort, despite an offer of 93 mayoralties

A special meeting of the ANC’s national executive on Monday backed working with the MK Party wherever no party has a majority. The DA had published a written offer that would have given the ANC most of the country’s hung councils.

Politics Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • The ANC concluded an agreement with the MK Party to work together in municipalities where no party has a majority
  • Business Day reports that Ramaphosa told the NEC a DA partnership should be a “last resort”
  • The DA’s written offer would have given the ANC 93 hung councils, the DA 24 and the IFP 20
  • eThekwini’s council is due to sit on Tuesday, with reports of an ANC-MK arrangement in Durban

The ANC has chosen its partners for the next five years of local government, and the DA is not first on the list. At a special National Executive Committee meeting in Boksburg on Monday 23 November, the party considered recommendations from a negotiating team led by secretary-general Fikile Mbalula, and concluded an agreement with the MK Party to work together in municipalities where no party won a majority, newsnote reported.

The EFF, PA, BOSA, IFP and Rise Mzansi are also expected to feature in local arrangements. ANC spokesperson Mahlengi Bhengu said there was consensus among the parties, and that the talks were not about who gets the mayoral chain or the purse.

Business Day, citing two Luthuli House insiders, reported that President Cyril Ramaphosa directed the party’s national working committee to avoid coalitions with the DA, and told the NEC to treat a DA partnership as a last resort. The Mail & Guardian reported the same ranking.

The offer the ANC set aside

The decision came despite a detailed written proposal from the DA. The party sent its offer to Mbalula on Friday 20 November, and DA leader Geordin Hill-Lewis published it on Sunday, Daily Maverick reported. Its so-called stability framework would have given the ANC 93 hung municipalities, the DA 24 and the IFP 20, subject to the IFP agreeing.

The DA also promised not to back a motion of no confidence in the national government, or a motion to dissolve the National Assembly, for at least 18 months. In return, it wanted Johannesburg, Tshwane, Ekurhuleni, Mogale City and Emfuleni in Gauteng. Hill-Lewis said South Africans deserved to see the alternative before the NEC made its choice.

ActionSA, whose support the DA needed in Gauteng, rejected the DA’s final Gauteng offer as unworkable. Mashaba wrote on X that it did not deserve a response, Daily Maverick reported.

Durban and the wider picture

In KwaZulu-Natal, the African News Agency, citing unnamed insiders, reported that the ANC and MK had agreed to co-govern eThekwini, with MK reportedly taking both the mayor and deputy mayor posts. This has not been formally confirmed. TimesLIVE reported that the ANC’s provincial convenor, Mike Mabuyakhulu, is said to prefer the existing ANC, IFP and DA model in the city. Business Day listed eThekwini’s council sitting for Tuesday 24 November, so the outcome should be known soon.

The choice exposes divisions inside the ANC. One Luthuli House source told Business Day that pacts with parties that are taking ANC votes were suicidal, while Gauteng premier Panyaza Lesufi has opposed any deal with the DA. Because the ANC and DA are partners in the national unity government, every local deal will be read for what it means for national stability.

Councils are expected to finish electing their leaders by Friday 27 November. Until votes are actually taken in each chamber, reported deals remain just that.
