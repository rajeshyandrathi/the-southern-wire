KwaZulu-Natal braces for more severe storms as a Highveld heatwave bakes the other half of the country

Weather Desk·Tuesday, 3 November 2026

KwaZulu-Natal's run of severe weather shows no sign of easing this week, with the South African Weather Service issuing a fresh yellow Level 4 warning for severe thunderstorms across the province's northern reaches on Tuesday, alongside a Level 2 warning for the south. The warnings extend a sequence of storm alerts that has barely let up since late October, when disaster management teams across the province were first placed on high alert as a Level 4 system swept through, excluding only the north-eastern corner.

Districts including Uthukela and Amajuba have featured repeatedly in the province's daily severe weather bulletins over the past several days, with towns such as Estcourt, Ladysmith, Ekuvukeni and Dannhauser named under specific thunderstorm alerts as the unstable weather pattern has shifted its focus from the province's west to its north-east and back again. Provincial disaster response teams have remained on standby throughout, a precaution born of recent memory: KwaZulu-Natal has suffered repeated flood disasters in recent years that turned what began as routine summer thunderstorms into genuine humanitarian emergencies.

Hundreds of kilometres away, the Highveld is dealing with the opposite extreme. A heatwave has settled over Gauteng and parts of the Free State, pushing temperatures well above seasonal norms just as KwaZulu-Natal counts its storm warnings — a split-screen weather pattern that is becoming an increasingly familiar feature of South African spring, as moist, unstable air feeds repeated storm systems along the eastern coast while the interior bakes under clear, dry skies.

The contrast carries real consequences beyond mere discomfort. Elevated Highveld temperatures put additional strain on an electricity grid that, while enjoying its longest stretch without load shedding in years, has not yet been tested by genuine peak summer demand, while KZN's repeated storm cycles continue to test municipal drainage and flood infrastructure that has been repaired, but not comprehensively upgraded, since the catastrophic floods of recent years.

With no clear sign that either pattern — the Highveld heat or the KZN storms — is about to break, forecasters are urging residents in both regions to treat this week's warnings as more than routine seasonal noise.
