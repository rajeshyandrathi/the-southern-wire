SpaceX's record IPO anchors a sprawling Musk empire

World Desk·Saturday, 19 September 2026

Elon Musk's sprawling business empire remains under intense scrutiny months after SpaceX's public listing priced shares at $135, valuing the rocket company at roughly $1.77 trillion. The listing, one of the largest in history, cemented SpaceX's position not just as a launch provider but as one of the most valuable private-turned-public companies on the planet.

What makes the scale of the valuation notable is that it sits alongside, not in place of, Musk's other commitments. He continues to run Tesla, the social platform X, AI venture xAI, brain-interface company Neuralink, and infrastructure venture The Boring Company simultaneously — a portfolio that would represent a full-time job for most executives several times over, let alone one person managing it all at once.

The current stretch follows a brief and closely watched detour into government, where Musk led the Trump administration's Department of Government Efficiency earlier this year before stepping back to refocus on his companies. That period drew sustained attention to how his political involvement might affect his business interests, and his return to a purely corporate focus has been read by many observers as an attempt to draw a clearer line between the two roles.

For now, the SpaceX valuation gives Musk a financial foundation that dwarfs even his other ventures, and it raises the obvious question of how a single individual continues to steer that much simultaneously — a question that has followed him for years without ever quite being answered.
