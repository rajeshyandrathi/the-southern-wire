As the count deepens, small-town upsets tell a bigger story than the national tally

Politics Desk

By Thursday afternoon the Electoral Commission's count had moved well past the thin opening slice of results that shaped this morning's headlines, and the picture emerging is less a single national verdict than a patchwork of local upsets, each one chipping away at the assumption that South African local politics still runs on two or three dominant parties.

Nowhere is that clearer than in the small Karoo municipality of Laingsburg, where ActionSA, contesting its first election there, emerged as the largest party on the council. The result splits the chamber three ways, with the DA and ActionSA each taking three seats and the ANC left with two, a council arithmetic that will force a coalition in a town with barely a few thousand registered voters. A short drive away in Prince Albert, the Patriotic Alliance did even better, winning five of the municipality's ten seats, just one shy of outright control and enough to make it the dominant force in talks over who governs next.

The ANC's losses are showing up in places that mattered to its old coalition map rather than only in the big metros. In the Eastern Cape's Makana municipality, home to Makhanda, the party took just ten of twenty-five council seats, short of the thirteen needed to govern alone and losing an outright majority it had held going into the vote. In Gauteng's Emfuleni, the DA was running well clear with a majority share of the vote counted so far, with the Freedom Front Plus a distant second and the ANC pushed back into third place in a municipality it has governed for most of the post-apartheid era.

The bigger metros are moving more slowly but in the same direction. ActionSA is positioning itself for a coalition role in Tshwane as early counting shows real gains there, while the DA has stretched its lead in Mangaung, the Free State capital built around Bloemfontein, a city it has never previously controlled. In the Western Cape, where the IEC says roughly a quarter of ballots have now been captured, the DA is holding its established strongholds and extending into outlying wards, consistent with the party's showing in the ward-by-ward count released so far.

Taken together, the emerging results point to a national mood that is less about any single party surging and more about the ANC's local-government base continuing to erode, with no single rival positioned to inherit it everywhere at once. Analysts tracking the count say that is likely to produce exactly the kind of fractured, coalition-dependent councils the morning's early returns already hinted at, just with far more detail now attached to which parties will actually have to sit across the table from each other once the IEC's seven-day declaration window closes.
