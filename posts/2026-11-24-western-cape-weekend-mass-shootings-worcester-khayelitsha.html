Ten people killed in two Western Cape mass shootings on one Saturday, as two Worcester suspects face court today

Six people died when masked gunmen opened fire in a yard in Worcester’s Zwelethemba, and four men were killed at a Khayelitsha tavern that night. Police have made two arrests in the Worcester case.

Crime Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Six people were killed and three wounded in Zwelethemba, Worcester, on Saturday morning
  • Four men died and five people were wounded in a tavern shooting in Khayelitsha late on Saturday
  • Two Worcester suspects are due in the Worcester Magistrates’ Court today; no arrests yet in Khayelitsha
  • SAPS says its first week of festive-season operations led to more than 18,000 arrests

Two mass shootings in the Western Cape on Saturday 21 November left 10 people dead and eight wounded, adding to a run of attacks across the country this month.

The first happened on Saturday morning in the Zwelethemba informal settlement in Worcester. Six masked, armed men entered a yard and opened fire, EWN reported. Four men and two women were killed, and three people were wounded and taken to hospital.

Western Cape police commissioner Lieutenant General Sizakhele Dyantyi visited Worcester and told the media on Sunday that two suspects had been arrested and would appear in the Worcester Magistrates’ Court on Tuesday, EWN reported. Their names, the charges and a possible motive have not been made public.

A tavern attack in Khayelitsha

At about 23:30 the same night, gunmen stormed a tavern on Hlobe Street in the Greenpoint section of Khayelitsha. Police spokesperson Brigadier Novela Potelwa said four men died at the scene and five wounded people were taken for treatment, TimesLIVE reported. The attackers hijacked a vehicle at the scene and abandoned it a few kilometres away.

The provincial serious and violent crime investigation unit is handling the case. No arrests had been reported and the motive is not yet known.

Part of a wider pattern

The weekend’s attacks follow other mass shootings in recent days, including nine people killed in Atteridgeville on 16 November, eight men in Sweetwaters in KwaZulu-Natal, and six people in the Free State on 19 November, a TimesLIVE editorial noted. The newspaper asked where the soldiers deployed under Operation Prosper were. That deployment was set at 2,200 troops from March 2026 to March 2027, at an estimated cost of R823.15 million.

Nationally, SAPS said its Safer Festive Season operations from 16 to 22 November led to 18,049 arrests, including 164 for murder and 198 for attempted murder, SAnews reported. Officers seized 157 unlicensed firearms and 1,954 rounds of ammunition, inspected 6,863 liquor outlets and closed 1,014 that were operating without a licence.

The victims have not been publicly named. Police have not said whether the Worcester and Khayelitsha attacks are connected, and we will report on Tuesday’s court appearance when details are available.
