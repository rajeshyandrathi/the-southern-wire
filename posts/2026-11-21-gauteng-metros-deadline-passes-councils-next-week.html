Politics — SA & Africa

ActionSA’s 8pm deadline has passed. The DA looks shut out of Gauteng’s big three metros.

With no public sign of a revised DA offer, an ANC-led bloc with ActionSA, the EFF and MK appears set to take Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni. Councils are expected to sit next week to elect mayors.

Politics Desk·Saturday, 21 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • ActionSA gave the DA until 8pm on Friday to improve its offer; no new deal had been announced by Saturday morning
  • Reports say Herman Mashaba would become Johannesburg mayor and Nasiphi Moya would keep Tshwane, with the ANC naming Ekurhuleni’s mayor
  • Premier Panyaza Lesufi is leading a task team to share out positions
  • Councils are expected to sit next week; Tshwane’s sitting is expected on Thursday

The deadline came and went quietly. On Friday morning ActionSA wrote to the DA giving it until 8pm to revise a power-sharing offer for Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni, after a Thursday-night meeting between the two parties ended badly. By Saturday morning neither party had announced a new agreement, and every public signal pointed the other way.

ActionSA leader Herman Mashaba was blunt after Thursday’s meeting. “The DA’s offer is not a proposal but an insult,” he said, according to EWN, adding that his party was more satisfied with what the ANC had put on the table. He told the Mail & Guardian the DA had wasted a week and a half and that “the train has moved”.

That train is an ANC-led arrangement that would bring together the ANC, ActionSA, the EFF and the MK Party, with support from several smaller parties. The Mail & Guardian reports that under the plan Mashaba would become mayor of Johannesburg, Nasiphi Moya of ActionSA would keep the Tshwane mayoral chain, and the ANC would choose Ekurhuleni’s mayor. Mayoral committee posts would be shared according to each party’s seats.

Gauteng Premier Panyaza Lesufi chaired a multiparty meeting in Parktown on Thursday and is leading a task team to finalise who gets which positions. Parties at that meeting included the ANC, MK, EFF, ActionSA, Build One South Africa, Rise Mzansi, the SACP, IFP, ATM and the Patriotic Alliance, according to the Mail & Guardian.

Why the DA is on the outside

The DA won the most seats in all three metros in the 4 November local elections but nowhere near a majority. In Johannesburg, a 270-seat council where 136 votes are needed, Sunday World puts the DA on 91 seats. The ANC, ActionSA, EFF and MK together have about 138, according to the Mail & Guardian, just enough to govern.

ActionSA had set two conditions for working with the DA: that its mayoral candidates, Helen Zille in Johannesburg and Cilliers Brink in Tshwane, step aside. DA leader Geordin Hill-Lewis rejected reports that he had offered to swap places with Zille. “I have made no such proposal,” he told The National, noting that a Johannesburg mayor must live in the city.

Hill-Lewis has called an ANC-ActionSA deal “a profound error” and said the DA would go into opposition if it happened, while insisting the final whistle had not yet blown. ActionSA chairperson Michael Beaumont said earlier in the week that his party was still far from a deal with anyone, but by Friday he was calling the ANC’s offer attractive.

What happens next

New councils in all three metros are expected to sit next week to swear in councillors and elect mayors and speakers. Tshwane’s first sitting was postponed this week and is now expected on Thursday, The National reports. Dates for Johannesburg and Ekurhuleni have not been published.

Until those votes take place, nothing is final, and coalition arithmetic in these councils has shifted at the last minute before. But if the ANC-led bloc holds together, the DA will lead the opposition in the three metros where it won the most votes, a result that is already unsettling investors and could test relations inside the national unity government. The rand and JSE both slipped this week partly on that uncertainty.
