Eisenkot signs up Lieberman as his first coalition partner, but the Democrats walk out

Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party has agreed a coalition pact with Avigdor Lieberman’s Yisrael Beytenu, which would take the defence and justice ministries. The left-wing Democrats have suspended talks in protest at his negotiations with Likud.

World Desk·Thursday, 26 November 2026·4 min read

Key points

  • Yashar and Yisrael Beytenu agreed on Tuesday, 24 November, to sign a coalition pact, the first since the 27 October election
  • Under the deal, Lieberman’s party would get the defence and justice ministries, Israel Hayom reported
  • Yair Golan’s Democrats suspended talks with Yashar over its negotiations with Netanyahu’s Likud and a dispute over ministries
  • Eisenkot’s mandate runs until 6 December, with a possible 14-day extension; his associates say a government could be sworn in as early as next week
  • Separately, Netanyahu and US envoy Jared Kushner agreed to keep working together on Gaza after Kushner warned Israel risked becoming a “global pariah”

Gadi Eisenkot has secured his first partner in his bid to form Israel’s next government. His centrist Yashar party said on Tuesday, 24 November, that it had agreed to sign a coalition pact with Yisrael Beytenu, the party of former defence and foreign minister Avigdor Lieberman, after a meeting between the two leaders, Al Jazeera reported. It is the first such agreement since the 27 October election, which left no bloc with the 61 seats needed for a majority.

Under the deal, Yisrael Beytenu would take the defence and justice ministries, according to Israel Hayom. Lieberman last served as defence minister until 2018. The paper also reported that Lieberman has dropped his opposition to a minority government, which matters because Eisenkot has said he could fall back on one if broader talks fail.

Eisenkot, who was given the mandate by President Isaac Herzog, has until 6 December to form a government and can ask for a 14-day extension. If he fails, Benjamin Netanyahu, who remains caretaker prime minister, would get a chance. If neither succeeds, Israel would go back to the polls next year.

Trouble on the left

The progress with Lieberman came as another potential partner stepped away. The left-wing Democrats, led by Yair Golan, suspended their talks with Yashar in protest at Eisenkot’s negotiations with Likud over a possible unity government, Haaretz and Al Jazeera reported. Golan prefers a government without Netanyahu that includes the Arab parties.

Israel Hayom reported that disputes over jobs were also a big part of the row. Eisenkot has told partners he wants a lean cabinet, with each party getting only three or four portfolios. Yashar refused to give the education ministry to Democrats MK Naama Lazimi or the justice ministry to Golan, and the only post offered to Golan was national security. The Democrats were expected to receive three posts in total, and Naftali Bennett’s Beyahad two besides Bennett himself.

Eisenkot’s associates told the paper that a new government could be sworn in as early as the start of next week, depending on progress in talks with the ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism party. Talks between Yashar and Likud on a unity government are continuing, with a fourth round of meetings reported by KhanList on Wednesday.

The numbers

Al Jazeera counts 53 seats in the 120-member Knesset for Likud and its far-right and ultra-Orthodox allies, against 48 for Yashar and its partners, including Lieberman’s party. Parties representing Israel’s Palestinian citizens hold 19 seats; they are aligned with Eisenkot in opposing Netanyahu but are not counted in his bloc’s total. That arithmetic is why both a unity deal with Likud and a minority government backed from outside by the Arab parties remain on the table.

Gaza in the background

Whoever governs will face pressure over Gaza. The Times of Israel reported that Netanyahu and US special envoy Jared Kushner spoke by phone on Tuesday about the humanitarian situation there and agreed to keep working together. The call followed an interview in which Kushner warned that Israel was heading towards becoming a “global pariah” if it did not change course. The US-led Board of Peace, which oversees the fragile Gaza truce, has accused Netanyahu’s government of blocking its plan to disarm Hamas.
