Nine wins in ten against France, and tonight the Boks go back to Paris to defend the run

Sport Desk

South Africa have beaten France nine times in their last ten meetings, and the one match this fixture cannot settle in advance is whether that record means anything once the whistle actually goes in Saint-Denis. Kick-off is just after nine in the evening in Paris, ten o'clock in Johannesburg, and by the time most of the country wakes up to read about it, the result will already belong to history rather than anticipation. For now, it still belongs to nerves.

Fabien Galthie has gone back to his most experienced names rather than his most exciting ones, recalling senior figures across the French spine in a line-up built to deny the Springboks the broken-field chaos that undid them in Paris a year ago. That November 2025 meeting ended 32-17 to South Africa, a Springbok side reduced to fourteen men for most of the second half after an early red card, somehow finding another gear anyway as Siya Kolisi marked his hundredth Test with a win instead of a scare. France have spent the months since replaying that collapse in their own heads, and every selection this week reads like an attempt to exorcise it.

Erasmus, for his part, has made the changes that matter and left the rest alone. Manie Libbok drops to the bench rather than starting at flyhalf, a selection call that says as much about what South Africa expect from this game as any press conference would: control first, flair as a finishing move. The forward pack that has carried this Springbok side through a punishing end-of-year tour returns largely intact, trusted again to do the unglamorous work of winning territory before anyone worries about winning the crowd.

What makes tonight different from the eight previous wins in this streak is where France find themselves in their own cycle. Les Bleus are no longer the callow side that folded under Springbok pressure at the last World Cup; this is a team that has had two years to rebuild around the players coming back into tonight's twenty-three, and home advantage at a sold-out Stade de France is not nothing against a Springbok side playing its last fixture of a long season. South Africa have won five straight on this ground, a run few visiting nations in the professional era have managed, and every one of those five games has been treated by French media as the aberration rather than the pattern.

There is no safety net in a result like this for either side, only reputational stakes dressed up as a November fixture. A Springbok win extends a streak that is starting to look less like form and more like a standing order over this fixture; a French win, their first at home against South Africa in years, resets a rivalry that has tilted hard in one direction since the last World Cup. Nothing on tonight's team sheet suggests South Africa are approaching this as anything other than business as usual. Whether France can finally make that complacency costly is the only question this match was ever really asking.
