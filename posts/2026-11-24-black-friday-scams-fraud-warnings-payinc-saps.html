Black Friday is this week, and so are the scammers: how to keep your money safe

Banks, the payments industry and police say fake shops, parcel SMSes and cloned holiday listings are the main traps. Digital banking fraud cost South Africans R2.4 billion last year.

Lifestyle Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • SABRIC put digital banking fraud losses at R2.4 billion in 2025
  • TransUnion found victims of digital fraud lost just over R11,000 on average
  • Common traps: fake online shops, “parcel held” SMSes and cloned holiday rentals
  • Never share your PIN, password or OTP, and only approve payments you started

Black Friday falls on 27 November, and alongside the deals comes a rise in fraud. The Mail & Guardian reported on Monday that digital banking fraud cost South Africans R2.4 billion in 2025, according to the South African Banking Risk Information Centre (SABRIC), and that TransUnion found victims lost just over R11,000 each on average.

Criminals are also getting better at it. Interpol’s 2026 global financial fraud assessment says fraud boosted by AI, using tools such as cloned voices and fake bank notifications, is 4.5 times more profitable than traditional methods, the newspaper reported.

Ursula Pearson-Williams, head of fraud intelligence at the payments company PayInc, said criminals exploit fast digital payments by putting pressure on people before they have time to question what they are being asked to do.

The main traps

The scams to watch for include fake online shops and social media adverts for cheap phones, consoles and appliances that never arrive; SMS messages saying a parcel is being held until you pay a fee, which are designed to steal card details; and fake or cloned listings for December holiday accommodation that ask for a deposit, the Mail & Guardian reported. Fidelity Services Group also warned that crowded malls and parking areas create opportunities for theft from cars.

Standard Bank said its Black Friday transaction volumes rose 6.6% in 2025, with online volumes up 75%, South African Daily reported. Its head of digital and e-commerce, Dr Belinda Rathogwa, said that as online shopping grows, so do the opportunities for fraudsters.

How to protect yourself

Go straight to a retailer’s official website or app rather than following links in messages or adverts, and check web addresses for misspellings. Check seller reviews on marketplaces, and be wary of anyone who insists on an EFT or instant payment. Use virtual cards where your bank offers them, never approve a payment you did not start, and never share your PIN, password or one-time PIN.

In stores, police advise having your card ready before you reach the till or ATM, covering the keypad, refusing help from strangers, avoiding isolated ATMs and carrying as little cash as possible. If you think you are being followed, go to a police station, SAnews reported.

If you suspect fraud, call your bank on the number printed on your card or its official website, not a number given to you in a message. Acting quickly gives the bank the best chance of stopping a payment.
