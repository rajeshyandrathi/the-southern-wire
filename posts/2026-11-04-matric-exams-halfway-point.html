Matric's class of 2026 clears the halfway mark, with the hardest papers still to come

Lifestyle Desk·Wednesday, 4 November 2026

Three weeks into the National Senior Certificate exams, South Africa's matric class of 2026 has reached the point in the calendar where adrenaline starts giving way to fatigue. The exams, which opened on 13 October with the Computer Applications Technology practical paper, run all the way through to 26 November, and the Department of Basic Education's most recent progress update, issued on 30 October, described the process as having reached its halfway mark with steady, largely incident-free progress.

That steadiness matters more than it might seem from the outside. Previous exam cycles have been disrupted by leaked papers, exam-centre protests and logistical breakdowns serious enough to force entire subjects to be rewritten nationally, so a cycle that simply proceeds on schedule counts, by recent standards, as a genuine institutional win. The department has credited tightened security measures introduced after irregularities in the prior year's cycle, along with contingency plans that allow pupils caught up in protest action to write at an alternate centre rather than lose the sitting entirely.

Behind the administrative milestones sit nearly a million individual stories of pressure. Nearly 963,000 candidates registered for this year's exams, with Umalusi placing the total number actually sitting closer to 1.08 million once repeat candidates and part-time learners are included, spread across more than 9,000 examination centres nationwide. Each of those candidates signed an integrity pledge before the exams began, a now-standard ritual meant to reinforce the stakes of a process that, for many, determines access to university, bursaries and a first foothold into adult life.

The subjects still to come carry a reputation for being the ones that decide outcomes rather than simply confirm them. Mathematics, physical sciences and life sciences, often cited by pupils and teachers alike as the papers most likely to separate a good overall result from a disappointing one, remain ahead on the timetable, meaning the psychological relief of reaching the halfway point is tempered by the knowledge that the toughest stretch has not yet arrived.

Results for this year's cohort are expected only in January, following the pattern set by the class of 2025, whose outcomes were released on 12 January 2026. For now, matrics across the country are simply trying to get through the next three weeks with their concentration, and their nerves, intact.
