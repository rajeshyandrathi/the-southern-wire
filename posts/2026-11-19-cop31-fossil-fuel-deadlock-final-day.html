COP31’s last day arrives with the fossil-fuel question still unresolved

World Desk

COP31 was always going to come down to its final hours, and Antalya is living up to that script. With the summit's closing session due on Thursday, the text that matters most — the cover decision meant to capture whatever consensus two weeks of negotiation can produce — remains unresolved on the points that have defined every COP since Paris: how fast fossil fuels get phased out, and who pays for the transition away from them.

Secretary-General António Guterres set the tone at the Leaders' Summit that opened the conference, pushing governments to accelerate emissions cuts, commit to phasing out unabated coal, and significantly increase adaptation finance. Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan has instead steered discussion toward the COP31 Action Agenda's own priorities — electrification, green industrialization, resilience and zero waste — anchored by a Global Electrification Pledge targeting 35% renewable-powered electrification by 2035.

That fight has not gone away; it has just been deferred to the final days, as it was at COP30 in Belém, which closed without any text on a fossil-fuel phase-out despite months of pressure. Europe and a bloc of small island states are pushing to get phase-out language explicitly into Antalya's cover decision this time, arguing that another summit ending in euphemism would be the clearest possible signal that the process has stalled.

The Umbrella Group of wealthier non-EU nations has called publicly for "a positive outcome," language vague enough to mean almost anything, which is itself a tell: when the bloc most able to shape financing commitments speaks in generalities this close to the deadline, it usually means the hard numbers are still being fought over behind closed doors.

Antalya's organizers have one advantage Belém did not: a full extra day built into the schedule specifically to let negotiators grind through exactly this kind of impasse. Whether that time produces a cover decision with real fossil-fuel language, or another carefully worded document that leaders can present as progress while avoiding the question entirely, will not be clear until the gavel actually comes down.
