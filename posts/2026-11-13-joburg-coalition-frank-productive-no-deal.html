Frank, productive, and still no mayor: Johannesburg's coalition talks run into a second week

Politics Desk

Twelve days after Herman Mashaba first put his proposal in writing, Johannesburg's coalition math has advanced by exactly one adjective. Both sides to Thursday's meeting between Mashaba and DA federal leader Geordin Hill-Lewis described the conversation as "frank and productive" — the kind of phrase parties reach for when they want to signal progress without admitting that none of the hard questions got answered. No mayoral candidate has been withdrawn. No deputy mayoralty has been confirmed. The letter that started this, dated 7 November, still sits on the table exactly as it did last week.

The offer itself hasn't moved. Mashaba wants Hill-Lewis to pull Helen Zille from the Johannesburg mayoral race in exchange for Mashaba pulling himself, clearing the way for a DA candidate ActionSA can live with, with ActionSA taking the deputy mayor's chain as the price of its support. It is, on its face, a trade Mashaba can defend to his own caucus — he gets to say he put country over candidacy — but it asks the DA to treat its own leadership choices as a bargaining chip, which is precisely the sticking point party insiders have been signalling they won't accept.

What has changed is the scale of the deal Mashaba is actually trying to strike. He is not simply negotiating Johannesburg; he is proposing a three-metro trade that hands the DA both Johannesburg and Ekurhuleni while ActionSA keeps Tshwane under Nasiphi Moya, with DA mayoral hopeful Cilliers Brink expected to step aside there in return. That is a far bigger ask than a single deputy mayorship, and it means Thursday's meeting was never going to produce a signed agreement — it was a negotiation over the shape of Gauteng's local government for the next five years, conducted under a deadline nobody has had the nerve to extend.

Behind both delegations, the clock that actually matters keeps running. Council sittings cannot be deferred indefinitely while party principals workshop their pride, and every metro left mayor-less invites the kind of administrative drift Johannesburg residents have already lived through three mayors' worth of cautionary tale. The ANC has not disappeared from this picture either: Mashaba has made clear, more than once, that a DA-ANC arrangement remains his fallback if the ActionSA deal collapses, a threat aimed as much at Hill-Lewis's own caucus as at ActionSA's negotiating table.

What happens next depends on which side blinks first on the only question that actually matters: whether the DA will put Zille's candidacy up for negotiation to get ActionSA's votes. Thursday's talks kept that question alive rather than settling it, which is itself a kind of outcome — Johannesburg's coalition impasse didn't break this week, but for the first time in this round of talks, nobody walked away either.
