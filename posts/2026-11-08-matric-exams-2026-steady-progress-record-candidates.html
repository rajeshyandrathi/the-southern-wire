Nearly a million matrics are halfway through the hardest exams of their lives, and the Department says it's going to plan

Lifestyle Desk

South Africa's 2026 National Senior Certificate examinations have passed their midpoint, with the Department of Basic Education reporting steady progress for the largest matric cohort the country has ever registered. Official figures put the number of candidates at 962,832 through the Department's own count, while Umalusi, the body responsible for certifying the exams, cites a broader figure of roughly 1.08 million candidates once supplementary and part-time writers across more than 9,000 centres nationwide are included.

The exam period has had to absorb a disruption most years never face: a national election falling squarely inside the writing schedule. With the 4 November municipal elections landing in the middle of the exam timetable, authorities built a non-exam day into the schedule for 9 November, giving candidates, invigilators and marking staff a buffer around the vote itself, alongside broader contingency planning for possible disruption at or near polling stations doubling as exam venues in some areas.

Umalusi's "all systems go" assessment ahead of the season, delivered in early October, has so far held up through the exam period's first half, according to the Department's own late-October update describing steady progress. That framing matters for a cohort this large: logistical strain multiplies with scale, from printing and distributing papers securely to managing marking capacity once the final scripts are in, and a smooth first half reduces, without eliminating, the risk of the kind of leaks or distribution errors that have marred previous years' exam cycles.

For the roughly one million young people sitting these papers, the stakes extend well beyond the logistics that dominate official briefings. The NSC result remains the primary gateway to university admission, bursary eligibility and a wide range of early-career opportunities in South Africa, and a cohort this size competing for a largely fixed number of tertiary placements means the margins between a pass that opens doors and one that merely satisfies the minimum requirement matter more than ever.

With the final papers scheduled to wrap up in the final week of November, candidates now face the harder, more subject-specific papers that typically arrive in the exam period's second half. The Department's optimism at the halfway mark offers some reassurance on the administrative side, but for nearly a million matriculants, the exams that will actually shape their results are still, for the most part, ahead of them.
