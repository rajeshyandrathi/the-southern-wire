Colombia’s government asks X to suspend former president Petro’s account after he calls for the president to resign

The government of Abelardo de la Espriella says Gustavo Petro’s posts risk inciting violence. Petro calls it censorship, and the opposition is talking about a national strike.

World Desk·Monday, 23 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • Colombia’s ICT ministry asked X on Sunday to suspend Gustavo Petro’s account, which has 9.1 million followers
  • Petro had posted a call for President Abelardo de la Espriella to resign
  • Petro does not recognise the June election result; observers backed the official count
  • X had not responded by Monday

Colombia’s government has asked the social media platform X to suspend the account of former president Gustavo Petro, citing possible incitement to violence, MercoPress reported on Monday 23 November.

The request came on Sunday from the Ministry of Information and Communications Technologies under President Abelardo de la Espriella. It said the network should not become the main tool for spreading what it called an anti-democratic and destabilising plan, and asked X to consider the reach of Petro’s account, which has 9.1 million followers, and the role social media played in Colombia’s 2021 protests.

What triggered it

On Saturday, Petro posted video of a speech he gave in Cali during opposition marches, with a message asking Colombians to demand the president’s resignation now.

Petro called the request censorship and said the ICT minister, Alexandra Falla, was violating the constitution. Senator Iván Cepeda of the Historic Pact called it despotic and said the opposition was preparing a national strike and civil disobedience.

Even some of Petro’s critics questioned the move. Lawyer Carlos Cortés, a former Twitter employee, said Petro’s post did not break X’s rules, and political scientist Andrés Celis said the campaign against Petro would end up strengthening him, MercoPress reported.

Background

De la Espriella won a runoff election on 21 June. Petro has refused to recognise the result and alleges fraud, although election observers backed the national registry’s count. In late October the Council of State ordered Petro to delete posts accusing the registrar of fraud.

What happens next

X had not responded by Monday, MercoPress and the Rio Times reported. The dispute is a test of how far governments can push platforms to silence opposition figures, and of how deep Colombia’s post-election divide runs ahead of regional elections in 2027.
