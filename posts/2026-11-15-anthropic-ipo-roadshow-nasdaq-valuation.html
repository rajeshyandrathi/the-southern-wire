Anthropic is on the road selling a trillion-dollar question: what is an AI company actually worth?

Tech Desk

With its Nasdaq debut now four days away, Anthropic's roadshow has spent this past week doing the part of an IPO that rarely makes headlines but decides everything: convincing institutional investors that the price on the prospectus is the right one. The company is offering 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each, a range that puts enormous weight on where exactly within it the final price lands, since the gap between the low and high end of that band is worth billions once multiplied across the full offering.

The harder number to pin down is the one that actually matters most: what Anthropic is worth as a company, not just what its shares are priced at. Reporting through the roadshow has shown investors converging on strikingly different figures, with some camps discussing a valuation as low as $1.2 to $1.3 trillion and others treating $1.8 to $2 trillion as the fair number, a spread wide enough to represent an entire extra Anthropic sitting between the two estimates. Any of those figures would roughly double the $965 billion valuation the company commanded at its last private funding round in May, which is itself the real story here: even the most conservative number on the table assumes the AI boom's pricing logic holds for another six months.

Three of the industry's biggest names, Google, Nvidia and TSMC, have already signed on as cornerstone investors, a move that functions less like an ordinary allocation and more like a public vote of confidence from the companies with the clearest view of Anthropic's actual compute demand and chip orders. Cornerstone investors buying in before a stock even opens are betting their own capital on a valuation nobody outside the roadshow room has fully agreed on yet, which tells generalist investors something specific: the people closest to Anthropic's supply chain are comfortable with the price range, whatever the eventual number turns out to be.

The listing itself remains set for Thursday under the ticker ANT, a date that was fixed before this week's roadshow even started and has not moved despite the valuation uncertainty still working itself out in real time. That is unusual discipline for an offering this large; most IPOs of comparable scale leave themselves room to slip the date if investor appetite looks shaky. Anthropic holding the line on Thursday suggests either genuine confidence in demand, or a calculated bet that further delay would do more reputational damage than a messy price discovery process playing out in public.

What happens Thursday will settle more than one company's valuation. A strong open at the top of the range would be read across the AI sector as confirmation that the industry's eye-watering private valuations translate into public market demand. A soft open, or a price that settles toward the bottom of the band, would hand every skeptic of the AI investment cycle their most concrete data point yet. Either way, the roadshow's job was never really to sell Anthropic. It was to find out, for the first time with real money on the table, what the market actually believes an AI company at this scale is worth.
