Recovered chats place a suspended JMPD officer among alleged hitmen, the Madlanga commission hears

An intelligence witness told the commission that 109 messages recovered from a wiped phone link a senior metro police officer to men accused of contract killings, including the murder of DJ Sumbody.

Crime Desk·Tuesday, 24 November 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Witness C told the commission that 109 screenshots were recovered from suspended JMPD officer Johannes Mokgatle’s wiped phone
  • The messages allegedly show him passing vehicle owners’ details and police reports to alleged contract killers
  • The witness linked him to a car used in the 2022 killing of DJ Sumbody; Mokgatle has not given his side publicly
  • Another witness, “Witness T”, is due to testify today

The Madlanga commission into alleged corruption and political interference in the criminal justice system heard on Monday 23 November how a senior Johannesburg metro police officer allegedly kept company with men accused of being contract killers.

The evidence came from Witness C, a member of the Gauteng counter-intelligence operations team whose identity is protected. Part of the testimony was heard behind closed doors, TimesLIVE reported. It concerned Johannes “Johnny” Mokgatle, a senior officer in the JMPD dog unit, who is suspended, faces internal charges and remains under investigation. He has not publicly responded to the allegations, and none of them has been tested in court.

Witness C said he interviewed Mokgatle at home in February 2026 and took his phone. The phone had been wiped, but investigators recovered 109 screenshots of messages, Daily Maverick reported.

What the messages allegedly show

According to the testimony, the messages show Mokgatle in contact with alleged hitmen linked to a group known as the “Big Five” or “The Firm”. He allegedly ran vehicle registration numbers through police systems and sent back the owners’ details, and passed on internal police operational reports. In one message, former detective Michael Pule Tau allegedly wrote that he wanted the group to kill someone, and asked for .38 Special bullets.

Witness C told the commission that two days before engineer Armand Swart was shot in 2024, Mokgatle allegedly received four registration numbers to check and passed the owners’ details to Katiso “KT” Molefe. Mokgatle had told investigators he did not know why the details were wanted, Daily Maverick reported.

The witness also linked Mokgatle to a car used in the November 2022 killing of DJ Sumbody, who was shot dead with his two bodyguards in Woodmead. He said the vehicle was still in the hands of the accused about eight months later, and that the chats placed Mokgatle as part of the group, TimesLIVE reported. Molefe is accused of orchestrating that murder.

A pattern the commission is tracing

The commission also heard that in December 2024, while booked off as unfit for duty, Mokgatle allegedly drove to Molefe’s house in a JMPD vehicle after Molefe reported a suspicious car outside, and confronted the police officers who were watching the house. Molefe was arrested later that day. Tau, Molefe, Musa Kekana and Tiego Floyd Mabusela face criminal charges in related cases.

The evidence goes to the heart of what the commission was set up to examine: whether criminal networks have been able to use people inside law enforcement. Witness T is due to testify on Tuesday. The commission’s report is due to President Ramaphosa by 25 January 2027, Daily Maverick reported.
