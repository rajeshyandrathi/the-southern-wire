Laura Wolvaardt leads a Proteas Women's side building quietly toward T20 World Cup contention

Sport Desk·Tuesday, 3 November 2026

While the men's team basks in the afterglow of a series win over Australia, South Africa's Proteas Women are going about their own preparations with far less fanfare and, by captain Laura Wolvaardt's own account, a growing sense that this squad is finally assembling the pieces to go all the way at the upcoming T20 World Cup. Wolvaardt has spoken of a team peaking at the right moment, a notable change in tone from a programme that has spent much of the past decade being talked about as perennial underachievers relative to its talent.

That reputation has always been somewhat unfair. South Africa's women have reached multiple global semi-finals and a World Cup final without ever closing out the biggest prize, a pattern of heartbreak that has shaped how the current squad is now approaching its build-up — with a deliberate emphasis on composure under pressure rather than raw talent, which the team has rarely lacked.

Wolvaardt's own form has been central to that shift. Her captaincy has coincided with a more settled top order and a bowling attack that has started to look genuinely dangerous in the format's tightest margins, the ones that have historically separated South Africa from the very best sides rather than any gap in skill. Domestic structures feeding into the national side have also matured, giving selectors deeper pools to draw from than in previous World Cup cycles.

None of that guarantees anything once the tournament itself begins, where South Africa's past near-misses have often come down to a single over or a single dropped catch rather than any structural weakness. But the squad heading into this cycle carries a different energy than previous vintages — less burdened by the weight of unfulfilled potential, more focused on simply finishing what it starts.

If the Proteas Women do go one step further than their men's counterparts managed in white-ball cricket in recent years, Wolvaardt's calm insistence that this group is building toward something, rather than merely hoping for it, will look like the understatement of the tournament.
