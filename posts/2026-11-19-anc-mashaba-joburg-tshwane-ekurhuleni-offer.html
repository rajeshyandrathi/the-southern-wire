The ANC’s price for a Gauteng deal: Mashaba gets Johannesburg, the DA gets nothing

The Southern Wire Desk·Johannesburg

The ANC has finally put a number on what it wants from Gauteng's coalition scramble, and the number is zero — as in zero metros for the party that actually won the most votes. Under the proposal now on the table, Herman Mashaba would be installed as mayor of Johannesburg, Nasiphi Moya would stay exactly where she is as Tshwane's mayor, and the ANC would keep Ekurhuleni for itself. The Democratic Alliance, which topped the ballot in Johannesburg and ran close behind elsewhere, would be shut out of executive power across the entire province it dominated at the polls.

It is a strikingly blunt offer, and it tells you precisely what the ANC has decided it can live without. Having lost its outright majority almost everywhere in Gauteng, the party has concluded that governing with ActionSA as the senior partner beats governing with the DA as any kind of partner at all — even if that means ceding the mayoralty of the country's largest city to a man who spent the last election campaign attacking the ANC's governance record as hard as he attacked the DA's. Mashaba gets the chain he has openly coveted; the ANC gets to keep its fingerprints on all three administrations without sharing a council chamber with Geordin Hill-Lewis.

The clean part of the deal is also its weakest point. Reports diverge sharply on who takes the deputy mayoralships that come with it: one account has the MK Party slotted in as deputy somewhere in the arrangement, while another insists no agreement on deputy posts exists at all. That is not a rounding error — deputy mayor seats are where coalition partners bank the committee chairs, oversight roles and patronage that make a junior partnership worth signing up for, and a proposal that is silent or contradictory on them is a proposal that is not actually finished.

Johannesburg remains the single hardest knot to untie, and it is not the DA holding it up this time — it is Mashaba himself, who is understood to be pushing for guarantees on the mayoralty that go beyond what even this ANC offer currently promises in writing. Meanwhile Hill-Lewis has flatly dismissed the idea that he would step aside for Helen Zille to unlock a deal with ActionSA, closing off the one personnel trade that briefly looked like it might break the impasse from the DA's side. The DA has not simply absorbed the snub either: it has sent its own competing coalition proposal into the mix, a reminder that it still commands enough council seats in Johannesburg to make any ANC-ActionSA arrangement there uncomfortable to govern with.

All of this is happening against a deadline that does not bend for internal party arithmetic. Parties in South Africa's hung municipalities have fourteen days from the declaration of results to conclude coalition talks and elect mayors, and Gauteng's three biggest metros are now deep into that window with Johannesburg still unresolved. A council that fails to elect a mayor inside that period risks an administrative vacuum at exactly the moment ratepayers, infrastructure contracts and municipal budgets need a functioning executive signing off on them.

What is emerging, deal or no deal, is a map of Gauteng governance that treats the DA's plurality in Johannesburg as politically irrelevant. Whether that holds depends on whether Mashaba can get the guarantees he wants, whether the MK Party's role gets nailed down or quietly dropped, and whether the DA's rival proposal gives any ANC negotiator pause before they sign something that leaves the country's largest city opposition-free in name but still watched by an opposition with enough seats to make life difficult.
