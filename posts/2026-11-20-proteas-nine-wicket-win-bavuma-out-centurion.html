Harmer finishes Bangladesh off, the Proteas go top of the table, and Bavuma misses Centurion

Simon Harmer’s 6 for 51 sealed a nine-wicket win at the Wanderers before lunch on day five. South Africa lead the series 1-0, but captain Temba Bavuma’s calf strain rules him out of the second Test.

Sport Desk·Friday, 20 November 2026·2 min read

Key points

  • South Africa beat Bangladesh by nine wickets
  • Simon Harmer took 6 for 51 on the final morning
  • Proteas lead the two-Test series 1-0
  • South Africa move to the top of the World Test Championship table
  • Temba Bavuma out of the Centurion Test with a calf strain

It was not the innings win South Africa had hoped for at the start of the final day, but it was close enough. Simon Harmer took six wickets on a turning Wanderers pitch to bowl Bangladesh out for 168, leaving the Proteas just 12 to win. They got there for the loss of one wicket before lunch on Thursday to take a 1-0 lead in the two-Test series.

Bangladesh began the day on 100 for 5, still 57 runs behind, and for a while Litton Das made it uncomfortable. He hooked and pulled the seamers for 39 off 38 balls before Kagiso Rabada found the inside edge onto his stumps. After that, Harmer took over. Mushfiqur Rahim fell for 22 after a successful review, and the off-spinner worked through the tail to finish with 6 for 51.

Rabada had a big match with bat and ball. He made a career-best 72 on the fourth morning and took eight wickets across the two innings. Marques Ackerman’s maiden Test hundred and Ryan Rickelton’s runs had built a total of 557 in reply to Bangladesh’s 400, a lead that left the visitors batting to survive for most of the last two days.

The win lifts South Africa to the top of the World Test Championship table. The bad news is Bavuma. Scans before day four showed a strained right calf, and although he batted bravely to 68 not out, he has been ruled out of the second Test at Centurion next week.

Coach Shukri Conrad also gave a nudge to Keshav Maharaj, who was not in the side in Johannesburg. Conrad said the left-arm spinner can force his way back into the Test team, which will be worth watching on a Centurion surface that could suit two spinners.

How the match was won

Bangladesh actually batted well in their first innings, with Towhid Hridoy’s 137 helping them to 400, their highest total against South Africa. The visitors recovered from 121 for 5 thanks to a big sixth-wicket stand, and for two days the match looked even.

South Africa answered with 557, a lead of 157, built on centuries and some stubborn lower-order batting. Rabada, Bavuma and Dane Paterson added 171 runs on the fourth morning, a stretch that took the game away from Bangladesh. Late wickets that evening, two of them to Harmer, left the tourists on 100 for 5 and facing defeat.

Who leads at Centurion

Bavuma’s injury means South Africa need a stand-in captain for the second Test, a choice that will be closely watched given how settled the side has been under him.

Centurion has traditionally helped fast bowlers more than spinners, but Conrad’s comments about Maharaj suggest the team is at least considering a second spinner. With Harmer in this kind of form, a two-spinner attack would test a Bangladesh batting line-up that had no answer to Harmer on the final morning at the Wanderers.
